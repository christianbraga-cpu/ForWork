Entry Rules by Passport

US passport. No visa is needed for stays of 90 days or less. Your passport needs 3 months' validity beyond your departure date and 2 blank pages. Source: US Department of State.

UK passport. Travel to the Schengen area without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Your passport must be issued less than 10 years before you arrive and expire at least 3 months after you leave. EES may record fingerprints and a photo. Source: GOV.UK entry requirements.

Australian passport. Visit the Schengen area, which includes Portugal, without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Under the Entry/Exit System (EES), fingerprints and a photo are taken on arrival at no cost. ETIAS has no confirmed start date. Source: Smartraveller Portugal advice.

Marrying Legally, by Passport

Portugal's civil registry rules apply to every couple, whatever your passport. We walk you through the exact route for your nationality.

  1. Contact the Registo Civil. Start the marriage process online or in person at any Registo Civil (Portugal's civil registry office).
  2. Gather your documents. ID, a birth certificate, and a certificate of capacity to marry from the last six months, or a statement that your country issues none.
  3. Submit at least one month before your date. The registrar agrees the date, time and place; a ceremony away from the registry or out of hours is agreed in advance.
  4. Marry within six months of approval. The registrar's authorisation gives you up to six months. Your marriage is valid only once it is registered.

Good to know:

  • Documents in English, French or Spanish need no translation; others need a certified translation into Portuguese.
  • If you do not speak Portuguese and the official does not speak your language, an interpreter is appointed.
  • Witnesses are optional: two to four may take part.
  • Both partners must be 18 or over.
  • You declare one of three forms: civil, Catholic, or civil marriage in religious form.
  • The civil registry charges a fee for the ceremony.

US Passport

The US Embassy in Lisbon does not issue a CNI, because no US authority exists to issue one.
Check the US Embassy's marriage page for the document Portugal accepts.
The Embassy advises allowing at least four weeks for processing. These steps apply to US citizens only.

UK Passport

If you live in Portugal or another country outside the UK, you do not need a CNI to marry in Portugal. If the registrar asks for one, show the British Embassy's notice on marriage in Portugal. If you live in the UK, use the FCDO online service to see which document you might need; you need a valid British passport to use it.

Australian Passport

To prove you are free to marry, the usual documents are a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) or certificates from a registry of births, deaths and marriages. The Australian Embassy in Lisbon can issue a CNI. DFAT also issues CNIs in Australia, but some countries accept only a CNI issued locally. Confirm with the Registo Civil or the Embassy of Portugal in Canberra which document you need and where it must be issued. An overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia, but in most cases it is legally recognised if it was valid in Portugal and would be valid under Australian law.

Sources: Justiça and IRN; the US Embassy in Lisbon; GOV.UK on living in Portugal and the FCDO service; Smartraveller on marrying overseas and the Australian Embassy in Lisbon.

Get Answers Before You Enquire

1. What Are the Requirements for Getting Married in Portugal?

Foreign couples marry through Portugal's civil registry: start the process online or at any Registo Civil at least one month before your date, and marry within six months of approval. You need ID, a birth certificate and a certificate of capacity to marry from the last six months, or a statement that your country issues none. The full steps by passport are under Marrying legally.

2. Can US, UK or Australian Citizens Get Legally Married in Portugal?

Yes, but each passport proves freedom to marry in a different way. US citizens: the US Embassy in Lisbon does not issue a CNI (Certificate of No Impediment). Check the US Embassy's marriage page for the document Portugal accepts. UK nationals living outside the UK need no CNI; UK residents check the FCDO online service. Australians: the Australian Embassy in Lisbon can issue a CNI; confirm with the Registo Civil or the Embassy of Portugal in Canberra which document you need.

3. Is a Catholic or Symbolic Ceremony Legal in Portugal?

Yes for a Catholic ceremony: Catholic marriage is one of three legally recognised forms in Portugal, and it is valid once registered with the civil registry. A symbolic ceremony is not a legal marriage by itself. Other faiths can marry through civil marriage in religious form, with a church or religious community established in Portugal.

4. How Much Is an Average Wedding in Portugal?

There is no single average: the cost depends on your guest count, the region, the season, the nights you take the venue and whether you book it for exclusive use. Wedded Concierge talks you through the options for your plans.

5. When Can You Get Married in Portugal?

On a date you agree with the registrar, at least one month after you organise the civil process and within six months of approval. For weather, travel guides often recommend spring or autumn. Heat waves are common in July and August, wildfire risk is highest from June to September, and heavy rain is most common from October to February.

6. Where Is the Best Place to Get Married in Portugal?

Portugal has five wedding regions, served by four airports: Lisbon, Sintra and Cascais; the Algarve; Porto and the Douro Valley; Comporta and the Alentejo; and Madeira. Choose Sintra for palaces, the Algarve for clifftops and beaches, the Douro for vineyards, Comporta for the Alentejo coast and Madeira for an Atlantic island. Fly into Lisbon (LIS), Faro (FAO), Porto (OPO) or Madeira (FNC).

7. What Are Some Good Wedding Venues in the Algarve, Portugal?

Algarve wedding venues include clifftop and beach settings, and Albufeira City Council offers a public municipal space for civil ceremonies. Ask us which suit your guest count.

8. Can You Have a Beach Wedding in Portugal?

Yes, a beach wedding in Portugal is possible with a permit. On designated bathing beaches, the local council licenses events under Decree-Law 97/2018; elsewhere on the coast, the local Captain of the Port is responsible. Ask us which applies to the beach you have in mind.

9. Can You Have a Small Wedding or Elopement in Portugal?

Yes. Witnesses are optional in a Portuguese civil ceremony, so the two of you can marry with no guests at all, once the civil process has started at least one month ahead. The simplest route is a civil ceremony at the registry, for a fee. Or hold a symbolic ceremony in Portugal and marry legally at home, as some couples do.

10. Can You Get Married in a Sintra Palace?

Yes: Parques de Sintra hires out Monserrate, Pena, Queluz, the National Palace of Sintra and the Portuguese School of Equestrian Art for private celebrations, including weddings. State events can cancel bookings at Queluz or the National Palace of Sintra, and Monserrate's roof works run until the first quarter of 2027.
