\### **Marrying Legally, by Passport**

(Tab label: Marrying legally; anchor: \#marrying-legally)

Portugal's civil registry rules apply to every couple, whatever your
passport. We walk you through the exact route for your nationality.

1\. **Contact the Registo Civil.** Start the marriage process online or
in person at any Registo Civil (Portugal's civil registry office).

2\. **Gather your documents.** ID, a birth certificate, and a
certificate of capacity to marry from the last six months, or a
statement that your country issues none.

3\. **Submit at least one month before your date.** The registrar agrees
the date, time and place; a ceremony away from the registry or out of
hours is agreed in advance.

4\. **Marry within six months of approval.** The registrar's
authorisation gives you up to six months. Your marriage is valid only
once it is registered.

Good to know:

- Documents in English, French or Spanish need no translation; others
  need a certified translation into Portuguese.

- If you do not speak Portuguese and the official does not speak your
  language, an interpreter is appointed.

- Witnesses are optional: two to four may take part.

- Both partners must be 18 or over.

- You declare one of three forms: civil, Catholic, or civil marriage in
  religious form.

**Q1. What Are the Requirements for Getting Married in Portugal?**

Foreign couples marry through Portugal's civil registry: start the
process online or at any Registo Civil at least one month before your
date, and marry within six months of approval. You need ID, a birth
certificate and a certificate of capacity to marry from the last six
months, or a statement that your country issues none. The full steps by
passport are under <u>Marrying legally</u> (#marrying-legally).

**Q2. Can US, UK or Australian Citizens Get Legally Married in
Portugal?**

Yes, but each passport proves freedom to marry in a different way. US
citizens: the US Embassy in Lisbon does not issue a CNI (Certificate of
No Impediment).

**APPLY AFTER SIGN-OFF (F043, Nic):** The Embassy says you sign a
statement of eligibility before a consular officer instead.

**USE UNTIL SIGNED OFF:** Check the US Embassy's marriage page for the
document Portugal accepts.

UK nationals living outside the UK need no CNI; UK residents check the
FCDO online service. Australians: the Australian Embassy in Lisbon can
issue a CNI; confirm with the Registo Civil or the Embassy of Portugal
in Canberra which document you need and where it must be issued.

**Q3. Is a Catholic or Symbolic Ceremony Legal in Portugal?**

Yes for a Catholic ceremony: Catholic marriage is one of three legally
recognised forms in Portugal, and it is valid once registered with the
civil registry. A symbolic ceremony is not a legal marriage by itself.
Other faiths can marry through civil marriage in religious form, with a
church or religious community established in Portugal.

**Q4. How Much Is an Average Wedding in Portugal?**

**APPLY AFTER SIGN-OFF (F069, Nic):** EUR 25,000 to 70,000 for about 100
guests, according to Rita Plácido Photography (listed October 2026).
Catering runs EUR 80 to 200 per person including drinks, and venue
rental about EUR 3,000 to 15,000 or more. These are third-party figures,
not Wedded Wonderland prices, and amounts in AUD, USD or GBP vary with
the exchange rate.

**USE UNTIL SIGNED OFF:** There is no single average: the cost depends
on your guest count, the region, the season, the nights you take the
venue and whether you book it for exclusive use. Wedded Concierge talks
you through the options for your plans.

**Q5. When Can You Get Married in Portugal?**

On a date you agree with the registrar, at least one month after you
organise the civil process and within six months of approval. For
weather, travel guides often recommend spring or autumn. Heat waves are
common in July and August, wildfire risk is highest from June to
September, and heavy rain is most common from October to February.

**Q6. Where Is the Best Place to Get Married in Portugal?**

Portugal has five wedding regions, served by four airports: Lisbon,
Sintra and Cascais; the Algarve; Porto and the Douro Valley; Comporta
and the Alentejo; and Madeira. Choose Sintra for palaces, the Algarve
for clifftops and beaches, the Douro for vineyards, Comporta for the
Alentejo coast and Madeira for an Atlantic island. Fly into Lisbon
(LIS), Faro (FAO), Porto (OPO) or Madeira (FNC).

**Q7. What Are Some Good Wedding Venues in the Algarve, Portugal?**

Algarve wedding venues include clifftop and beach settings, and
Albufeira City Council offers a public municipal space for civil
ceremonies. Ask us which suit your guest count.

**Q8. Can You Have a Beach Wedding in Portugal?**

Yes, a beach wedding in Portugal is possible with a permit. On
designated bathing beaches, the local council licenses events under
Decree-Law 97/2018; elsewhere on the coast, the local Captain of the
Port is responsible. Ask us which applies to the beach you have in mind.

**Q9. Can You Have a Small Wedding or Elopement in Portugal?**

Yes. Witnesses are optional in a Portuguese civil ceremony, so the two
of you can marry with no guests at all, once the civil process has
started at least one month ahead.

**APPLY AFTER SIGN-OFF (F024, Nic):** A civil ceremony at the registry
costs EUR 120, or EUR 200 outside the registry or out of hours.

**USE UNTIL SIGNED OFF:** The simplest route is a civil ceremony at the
registry, for a fee.

Or hold a symbolic ceremony in Portugal and marry legally at home, as
some couples do.

**Q10. Can You Get Married in a Sintra Palace?**

Yes: Parques de Sintra hires out Monserrate, Pena, Queluz, the National
Palace of Sintra and the Portuguese School of Equestrian Art for private
celebrations, including weddings. State events can cancel bookings at
Queluz or the National Palace of Sintra, and Monserrate's roof works run
until the first quarter of 2027.
