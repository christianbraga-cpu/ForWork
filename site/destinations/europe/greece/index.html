Season and weather risks

  • The Meltemi wind blows across the Cyclades in July and August, so plan a sheltered option for Santorini and Mykonos.
  • Wildfires are frequent from April to October, especially in southern and island regions.
  • Heatwaves can top 38°C (100°F) and have closed the Acropolis.
  • Flash floods are most common from October to February.
  • 1 to 15 August is a Greek holiday period, so ferries, hotels and vendors book out early.

Sources: travel.state.gov, Smartraveller, Destination Wedding Directory, The Knot.

Getting there

Athens is the main international gateway. From there, domestic flights reach Santorini, Mykonos, Chania, Heraklion, Rhodes and Corfu, and ferries run to the Cyclades and beyond.

  • From Australia: Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane and Perth connect to Athens through a Gulf or Asian hub.
  • From the US: fly into Athens from New York and Los Angeles, direct or with one connection depending on the season.
  • From the UK: London has flights to Athens all year, and seasonal flights to many islands.
  • Between islands: ferries suit the Cyclades; domestic flights are faster for Crete, Rhodes and Corfu.

Routes and schedules change by season. Your Wedded Advisor checks current flights for you and your guests.

Marrying legally

You can marry legally in Greece in a civil or religious ceremony. What you need depends on your passport, and the paperwork takes time on the ground, so many couples hold a symbolic ceremony in Greece and marry legally at home.

PassportWhat you needSource
AustralianA Certificate of No Impediment for each non-Greek partner, from the Australian Embassy in Athens or a DFAT office. Most Greek authorities want documents in Greek, translated officially, so confirm with your priest, celebrant or planner where the certificate should be issued.Australian Embassy Athens
USNo residency needed. Contact the town hall before you travel. The licence is generally issued 8 days after you apply, so plan time on the ground, including at least 2 days in Athens for paperwork. The marriage must be registered locally within 40 days.US Embassy Athens
UKCheck local law, then use the gov.uk service to find which document proves you are free to marry and how to apply.gov.uk

Same-sex couples: same-sex marriage has been legal in Greece since 2024 (travel.state.gov).

Greek Orthodox ceremonies: clergy may ask for baptism certificates. Contact the Archdiocese of Athens early.

What a Greek quote leaves out

  • VAT: the standard rate is 24%, so check whether a quote includes it.
  • Service charge: often 10% to 20% on food and drink.
  • Minimum spend: many venues set one, whatever your guest count.
  • Island suppliers: some venues name the sound, lighting or other suppliers you must use, and vendors from outside may need permits.
  • Cash and transfers: some local vendors ask to be paid in cash, and boats, transfers and ferries for guests add up.

Sources: Big Fat Greek Day (2026), Destination Wedding Directory (2026), Weddings Abroad Guide (2026), Johnny Africa (2025).

FAQs

Get answers before you enquire

Are you legally married if you get married in Greece?

Yes, if you hold a civil or religious ceremony with the right documents and the marriage is registered. For US citizens, the US Embassy in Athens says the marriage must be registered at the local registry office within 40 days and is not legal until it is. Australians need a Certificate of No Impediment, and UK citizens check gov.uk for the document that proves they are free to marry. A symbolic ceremony is not a legal marriage, so many couples marry at home first.

Can same-sex couples marry legally in Greece?

Yes. Same-sex marriage has been legal in Greece since 2024. Smartraveller notes that attitudes vary, and that the islands, including Mykonos and Santorini, are generally more accepting.

Do you need a civil, religious or symbolic ceremony in Greece?

Choose a civil ceremony through the local town hall if you want to marry legally in Greece, a Greek Orthodox ceremony if you belong to the faith (clergy may ask for baptism certificates), or a symbolic ceremony if you will marry legally at home. A symbolic ceremony needs no Greek paperwork and can be held at almost any venue.

How much does a small wedding in Greece cost?

Published 2026 guides put a wedding for about 20 guests at EUR 8,000 to 18,000 (Big Fat Greek Day) and up to 20 guests at EUR 6,000 to 15,000 (Weddings Abroad Guide). Island, season, VAT and service charges move the number, so your Wedded Advisor costs it on your date. See Costs.

What does a wedding in Crete cost compared with Santorini?

Big Fat Greek Day (2026) puts 100 guests on Crete or the mainland at EUR 20,000 to 40,000, and 100 guests on Santorini at EUR 40,000 to 70,000. Santorini costs more because caldera venues are small and in high demand.

Which Greek island is best for a wedding?

Santorini for a caldera sunset with a small group, Mykonos for a beach club party, Crete for olive estates and bigger guest lists, the Athens Riviera for the easiest guest access, Rhodes for the Lindos chapel and a medieval town, and Corfu for a green Ionian setting and a UNESCO-listed old town. Compare the islands.

Is Greece or Italy better for a destination wedding?

Both suit May, June and September. Greece gives you islands, sea views and a weekend that can end on a boat, with Athens as the single gateway. Italy suits couples who want villas, vineyards and cities within driving distance. Both let foreign couples marry legally with documents from home, and your Wedded Advisor checks the steps for your passports. See weddings in Italy.

What is the best month to get married in Greece, and how far ahead should you book?

May, June, September and early October. July and August bring heat, crowds and the Meltemi wind on the Cyclades. Book 12 to 18 months ahead for Santorini, Mykonos and peak Saturdays.

How long does a Greek wedding usually last?

The ceremony is the short part. Dinners start late and often run past midnight, and most destination couples stretch the celebration into a 3-day weekend with welcome drinks, the wedding and a boat day or farewell lunch.

What happens at a Greek wedding?

At a Greek Orthodox wedding the couple are crowned with the stefana, share a common cup and walk the Dance of Isaiah three times around the table, guided by the koumbaros. Guests take home koufeta, sugared almonds in odd numbers. Couples holding a civil or symbolic ceremony often borrow one or two of these customs.
