Get married in France, from a Loire château to a Riviera terrace

A wedding in France can be a three-day château weekend in the Loire, a palace-hotel dinner in Paris, a long table under plane trees in Provence or a terrace above the Mediterranean. Here are the regions and wedding venues in France that suit each, the legal and symbolic routes for your passport, the best months, real costs, and one Wedded Advisor to plan it with you.

Rows of lavender, cicadas at dusk, a stone mas and a long table under the plane trees, with calissons from Aix-en-Provence as favours.

A UNESCO World Heritage city with the Cité du Vin, and château estates near Saint-Émilion for a wine-led weekend.

  • The traiteur. Food usually comes from an outside caterer the venue approves.

Getting there

  • Fly into Paris or Nice. Paris Charles de Gaulle for Paris, the Loire, Champagne and Burgundy; Nice Côte d'Azur for the Riviera; Marseille for Provence; Bordeaux-Mérignac for Bordeaux.
  • Then the TGV. High-speed trains run from Paris to Provence (Avignon, Aix-en-Provence), Bordeaux, Tours for the Loire, Reims and Dijon.
  • From London. Eurostar runs to Paris Gare du Nord in about 2 hours 16 minutes.
  • From Australia and the US. Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane and Perth connect to Paris with one stop; Los Angeles and New York fly to Paris direct. Your Wedded Advisor plans routes and group fares for your guests.

On arrival, guests usually take a shuttle from the TGV station or airport to the venue. Your advisor books the transfers.

Entry rules by passport

France is in the Schengen area. Australian, American and British passport holders can visit for short stays of up to 90 days in any 180 days without a visa.

  • Check that passports are valid for at least 3 months after you leave the Schengen area.
  • The EU's ETIAS travel authorisation for visa-free visitors is being introduced. Check the current start date on the official EU site, travel-europe.europa.eu, before you book.
  • Guests on other passports may need a Schengen visa. Your advisor can send invitation letters.

Marrying legally in France

In France, only the civil ceremony at the mairie (town hall) is legally binding, and any religious ceremony must come after it. To marry there, one of you, or a parent of one of you, needs a lasting link to that commune.

1. Symbolic in France, legal at homeMost Australian, American and British couples sign the legal paperwork at home, before or after, then hold a symbolic ceremony at their venue on any date and in any setting. Marriages made in France are generally recognised by US states, but check with yours.
2. Legal in France, through residenceOne of you lives in the commune for at least 1 month continuously by the date the banns are published. Banns are posted for 10 days, and the wedding cannot take place before the 10th day after publication. In practice that is about 40 days in France.
3. Legal in France, through a parent's townIf a parent of either of you has their home or residence in a French commune, on the same 1-month rule, you can marry there. This suits couples with a French parent.

Both partners must be at least 18. Same-sex couples who live in a country that does not allow their marriage can marry in the French commune of their birth or last residence, or where a parent lives. Expect to provide passports, birth certificates, proof of address and, where relevant, divorce or death certificates; each mairie sets its exact list.

FAQ

Get answers before you enquire

Can you legally get married in France as an Australian, American or British citizen?

Yes, but only with a lasting link to the town. French law requires that one of you, or a parent of one of you, has their home or residence in the commune, with residence shown by at least 1 month of continuous living there on the date the banns are published. Banns are then posted for 10 days before the civil ceremony at the mairie. Most Australian, American and British couples marry legally at home and hold a symbolic ceremony in France. Same-sex couples who live in a country that does not allow their marriage can marry in the French commune of their birth or last residence, or where a parent lives. See Marrying legally.

Can you get married in Paris?

Yes. A symbolic ceremony can be held at a palace hotel, a hôtel particulier or a private salon on any date the venue is free. A legal civil wedding takes place at a Paris town hall (mairie) and needs the same residence link as anywhere in France: 1 month of continuous residence in that commune, for you or a parent, plus 10 days of banns.

How much does a wedding in France cost?

WeddingsAbroadGuide.com (2026) puts micro weddings for 2 to 20 guests at EUR 8,000 to 20,000, intimate weddings for 20 to 30 guests at EUR 20,000 to 40,000, mid-sized weddings for 50 to 80 guests at EUR 40,000 to 85,000, and luxury weddings for 100 or more guests at EUR 85,000 to 150,000 or more. Flights and guest rooms are on top. Your Wedded Advisor costs your own dates, region and guest count before you commit.

Is it expensive to get married in the south of France?

The Riviera is the most expensive part of France to marry in. WeddingsAbroadGuide.com (2026) says a Riviera or central Paris wedding for 50 to 80 guests commonly costs EUR 60,000 to 110,000, and FrenchWeddingVenues.com lists venue hire across Provence and the Riviera from about EUR 6,750 to over EUR 32,900. Inland Provence and the south-west usually cost less than the coast for the same guest count.

Is a wedding in France cheaper than in the UK or the US?

Not like for like. A wedding in France adds flights, guest rooms and often two or three days of events, and venues price in euros. It can cost less overall when it replaces a large wedding at home with a smaller guest list, because a château weekend for 40 guests can cost less than a 150-guest reception at home. Your Wedded Advisor can compare both on your numbers.

Where are the best places to get married in France?

Six regions lead: the French Riviera for sea-view hotels and villas, Provence for stone mas and vineyard domaines, Paris and Île-de-France for palace hotels and châteaux near the city, the Loire Valley for Renaissance châteaux, Bordeaux and the Dordogne for wine estates and château buyouts, and Champagne and Burgundy for cellars and vineyards. Compare the regions.

When is the best time of year for a wedding in France, and how far ahead should you book?

May, June and September are the best months across most of France, with the Riviera, Provence, Bordeaux and Paris also good into October. Peak season runs June to September, and many local suppliers holiday in August. Book prime Provence, Loire and Riviera venues 18 to 24 months ahead; the Dordogne and Normandy usually need 9 to 15 months.

Can your guests stay at the château?

Often, yes. Many French wedding venues are booked as a whole property for 2 or 3 nights with rooms on site. For example, Château de Challain in the Loire hosts 150 guests with lodging for 50, and Domaine d'Aveny near Paris hosts 200 with beds for 94. Guests who do not fit stay in nearby gîtes and hotels, with shuttles to the venue.

What is a vin d'honneur, and what other French traditions can you add?

The vin d'honneur is a drinks reception straight after the ceremony, to which you can invite more people than the dinner. Couples also add a croquembouche in place of a tiered cake, a Champagne tower, onion soup served to the last dancers after midnight, and témoins (witnesses) instead of a full bridal party.

Do you need a planner who speaks French?

It helps. Many French venues are dry hire, so catering, rentals and staff are booked separately, contracts are often in French, and rules such as music curfews differ by venue.
