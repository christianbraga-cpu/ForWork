# The American couple: Lauren and Daniel

**Snapshot.** Lauren (34, product director at a tech company) and Daniel (36,
attorney), Brooklyn, New York. Engaged 6 months. Want to marry in Italy in
about 12 to 15 months with 60 to 90 guests, most flying from the US East
Coast. Budget up to USD 110,000, mostly their own money with some help from
Lauren's parents. Have been to Italy twice (Rome, Florence, a few days on the
Amalfi Coast) but never dealt with anything official there.

**Goals**
- A wedding in Italy that is legally valid back home, without surprises.
- To understand exactly which documents they need, where to get them and in
  what order, from the US side.
- To decide between a legal ceremony in Italy and getting married legally at
  home first with a symbolic ceremony in Italy.
- To keep both careers going while planning, with as little admin as possible.

**Fears**
1. Getting the paperwork wrong (wrong document, missing apostille or
   translation, missed appointment) and finding out too late that the marriage
   isn't legal or can't happen on the date.
2. The marriage not being recognized in the US, or problems later with a name
   change, taxes, insurance or immigration paperwork.
3. Not knowing who to trust for the legal side in Italy, and paying for help
   they didn't need.
4. Costs creeping up across currencies, fees and translations on a budget that
   is generous but has a ceiling.

**Knowledge level.** Daniel is a lawyer and reads legal steps closely, but
knows nothing of Italian civil procedure. Neither knows what a Nulla Osta,
an apostille or a "dichiarazione giurata" is, or whether a US state matters.

**Trust signals.** Official sources (State Department, US Embassy and
Consulates in Italy, Italian authorities) with dates; steps in order; honest
statements of what varies by town or consulate; clear advice on when to get
help and when not to.

**Turn-offs.** Vague "it's easy" claims, unsourced lists copied from other
blogs, advice written for other nationalities, upselling, clichés, prices
with no currency or date.

**Reading behavior.** Reads on a laptop in the evening, opens the official
links in new tabs, sends the article to their partner with notes. Comes back
to it as a checklist.

**Would enquire when.** They trust the legal steps, can see which parts a
professional actually helps with, and the next step is low-pressure (a free
call, not a hard sell).
