# The British couple: Sophie and James

**Snapshot.** Sophie (33, management consultant) and James (35, architect),
London. Engaged 8 months. Want to marry in Greece, most likely an island, in
about 12 months with 50 to 80 guests flying from the UK. Budget is generous
and comfortably luxury, mostly their own money with some help from both
families. Have holidayed in Greece several times but never dealt with anything
official there.

**Goals**
- A wedding in Greece that is legally recognised in the UK, without surprises.
- To know exactly which documents they need, where to get them and in what
  order, from the UK side (and whether it differs for England and Wales,
  Scotland or Northern Ireland).
- To decide between a legal ceremony in Greece and a legal ceremony at home
  with a celebration in Greece.
- To keep both careers going while planning, with as little admin as possible.

**Fears**
1. Getting the paperwork wrong (wrong document, missing apostille or
   translation, missed deadline) and finding out too late that the marriage
   isn't legal or can't happen on the date.
2. The marriage not being recognised at home, or problems later with a name
   change, a passport or a mortgage application.
3. Not knowing who to trust in Greece, and paying for help they didn't need.
4. Costs creeping up across fees, translations and currencies.

**Knowledge level.** Organised and research-minded, but knows nothing of Greek
civil procedure. Doesn't know what a certificate of no impediment, an
apostille or a Greek marriage licence involves, or which UK office issues what.

**Trust signals.** Official sources (gov.uk, the FCDO, the British Embassy
Athens, the General Register Office, Greek authorities) with dates; steps in
order; honest statements of what varies by municipality; clear advice on when
to get help and when not to.

**Turn-offs.** Advice written for Americans or Australians, vague "it's easy"
claims, unsourced lists copied from other blogs, upselling, clichés, prices
with no currency or date.

**Reading behaviour.** Reads on a phone on the commute and on a laptop at
night, opens the gov.uk links, sends the article to their partner. Comes back
to it as a checklist.

**Would enquire when.** They trust the legal steps, can see which parts a
professional actually helps with, and the next step is low-pressure.
