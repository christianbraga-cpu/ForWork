# The Multicultural Family: Priya and Daniel

**Snapshot.** Priya (31, pharmacist, Indian-Australian, Melbourne) and Daniel
(32, Greek-Australian). Planning a 3-day celebration abroad for 180 guests:
a mehndi and sangeet, a Hindu ceremony, a Greek Orthodox blessing and a
reception. Both families contributing; grandparents travelling from Chennai and
Thessaloniki.

**Goals**
- Both traditions honoured properly, not as decoration.
- Venues that allow fire ceremonies, late music, specific catering and multi-day hire.
- Guests from three countries able to travel and stay together.

**Fears**
1. Being treated as a novelty, or a planner who does not understand the rituals.
2. Venue restrictions discovered too late (fire, noise curfews, outside caterers, priests).
3. Visa and travel complexity for relatives from different countries.
4. Dietary needs (vegetarian, Jain, halal) handled badly.

**Knowledge level.** High on their own traditions, low on what venues abroad allow.

**Trust signals.** Evidence of past multi-faith or multi-day weddings, specific
logistics (curfews, priests, caterers), language that respects tradition,
diverse imagery.

**Turn-offs.** One-size-fits-all "white wedding" framing, exoticising language,
imagery that only shows one kind of couple, no mention of multi-day events.

**Reading behaviour.** Shares in family WhatsApp groups; several people read
and comment. Looks for photos of people like them.

**Would enquire when.** They see proof the brand can handle their traditions and scale, and that restrictions will be checked up front.

- Brand pages: "Multi-day celebrations" tag on packages; 20 to 300 guest capacity.
- To collect: number of multi-faith or multi-day enquiries, common venue restrictions.
