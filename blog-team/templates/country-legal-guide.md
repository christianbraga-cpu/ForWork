# Template: "Getting Married in {Country}" legal guide

Use for every article in the cluster "Getting Married in {Country}". First built
from the France guide (articles/2026-11-16-getting-married-in-france, 16 Nov
2026), with lessons from Italy (2026-10-26) and Greece (2026-11-12).

## Inputs the strategist confirms
- Can two foreign non-residents marry legally in {Country}? (yes, yes with
  conditions, or no). This decides the article's opening and the order of the
  route sections.
- Official sources for {Country}: the national government portal, the town
  hall or registry, {Country}'s embassy or consulates in Australia, the
  Australian Embassy in {Country}, Smartraveller's {Country} page.
- Reuse the Australian-side research pack (CNI, apostille, recognition, name
  change, passports) from the latest country guide; re-check fees after each
  1 January.

## H2 structure (fixed; adapt wording to the keyword)
1. **Getting married in {Country}: can foreigners marry legally?** The plain
   answer in the first 60 words, sourced. Include the keyword here.
2. **Residency and eligibility.** Exactly as the official source states it.
3. **Can't marry legally in {Country}? Marry in Australia, celebrate in
   {Country}.** Always include, even where the legal route is open. Say plainly
   how much paperwork this route avoids. Link the Australian licence pillar
   (/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements) once it is live.
4. **Documents from Australia.** Table: document, who issues it, translation,
   apostille. Fees with year. Avoid "ask your town hall" in every cell; say it
   once above the table.
5. **Translations and apostilles.** Define terms on first use; give the local
   term only if sourced.
6. **Banns and timeline.** Backward timeline from the wedding date.
7. **The civil ceremony.** Where, who officiates, witnesses (link the
   witnesses spoke once live).
8. **Religious and symbolic ceremonies.** Short; legal order of ceremonies.
9. **Recognition and name change back in Australia.** Smartraveller wording,
   "usually accepted"; state registry name-change rules.
10. **Planning the wedding week.** Illustrative only, no promises.
11. **FAQ** (4 to 6, from People Also Ask, no repeats of the body).

## Rules carried from earlier runs
- One sentence comparing with a country we already cover, when it helps the
  reader choose (e.g. France does not allow non-resident couples; Italy does).
- Confirm requirements with the deciding authority (town hall, embassy,
  consulate, DFAT, registry), never the Wedded Advisor or concierge.
- No "most couples", no outcome promises, no unsourced definitions.
- Concierge only as brand-profile.md describes it; no price, referral or named
  contact until the user supplies CONCIERGE-PAY.
- Destination link: /destinations/<region>/<country>; plus the country's live
  inspiration articles from link-map.md.
- 1,900 to 2,500 reader words. Writers overshoot: brief for 2,300 and do not
  repeat body paragraphs in the FAQ.
- (Vietnam, 17 Nov 2026) The timeline table must agree with the one-month NOIM
  rule and the destination's processing time: on the marry-in-Australia route,
  the NOIM goes in about 12 months out and the legal ceremony happens a few
  weeks before you fly; the destination week holds no legal step.
- (Vietnam) When a country has more than one Australian mission, state each
  mission's CNI procedure separately; never extend one post's fee, language or
  timing to another ("Ask the Embassy" when unsourced).
- (Vietnam) No research-method language in reader copy ("search snippets",
  "we could not read"); write "We last reviewed these sources in <month year>",
  never a single-day "we checked every source" claim.
- (Vietnam) When the legal answer is uncertain, advise not to pay venue
  deposits until the deciding authority answers in writing.
- (Malta, 17 Nov 2026) When the country IS open to non-residents, H2 3 becomes
  "Prefer to marry in Australia? Marry at home, celebrate in {Country}" so the
  heading does not contradict the body.
- (Malta) Collect the unanswered "ask the registry" points into one short
  "Questions to ask the {registry}" list instead of repeating the line in prose.
  Gloss each form code once in plain words.
- (Malta) Write residency as the source does ("its guideline states no
  residency requirement"), not as a stronger rule; never place a venue in a
  town without a source.
- (Jamaica, 17 Nov 2026) Write fees exactly as the source does: DFAT's notarial
  page says "from AUD 181" and "from AUD 105", so keep "from" with the year.
  Never comment on what other articles or sites say ("older articles mention a
  blood test"); state the official rule only.
- (Jamaica) Do not define legal terms (banns, apostille) unless a fact in the
  pack defines them, and never close an open question in the same sentence
  that sends it to the authority.
- (Germany, 17 Nov 2026) Related links: the map's 2 or 3 plus the
  concierge, destination and one service page; never link DRAFT siblings in a
  country guide (they become 404s if publish order slips). Brief writers for
  about 22 links in total.
- (Germany) Where rules are set city by city, name the city in every sentence
  (Berlin, Munich) and never generalise to the country; quote embassy changes
  with their exact date wording ("from 1 December 2021") and every post named.
- Categories: the primary category only unless categories.md confirms others.
  SEO title 60 characters or fewer, no year unless the content is dated to it.
