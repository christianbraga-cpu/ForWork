# Style guide

The writer follows this guide and the editorial director reviews against it.
ICP readers never see it.

## Voice

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Specific: names, distances, months, guest counts | Vague: "stunning", "breathtaking", "magical" |
| Calm and assured | Breathless or salesy |
| Expert without jargon; terms explained on first use | Insider shorthand |
| Warm, second person ("you", "your guests") | Corporate ("clients", "the customer") |
| Honest about trade-offs (heat in August, transfer times) | Promising perfection |

**Benchmark:** would Condé Nast Traveller run it, and would a parent paying
AUD 200K trust us after reading it?

## Structure of every article

1. **H1** containing the primary keyword, written for a human.
2. **Intro (80 to 140 words):** name the reader's situation, promise what the
   piece answers, use the keyword naturally in the first 100 words.
3. **Key takeaways:** 3 to 5 bullets, each a complete, specific sentence.
4. **Body:** H2 sections that each answer one question a reader actually has.
   At least one section shows the invisible work of end-to-end planning.
5. **Mid-article soft CTA** (one sentence, links to `/concierge` or a package).
6. **FAQ:** 4 to 6 questions with direct answers (feeds FAQ schema).
7. **Closing CTA** from `cta-library.md`.

Paragraphs: 2 to 4 sentences. Sentences: average under 22 words. Use lists for
steps and comparisons, prose for atmosphere.

## Banned words and phrases (auto-major if used)

dream wedding, fairytale, magical, once in a lifetime, breathtaking, stunning,
nestled, boasts, hidden gem, picture-perfect, say "I do" (as a cliché opener),
tie the knot, big day (more than once), elevate, unforgettable, seamless,
effortless, bespoke (unless describing an actual custom-made item), curated
(as filler), luxury (more than twice per article), unparalleled, world-class,
look no further, in today's world, whether you're... or..., when it comes to,
it's no secret, embark, journey (for planning), delve, tapestry, testament.

## House mechanics

- **No em dashes.** Use a comma, colon, full stop or parentheses.
- Australian spelling. Numbers: words for one to nine, digits from 10; always
  digits for guest counts, prices, distances and times.
- Prices: "From AUD 38,550". Never "$" alone. Never a price without "From".
- Distances in km. Temperatures in °C.
- Dates: 12 May 2027. Seasons named by month, since hemispheres differ.
- Brand names exactly: Wedded Wonderland, Wedded Concierge, Wedded Advisor,
  Wedded Marketplace, Wedded Privileges.
- Headings in sentence case.

## Writing for two readers

Every article must give the couple something to imagine and the payer
something to trust. Check: is there at least one concrete reassurance (who is
on the ground, what happens if it rains, how money is handled, what is legal)?

## Claims

Every factual statement (law, price, distance, capacity, season, statistic) gets
a claim ID in the claims register and maps to a research fact. If there is no
source, cut it or mark it `[[NEEDS: ...]]`.
