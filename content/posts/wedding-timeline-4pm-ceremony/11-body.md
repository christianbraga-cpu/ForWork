A wedding timeline for a 4pm ceremony puts getting ready across the morning, photos either before the ceremony (with a first look) or straight after it, drinks as the light softens, and dinner and speeches into the evening. The one time that fixes everything else is sunset at your venue on your date, so look that up first and build the photos, the lighting and the reception entrance around it.

## Key takeaways

- Look up sunset for your exact venue and wedding date, then build the photos and dinner back from it.
- A first look moves the couple and wedding party photos before 4pm, so you join your guests for drinks.
- Without a first look, photos follow the ceremony and the reception starts later in the evening.
- Moving the ceremony to 2pm, 3pm or 5pm changes the light, the heat, the gap before dinner and how guests travel.

## Wedding timeline for a 4pm ceremony at a glance

In the two example days below, hair and makeup begins at 9am with a first look or 10am without one, guests arrive at 3.30pm, the reception entrance is at 6pm or 6.30pm, and the send-off is at 10.45pm or 11pm.

Every clock time on this page is a planning example to adapt, not a rule. Your team, your venue and your sunset will each move them.

## A 4pm wedding timeline with a first look

A first look is a private moment before the ceremony when the couple see each other dressed, with only the photographer nearby. It lets you finish the bulk of the photos before guests arrive. A 4pm ceremony with a first look might run like this, for example:

- **9am.** Hair and makeup begins.
- **12.30pm.** The photographer arrives for details: rings, invitation suite, shoes, florals.
- **1pm.** Getting-ready photos while the last touches are done.
- **1.45pm.** Dressed and ready, with a quiet quarter hour to eat something.
- **2pm.** First look, then couple portraits close to where you are getting ready.
- **2.45pm.** Wedding party photos.
- **3.15pm.** Any family photos you are happy to take before the ceremony.
- **3.30pm.** The couple goes out of sight. Guests arrive and are seated.
- **4pm.** The ceremony begins.
- **4.30pm.** The ceremony ends. A short family photo list, then you join your guests for drinks.
- **Before sunset, if it falls during drinks.** Slip away for 15 to 20 minutes of portraits in the low light.
- **6pm.** Guests are seated and the couple makes the reception entrance.
- **6.15pm.** Entrée.
- **6.45pm.** Speeches, kept together in one block.
- **7.30pm.** Main course.
- **8.15pm.** Cake cutting, then the first dance.
- **8.30pm.** The dancing opens.
- **10.30pm.** The final song.
- **10.45pm.** The send-off.

The ceremony line assumes half an hour. Readings, rituals and music change that, so check [how long a wedding ceremony takes](/expert-advice/how-long-is-a-wedding-ceremony) before you lock in drinks.

## A 4pm wedding timeline without a first look

Keeping the first sight for the aisle simply moves the photo work to after the ceremony. A 4pm ceremony without a first look might look like this, for example:

- **10am.** Hair and makeup begins.
- **1.30pm.** The photographer arrives for details and getting-ready photos, each side separately.
- **2.30pm.** Dressed. Individual portraits and photos with each half of the wedding party.
- **3.30pm.** Out of sight. Guests arrive and are seated.
- **4pm.** The ceremony begins.
- **4.30pm.** The ceremony ends. Family photos straight away, while everyone is still gathered.
- **5pm.** Full wedding party photos.
- **5.30pm.** Couple portraits. If sunset falls later, keep 15 minutes of them back for the low light.
- **6.15pm.** You join the last of drinks, then guests are seated.
- **6.30pm.** Reception entrance and entrée.
- **7.15pm.** Speeches.
- **8pm.** Main course.
- **8.45pm.** Cake cutting and the first dance.
- **9pm.** The dancing opens.
- **11pm.** The final song and the send-off.

The cost is time with your guests, so ask the venue to hold canapés and a drink for you both, and keep the family photo list short and written down. ## How sunset shapes a 4pm wedding day

### Find the sunset for your venue and date

Sunset is the anchor for a 4pm day, and the time that matters is the one for your venue on your date. [Geoscience Australia's sunrise, sunset and twilight calculator](https://geodesyapps.ga.gov.au/sunrise) works these times out using the National Gazetteer of Australia. You can search for a place name or enter a latitude and longitude, including places outside Australia, choose a time zone, and get sunset and twilight times for a single day or a whole year.

Look up the single day of your wedding at the venue itself, not the nearest city. Then work back: portraits finish around sunset, drinks sit either side of them, and the reception entrance follows.

[Geoscience Australia defines sunset](https://www.ga.gov.au/scientific-topics/astronomical/astronomical-definitions) as the moment the upper edge of the sun meets an ideal horizon, and the published time assumes flat surrounding ground. At a venue with hills, cliffs, trees or buildings to the west, the sun drops out of view before the listed time. Walk the site at the same hour, or ask your photographer when the light leaves that lawn or terrace.

### Plan the light after sunset

Soft light lingers after sunset, but it does not last. Have the festoons, candles and uplights on before evening civil twilight ends, not after guests notice the dark. On the same definitions, by the end of civil twilight large objects can still be seen, but no detail can be made out without artificial light. That point comes when the centre of the sun is 6 degrees below the horizon.

Later, at the end of evening nautical twilight, it is dark for normal practical purposes, with the centre of the sun 12 degrees below the horizon. If guests walk between the ceremony and reception spaces outdoors, along garden paths, terraces or a coastal road, move them before then or light the route, and check the path is safe for anyone unsteady on their feet.

Read against a 4pm ceremony, the calculator tells you which version of the day you have. If sunset falls during drinks, your golden portraits come straight after the family photos. If it falls during dinner, plan a quiet 15 minutes away between courses. If it falls before the ceremony ends, the ceremony takes the last of the light, so portraits belong before it, a first look is worth a serious thought, and the ceremony site needs its own lighting.

## Where the buffer belongs in a 4pm day

A buffer is not padding spread evenly. Place it at the handovers, where one part of the day waits on another.

- **Hair and makeup.** Add 15 minutes before the dress goes on. This is the block that runs late first.
- **Between the ceremony and the family photos.** Guests want to hug you. Give it ten minutes, then have someone call the first group.
- **Before the reception entrance.** A quarter hour lets the couple catch their breath and lets the venue seat stragglers.
- **Speeches.** Agree a length with each speaker, and run them in one block so the kitchen can plan around it.

The drinks hour is where guests feel the timeline. Make sure food arrives with the first drink, there is shade or warmth depending on the season, and there is somewhere to sit. Once the room is seated, the order of courses, speeches and dances is covered in our [wedding reception timeline](/expert-advice/wedding-reception-timeline).

## Wedding day timeline for a 2pm ceremony

A 2pm ceremony might look like this, for example: hair and makeup from 7.30am, first look at 12pm, guests arriving at 1.30pm, the ceremony at 2pm, drinks from 2.30pm, the reception at 5pm and the send-off at 10.30pm.

**What changes.** The sun is higher and harder at 2pm, which flatters fewer faces and makes shade at the ceremony site a real question. In warm months, heat is the issue, especially for older guests: water at the guests' seats, a covered aisle or a short service.

The gap before dinner is long, so choose a plan rather than letting it drift: a generous drinks with substantial canapés, an afternoon tea, or a break for guests to return to their rooms before an evening reception. Guests travel in the late morning, so anyone arriving the same day has the least room for a delayed flight or a slow drive.

## Wedding timeline for a 3pm ceremony

A 3pm ceremony might look like this, for example: hair and makeup from 8.30am, first look at 1pm if you want one, guests arriving at 2.30pm, the ceremony at 3pm, drinks from 3.30pm, the reception at 5.30pm and the send-off at 10.30pm.

**What changes.** The light is gentler than at 2pm, though still bright in summer. The gap before dinner is long enough for every photo without a first look, which is why this start suits couples who want to keep the aisle reveal and still spend time at drinks. In warm months, the ceremony may still sit in the heat of the day, so ask where the shade falls at 3pm. Guests travel after lunch, which is easier for those coming in the same morning.

## Wedding timeline for a 5pm ceremony

A 5pm ceremony might look like this, for example: hair and makeup from 11am, first look at 3pm, guests arriving at 4.30pm, the ceremony at 5pm, drinks from 5.30pm, the reception at 7pm and the send-off at 11.45pm.

**What changes.** The ceremony itself may sit in the low light, which flatters photos and is kind to guests in warm weather. In winter, sunset may come before or during the ceremony, so lighting at the ceremony site moves from optional to essential. Daylight after the ceremony may be short, which makes a first look close to necessary if you want couple and wedding party portraits in daylight. The gap before dinner matches the 4pm first look day, but the evening runs an hour later, so if the venue has an early curfew, that hour comes out of the dancing.

### A 4.30pm ceremony

A 4.30pm ceremony sits between the two. It keeps the 4pm shape, pushes dinner and the send-off back half an hour, and narrows the photo window if sunset is early. Check the curfew before you choose it.

## Planning a 4pm ceremony at a destination wedding

A destination day has more fixed points than a day at home, and several of them belong to the venue rather than to you. Ask these questions before you set the ceremony time:

- **Curfews and noise rules.** What time must amplified music stop, and does it stop earlier outdoors? When must guests leave the grounds?
- **Local timing customs.** What time do local guests and vendors expect dinner to begin? Is a late dinner normal here, and does the kitchen staff for it? If family or religious customs fix any part of the day, set those times first and tell the venue early.
- **Guests arriving the same day.** What time can guests check in, and how far is the ceremony from where they are staying? Is there a quiet fallback if a flight lands late?
- **Older and less mobile guests.** How far is the walk to the ceremony spot, and is there a route without steps between the ceremony, drinks and dinner spaces? Where can they sit in shade, or out of the cold, while photos are taken?
- **The welcome dinner the night before.** How late can it run without taking the edge off the wedding morning? Does it share the venue's curfew?
- **Guests watching remotely.** Can the venue's connection carry a livestream from the ceremony spot, and how will access to it be kept private? What time will a 4pm ceremony be for family watching at home?
- **The sun.** For a venue outside Australia, cross-check the time with [the US Naval Observatory's one-day calculator](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/RS_OneDay), and ask the venue which way the ceremony site faces.

## Frequently asked questions

### What time should hair and makeup start for a 4pm ceremony?

It depends on the number of people being styled and how long each look takes. In the examples above, it starts at 9am with a first look and 10am without one. Ask your team to work back from the time you need to be dressed, then add 15 minutes.

### What time should guests arrive for a 4pm ceremony?

For example, put 3.30pm on the invitation as the arrival time and 4pm as the start, so guests can find a seat and settle. With a large guest list or formal seating, ask the venue how long seating takes and move the arrival time earlier. At a destination venue, allow for the journey from where they are staying.

### When should the photographer arrive for a 4pm wedding?

Early enough to capture details and getting ready before you are dressed: 12.30pm with a first look and 1.30pm without one in these examples. Confirm where your coverage ends.

### What time does a 4pm wedding finish?

That is set by the venue's curfew and the hours you have booked. In these examples, the send-off is at 10.45pm with a first look and 11pm without one. Ask the venue for the exact time music must stop and guests must leave.

### Is 4pm a good time for a wedding ceremony?

It suits couples who want a relaxed morning, gentler light than midday and a reception that runs into the evening. Check sunset at your venue on your date before you commit.

### How long is the gap between a 4pm ceremony and the reception?

In these examples, about an hour and a half, filled with photos and drinks, and longer without a first look. Feed guests early and give them shade or warmth.
