## How the topic is handled
- Three countries: the article says once that Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania each have their own language and customs, then ties every custom to the country its source names. Nothing is presented as pan-Baltic.
- Latvia: mičošana, the headdress change, from latvia.travel; bride stealing and the pūrs (dowry chest) as family custom ("often told").
- Lithuania: gaubtuvės (the rue wreath removed and the bride's head covered), the matchmaker and the traditional sequence ending with atgrąžtai, bread and salt, the kraitis (dowry) and the gates on the way, from the Lithuanian encyclopaedia (vle.lt), the national broadcaster and lithuania.travel. The painted chest and rue meanings as family custom.
- Estonia: tanutamine (the bonnet ceremony), with the apron, gifts and Kihnu roles, from an Estonian Literary Museum wedding glossary (in Estonian).
- UNESCO: Kihnu cultural space, Suiti cultural space and sutartinės are tied to weddings, because their own pages mention weddings. Seto leelo and the Song and Dance Celebrations appear as cultural context only.
- A short "myths worth leaving out" section corrects the common unsourced claims without referring to an earlier version of the page.
- Culture, not politics. Travel and legal (as at November 2026): Smartraveller Estonia and Lithuania at normal precautions; Latvia normal, with "Reconsider your need to travel" near the Belarus border, stated plainly and with suggestions limited to places well away from it. The Australian Embassy in Stockholm covers Estonia and Latvia, Warsaw covers Lithuania. Smartraveller on certificates of no impediment and recognition in Australia. Registry steps: "check with the registry and the embassy, including any fees".
