Why do we have bridesmaids? Because a wedding is easier, and happier, with people you trust beside you. A bridesmaid is a young woman or girl who helps a bride before and during the marriage ceremony, and that simple job has carried the role through centuries of change. <!-- fact_102 -->

This guide follows the bridesmaid from the wedding feasts of classical Athens to Queen Victoria's twelve in white, then turns to the decisions in front of you now: maid or matron of honour, how many to ask, bridesmen, and asking friends to travel. For the wider picture, see our guide to [wedding traditions](/expert-advice/wedding-traditions).

## Key takeaways

- We have bridesmaids so a bride has people she trusts beside her, helping before and during the ceremony, with the maid of honour leading them. <!-- fact_102 --> <!-- fact_100 -->
- Queen Victoria had twelve bridesmaids in 1840, all eldest daughters of peers, dressed in white to her own design. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_05 -->
- Traditionally, bridesmaids received a piece of jewellery, such as a brooch or locket, as a gift or souvenir of the wedding. <!-- fact_08 -->
- For a wedding abroad, ask attendants early and talk openly about travel, time off and who covers what before anyone books.

## 1. Why do we have bridesmaids today?

Couples ask friends to stand beside them for support, company and a witness to the moment. A bridesmaid helps the bride before and during the marriage ceremony. <!-- fact_102 --> In practice that means a steady hand on the morning, a voice of calm when plans wobble, and a familiar face at the front.

Many couples now choose attendants by closeness, not convention. A sister, a school friend, a brother, a partner's cousin: the role goes to the people who have been there all along.

The role carries a little teasing too. An early form of ["Always a bridesmaid, never a bride"](https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/acref/9780198734901.001.0001/acref-9780198734901-e-248) appeared in 1882, and the "often a bridesmaid" version was popularised by mouthwash advertisements from the 1920s. <!-- fact_03 -->

## 2. Where bridesmaids come from

Attendants at weddings are very old. In classical Athens, bridesmaids danced at wedding feasts, and at a [Roman wedding](https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/acref/9780199545568.001.0001/acref-9780199545568-e-3980) the matron of honour, the pronuba, linked the right hands of the bride and groom. <!-- fact_13 --> <!-- fact_10 --> The Roman bride's look has its own story, which we follow in our guide to [why brides wear veils](/expert-advice/why-do-brides-wear-veils).

The English word arrived much later. "Bridesmaid" dates from the late eighteenth century, an alteration of the earlier "bridemaid". <!-- fact_102 -->

By the nineteenth century, when our notions of wedding customs began to settle, groups of eight or twelve girls or women often accompanied and supported the bride. They were often young sisters and cousins of the bride and groom, as well as female friends. <!-- fact_07 -->

## 3. Queen Victoria's bridesmaids and royal attendants

Queen Victoria married Prince Albert on 10 February 1840 in the chapel at St James's Palace. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_04 --> She had twelve bridesmaids, all the eldest daughters of peers. <!-- fact_05 -->

The Queen [sketched their dress herself](https://www.rct.uk/collection/980021-o/design-for-queen-victorias-bridesmaids-dresses): a simple white design trimmed with sprays of roses. She gave the sketch to her Mistress of the Robes, the Duchess of Sutherland, who supervised the making. <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_05 -->

The bridesmaids also had work to do. They carried the Queen's 18-foot train, and they struggled to shepherd it down the aisle. <!-- fact_01 -->

That detail still matters. A long train or cathedral veil goes best with one or two named attendants who have rehearsed the actual aisle, steps and turns of your ceremony venue.

Prince Albert designed a gift for the twelve: an eagle-shaped turquoise brooch, taken from the heraldic German eagle. The blue of the stones suggests forget-me-nots, and the pearls represent true love. <!-- fact_06 -->

## 4. Maid of honour or matron of honour: what is the difference?

The maid of honour is the most important bridesmaid at a wedding. <!-- fact_100 --> The term is used especially in the US, where the maid of honour is the main bridesmaid. <!-- fact_103 --> A [matron of honour](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/matron-of-honour) is a married woman who helps the bride. <!-- fact_101 -->

So the difference is marriage, not rank. Both lead the bridal party. Whether your lead attendant is married or not makes no difference to what they do.

Many couples now skip the labels altogether. A "person of honour" can be a brother or a best friend, and the lead attendant can stand for a partner of any gender.

Some couples name two: a sister and a closest friend, sharing the speech and the jobs. Agree early who does what, so neither feels second.

## 5. Why bridesmaids wear matching dresses

Royal bridesmaids dressed alike in 1840, all in white to the single design the Queen drew. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_04 -->

Later in the century, parties were coordinated too. At the 1884 wedding of Val Prinsep and Florence Leyland, the bridesmaids wore canary yellow, with [brooches engraved "Val and Flo"](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/brooches-for-bridesmaids) from the groom. <!-- fact_09 --> The design sheet for the Gibson wedding specifies six matching bridesmaids' brooches in silver. <!-- fact_09 -->

Younger attendants had their own fashion. Around 1903 it was fashionable for younger bridesmaids and pageboys to wear outfits based on historic or national costume, and one ivory satin dress of the time copies English styles of the 1630s and 1640s. <!-- fact_12 -->

For colours, cuts and how to choose, see our guide to [what bridesmaids wear](/expert-advice/what-do-bridesmaids-wear).

## 6. How many bridesmaids should you have?

There is no right number. Nineteenth-century parties of eight or twelve were common, but a single attendant is just as complete. <!-- fact_07 -->

Many couples decide with a few questions:

- **Who would you be sorry to leave out?** Start there, then stop.
- **How does it look across the aisle?** Balanced sides are a choice, not a rule. A bride with five and a groom with two is fine.
- **What does the space allow?** A small chapel or a clifftop ceremony may suit three, not ten.
- **Who else will be travelling?** At a destination wedding, every attendant is a guest making a real commitment.

## 7. What bridesmaids and the maid of honour do, before and on the day

The job is help, before and during the ceremony. <!-- fact_102 --> Every party shares it differently, and the best ones agree it out loud. For the personalities that make a party work, read [the types of bridesmaids every bride needs](/5-types-of-bridesmaids-every-bride-needs).

### Before the wedding

- **Listen.** The lead attendant is often the first call when plans change.
- **Plan the celebrations.** A pre-wedding celebration that suits the bride, not the group chat.
- **Coordinate.** When attendants live in different cities, one trusted person keeping fittings and dates on track saves everyone time.
- **Keep the list.** Names, arrival times, dietary needs and phone numbers in one place.

### On the wedding day

- **The morning.** Calm, coffee, and keeping time while hair and make-up run.
- **The dress and train.** Buttons, bustle, and the train on the aisle, rehearsed beforehand.
- **The bouquet.** Holding it during the vows and the signing.
- **The details.** Tissues, pins, a phone charger, and a quiet word with the photographer.
- **The evening.** Getting the bride a plate of food and a glass of water, and a speech if asked.

## 8. Bridesmen and mixed wedding parties

A bridesman stands on the bride's side. A groomswoman stands on the groom's. Both are now familiar, and few guests look twice.

The [groomsman](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/groomsman), or usher in the UK, is a friend of the groom with special duties at the wedding, including showing people to their seats. <!-- fact_104 --> A bridesman takes on the bridesmaid's jobs in the same way: help, company and a steady presence.

A few practical points for a mixed party:

- **Getting ready.** Decide where everyone dresses, and who joins the bride for the morning.
- **The procession.** Walk in pairs, in order of height, or by closeness. There is no fixed pattern.
- **The name.** "Wedding party" or "attendants" includes everyone on the invitation and in the speeches.

## 9. Bridesmaids at a destination wedding

Asking someone to be a bridesmaid abroad is asking for their leave, their travel and several days of their year. Ask early and in person, and give them room to say no.

### Questions to talk through together

- Who covers flights and accommodation, and is that the same for every attendant?
- Who covers hair and make-up, and does anyone need a different arrangement?
- How many days are attendants expected, and which events are optional?
- Can partners and children come, and where do they stay?
- If family are helping to pay for the wedding, should they be part of this conversation?

Have the conversation before anyone books, so every attendant knows what is covered and what is theirs to arrange.

### Before anyone flies

As at November 2026, Smartraveller advises getting travel insurance whoever you are and wherever you are going, and says the right policy is as essential as a passport. <!-- fact_105 --> A policy should cover every country on the route, including stopovers. <!-- fact_106 --> Each attendant should tell their insurer about any pre-existing health conditions. <!-- fact_108 --> Read the [Smartraveller travel insurance guidance](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) together.

Each attendant checks their own passport and visa, including for every country they transit through. <!-- fact_109 --> <!-- fact_110 --> Sharing the itinerary with family at home and carrying hard copies of flight, accommodation and event bookings are sensible habits for the whole party. <!-- fact_111 -->

### On the ground

- **Time off.** Build in a free afternoon. Attendants who flew a long way want to see the place.
- **One contact.** Name one person from your side, such as the maid of honour, as the venue team's contact for the party's timings, so the venue hears one voice, not every attendant.
- **Culture.** Learn a few local words, ask how guests dress for a church or temple, and follow the local rhythm of the day.
- **Wellbeing.** Long flights, heat and late nights add up. Water, shade and sleep before the wedding day.

## 10. How to thank your bridesmaids

Thanking bridesmaids is a long-standing part of the role. Traditionally, bridesmaids received a piece of jewellery, such as a brooch or locket, as a gift or souvenir of the wedding. <!-- fact_08 --> In the UK the groom customarily gave the gifts, while nineteenth-century American brides gave gifts to their own bridesmaids. <!-- fact_08 -->

In 1884 Val Prinsep gave his bridesmaids brooches engraved "Val and Flo". <!-- fact_09 --> Prince Albert went further and designed his own. <!-- fact_06 -->

A personal keepsake still works best, and it travels easily to a wedding abroad. Pair it with a handwritten note that names one thing each person did. Something with a family story can carry [something old, something new](/expert-advice/something-old-something-new) into the gift.

The other thanks happen on the day: each attendant thanked by name in the speeches, a quiet moment before the ceremony, and time with them after the photographs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do we have bridesmaids?

To help the bride before and during the ceremony, and to stand with her through the day. <!-- fact_102 --> Weddings in classical Athens and Rome had attendants too. <!-- fact_13 --> <!-- fact_10 -->

### What is the difference between a maid of honour and a matron of honour?

The maid of honour is the most important bridesmaid. <!-- fact_100 --> A matron of honour is a married woman in the same lead role. <!-- fact_101 --> The duties are the same.

### Is it okay to have no bridesmaids?

Yes. Many couples marry with one attendant or none, especially at a small ceremony abroad. A sibling or friend can still hold the rings and stand beside you.

### Can a man be a bridesmaid?

Yes. A bridesman takes on the same role on the bride's side, and many couples now have mixed wedding parties.

### Do bridesmaids have to travel for a destination wedding?

No one has to. Ask early, talk openly about who covers travel and time off, and make it easy for a friend to decline and still come as a guest.
