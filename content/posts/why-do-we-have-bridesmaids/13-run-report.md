## How the topic is handled
- History in brief, from reference and museum facts: Athenian bridesmaids dancing at wedding feasts and the Roman matron of honour joining the couple's hands (one sentence); the "three times a bridesmaid, never a bride" saying, with its 1882 early form; Queen Victoria's twelve bridesmaids in 1840, all eldest daughters of peers, in white to her own design, carrying her 18-foot train, each given Albert's eagle brooch.
- No origin story without a source. The "confuse evil spirits" story is not used and not mentioned.
- Maid versus matron of honour: "the difference is marriage, not rank", from dictionary definitions.
- Today, as living practice: how many to ask, duties before and on the day, bridesmen and mixed parties, and thanking attendants.
- Destination: asking early, travel questions to talk through (who covers what, including family helping to pay, with no amounts), one contact from the couple's side for the venue team, Smartraveller's insurance advice dated "as at November 2026", and each attendant checking their own passport and visa.
