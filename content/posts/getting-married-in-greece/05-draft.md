Getting married in Greece as an Australian comes down to one early decision: how you want to be married. You can marry legally in a civil ceremony performed by the municipality, marry in a Greek Orthodox church, or marry at home first and hold a symbolic celebration on the island.

The legal steps come first, because they set the order of everything else. Paperwork starts in Australia, passes through translation and the Apostille, and finishes with a Greek registrar and a certificate you carry home. Once that path is clear, the island, the month and the venue fall into place around it.

## Key takeaways

- A Greek marriage that was valid where it took place, and would have been valid under Australian law, is in most cases legally recognised in Australia, although it cannot be registered here. <!-- fact_15 -->
- Each Greek municipality sets its own document list, and in Rhodes that list includes a Certificate of No Impediment, Greek translations and the Apostille on documents from outside the European Union. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_02 -->
- A Greek Orthodox wedding follows church rules, including which partners may marry in the Church and which dates are closed, so the church date comes before the venue. <!-- fact_24 --> <!-- fact_30 -->
- Weather differs by island, so read temperatures and rain days against the official station for each region rather than a single figure for Greece. <!-- fact_34 --> <!-- fact_36 --> <!-- fact_38 --> <!-- fact_40 -->

## Can Australians marry legally in Greece?

Yes. Australians can marry legally in Greece, and the marriage can count at home. As at November 2026, an overseas marriage is in most cases legally recognised in Australia if it was valid under the law of the country where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law had it happened here. <!-- fact_15 -->

There is one rule that surprises many couples. You cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia. <!-- fact_15 --> The Greek marriage certificate is the record you keep, and it is usually accepted as proof of the marriage. <!-- fact_15 -->

Australia does not recognise an overseas marriage where either person is already married to someone else, is not old enough to marry, is too closely related to the other, or was forced to marry. <!-- fact_14 -->

Recognition also closes a door at home. If Australia recognises your Greek marriage, you cannot marry again in Australia, though you may hold another kind of ceremony, such as a confirmation of vows or a recommitment ceremony. <!-- fact_16 -->

Same-sex couples: our guide to [same-sex marriage in Greece](https://weddedwonderland.com/destination-weddings/same-sex-marriage-greece) covers what you need.

The Australian Government's guide to [getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) is the starting point for the Australian side. The country you marry in may have rules of its own, such as a residence period, and the Greek authorities are the ones to ask about them. <!-- fact_12 -->

Three routes lead to a Greek wedding day. Two of them make you legally married in Greece. The third is a celebration of a marriage you have already made at home.

### A civil ceremony at the town hall

A civil ceremony is the legal route that does not depend on faith. The municipality issues the licence and its registrar performs the marriage.

In Rhodes, for example, the Town Hall ceremony takes place every Wednesday and Friday, and two adult witnesses over 18 must be present. <!-- fact_05 --> The Municipality of Rhodes also lists outdoor civil ceremony locations in Rhodes City, from a park and a hillside street to the Town Hall's own entrance staircase. <!-- fact_06 -->

This route suits couples who want the legal marriage to happen on the island itself.

### A Greek Orthodox church wedding

A church wedding is both a sacrament and, for many families, the heart of a Greek wedding. The Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Australia describes the service in two parts: the Betrothal Service with the blessing of the rings, and the Marriage Service with the crowning of the bride and groom. <!-- fact_23 -->

Church rules decide who can marry this way, and the next section covers them in brief. For the full picture, read [what a Greek Orthodox church wedding requires](https://weddedwonderland.com/destination-weddings/greek-orthodox-wedding).

### Other religious ceremonies

Couples of another faith, or two Christian partners outside the Orthodox Church, should speak with their own religious community and with the Greek municipality early. [[VERIFY: whether a non-Orthodox religious ceremony performed in Greece is legally binding without a separate civil ceremony, and under what conditions]] Until that is confirmed for your faith and your island, plan as though the civil ceremony is the legal step.

### A symbolic celebration after marrying at home

Some couples sign the legal papers in Australia, then gather everyone in Greece. The Greek day carries no paperwork, so the script and setting are yours.

This route suits couples who want an island ceremony without the documents, or whose circumstances make the Greek legal steps hard to meet. It also leaves room for the rituals families love: our guide to [Greek wedding traditions to weave into the day](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/greek-wedding-traditions) covers the customs couples bring into both legal and symbolic ceremonies.

The rules below are those of the [Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Australia](https://greekorthodox.org.au/services-and-sacraments/marriage/). For a wedding in Greece, ask your priest how they apply to the parish and island you have in mind.

- **Who may marry.** The Archdiocese states that a marriage cannot take place in the Orthodox Church between an Orthodox Christian and a non-Christian. The non-Orthodox partner must belong to a Christian denomination the Church accepts, such as Roman Catholic, Anglican, Uniting, Lutheran or Presbyterian. <!-- fact_24 -->
- **Baptism.** Under the Archdiocese's rules, a person who is not Christian or was never baptised can marry in the Church only after baptism in an accepted denomination, and a baptism performed by a female pastor or member of clergy is not accepted. <!-- fact_24 --> <!-- fact_25 -->
- **The non-Orthodox partner.** The Archdiocese notes that a non-Orthodox spouse does not become a member of the Church and may not receive Holy Communion, and non-Orthodox clergy may not co-celebrate. <!-- fact_25 -->
- **Children.** Both partners are asked to declare that any children will be baptised in the Eastern Orthodox Church and raised in the Orthodox Faith. <!-- fact_26 -->
- **Documents.** The Archdiocese asks for a birth certificate (if born in Australia) or passport (if born overseas) and a baptismal certificate. An Orthodox partner never married before also needs a Certificate of Celibacy from their local parish priest, signed by two witnesses. <!-- fact_27 -->
- **Earlier marriages.** A divorced partner needs a civil divorce and then an ecclesiastical divorce, and for a second or third marriage the Archdiocese requires both to be issued at least one calendar month before the wedding. A widowed partner provides the late spouse's death certificate. <!-- fact_27 --> <!-- fact_28 -->

In Australia, the Archdiocese asks couples to complete the paperwork in the presence of the priest at least one calendar month before the wedding, and preferably three. <!-- fact_29 --> For a wedding in Greece, take that as a floor, not a target, and ask your priest for the timeline.

Your chosen municipality confirms its own list, and it can differ from Rhodes. Ask for it in writing before you order a single certificate in Australia.

### Certificate of No Impediment

A Certificate of No Impediment, or CNI, is a document stating there is no legal reason you cannot marry. Our guide explains [what a Certificate of No Impediment is and who issues it](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/what-is-a-certificate-of-no-impediment-and-who-issues-it).

In Rhodes, the CNI must name the person you are marrying and is valid for six months. <!-- fact_01 --> Time its issue so it is still current on your wedding day.

DFAT can issue a CNI in Australia, but some local authorities accept only CNIs issued in their own country. <!-- fact_11 --> Ask the Greek municipality which it wants before you apply, and whether the CNI also needs an authentication or apostille. <!-- fact_11 -->

The [Australian Embassy in Athens](https://greece.embassy.gov.au/athn/notarial.html) witnesses applications for, and issues, Certificates of No Impediment, and witnesses notices of intended marriage. <!-- fact_07 --> All notarial services are by appointment, Monday to Friday, 9am to 12.30pm, though Apostille and CNI documents may be submitted by post. <!-- fact_08 --> Legalised documents and CNIs are ready for collection within two working days unless otherwise arranged, and a fee applies. <!-- fact_08 -->

The Embassy does not give legal advice, and the onus is on you to present documents in the correct form. <!-- fact_09 --> Honorary Consuls cannot provide notarial services. <!-- fact_09 -->

### Apostille stamps

An Apostille is a certificate that confirms an Australian public document is genuine, so a foreign authority will accept it. See [when your paperwork needs an apostille](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/what-is-an-apostille-and-does-my-paperwork-need-one) for the detail.

In Rhodes, documents from countries outside the European Union need the Apostille stamp, and the stamped documents must also be translated into Greek. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_04 -->

On the Australian side, ask the Greek authority which documents it needs and which must be legalised, and whether it wants an apostille or an authentication certificate. <!-- fact_17 --> Requests use the Document Legalisation Request Form, signed in front of an authorised witness, and are lodged by appointment at an Australian Passport Office, in person at many Australian embassies and consulates overseas, or by registered mail to the Authentications Section in Melbourne or Sydney. <!-- fact_19 --> A fee applies. <!-- fact_19 -->

Documents that are not original Australian public documents must first be notarised by an Australian Notary Public. <!-- fact_18 --> The apostille is applied directly to the document, so if you want to keep an original unmarked, have a notarised copy legalised instead and check that the Greek authority accepts it. <!-- fact_18 -->

Processing times vary, with delays at the end of the school year and in peak holiday periods, and for incomplete requests or unpaid fees. <!-- fact_20 --> An Australian-issued apostille can be verified online through DFAT. <!-- fact_20 --> The full process sits on the government's page on [how to get your documents legalised](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised).

### Greek translations

In Rhodes, documents issued by foreign authorities must be originals of recent issue and officially translated into Greek. <!-- fact_02 --> The translation can be made by a Greek lawyer, by the Greek Embassy abroad or by the Greek Ministry of Foreign Affairs. <!-- fact_02 -->

The order matters. In Rhodes, the Apostille is translated into Greek along with the document, so have each document stamped before it goes to the translator. <!-- fact_04 -->

If a detail the municipality needs is missing from your documents, the Municipality of Rhodes allows it to be recorded through a solemn declaration under Law 1599/1986, using its own template. <!-- fact_03 -->

### If either of you was married before

Australia will not recognise a marriage where one partner is still married to someone else, so proof that an earlier marriage has ended is central. <!-- fact_14 --> Local authorities may ask for divorce papers or the death certificate of a late spouse, and the Rhodes list includes both where they apply. <!-- fact_13 --> <!-- fact_04 -->

Your state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages can also provide a statement that there is no record of a previous marriage, a Single Status certificate or a No Record Result certificate. <!-- fact_13 --> Ask the municipality whether it wants one.

For a church wedding, the Archdiocese adds its own requirement of a civil and an ecclesiastical divorce, as listed above. <!-- fact_28 -->

### Passports and witnesses

Bring your original birth certificate and passport, which local authorities may ask to see. <!-- fact_13 --> In Rhodes, a copy of each passport sits in the file and two adult witnesses over 18 attend the ceremony. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_05 -->

Not published: whether witnesses must bring their own passports or identification on the day. Ask the municipality when you confirm your document list.

## How the process runs, from home to the ceremony

Getting married in Greece is simpler once the steps are in order: work backwards from the registrar's deadline.

### Before you leave Australia

1. **Choose the municipality.** The document list, the ceremony days and the registrar all belong to the municipality where you marry, so this comes first. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_05 -->
2. **Ask for its list in writing.** Confirm whether it accepts an Australian-issued CNI or wants one from the Embassy in Athens. <!-- fact_11 -->
3. **Order certificates from your registry.** Full birth certificates and any single status, divorce or death documents the list names. <!-- fact_13 -->
4. **Have them apostilled.** Allow for slower processing at the end of the school year and in peak holiday periods. <!-- fact_20 -->
5. **Arrange the Greek translations.** By a Greek lawyer, the Greek Embassy abroad or the Greek Ministry of Foreign Affairs. <!-- fact_02 -->

Australians can visit Greece, as part of the Schengen area, for short trips without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. <!-- fact_31 --> Fingerprints and a photo are registered on arrival at no cost, which may cause border delays, and entry conditions can change at short notice. <!-- fact_31 --> Check the [Smartraveller advice for Greece](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) before you book flights; its overall level for Greece is "Exercise normal safety precautions". <!-- fact_45 -->

### When you arrive in Greece

If the municipality wants an Embassy-issued CNI, your appointment in Athens comes first. The Australian Embassy is at 5 Hatziyianni Mexi Street, Athens, phone +30 210 870 4000. <!-- fact_10 --> Allow two working days for collection unless the Embassy arranges otherwise. <!-- fact_08 -->

### Notice and the marriage licence

In Rhodes, the original documents must be presented to the registrar two days before the wedding. <!-- fact_04 --> The municipality then issues the licence for the civil marriage under Presidential Decree 391/1982, and the fee is paid at the registry office. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_04 -->

Not published: how far ahead Rhodes accepts a booking for a Town Hall ceremony. Your chosen municipality confirms its own booking window when you apply.

### On the day

In Rhodes, the Town Hall ceremony runs on Wednesdays and Fridays, with two adult witnesses present. <!-- fact_05 --> The marriage certificate is not handed over at the ceremony. The couple returns to the registry office the next working day to sign and have it drawn up. <!-- fact_05 -->

Build that working day into the itinerary before guests book their departures. [[VERIFY: Rhodes registry office working days and whether public holidays move the signing date]]

## After the wedding: registration and recognition at home

### Registering the marriage in Greece

The marriage is recorded where it happened. In Rhodes, the certificate is drawn up at the registry office the working day after the ceremony, when you both sign. <!-- fact_05 -->

Ask for the number of certified copies you will need at home before you fly out. [[VERIFY: how Australians order additional or apostilled copies of a Greek marriage certificate from the municipality]]

### Using your Greek certificate in Australia

There is nothing to register in Australia, because Australia does not register overseas marriages. <!-- fact_15 --> Read more on [whether you need to register an overseas marriage at home](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/do-you-have-to-register-an-overseas-marriage-at-home).

The foreign certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, but it may not work as proof of a change of name, so check with your local registry of births, deaths and marriages. <!-- fact_15 --> The Australian Government also suggests speaking with a lawyer to confirm the certificate is valid in Australia. <!-- fact_15 -->

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a foreigner get married in Greece?

Yes. Municipalities perform civil marriages for foreign nationals, and in Rhodes the document list for foreigners is set out under Presidential Decree 391/1982. <!-- fact_01 --> Each municipality confirms its own list.

### Are you legally married if you marry in Greece?

A civil marriage performed by the municipality is a legal marriage. In most cases Australia recognises it if it was valid under Greek law and would have been valid under Australian law. <!-- fact_15 --> A symbolic ceremony is not a legal marriage, so couples taking that route marry in Australia first.

### Is it difficult to get married in Greece?

It is orderly rather than difficult. Most of the work is the paperwork: a CNI, certificates from your registry, the Apostille and Greek translations, with originals in front of the registrar two days before the wedding in Rhodes. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_04 -->

### Do we need to register a Greek marriage in Australia?

No. You cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia, and the Greek certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage. <!-- fact_15 --> For a change of name, check with your local registry of births, deaths and marriages. <!-- fact_15 -->

### Can we marry in a Greek Orthodox church if we are not Orthodox?

Under the rules of the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Australia, a non-Orthodox partner may marry in the Church if they belong to an accepted Christian denomination, but not if they are a non-Christian or were never baptised. <!-- fact_24 --> <!-- fact_25 --> For a church in Greece, ask your priest.

### How long does a Greek wedding ceremony go for?

An Orthodox service has two parts, the Betrothal Service with the blessing of the rings and the Marriage Service with the crowning. <!-- fact_23 --> [[VERIFY: typical running time of a Greek Orthodox wedding service and of a civil ceremony at a Greek town hall]] Allow time the next working day as well, when the couple signs the marriage certificate at the registry office in Rhodes. <!-- fact_05 -->
