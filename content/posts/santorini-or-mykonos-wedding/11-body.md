Choosing between a Santorini or Mykonos wedding is a choice between two settings, not two standards. Both islands suit a luxury wedding. Santorini is the caldera: cliff-top villages along the rim of a volcano, with ceremonies that face the water and the sunset. Mykonos is beaches, bays and Mykonos Town, with its whitewashed lanes, windmills and Little Venice.

The right island depends on the setting you picture, the ceremony you want, the experience you want for your guests and the pace of the week. This guide sets Santorini vs Mykonos side by side on the same terms, in the same order. For the wider picture, browse [our curated Greece wedding collection](https://weddedwonderland.com/destinations/europe/greece).

## Key takeaways

- Santorini weddings face the caldera from cliff-top terraces, while Mykonos weddings sit on beaches and bays such as Ornos, Psarou and Agios Ioannis.
- Ceremony type narrows the shortlist on both islands, because each venue states its own options, from terraces and a winery on Santorini to whitewashed chapels on Mykonos.
- Both islands have their own airport, and Smartraveller warns that ferries can be delayed or cancelled in strong winds, so guests should plan their arrival with time to spare.
- The Australian paperwork is the same whichever island you choose, starting with proof that neither of you is already married, such as a Certificate of No Impediment.

## Santorini or Mykonos for your wedding: the short answer

Neither island wins. They answer different questions.

If the picture in your head is a terrace on the edge of a cliff, with the volcano across the water and the light going gold, that is Santorini. The island's fame rests on its active volcano, and its hamlets spread along the rim of the caldera. Its wedding venues sell exactly that view: Grace Hotel holds ceremonies on a clifftop facing caldera sunsets.

If the picture is bare feet near the sea, a chapel above a beach and a town to wander into after dinner, that is Mykonos. Mykonos is its Hora, its windmills, its chapels and dry-stone walls, and beaches among the most beautiful in the Aegean.

Most couples already lean one way. The sections below test that lean against the practical questions: venues, ceremonies, guests, travel, season and paperwork. To go straight to the scenarios, jump to [which island suits you](#which-island-suits-you).

### Santorini

Santorini, known in ancient times as Thera, sits at the southernmost point of the Cyclades. It is not one island but a complex of five: Thera, Thirasia, Aspronisi, Palea Kameni and Nea Kameni. The ring they form is the caldera of an active volcano, and you can stay right on its edge.

That geography shapes every wedding here. Hamlets spread across the rim of the caldera, so many ceremony spaces look down, over water, to the volcanic islands. Below the cliffs, the beaches are black, red and white sand, multicoloured pebbles and steep cliffs.

Inland, the island is arid but fertile, growing mainly vines, yellow split peas and tomatoes. A winery is among Santorini's wedding venues. Read more on the [Visit Greece Santorini page](https://www.visitgreece.gr/en/islands/cyclades/santorini).

### Mykonos

Mykonos sits in the heart of the Cyclades. Its look is paved streets, white houses, chapels, dry-stone walls and windmills, and the windmills are the island's trademark. In Little Venice, houses are built right against the sea.

Where Santorini's venues sit high above the water, Mykonos venues sit on it. Santa Marina is a beachfront retreat on a private peninsula at the southern tip of Ornos Bay. Mykonos Grand is on Agios Ioannis beach, opposite the island of Delos. Mykonos Blu holds weddings on Psarou beach. The Myconian Collection's wedding hotels are in Elia.

Mykonos is its Hora, chapels, windmills and Little Venice, and the wedding hotels sit on bays and beaches at Ornos, Psarou, Agios Ioannis and Elia. Read more on the [Visit Greece Mykonos page](https://www.visitgreece.gr/en/islands/cyclades/mykonos).

## Venue types on each island

Both islands have hotel venues, and that is where the likeness ends. On Santorini the hotel is usually a cliff-top property built for the view; on Mykonos it is usually a beachfront resort built around a bay. ## Ceremony types each island offers

The setting decides the island. The ceremony decides the venue.

### On Santorini

Santorini ceremonies are mostly terrace and balcony ceremonies, facing the caldera. Canaves lists religious, civil, symbolic and vow-renewal ceremonies, and says it caters for everything from traditional weddings to modern elopements. At Katikies Santorini in Oia, Botrini's Restaurant is described as ideal for wedding ceremonies, lunches, dinners and standing cocktail receptions.

Some venues add a ceremony of their own. Grace Hotel offers a unity ceremony in which the two of you combine black and white volcanic sand collected from Santorini's beaches. Santo Wines holds ceremonies at the winery, facing the caldera at sunset.

### On Mykonos

Several Mykonos venues have a chapel of their own. Santa Marina has a traditional whitewashed stone chapel. Mykonos Grand's chapel to the Virgin Mary stands on a hill above Agios Ioannis beach and also serves as a backdrop for outdoor ceremonies. At Saint John, vows can be taken at Agia Fotini Chapel, on the sunset balcony or on the deck.

There are open-air options too. Mykonos Blu's Ocean Balcony sits just above Psarou beach with an uninterrupted sea view. At Katikies Mykonos, the Upper deck is the space marked for wedding ceremonies. The Myconian Collection assists in officiating ceremonies across all denominations.

One house rule shows why the ceremony comes first. Mykonos Grand states that both partners must be Christian Orthodox for a religious wedding on the hotel premises. Decide whether you want a religious, civil or symbolic ceremony before you fall for a chapel. Canaves lists civil ceremonies among its ceremony types. Where a civil ceremony can be held is a question to ask your venue and the authority that will marry you.

## Guest experience and pace

A wedding abroad is rarely one day. Your guests will spend several days on the island, so the question is what those days feel like.

### Santorini: villages, volcano and wine

Santorini's days are built around the caldera. Guests stay in the hamlets along the rim, and the volcano is the view across the water.

Wine is the island's other thread. The vines grow in arid but fertile soil, and Santo Wines runs a Wine Tourism Center beside its winery that is open all year round. For a welcome dinner, Andronis in Oia is home to Lycabettus Restaurant, which holds two Michelin stars.

Guests who want to walk, look and eat well find plenty to fill the days. The pace is set by the light: mornings on the beaches, evenings on the rim.

### Mykonos: town, beaches and bays

Mykonos days move between the town and the coast. Mykonos Town, the Hora, is paved streets, white houses and chapels, with the windmills above and Little Venice at the water's edge. The beaches, among the most beautiful in the Aegean, are the other half of the week.

The Myconian Collection has certified halal and kosher kitchens and a South Asian chef for traditional Indian cuisine. Mykonos Grand and Katikies Mykonos both look across the water to Delos.

Guests who want a beach by day and a town to walk into at night will feel at home. The island's culture is in the lanes, chapels and harbour as much as in its beach life.

Both islands have their own airport, each with flights from Athens International Airport and from Thessaloniki.

### Santorini Airport (JTR)

The airport has Schengen and non-Schengen departure areas and an Accessible Travel section for passengers with reduced mobility. Details are on the [Santorini Airport website](https://www.jtr-airport.gr/en).

### Mykonos Airport (JMK)

The airport lists accessible travel information, parking and a Private Flights Lounge with VIP welcoming services. Details are on the [Mykonos Airport website](https://www.jmk-airport.gr/en).

### By sea

As at November 2026, Smartraveller says ferries and large catamarans carry most visitors to the Greek islands. In most cases passengers carry their own luggage up and down stairs, and some services lack suitable access for people with disabilities. Services can be delayed or cancelled in bad weather, mainly because of strong winds, so check the forecast and reconfirm departures.

From May to October, expect high temperatures and large crowds at popular seaports. On Santorini, smaller boats or tenders carry cruise passengers to and from shore, and passengers using them may find the stairs difficult. Older relatives and anyone with limited mobility are worth planning around on either island, so ask each venue for a step-free route from room to ceremony to reception before you commit.

### Entry for Australian guests

Greece is in the Schengen area, so Australians can make short trips without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. On arrival, Australian passport holders register fingerprints and a photo under the European Entry/Exit System, which needs no action before travel but can mean delays at the border. Entry conditions can change at short notice, so check [Smartraveller Greece](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) close to your dates.

Season is the one row where the two islands share an answer. The [Hellenic National Meteorological Service](https://www.hnms.gr/) lists no climate station for Santorini or Mykonos; for the South Aegean it lists only Naxos and Rodos. Naxos is the nearest listed station to both, so the figures below apply to either island and are labelled as Naxos.

### Naxos station averages (nearest listed HNMS station to both islands)

| Month | Mean temperature | Mean maximum | Mean rain days |
|---|---|---|---|
| April | 16.1°C | 18.7°C | 6.0 |
| May | 19.6°C | 22.1°C | 3.6 |
| June | 23.5°C | 25.9°C | 1.1 |
| July | 25.1°C | 27.1°C | 0.4 |
| August | 25.0°C | 27.0°C | 0.3 |
| September | 23.0°C | 25.1°C | 1.9 |
| October | 19.7°C | 21.9°C | 5.5 |

At Naxos, mean rainfall drops below 1 mm in July and rises to 38.6 mm in October. The prevailing wind at Naxos is from the north in every month from April to October. Read these as a guide to the region's pattern, not a forecast for your terrace.

### Heat, wildfire and wind

As at November 2026, Smartraveller says heatwaves are frequent over the summer months, and advises water, light clothing and shade or air conditioning. Wildfires occur during the extended summer period from June to September, including near tourist locations, and can start with little warning. Ferry services can be delayed or cancelled in bad weather, mainly because of strong winds.

Smartraveller also notes that Greece sits in an active seismic zone, with earthquakes and volcanic activity near major destinations including Santorini, and advises following local authorities. Extreme weather can affect Greece throughout the year. Choose your month by the ceremony time, the shade at your venue and how much buffer your guests have for travel, not by a single number in a table.

## Getting legally married on either island, in brief

The Australian side of the paperwork does not change with the island. As at November 2026, Smartraveller says some countries ask you to prove you are not already married, most often with a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) or a certificate from a registry of births, deaths and marriages. Local authorities may also ask for divorce papers, a deceased spouse's death certificate, and your original birth certificate and passport.

DFAT can issue a CNI in Australia, but some local authorities only accept CNIs issued in their own country. Ask whether you need one, where it should be issued, and whether it needs an authentication or apostille. Find out from the authority that will marry you which documents it needs and which must be legalised. Processing times rise at the end of the school year and in peak holiday periods.

The [Australian Embassy in Athens](https://greece.embassy.gov.au/athn/notarial.html) issues CNIs, witnesses notices of intended marriage and legalises documents by authentication or apostille. An appointment is needed for all notarial services, and a fee applies. The embassy does not give legal advice.

## Which island suits you

No verdict, only fits. Find the scenario closest to yours.

**You want the ceremony on a cliff, facing the view.** Santorini. Its venues are built along the caldera rim, and ceremonies at places like Grace Hotel and Canaves face the caldera and the sunset.

**You want a chapel ceremony.** Mykonos. Santa Marina, Mykonos Grand, Saint John and the Myconian Collection each have a chapel, and Mykonos Grand's stands on a hill above Agios Ioannis beach.

**You want a beach-side weekend.** Mykonos. Its wedding hotels sit on Ornos, Psarou, Agios Ioannis and Elia, so the beach is part of the venue rather than a day trip.

**Your guests want villages, walking and wine.** Santorini. The hamlets on the caldera rim, the volcanic beaches and a winery open all year give a slower, looking-and-tasting week.

**You want the week centred on a town.** Mykonos. The Hora, Little Venice and the windmills are the island's heart, and venues such as the Myconian Collection hold receptions overlooking Mykonos Town.

**You are planning a small wedding or an elopement.** Either island. Grace Hotel works at an intimate scale on Santorini, Canaves caters for modern elopements, and Santa Marina on Mykonos plans intimate gatherings and elopement parties.

**You want the whole property for your family.** Either island, venue by venue. Katikies lists hotel takeovers among its event types on Santorini, and Santa Marina on Mykonos plans grand celebrations as well as intimate gatherings. Ask each venue about capacity and exclusive use before you shortlist it.

## Santorini or Mykonos wedding questions, answered

### Is Santorini or Mykonos better for a wedding?

It depends on the setting, the ceremony and the week you want for your guests. Santorini suits a cliff-top ceremony facing the caldera; Mykonos suits a beach, a chapel by the sea and a town close by. Both have hotel venues that host weddings.

### Can we marry legally on either island?

The Australian documents are the same for both, starting with proof that neither of you is already married, such as a CNI. Ask the authority that will marry you which documents it needs and which must be legalised.

### How do guests get to Santorini and Mykonos?

Each island has its own airport, with flights from Athens and Thessaloniki. Ferries and large catamarans carry most visitors to the Greek islands, but services can be delayed or cancelled in strong winds.

### When can we marry on each island?

Neither island has its own HNMS station, so the same Naxos figures guide both. At Naxos, July and August average under one rain day a month, and Smartraveller warns of frequent summer heatwaves and wildfires from June to September.

### Is a wedding on Santorini or Mykonos more expensive?

Cost turns on your guest count, your venue and its spaces, whether you take over a whole hotel, and when you travel. Several venues publish wedding brochures, including Canaves, Santa Marina and Andronis, and your inclusions should be set out in writing before you commit.
