Getting married in Brazil legally means the civil process at a Cartório de Registro Civil, the civil registry office. You apply to be cleared to marry, a step called the habilitação, at the civil registry of the district where one of you resides, then marry on a day, at a time and in a place the registrar sets with you. <!-- fact_200 --> <!-- fact_204 -->

Because the law ties the application to the district where one of you resides, the first question to put to the cartório, before you book flights or a venue, is whether and how a couple living abroad can apply, and which office takes the application. <!-- fact_200 -->

What follows is the order to work in: documents first, then the cartório, then the certificate you bring home. Steps that apply only to Australians are marked as they come.

## Can foreigners legally marry in Brazil?

The legal route is the civil process. Brazil's Public Registries Law sets out how it works, as at November 2026: you present the documents the civil law requires and ask the civil registrar of the district where one of you resides to certify that you are cleared to marry. <!-- fact_200 -->

If neither of you lives in Brazil, the first question for the cartório is whether and how you can apply, and which office takes your application. The second set of questions is how long to allow:

- How long does the proclamas, the public notice, run before you can marry?
- Once issued, how long does our certificate of habilitação stay valid?
- How many days in Brazil should we allow between arriving and the ceremony?

The sworn Portuguese translation of your documents can only be done in Brazil, so it counts inside that time. <!-- fact_03 -->

The civil marriage is the legal one. A celebration on a beach or in a garden can follow it, or the civil ceremony itself can be held there, and both paths are set out further down.

## What are the legal requirements for getting married in Brazil?

The requirements fall into three parts: the habilitação, a public notice, and the ceremony with its record.

In short: the cartório is the civil registry office, the habilitação is your application to marry, the proclamas are the banns, and the tradutor juramentado is the sworn translator who puts your documents into Portuguese. <!-- fact_200 --> <!-- fact_03 -->

### Habilitação at the cartório

The habilitação is your application to marry. You bring the documents the civil law asks for, and the registrar checks them. <!-- fact_200 --> Identification and the documents can be presented electronically, with proof that they are authentic and unaltered. <!-- fact_202 --> Ask the cartório whether it takes applications from abroad this way, and what it needs in person.

If the documents are in order, the registrar publishes your application electronically and issues the certificate of habilitação within up to 5 days. <!-- fact_201 --> With that certificate, you can marry before any civil registry office you choose, within the validity period the Civil Code sets. <!-- fact_201 -->

If anyone raises an impediment or objection, the registrar tells you, the matter goes to a judge with the Public Prosecutor's office informed, and the judge decides. <!-- fact_203 -->

### Public notice

The public notice of your intention to marry is called the proclamas, the banns. The electronic publication can be dispensed with in cases the law provides for, if you petition the registrar with proof of urgency, and the registrar decides within 24 hours. <!-- fact_206 -->

Start with the cartório's own list, because it decides what it accepts. Ask it for the exact documents it needs from foreign couples, in writing, before you order anything at home.

### Apostille

As a general rule, documents issued abroad must be legalised and translated before Brazilian authorities accept them. <!-- fact_01 --> The apostille is the faster form of legalisation: under the Hague Apostille Convention, in force in Brazil since 2016, documents from member countries are accepted with a single apostille, with no separate consular step. <!-- fact_02 --> An apostille is a certificate that authenticates the origin of a public document. <!-- fact_05 --> Brazil's National Council of Justice explains the system on its [apostille pages](https://www.cnj.jus.br/poder-judiciario/relacoes-internacionais/apostila-da-haia/).

Have your documents apostilled in the country that issued them, before you travel. Then ask the cartório whether it wants originals or certified copies.

### Sworn Portuguese translation

In Brazil, foreign documents, even when apostilled, must be translated by a tradutor juramentado, a sworn public translator, to be valid, and that translation can only be done in Brazil. <!-- fact_03 --> Book the translation as soon as your apostilled documents are ready, and allow time in Brazil for it before your cartório appointment.

### Certificate of No Impediment (for Australians)

For Australians: a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage, or CNI, is a DFAT document that proves you are not already married, for when local authorities ask. <!-- fact_100 --> At least one of you must be an Australian citizen or permanent resident. <!-- fact_100 --> A fee applies, paid when you apply. <!-- fact_101 -->

DFAT does not advise on another country's legal process. Ask the cartório whether it needs a CNI at all, whether a Single Status Certificate or No Record Result Certificate from your state or territory registry will do instead, whether the CNI must be issued in Brazil, and whether it needs an apostille. <!-- fact_102 --> Local authorities may also ask for divorce papers, a deceased spouse's death certificate, your original birth certificate and your passport. <!-- fact_110 -->

The CNI form is signed in front of an authorised witness, such as a Justice of the Peace or Australian Passports official at home or a consular official overseas. <!-- fact_103 -->

DFAT issues CNIs through Australian Passport Offices, embassies and consulates, and by mail. <!-- fact_100 --> In Brazil, the Australian Embassy in Brasília and the Consulate-General in São Paulo provide notarial services, including CNIs and apostilles, by appointment only. <!-- fact_104 --> Staff will not say which documents you need, and a CNI application mailed from within Brazil is accepted only if a local notary at a cartório witnessed the signature. <!-- fact_105 --> The Honorary Consulate in Rio de Janeiro does not provide notarial services. <!-- fact_106 -->

### Witnesses

The marriage record is signed by the person presiding, the two of you, your witnesses and the registrar, and there must be at least two witnesses unless the law provides otherwise. <!-- fact_207 --> <!-- fact_208 --> Ask the cartório what it requires of witnesses, including age and identification, and whether either of you or your witnesses will need an interpreter on the day.

Once the certificate of habilitação is issued, the civil marriage is celebrated on the day, at the time and in the place you request and the registrar sets. <!-- fact_204 --> Ask the cartório who will conduct your ceremony, because the person presiding signs your marriage record with you. <!-- fact_207 -->

Straight after the ceremony, a record is drawn up and signed. It notes your details, the date of the banns and of the ceremony, the documents you presented and your marriage property regime. <!-- fact_207 --> Ask the cartório to explain the property regime options before the day. Ask the cartório how and when you receive your certidão de casamento, the marriage certificate.

### A religious ceremony with civil effect

You can ask the registrar for the certificate of habilitação so that you can marry before a religious authority or minister, and it states how long the clearance is valid. <!-- fact_209 --> The record of a religious marriage is signed by the celebrant, the two of you and two witnesses. <!-- fact_210 -->

Within 30 days of the religious ceremony, the celebrant or anyone with an interest can ask the registry office that issued the certificate to register the marriage, and the registrar registers it within 24 hours. <!-- fact_211 --> Registration takes legal effect from the date of the ceremony. <!-- fact_212 --> Agree with your celebrant, in writing, who will lodge it and when.

The rituals around the day are a separate pleasure. Our guide to [Brazilian wedding traditions](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/brazilian-wedding-traditions) covers the customs couples weave in.

### Beach and outdoor ceremonies

The place of the civil ceremony is one you request and the registrar sets. <!-- fact_204 --> So a ceremony on sand, a clifftop lawn or a fazenda veranda starts as a conversation with the cartório. Ask:

- Will the registrar and presiding officer travel to our venue, and on which days and at which times?
- Does a ceremony outside the registry office change what we lodge or pay?
- If rain moves us under cover on the day, does the new spot need approval?

Then ask the venue:

- Is the ceremony space private, or can the public walk through it?
- What is the covered or indoor alternative, and when is the call made?
- Who gives permission to use a beach or public space, and who applies for it?

You can also marry at any civil registry office of your choice while your clearance is valid, and hold the celebration separately. <!-- fact_201 --> Either way, the legal marriage and the celebration are planned as two separate pieces.

For Australians: you cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia, but in most cases it is legally recognised if it was valid under the law of the country where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law had it happened here. <!-- fact_112 --> Australia does not recognise an overseas marriage where either person is already married, not old enough to marry, too closely related, or forced to marry. <!-- fact_111 --> Smartraveller sets this out in its guide to [getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas).

### Getting your certificate ready

A foreign marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, but it may not work as proof of a name change, so check with your state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages. <!-- fact_112 --> Smartraveller suggests talking to a lawyer to confirm your certificate is valid in Australia. <!-- fact_112 -->

In Brazil, apostilles on public documents are issued by authorised cartórios, which the National Council of Justice lists by state and city. <!-- fact_05 --> Ask each Australian organisation that will receive your certidão whether it wants an apostille, and whether it wants an English translation. Order more than one certified copy before you leave Brazil.

Once Australia recognises your marriage, you cannot marry again in Australia, though you may hold another kind of ceremony, such as a confirmation of vows. <!-- fact_113 --> That suits couples who marry in Brazil with close family and celebrate with everyone at home.

### Entry

For Australians: you need a visa to enter Brazil and must arrange it before arrival; it cannot be obtained at the airport, and travellers who arrive without one are sent back on the next available flight. <!-- fact_119 --> Entry and exit conditions can change at short notice, so check with a Brazilian embassy or consulate. <!-- fact_119 --> Passports are stamped on arrival and may be inspected on departure. <!-- fact_120 -->

Dual nationals must enter and leave Brazil on their Brazilian passport. <!-- fact_121 --> You may need a valid yellow fever vaccination certificate to enter Brazil, and some airlines may ask for a copy when you depart. <!-- fact_122 --> Guests on other passports should check their own entry rules early.

### Safety

Smartraveller advises Australians to "Exercise a high degree of caution" in Brazil due to the threat of violent crime. <!-- fact_118 --> It says violent crime including mugging, armed robbery and carjacking is common, more often in large cities and during festivals such as Carnival, and advises against going out alone at night and against isolated areas, including beaches. <!-- fact_124 --> Its precautions on official taxis and sealed drinks are in the full [Smartraveller Brazil advice](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/americas/brazil), worth sending to your guests. <!-- fact_126 --> <!-- fact_128 -->

What follows are practical questions to settle with your venue, in writing:

- **Deposits:** whose name is on the contract, and when do deposits become non-refundable?
- **Access:** which ceremony, dining and guest spaces have step-free access?
- **Guest travel:** how do guests travel safely between the airport, their rooms and each event, especially after dark?
- **Security:** how does your security work during weddings, and how would it work alongside any security a family brings?
- **Privacy:** can the ceremony space, including any beach in front of the property, be kept private, and who applies for that?

### Seasons by region

Brazil's wet season depends on where you are: January to July in the north, November to March in the south and south-east, and April to July in the north-east, and heavy rain can occur all year. <!-- fact_127 --> Landslides and flooding occur, and severe storms often delay flights. <!-- fact_127 --> Wildfires are common from May to September, especially in July and August. <!-- fact_127 -->

Domestic and international flight delays are common. <!-- fact_126 --> Choose the season for the wedding you picture, then ask your venue how it has handled the weather on your dates in past years.

## How much does it cost to get married in Brazil?

The cost turns on where you marry, how many guests travel, how many nights you take a property and whether the legal ceremony comes to your venue. Ask every venue to set out its quote in writing, line by line, and in which currency it is paid.

On the paperwork side, a fee applies for an [Australian CNI](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-certificate-no-impediment-marriage) and for the [Embassy's notarial services](https://brazil.embassy.gov.au/bras/notarialservices.html). <!-- fact_101 --> <!-- fact_104 --> A fee also applies for [legalising Australian documents through DFAT](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised). <!-- fact_116 --> Government fees change, so check the current amount on each official page before you set your numbers. Ask the cartório what it charges for the habilitação and the ceremony, and ask the sworn translator for a written quote per document.

Your guests will need travel insurance, because Smartraveller says private health care in larger cities is very expensive and that cover must include all medical costs, including evacuation. <!-- fact_123 -->
