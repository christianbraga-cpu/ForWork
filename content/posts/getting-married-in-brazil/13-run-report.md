## Please note
- **The residence rule is the first thing readers see.** Brazil's Public Registries Law (Lei 6.015/1973, as amended in 2022) ties the marriage application, the habilitação, to the civil registry of the district where one of the couple resides. No official Brazilian page read says couples who both live abroad can apply, so the article does not claim they can or cannot: it makes "can we apply if neither of us lives in Brazil?" the first question for the cartório (the civil registry office), in the intro and the first takeaway. The first draft had this as a VERIFY item; it was resolved by rewording, not by a source.
- **The legal steps, from the Public Registries Law (translated from Portuguese, as at November 2026):**
  - Apply at the civil registry with the documents the civil law requires; documents can be presented electronically.
  - The registrar publishes the banns electronically and, if all is in order, issues the certificate of habilitação within up to 5 days.
  - You can then marry at any civil registry office of your choice, on the day, time and place you request and the registrar sets, within the validity period set by the Civil Code (the period itself was not read, so no number is given).
  - At least two witnesses; the marriage is recorded straight after the ceremony.
  - A religious ceremony can have civil effect: the couple are cleared first, and the marriage is registered within 30 days of the ceremony.
- **Documents:** Brazil's Federal Police state that foreign documents generally need an apostille and a sworn translation done in Brazil. That page is about migration, so the article presents it as a general rule and tells couples to confirm the exact list with the cartório.
- **Left out on purpose:** who presides at a civil ceremony and how the certificate is issued (the Civil Code articles could not be read; both are now questions for the cartório); videoconference ceremonies (allowed by the law, not suited to this audience); Brazil's same-sex marriage position (standing rule); fee amounts (your rule).
- **For Australians:** a Certificate of No Impediment from DFAT or the Australian Embassy in Brasília (notarial services in Brasília and São Paulo by appointment; the Rio Honorary Consulate cannot do notarial work); recognition at home from Smartraveller and the Attorney-General's Department.
- **Safety and entry:** Smartraveller rates Brazil "Exercise a high degree of caution" due to violent crime, with no higher-level regions listed. Australians need a visa arranged before arrival. Safety lines use Smartraveller's words, trimmed, then go straight to venue questions (deposits, access, how guests travel safely, security for any couple).
