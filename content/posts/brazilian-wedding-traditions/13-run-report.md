## How the topic is handled
- Scope: customs vary by region, faith and family across Brazil and in Brazilian-Australian families; said once. No carnival clichés.
- Sourced: Michaelis and Priberam definitions (noivado, chá de panela, padrinhos and madrinhas, bem-casado as a small cake of two halves); Britannica on Santo Antônio (Lisbon, patron saint of Portugal); UNESCO Samba de Roda (2008) and Frevo (2012), presented only as music a couple might choose, since neither UNESCO page mentions weddings.
- "Often told" only: the ring moving from the right hand to the left, Santo Antônio as Brazil's marrying saint, the Festa Junina mock wedding, names in the hem, the groom's tie as keepsakes, docinhos and late parties.
- Legal and travel: Smartraveller (high degree of caution because of violent crime; border-state crime; visa before arrival), the Australian Embassy in Brasília and Consulate-General in São Paulo (notarial appointments only there; the Rio honorary consulate does no notarial work), DFAT certificate of no impediment ("a fee applies"), CNJ on the apostille (in force since August 2016) and CNJ Resolution 175 of 2013 on same-sex civil marriage. Brazil's Civil Code site (planalto.gov.br) blocked our page reader, so the habilitação steps, notice period and religious civil effect are written as "check with the cartório and the Embassy, including any fees".
