# Celebrant checklist: who can perform your marriage

These details sit with the couple and their authorised celebrant (or, if marrying legally abroad, the destination's authorities), so they don't block publishing. The Wedded Advisor plans the celebration; the choice of celebrant, the NOIM, the ceremony wording, the certificates and registration stay with the couple and their celebrant or the local authority. The article has no `[[VERIFY]]` markers in the copy and no prices (nothing for Nic to sign off); the only publishing blockers are image credits and licences (5 slots).

All items are as at November 2026, per the Attorney-General's Department (AGD), the Marriage Act 1961 and the Marriage Regulations 2017. Reconfirm each one with your celebrant for your date before relying on it.

## Marrying legally in Australia

### Check the register

- [ ] Search AGD's Register of Marriage Celebrants (marriage.ag.gov.au) by name, or filter by state, before you pay a deposit.
- [ ] Read the entry: registration status (for example "Registered" and "Active"), registration date, and "Civil Ceremonies" or a religious marriage celebrant label.
- [ ] Match the entry to their advertising, website, social media and business cards ("marriage celebrant" or "religious marriage celebrant").
- [ ] Ministers of religion and state and territory officers: check their own lists, reached from AGD's Find a marriage celebrant page.

### Celebrant type

- [ ] Confirm which of the four types your celebrant is: Commonwealth-registered marriage celebrant, Commonwealth-registered religious marriage celebrant, minister of religion from a recognised denomination, or state or territory officer.
- [ ] Civil ceremony: a Commonwealth-registered marriage celebrant or a registry (state or territory officer).
- [ ] Religious ceremony: ask your minister which category they are in and whether they appear on the register.

### NOIM timing

- [ ] Book your celebrant first: the Notice of Intended Marriage goes to the authorised celebrant who will marry you.
- [ ] The NOIM must reach your celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding.
- [ ] Less than one month to go: ask your celebrant whether a prescribed authority can approve a shorter notice period.
- [ ] Book the separate, in-person meeting each of you has with the celebrant before the ceremony, especially if one of you lives overseas.
- [ ] Changing celebrants: the NOIM can move to another authorised celebrant, including at your request.

### The words the law requires

- [ ] Ask your celebrant to take you through the words the law requires and to show you the full running order before the day.

### On the day: witnesses and certificates

- [ ] Arrange 2 witnesses, both over 18.
- [ ] Build the signing of the 3 marriage certificates into the run sheet (couple, celebrant and 2 witnesses).
- [ ] Check the certificates before anyone leaves: errors can be corrected on the day only while the couple and witnesses are still present.
- [ ] Receive the Form 15 Certificate of Marriage immediately after the ceremony and keep it safe; it cannot be reissued.

### Registry lodgement and official certificate

- [ ] Confirm your celebrant will submit the paperwork to the state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages within 14 days.
- [ ] Order the official marriage certificate from the registry (needed to change your name or prove you are married). Ask whether your celebrant will apply on your behalf and what the registry charges (not published in the article).
- [ ] After registration, errors are corrected only by the registry's authorised officer.

### To ask your celebrant: a friend or family member

- [ ] How a friend or family member can take part in the ceremony (for example a reading) while the authorised celebrant solemnises the marriage.
- [ ] If they want to register as a celebrant themselves: AGD has up to 6 months to assess a completed application, with no fast-tracking, and the NOIM still needs at least 1 month. Requirements are on AGD's Become a marriage celebrant page.
- [ ] Migration questions (prospective marriage or spouse visas): celebrants should not advise; use the Department of Home Affairs website or a registered migration agent.

## Marrying legally abroad

- [ ] Australian-authorised celebrants cannot perform a marriage overseas, and overseas marriages cannot be registered in Australia.
- [ ] Local rules: check the destination's marriage law and paperwork with its embassy or consulate, including any residence period.
- [ ] CNI: check whether the destination asks for a Certificate of No Impediment, and who issues it.
- [ ] Documents: check whether your paperwork needs an apostille, and ask the embassy about translations (not stated in the article).
- [ ] Start with Smartraveller's Getting married overseas page.
- [ ] Back home: check what you need to do with your overseas marriage certificate.
- [ ] Or marry legally in Australia first (NOIM at least 1 month ahead) and celebrate abroad.
