# Short wedding vows checklist

Use this when you write your vows and plan the ceremony. Get the answers that matter on the day in writing.

## Write your vows
- [ ] Decide together: matching vows, or each of you writes your own
- [ ] Agree on a similar length so neither of you feels outweighed
- [ ] Start with your partner's name
- [ ] One specific memory or detail, not a history
- [ ] Two or three promises you can keep
- [ ] End on a short closing line
- [ ] Choose the word that fits you: husband, wife or partner
- [ ] Read it aloud at the pace you would use in front of guests, with pauses, and time it on your phone (1 to 2 minutes at most)
- [ ] Cut anything that feels like padding
- [ ] Write the final version on a small, sturdy card in large print, and keep a copy with your celebrant

## Questions for your celebrant or registrar
- [ ] Which legal words must each of us say, and in what form? (In Australia, the section 45(2) words of the Marriage Act 1961, "or words to that effect", when the celebrant is not a minister of religion.)
- [ ] How do our own vows fit around the legal words?
- [ ] If we follow a faith or family tradition, where do personal lines fit?
- [ ] If we marry legally overseas, what wording does that country require, and who confirms it?
- [ ] What is the order on the day: who speaks first, where the legal words sit and when the rings come in?
- [ ] How long does the ceremony run, and how long do we have for our vows?
- [ ] Should we send you our vows before the day?
- [ ] Can we rehearse the vows and the running order at the rehearsal?
- [ ] Do you hold a copy of our vows in case we need it?

## Questions for the venue
- [ ] Is a microphone available for the ceremony?
- [ ] Who sets it up, and can we test it at the rehearsal?
- [ ] For an outdoor ceremony, will sound reach every seat over wind, waves or fountains?
- [ ] Who is responsible for the sound on the day?
- [ ] If the ceremony moves indoors for weather, does the microphone move with it?
