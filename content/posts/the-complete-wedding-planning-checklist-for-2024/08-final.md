A year sounds like a long time until you count the things that cannot be rushed: a venue on your date, a gown made to measure, a passport renewal, legal papers for marrying overseas.

This 12 month wedding checklist runs month by month, from the first decisions to the week of the wedding. Each block has a short core checklist, then a destination track for the tasks with fixed lead times that a local plan tends to miss. For every task beyond these twelve months, from engagement to thank-you notes, keep our [full wedding planning checklist](/expert-advice/wedding-planning-checklist) open beside this one.

## Key takeaways

- At 12 months out, settle what you will spend, the guest count, the style and the venue, then book key suppliers and send save-the-dates at 10 to 11 months.
- At 6 to 7 months out, choose rings and stationery and apply for any new passport, then send invitations with travel details at 4 to 5 months.
- Use the final month for the legal and supplier check, and the last 2 weeks to hand every job you can to someone else.
- If marrying in Australia, your Notice of Intended Marriage must reach your celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding, and on an Australian passport you should allow at least 6 weeks for a new or renewed one. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_19 -->

## How does a 12 month checklist change for a destination wedding?

A wedding at home and a wedding abroad share most of the same decisions. What changes is the order. Overseas, the paperwork, the passports and your guests' travel all have lead times you cannot shorten by paying more or working late.

That is why each month below has two parts. The core checklist covers the planning every wedding needs. The destination track covers the travel and legal steps, so they sit in the plan from the start rather than surfacing in the final weeks.

For the destination tasks in more depth (site visits, welcome dinners, weather and local holidays), see our [destination wedding checklist](/expert-advice/destination-wedding-checklist).

## 12 months out: what to do first

This is the month of big, slow decisions. Make them in order and the rest of the year gets easier.

**Core checklist**

- Agree what you will spend and who is contributing, in writing between the two of you and anyone contributing, such as parents, before any venue conversation.
- Draft a guest list with a rough number. The count decides which venues are realistic.
- Choose the style: a local wedding, a destination wedding, or a small ceremony at home with a celebration abroad.
- Pick a season and two or three possible dates, then check public holidays and school holidays at home and at the destination.
- Shortlist venues, visit or call the top two or three, and book the one that holds your date. Before you sign, get the deposit, payment schedule, and cancellation and postponement terms in writing.
- Decide whether you want a planner, and bring them in now so they can shape the venue choice rather than inherit it.
- Book your celebrant, particularly if you are marrying legally in Australia.

**Destination track**

- Check both passports now. Allow at least 6 weeks from lodging an application to receiving a new passport, and the same for renewals. <!-- fact_19 -->
- If you are in Australia, lodging a new or renewal application generally cancels your current passport, so you cannot travel on it while you wait. <!-- fact_20 --> Avoid travelling outside Australia while a renewal is being processed.
- Decide whether you will marry legally at the destination, or legally in Australia first with a ceremony abroad.
- If you are marrying legally overseas, contact the destination's embassy or consulate. Some countries ask you to prove you are not already married, most commonly with a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) and certificates from a registry of births, deaths and marriages. <!-- fact_06 -->
- Ask the same embassy whether you can get the CNI in Australia, because some local authorities only accept one issued in their country, and whether it also needs an authentication or apostille. <!-- fact_07 --> Smartraveller's guide to [getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) sets out the steps.
- On the site visit, ask the venue about a step-free route from arrival to the ceremony and reception, seating and shade for older guests, and accessible rooms and transfers.
- Take out travel insurance as soon as you book the site visit or flights. The earlier you buy it, the more likely you are to be covered if something unexpected happens before you travel. <!-- fact_15 -->

## 10 to 11 months out: book the people who shape the day

With the venue and date held, turn to the suppliers whose calendars fill first and whose work you will look at for the rest of your life.

**Core checklist**

- Book your photographer and videographer. Look at full galleries from real weddings, not just highlights.
- Book entertainment: ceremony musicians, a band or DJ, and anything planned for the welcome or farewell events.
- Book catering if your venue does not provide it, and ask about tastings.
- For every booking, get the deposit, payment schedule, and cancellation and postponement terms in writing, and ask what happens to your deposit if the supplier cancels.
- Choose your wedding party and tell them what you are asking of them, especially if it involves international travel.
- Set up a wedding website or RSVP page where guests can find travel details as they firm up. [Wonderland Studio](/wonderland-studio), our planning app, handles RSVPs and seating charts. Keep guest details private or password protected, and ask suppliers about confidentiality and posting on social media.

**Save the dates**

- Send save-the-dates as soon as the date and venue are confirmed. For a destination wedding, plan to send them around 9 to 12 months out, so guests can book leave and travel.
- Include the town, the dates of any welcome and farewell events, and where you will share accommodation and travel details.

**Destination track**

- Hold a block of rooms for guests close to the venue, with a release date agreed in writing, and ask who pays for unfilled rooms after that date.
- For a large guest list, ask whether exclusive use of the whole property is possible instead of a room block.
- Ask your travel insurer what your policy covers if a supplier or venue cancels.
- Note the nearest airports and how guests reach the venue, so the save-the-date and website carry the same information.
- If you are marrying legally in Australia, you can now lodge your Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM): it must reach your authorised celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. <!-- fact_01 --> See our guide to the [Notice of Intended Marriage](/expert-advice/notice-of-intended-marriage).
- As at November 2026, the NOIM can be witnessed in person or remotely via audio-visual link. <!-- fact_17 --> You can also complete it while you are overseas if you plan to marry in Australia. <!-- fact_16 -->

## 8 to 9 months out: attire, accommodation and the celebrant

The shape of the wedding is set. Now the long lead time pieces come into focus: what you will wear and where everyone will sleep.

**Core checklist**

- Start shopping for wedding attire. Ask each designer or boutique for their lead time to delivery, including alterations, and order with time to spare.
- Plan suits, outfits for the wedding party and anything that needs custom tailoring.
- Meet your celebrant to talk through the ceremony, readings and who will speak.
- Book your florist and stylist, and share the venue's floor plans and any restrictions on candles, flame or installations.
- Plan the honeymoon if it follows the wedding, so flights and transfers can join up.

**Destination track**

- Share accommodation and transfer details with guests on your website, and remind them of the room block release date.
- Ask the overseas authority which of your documents it needs and which must be legalised, then find out whether each needs an apostille or an authentication certificate. <!-- fact_11 -->
- Start the legalisation process early. Processing times vary, with delays at the end of the school year, in peak holiday periods, and for incomplete requests. <!-- fact_12 --> Smartraveller's page on [getting documents legalised](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised) lists what to prepare.
- Book honeymoon travel in the name on your current passport. <!-- fact_09 -->

## 6 to 7 months out: rings, stationery and the details

With the major bookings in place, this block is about the things guests will hold, read and notice.

**Core checklist**

- Choose your wedding rings, and allow time for sizing or engraving.
- Order your invitation suite, including any printed details for welcome and farewell events.
- Book hair and makeup, and schedule trials.
- Book transport for the day: the couple, the wedding party and guests between the hotel and venue.
- Start a ceremony plan with your celebrant: order of service, music, readings and any family customs.
- Choose your cake or dessert, and confirm who supplies it.

**Destination track**

- If either of you needs a new passport, apply now so it arrives well before you travel. A new adult passport, rather than a renewal, is needed if your most recent passport does not show your current name. <!-- fact_21 -->
- When a new passport is issued, the number changes, so update any travel bookings or visa applications with the new details. <!-- fact_21 -->
- When you buy travel insurance, declare everything upfront, including all pre-existing health conditions, or you may void the policy. <!-- fact_14 --> Smartraveller's [travel insurance guidance](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) covers what to ask.
- Plan welcome drinks and a farewell lunch or brunch, if you are hosting them.

## 4 to 5 months out: when to send wedding invitations

Invitations go out once every detail on them is fixed. The more travel you are asking of guests, the earlier they need the full picture.

**Core checklist**

- Send invitations once the venue, times and dress code are settled. For a destination wedding, plan to send them with travel details around 4 months out, and earlier if guests need visas or long-haul leave.
- Set an RSVP date before final numbers are due, leaving time to chase late replies.
- Plan the menu with your caterer, and collect dietary requirements with the RSVP.
- Finalise the ceremony and reception music lists.
- Book any rehearsal dinner venue.

**Destination track**

- Include travel, accommodation and dress code notes for the climate with the invitation, or a link to where guests can find them.
- Track who has booked their room in your block, and remind guests before the release date.
- Remind guests that the right travel insurance is as essential as a passport for travel overseas. <!-- fact_13 -->

## 3 months out: trials, fittings and paperwork

The plan is now about checking that what you booked matches what you imagined.

**Core checklist**

- Attend attire fittings, and book the final fitting close to the wedding.
- Do hair and makeup trials, ideally with the accessories you will wear.
- Confirm the full menu and drinks.
- Write a draft run sheet for the day with your venue and photographer.
- Start writing your vows if you are writing your own.

**Destination track**

- Check that every document for the overseas authority is complete, legalised where required and ready to travel. <!-- fact_11 -->
- As at November 2026, you cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia, but in most cases it is legally recognised here if it was valid under the law of the country where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. <!-- fact_08 -->
- If you are marrying legally in Australia first, your celebrant must meet each of you separately and in person before the marriage. <!-- fact_17 -->

## 2 months out: confirm and chase

Two months out is for closing loops. Few new bookings, many confirmations.

**Core checklist**

- Chase outstanding RSVPs and confirm final numbers with the venue by its deadline.
- Draft the seating plan.
- Confirm every supplier's arrival time, contact on the day and what they need from you.
- Buy gifts for the wedding party and anyone who has helped.
- Collect the small items: guest book, signage, place cards, favours.

**Destination track**

- Send guests a short travel note: transfer times, the welcome event and who to call on arrival.
- Confirm airport transfers for guests who have shared their flights.
- Check both passports are in hand, and that every booking matches the name and number on them. <!-- fact_09 --> <!-- fact_21 -->

## 1 month out: the final legal and supplier check

This is the deadline month for Australian paperwork, and the month to send the final timings to everyone involved.

**Core checklist**

- Finalise the seating plan and print place cards.
- Send the final run sheet to your venue, suppliers and wedding party.
- Pay outstanding balances on the dates in each contract.
- Pick up the rings, and check sizing and engraving.
- Book the final attire fitting for the week or two before.

**Destination track**

- As at November 2026, the NOIM must reach your celebrant at least 1 month before the wedding. <!-- fact_02 --> If there is less than 1 month to go, talk to your celebrant: you may still marry if a prescribed authority approves it. <!-- fact_03 --> The Attorney-General's Department's [Get married](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) page sets out the requirements.
- Keep original documents for the overseas authority together in your carry-on bag, never in checked luggage.
- Make sure every traveller in your party has travel insurance in place.

## The final 2 weeks: hand it over

The aim now is to step back. Every job that can be passed to someone else should be.

**Core checklist**

- Do the final attire fitting and collect everything.
- Confirm each supplier one last time, with arrival times and the on-the-day contact.
- Delegate jobs for the day: who looks after the rings, who collects gifts and cards, who gets you home.
- Hand over items for the venue (signage, place cards, guest book) in labelled boxes.
- Pack an emergency kit, and plan a meal for your suppliers if the venue does not.

**Destination track**

- Confirm the wet-weather plan with the venue, and check sunset time if the ceremony is outdoors.
- Pack passports, documents, insurance details and the rings in hand luggage.
- Share the local contact details and on-the-ground plan with the wedding party.

## The week of the wedding

The work is done. This week is for arriving, resting and enjoying the people who have travelled to be with you. For the day itself, hour by hour, see our [wedding day timeline](/expert-advice/wedding-day-timeline).

**Core checklist**

- Hold the rehearsal with your celebrant and wedding party.
- Confirm the run sheet with the venue coordinator in person.
- Brief the people you have given jobs to.
- Rest, eat well and keep the day before light.

**Destination track**

- Arrive with time to settle, and walk the venue at the hour of the ceremony, including the step-free route for older or less mobile guests.
- Check that the seating, shade and accessible transfers you asked for are in place.
- Greet guests at the welcome event.
- As at November 2026, for a marriage in Australia, the couple, the celebrant and 2 witnesses over 18 sign 3 marriage certificates on the day. <!-- fact_17 -->
- The celebrant must submit the paperwork to the state or territory registry within 14 days, and you then apply for the official marriage certificate, which you need to change your name. <!-- fact_18 --> Some states and territories do not accept overseas marriage certificates for a name change. <!-- fact_18 --> Our guide to [changing your name after marriage](/expert-advice/name-change-after-marriage) covers the next steps.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you plan a wedding in 11 months?

Yes. Start with the 10 to 11 months out block and fold the 12 month tasks into your first few weeks: what you will spend, the guest count, the style and the venue. For a destination wedding, check passports and the embassy's document requirements in the same first weeks, since those steps have lead times you cannot compress.

### Is 2 months enough time to plan a wedding?

For a small wedding in Australia, it can be, provided your NOIM reaches your celebrant at least 1 month before the wedding. <!-- fact_01 --> Overseas is harder: a new or renewed passport needs at least 6 weeks from lodging, <!-- fact_19 --> and legalising documents can take longer in peak holiday periods. <!-- fact_12 -->

### What do I need for a wedding checklist?

A list of tasks grouped by when they need doing, with an owner beside each one. Cover spend, guests, venue, suppliers, attire, stationery, rings, legal paperwork, travel and final confirmations. For a destination wedding, add passports, documents for the overseas authority, room blocks and insurance.

### When should you lodge the Notice of Intended Marriage?

As at November 2026, give the NOIM to your authorised celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. <!-- fact_01 --> Lodging early, as soon as the date and celebrant are booked, removes the deadline from the final month. The form can be witnessed in person or via audio-visual link. <!-- fact_17 -->

### What do couples forget before their wedding?

The small, practical things: meals for suppliers, a wet-weather plan, someone to collect gifts and cards, transport home at the end of the night and an emergency kit. Overseas, add original documents in hand luggage and bookings that match the name on each passport. <!-- fact_09 -->
