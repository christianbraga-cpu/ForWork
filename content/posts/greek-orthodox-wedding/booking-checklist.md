# Booking checklist: Greek Orthodox wedding

These details sit with the couple, their priest, the authorities and the venue, so they don't block publishing. The Wedded Advisor plans the celebration around the service and confirms venue details in writing for the couple's dates; the church and legal paperwork stays with the couple, their parish priest and the authorities. The article has no `[[VERIFY]]` markers in the copy and no prices (nothing for Nic to sign off); the only publishing blockers are image credits and licences (6 slots).

All items are as at November 2026. Reconfirm each one for the couple's date before relying on it.

## Church side (Australia, Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Australia rules)

- Meet the parish priest first, before holding any venue or date: confirm the Church can marry the couple and which jurisdiction's rules apply (Archdiocese of Australia here; for a wedding in Greece, also the local Metropolis).
- Eligibility: both baptised in a canonical Orthodox Church, or the non-Orthodox partner baptised in a Christian denomination the Church accepts (baptism by a female pastor or clergy is not accepted); no impediment of relationship (including godparent and godchild links).
- Second or third marriage: civil divorce certificate and ecclesiastical divorce, both issued at least one calendar month before the new wedding; widowed partner: the late spouse's death certificate.
- Application to the Archdiocese and Notice of Intended Marriage: completed in the presence of the priest at least one calendar month before the wedding, preferably three.
- Documents for each partner: birth certificate (born in Australia) or passport (born overseas); baptismal certificate (order early, especially from overseas parishes or another denomination); Certificate of Celibacy (*Agamias*) for an Orthodox partner never married, signed by two witnesses.
- Fees: Archdiocese and local church fees, as directed by the parish priest (they vary with each couple's circumstances).
- Closed dates: 13 December to Christmas Day; 5 January; Great Lent until Easter Sunday (moves each year); 1 to 15 August; 29 August; 14 September. Confirm the calendar for the couple's year with the priest.
- Sponsor (koumbaros or koumbara): usually Orthodox; a non-Orthodox Christian of an acceptable denomination may stand at the wedding but cannot be godparent to the couple's children.
- Stefana and rings: who provides them (the wedding cannot begin without them).
- Arrival times: groom and groomsmen at least 15 minutes before, the bride's party at least five minutes before.

## Greece side

- Local Metropolis: Church requirements, approval of documents (one venue states at least four weeks before), closed dates for that Metropolis, and any Metropolis fee.
- Municipality: civil requirements, registration of the marriage after the ceremony (one venue states within 40 days at the local Registry Office), and current fees.
- Australian Embassy in Athens: Certificate of No Impediment (CNI), notice of intended marriage, authentication or apostille; appointment needed for every notarial service (CNI and apostille documents can be submitted by post); fees apply; the Embassy does not give legal advice.
- Confirm whether a CNI is needed, where it should be issued (DFAT in Australia or the Embassy in Athens) and whether it needs an apostille.
- Document steps as the venue states them (example: Elounda SA lists an Apostille per document except passports, a Greek translation, documents issued within three months of the date).
- The venue's own wedding specialist, where the venue has one: its Orthodox ceremony process and which fees it charges separately (Elounda SA charges the Metropolis fee separately).
- Venue house rules for religious weddings (Mykonos Grand states both partners must be Christian Orthodox for a religious wedding on its premises).
- Back in Australia: an overseas marriage cannot be registered here; Smartraveller suggests a lawyer confirms the certificate is valid.

## Chapel or church (ask the priest and the venue, in writing)

- Chapel capacity: how many guests fit inside, and how the venue and priest handle guests who do not fit.
- Step-free access to the church or chapel.
- Photography and video: where the photographer may stand during the crowning, whether flash is allowed, phone rules for guests.
- Music: whether a chanter is provided, and whether musicians can play outside before and after.
- Dress expectation inside the church (covered shoulders).
- Timing: the time the priest serves and how long to allow; seating for older guests.
- Language: Greek, or partly English for guests.
- Rain and heat plan for any outdoor gathering before and after the service.
