# Seychelles civil marriage checklist

The documents and steps from "How to Get Married in the Seychelles", in order, plus questions to put to the Civil Status office and your resort. Requirements come from Immigration and Civil Status Seychelles, the Australian High Commission in Port Louis, Australian document legalisation guidance and Smartraveller; check their pages before you rely on any step. Fees apply to the special marriage licence, to registration where both of you are foreigners, and to legalising Australian documents: ask each office for the current amount. The article has no `[[VERIFY]]` markers in the copy and no prices (nothing for Nic to sign off).

As at November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Before you leave Australia

- [ ] Confirm you are both 18 or over.
- [ ] Decide on the civil marriage (the legal route), and whether to add a religious or symbolic ceremony. A church wedding without a civil ceremony is not legally recognised.
- [ ] Read the Getting Married page and the Marriage Registrar FAQ of Immigration and Civil Status Seychelles together.
- [ ] Plan at least two months ahead for the paperwork.
- [ ] Complete the marriage application (registration) form.
- [ ] Gather both birth certificates.
- [ ] Add divorce certificates, or the death certificate of a previous spouse, if they apply to either of you.
- [ ] Make a clear copy of both passports.
- [ ] Ask the Civil Status office whether it accepts certified copies of your birth and divorce certificates or wants the originals, and keep the answer in writing.
- [ ] Make sure every document is in English or French (translate any that are not).
- [ ] Have notarised by an Australian Notary Public any document that is not an original Australian public document.
- [ ] Lodge the Document Legalisation Request Form (signed in front of an authorised witness) for an apostille, by appointment at an Australian Passport Office or by registered mail; lodge early, well before the end-of-year rush.
- [ ] Contact the Civil Status office at Independence House, Victoria, Mahé (or by email, or through your hotel or a local wedding agent) to register the wedding eleven days in advance.
- [ ] Decide between the eleven-day banns and a special marriage licence, and ask the office what it needs with the licence application.
- [ ] If you want the ceremony away from the office, give the office at least a week's notice and confirm the exact location.
- [ ] If the ceremony is on the hotel grounds, get permission from both the hotel and the Civil Registrar.
- [ ] Settle witnesses with the office (see questions below).
- [ ] Check Smartraveller's Seychelles advice and subscribe to updates for your dates.
- [ ] Apply online for a Digital Travel Authorisation before you travel, with proof of accommodation and a return or onward ticket.
- [ ] Check whether anyone needs a yellow fever certificate (coming from a country with yellow fever), including guests.
- [ ] Take out travel insurance that covers medical evacuation, for the two of you and any parents travelling.
- [ ] See your doctor about health risks and vaccines, especially for a wet-season date.
- [ ] Book flights to arrive at least two days before the ceremony, with time after it for the certificate errands.
- [ ] Note the Australian High Commission in Port Louis, Mauritius as your consular contact.
- [ ] Pack the original documents in your hand luggage.

## In the Seychelles

- [ ] Be ready to show evidence of funds, accommodation, onward travel and travel insurance on arrival.
- [ ] Wait for the banns to run their eleven days, or apply for the special licence (normally issued within two days, subject to the Chief Officer of Civil Status).
- [ ] Attend the registration together, at the office or at the location agreed with a week's notice.
- [ ] If you marry on an island other than Mahé, Praslin or La Digue, cover the officer's transport, accommodation and meals.
- [ ] Have the marriage certificate apostilled by the Seychelles Supreme Court.
- [ ] Obtain the letter of authentication from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs before you leave.

## After the wedding

- [ ] Keep the original marriage certificate safe.
- [ ] Before using the certificate for a change of name, check with your state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages.
- [ ] Nothing to register in Australia. A recommitment ceremony at home is possible; a second marriage is not.

## Questions for the Civil Status office

- [ ] Has our application arrived, and is anything missing?
- [ ] Do you accept certified copies of our birth and divorce certificates, or do you need the originals?
- [ ] Do we need witnesses, how many, do we provide them ourselves, and what identification should they bring?
- [ ] Should we plan for the eleven-day banns or a special licence, and what do you need with the licence application?
- [ ] What is the current fee for the special licence, and for registration where both of us are foreigners, and how is it paid?
- [ ] Which officer attends, at what time of day, and how long does the formal part of the ceremony take?
- [ ] Can you confirm in writing the exact location for a ceremony away from the office?
- [ ] For an island other than Mahé, Praslin or La Digue, how are the officer's transport, accommodation and meals arranged?
- [ ] Where and when do we have the certificate apostilled by the Supreme Court and authenticated by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs?

## Questions for the resort

- [ ] Does the resort coordinate with the Civil Status office for an on-site ceremony, and who confirms the officer's time?
- [ ] Will you give written permission for the ceremony on your grounds, and where exactly can it take place?
- [ ] Is the beach in front of the resort private to its guests, and who else can walk through during the vows?
- [ ] Can we take the whole property, or the ceremony space alone, for the hours or days we need?
- [ ] Who controls access to the ceremony area, how are drones and outside photographers handled, and how does your security work alongside a private team?
- [ ] What is the covered alternative for the ceremony and the dinner if rain arrives, and when is that call made?
- [ ] What is due at each stage, what is refundable, and what happens if weather moves the date? Whose name is on the contract, and in what language and currency is it written?
- [ ] Can older guests and anyone using a wheelchair reach the ceremony space, the reception and their rooms without steps or sand?
- [ ] If you offer a wedding package, does it cover the civil formalities, the officer's attendance and the certificate errands, and will you list that in writing?
- [ ] For an outer or private island, how do you bring the officer across, and what happens if the sea is too rough on the day?
- [ ] What local advice do you give on currents and rips for guests who want to swim?
- [ ] Which inclusions will you confirm in writing before we commit?
