# Booking checklist: wedding centrepieces

Take this to your florist, stylist and venue once your venue and rough guest count are set. Tick each item as it is answered, and keep the answers in writing.

## Centrepiece planning

**Table shape**

- [ ] Confirm the table layout with the venue: rounds, long or banquet tables, or a mix.
- [ ] Round tables: brief one focal piece that reads well from every seat, with no back side.
- [ ] Long or banquet tables: brief a continuous run (a garland, repeating vessels, or clusters with candles between), not a single piece in the middle.
- [ ] A mix of long head table and guest rounds: choose one material, colour or vessel finish to carry across both.

**Height and sightlines**

- [ ] Keep arrangements low enough to talk over, or lift them well above seated eye level on a slim stand.
- [ ] Avoid the middle height: a solid wall of flowers at face level.
- [ ] At the sample or mock-up, sit down and check you can see and speak to the guest opposite.

**Scale against the room**

- [ ] Note the ceiling height and room size: tall rooms can carry height; intimate rooms and marquees usually suit low, abundant pieces.
- [ ] Confirm the number of tables, since each design repeats on every one.

**Style group**

- [ ] Choose one style group to lead the brief:
    - [ ] Low and lush florals
    - [ ] Tall and dramatic
    - [ ] Long table garlands and runners
    - [ ] Candlelit
    - [ ] Fruit, foliage and edible
    - [ ] Non floral
    - [ ] Destination and local styling
- [ ] Shortlist the ideas from that group and share them with your florist as a mood board.
- [ ] Ask your florist what is available for your date and place (see [which wedding flowers are in season in Australia](https://weddedwonderland.com/style-advice/fashionable-flowers-opt-for-in-season-or-order-imported)), rather than fixing on one flower.

**Where the money goes (no figures, just the levers)**

- [ ] Let foliage do more of the work, with fewer, larger focal blooms.
- [ ] Use candles and greenery to fill length on long tables.
- [ ] Decide which tables get a statement piece and which stay simpler.

**Candles or flameless**

- [ ] Ask the venue whether open flame is permitted before you design around real candles.
- [ ] If real candles are allowed: do they need to sit in glass, and who extinguishes them at the end of the night?
- [ ] Keep dried grasses, paper menus and trailing greenery well clear of any flame.
- [ ] Use sturdy holders that will not tip over, made of material that cannot burn, and think about draughts on terraces and in marquees.
- [ ] If flame is restricted: choose battery-operated candles with a warm, flickering light and real wax shells.

**Scent near food**

- [ ] Keep heavily scented blooms (such as lilies, tuberose and some jasmine) away from the tables.
- [ ] Save strong fragrance for the ceremony or the entrance, and choose softer scents for the tables.

**Reusing ceremony florals**

- [ ] Decide which ceremony pieces will move to the reception (low urns, aisle pieces and bud vases transfer most easily).
- [ ] Design those ceremony pieces to fit the tables.
- [ ] Agree who moves them during the drinks hour.

**Set-up and pack-down**

- [ ] Confirm who sets the tables, and at what time.
- [ ] Confirm who collects everything at the end of the night.
- [ ] Ask whether vessels, stands and candle holders are hired and must be returned.
- [ ] Agree what happens to the flowers afterwards.

## Questions for the venue

- [ ] What vessels, stands and candle holders do you hold, and are they included?
- [ ] What must we hire in, and who arranges delivery?
- [ ] Do you allow open flame, and do candles need to sit in glass?
- [ ] Are there rules on fixing anything to ceilings, beams or walls?
- [ ] When can the florist access the room to set up, and when must everything be cleared?
- [ ] Who is our contact on the day if something needs changing?

## Questions for a local florist

- [ ] Can you work from our mood board and send a sample photo before the day?
- [ ] Which flowers and foliage will you source locally, and what would need to be brought in?
- [ ] How will flowers be kept fresh between delivery and dinner, especially outdoors?
- [ ] What time will you deliver and set up, and will someone stay to adjust pieces?
- [ ] Who handles pack-down, and do you take back hired items?
- [ ] Can you put the quote, what is included and the timings in writing?
- [ ] What deposit do you need, and when is the balance due?
- [ ] If we cannot visit, can we walk through a sample centrepiece on a video call?
