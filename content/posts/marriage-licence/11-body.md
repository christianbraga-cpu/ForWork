If you are searching for a marriage licence in Australia, there is none to apply for. As at November 2026, you [give a notice of intended marriage to an authorised marriage celebrant](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. You then marry before that celebrant and 2 witnesses. Within 14 days, the celebrant submits the marriage paperwork to the registry of births, deaths and marriages in the state or territory where you married.

Marry abroad and the destination's law takes over, and some places do issue licences, so the first decision is which country makes your marriage legal. This is general information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

## Key takeaways

- Australia has no marriage licence: the equivalent step is giving a Notice of Intended Marriage to an authorised celebrant between 1 and 18 months before the wedding.
- A licence permits a marriage while a certificate records one, and in Australia the certificate that counts is the official copy from the state or territory registry, not the ceremonial one signed on the day.
- New Zealand and Hawaii issue marriage licences through their own authorities, each with its own timing rules.
- The decision that shapes everything else is which country makes your marriage legal: Australia, with a symbolic ceremony abroad, or your destination, under its own law.

## Marriage licence in Australia and the notice that replaces it

In Australia the notice, not a licence, is the step that comes before the wedding.

### Why Australia issues no licence

The Marriage Act 1961 and the Marriage Regulations 2017 set the rules for marrying in Australia. Under the [Attorney-General's Department guidance on getting married](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married), the step before the wedding is giving a notice of intended marriage form to an authorised marriage celebrant, not applying for a licence.

### The Notice of Intended Marriage

As at November 2026, section 42 of the Marriage Act requires written notice of the intended marriage to be given to the authorised celebrant who will marry you, and the [Notice of Intended Marriage form](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/publications/notice-intended-marriage) is that notice. It must reach your celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding, and you can complete it while you are overseas if you plan to marry in Australia.

The practical lesson: lodge the notice once your date and celebrant are settled, rather than in the final month. **For the form, witnessing and documents in full, read [our step by step guide to the Notice of Intended Marriage](/expert-advice/notice-of-intended-marriage-explained-step-by-step).**

### Your authorised celebrant

You must be married by an authorised marriage celebrant to be legally married in Australia. The [four types listed by the Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/find-marriage-celebrant) are Commonwealth-registered marriage celebrants, Commonwealth-registered religious marriage celebrants, ministers of religion from a recognised denomination, and state and territory officers.

Someone applying to become a Commonwealth-registered celebrant [cannot accept a notice of intended marriage or solemnise a marriage](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/become-marriage-celebrant/apply-be-marriage-celebrant) until they are registered. For more on this, see [who can legally perform a marriage in Australia](/expert-advice/who-can-perform-a-marriage).

### The words the law requires

Under [section 46 of the Marriage Act](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html), a celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination must say to you, in the presence of the witnesses, the words the Act sets out, or words to that effect, which include: "Marriage, according to law in Australia, is the union of 2 people to the exclusion of all others, voluntarily entered into for life."

Under [section 45](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html), it is sufficient for each of you to say to the other, before the celebrant and the witnesses, the words the Act sets out, or words to that effect. A minister of religion may instead use any form and ceremony their religious body recognises as sufficient.

### Signing and registration

On the day, 3 marriage certificates are signed by you and your spouse, your celebrant and 2 witnesses aged over 18.

Within 14 days, your celebrant must submit the marriage paperwork to the registry of births, deaths and marriages in the state or territory where you married.

Where licences exist, one comes before the wedding and the other after it. As at November 2026, the [Hawaii State Department of Health](https://health.hawaii.gov/vitalrecords/marriage-licenses/) treats them as different documents: the licence permits the marriage, and the certificate shows that a legal marriage has been performed.

Australia has no licence, so every document you hold is a certificate, and they are not equal.

### The certificates signed on the day

Your celebrant gives you a certificate of marriage on the day, and it is a ceremonial certificate of your marriage.

It is the one couples frame, but [the commemorative certificate from your wedding day is not the official, legal marriage certificate](https://www.bdm.vic.gov.au/changing-your-name-after-marriage-separation-divorce).

### The official registry certificate

You [apply for a copy of your official marriage certificate](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) from the registry, and your celebrant can do this on your behalf.

If you searched for a marriage licence in NSW or Victoria, the registry document to look for is the official marriage certificate, issued after the wedding. In NSW, see [official marriage certificate (NSW Registry of Births, Deaths and Marriages)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/family-and-relationships/marriages/applying-for-an-official-marriage-certificate). In Victoria, see [get a marriage certificate (Births, Deaths and Marriages Victoria)](https://www.bdm.vic.gov.au/get-marriage-certificate).

### Why the registry copy matters

You will need the official certificate to change your name or to prove that you are married. To update a married surname in NSW, you [prove the marriage with an official certificate from the registry in the state or territory where you married](https://www.nsw.gov.au/family-and-relationships/name-changes-and-corrections/change-your-surname-after-marriage), and ceremonial or commemorative certificates are not accepted.

[Centrelink does not accept ceremonial marriage certificates](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/how-to-update-your-name-with-centrelink?context=64107) as a supporting document for a legal name change either. Our guide to [changing your name after marriage](/expert-advice/name-change-after-marriage) takes you through the rest.

## How marriage licences work overseas

Outside Australia, the destination's law decides the paperwork. As at November 2026, an Australian-authorised celebrant [cannot perform an overseas marriage](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married), and an overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia.

### New Zealand marriage licence

In New Zealand you need a [marriage licence](https://www.govt.nz/browse/family-and-whanau/getting-married/get-a-marriage-licence/), which confirms it is legal for you to marry, and visitors can apply without being New Zealand citizens. Arrange it at least 3 working days before the wedding; it expires after 3 months.

Couples from outside New Zealand sign a declaration at a registry office after they arrive, separate from the ceremony, and can only marry at the places named on the application, so name a wet weather alternative too. For the full process, read [how to get married in New Zealand as an Australian](/destination-weddings/get-married-in-new-zealand).

### Hawaii marriage licence

To marry lawfully in Hawaii you need a licence from an authorised agent, as set out on the Hawaii State Department of Health's [Marriage and Civil Union Licenses](https://health.hawaii.gov/vitalrecords/marriage-licenses/) page. You apply online, then appear together in person before an agent no sooner than 30 days before the ceremony, each with valid government-issued photo ID.

The ceremony must take place within 30 days of picking up the licence, which is valid only within the State of Hawaii, and the person who marries you must hold a licence from the Department of Health. There are no state residence or US citizenship requirements, and our [Hawaii wedding guide](/destination-weddings/getting-married-in-hawaii) sets out the timeline.

### Other destinations

For anywhere else, ask the destination's embassy or consulate what you will need. As at November 2026, [Smartraveller's guidance on getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) notes that some countries ask you to prove you are not already married, commonly with a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) or certificates from a registry of births, deaths and marriages, and some set further rules, such as a residence period.

The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) can issue a CNI in Australia, but some local authorities only accept one issued in their own country, so ask the embassy or consulate whether you need a CNI, where to get it and whether it needs an apostille.

### Recognition back in Australia

An overseas marriage is generally recognised in Australia if it was valid in the country where it took place and would have been valid here. You need to have registered the marriage in that country and hold evidence such as the marriage certificate. Once your marriage is recognised, you [cannot marry again in Australia](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/marriage-equality-australia), though you can hold a confirmation of vows or a recommitment ceremony.

Plan the name change separately. Some states and territories do not accept overseas marriage certificates for a name change, and [in NSW you apply for an official change of name instead](https://www.nsw.gov.au/family-and-relationships/marriages/if-youre-not-getting-married-nsw), which does not mean the marriage is unrecognised. Ask your local registry of births, deaths and marriages before you travel.

## Choosing where to marry legally

With both systems in view, the choice is which country makes your marriage legal.

### Marry at home and celebrate abroad

You marry legally in Australia, perhaps in a small ceremony with your authorised celebrant and 2 witnesses, then hold the celebration overseas as a symbolic ceremony. The notice, the celebrant and the registry all stay at home, so the official certificate you need to change your name or prove your marriage comes from an Australian registry. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do you need a marriage licence to get married in Australia?

No. As at November 2026, you give a notice of intended marriage to an authorised celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding, under [the Attorney-General's Department guidance](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married).

### What is the difference between a marriage licence and a marriage certificate?

A licence permits a marriage and a certificate shows that a legal marriage has been performed, as the Hawaii State Department of Health explains. In Australia there is no licence, and the certificate you need to change your name or prove your marriage is the official one from the registry.

### How do you register your marriage in Australia?

Your celebrant submits the paperwork to the registry in the state or territory where you married within 14 days of the wedding. You then apply to the registry for the official certificate, or ask your celebrant to apply for you.

### What makes a marriage valid in Australia?

As at November 2026, you must not already be married, not be marrying a parent, grandparent, child, grandchild, brother or sister, and be at least 18 (unless a court has approved a marriage where 1 person is 16 to 18). You must also understand what marriage means, agree to marry voluntarily and use specific words in the ceremony.

### Can you get married without witnesses?

Not in Australia. Two witnesses aged over 18 sign the marriage certificates, and the celebrant must be physically present with you and the witnesses. Overseas, ask the destination's embassy or consulate.

### Is a marriage licence from New Zealand or Hawaii recognised in Australia?

What is recognised is the marriage, not the licence. [An overseas marriage](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) is generally recognised in Australia if it was valid under the law of the country where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. It cannot be registered here.
