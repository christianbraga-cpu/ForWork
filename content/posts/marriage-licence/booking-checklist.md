# Paperwork checklist: marriage licence and the Notice of Intended Marriage

These questions and checks sit with the couple, their authorised celebrant, the registry and, if marrying abroad, the destination's authorities, so they do not block publishing. The Wedded Advisor plans the celebration; the notice, documents, ceremony, certificates and registration stay with the couple and their celebrant or the local authority.

Everything here follows the article as at November 2026 (general information, not legal advice). It adds no legal claims and no fees. Confirm each point with your celebrant, the registry or the destination's embassy or consulate for your own date.

## The Australian steps, in order

### 1. Choose your authorised celebrant

- [ ] Is the person marrying you an authorised marriage celebrant, and which of the four types listed by the Attorney-General's Department are they?
- [ ] If they are applying to become a Commonwealth-registered celebrant, are they registered yet? (They cannot accept your notice or marry you until they are.)
- [ ] Is your date settled with your celebrant before you lodge the notice?

### 2. Lodge the Notice of Intended Marriage within the window

- [ ] Will the notice reach your celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding?
- [ ] Have you planned to lodge it once your date and celebrant are settled, rather than in the final month?
- [ ] If one or both of you are overseas, have you asked your celebrant how to complete the notice from abroad?

### 3. Documents

- [ ] Have you asked your celebrant exactly which documents each of you needs to bring, and in what form?
- [ ] Have you read our step by step guide to the Notice of Intended Marriage for the form, witnessing and documents in full?

### 4. The ceremony

- [ ] Have you arranged 2 witnesses aged over 18?
- [ ] Has your celebrant taken you through the words the law requires, and shown you where they sit in your ceremony?
- [ ] Is the signing of the 3 marriage certificates (you, your spouse, your celebrant and 2 witnesses) in the run sheet?

### 5. The official certificate from the registry

- [ ] Has your celebrant confirmed they will submit the paperwork to the registry of births, deaths and marriages in the state or territory where you married within 14 days?
- [ ] Who is applying for the official marriage certificate from the registry: you, or your celebrant on your behalf?
- [ ] Do you know the difference between the ceremonial certificate signed on the day and the official registry certificate you need to change your name or prove you are married?

## Questions for your celebrant

- [ ] Are you on the official register, and which type of authorised celebrant are you?
- [ ] By what date do you need our Notice of Intended Marriage for our wedding date?
- [ ] Which documents do you need to see from each of us, and when?
- [ ] Can we see the full ceremony wording, including the words the law requires, before the day?
- [ ] Will you apply for our official marriage certificate on our behalf, or should we apply to the registry ourselves?
- [ ] If we marry legally in Australia and celebrate abroad, is there anything we should know before planning the symbolic ceremony?

## Questions for the registry of births, deaths and marriages

- [ ] How do we apply for our official marriage certificate, and how long does it take to arrive?
- [ ] Which certificate do you accept to update a married surname?
- [ ] If we marry overseas, do you accept an overseas marriage certificate for a name change, or do we apply for an official change of name instead?
- [ ] What should we bring or prepare before we travel?

## If marrying abroad: questions for the destination's embassy or consulate

- [ ] What do we need to marry legally in your country as Australians, and in what order?
- [ ] Do you need proof that we are not already married, such as a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) or certificates from a registry of births, deaths and marriages?
- [ ] If a CNI is needed, where must it be issued (Australia or your country), and does it need an apostille?
- [ ] Is there a residence period or any other rule we must meet before the wedding?
- [ ] Is a marriage licence issued, by whom, how far ahead must we apply and how long is it valid?
- [ ] Do we need to appear in person, sign a declaration or attend an office after we arrive, separate from the ceremony?
- [ ] Is the marriage registered locally, and how do we obtain the marriage certificate afterwards?
- [ ] Do any of our documents need translating?

### New Zealand and Hawaii

- [ ] New Zealand: have you re-read the govt.nz marriage licence page for your date, including timing, expiry, the registry office declaration and the places named on the application (with a wet weather alternative)?
- [ ] Hawaii: have you re-read the Hawaii State Department of Health marriage licence page for your date, including the online application, the in-person appearance with photo ID, the 30-day windows and the licensed officiant?

## Before you leave and when you return

- [ ] Have you read Smartraveller's guidance on getting married overseas?
- [ ] Have you asked your local registry how your overseas marriage certificate will be treated for a name change?
- [ ] Will you hold evidence of the marriage, such as the marriage certificate from the country where you married?
