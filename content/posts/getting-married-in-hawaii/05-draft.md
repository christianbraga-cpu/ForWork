Hawaii is on your list, and before you fall for a lawn above the Pacific you want to know two things: will the marriage be legal, and what do you do first? Getting married in Hawaii as an Australian couple is straightforward. You apply online for a marriage licence, meet a licence agent together in person, and marry with a state-licensed officiant within the licence's 30 days. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_03 --> <!-- fact_05 --> <!-- fact_11 --> There is no residency requirement, no US citizenship requirement and no blood test. <!-- fact_06 -->

This guide takes each step in the order you will meet it, from entry documents to the certificate you bring home.

## Key takeaways

- Australians can marry legally in Hawaii by following the Hawaii State Department of Health marriage licence process, with no residency requirement. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_06 -->
- You both meet a licence agent in person no sooner than 30 days before the ceremony, and the wedding must take place within 30 days of picking up the licence. <!-- fact_03 --> <!-- fact_05 -->
- Your officiant must hold a State of Hawaii performer licence, whoever they are and wherever they come from. <!-- fact_11 -->
- Order certified copies of your marriage certificate early, because they are what you use as proof of the marriage back in Australia. <!-- fact_200 --> <!-- fact_104 -->

## Can Australians marry legally in Hawaii?

Yes. As at November 2026, to marry lawfully in Hawaii you need a marriage licence issued by an authorised agent of the Hawaii State Department of Health. <!-- fact_01 --> There are no state residence or US citizenship requirements, and blood tests are not required. <!-- fact_06 --> The legal age to marry is 18, and proof of age is required. <!-- fact_07 -->

Two documents matter, and they are easy to confuse. The marriage licence is what permits the marriage to take place. The marriage certificate shows that a legal marriage has been performed. <!-- fact_207 --> You collect the first before the wedding and order copies of the second afterwards.

The full requirements sit on the Department's [marriage licences page](https://health.hawaii.gov/vitalrecords/marriage-licenses/), which is worth reading once before you book flights.

Australia, for its part, does not recognise an overseas marriage where either person is already married, is not old enough to marry, is too closely related to the other, or is forced to marry. <!-- fact_103 --> Outside those cases, a marriage that was valid where it took place, and would have been valid under Australian law, is in most cases legally recognised at home. <!-- fact_104 -->

### Legal, symbolic or religious ceremony

A **legal ceremony** in Hawaii follows the licence process below and is performed by a state-licensed officiant. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_11 --> This is the route for couples who want the date on the beach to be the date on the certificate.

A **symbolic ceremony** carries no legal weight, so it needs no Hawaii licence. Couples who choose it usually marry legally at home, then travel for the celebration with every logistical question answered in advance.

A **religious ceremony** can also be the legal one. The test is the same: the person officiating must be licensed by the State of Hawaii Department of Health before performing a marriage. <!-- fact_11 -->

## Apply for your marriage licence

### Step 1: Complete the online application

The first step happens at home. Complete the online marriage licence application at emrs.ehawaii.gov and pay the required fee. <!-- fact_02 --> When you finish, write down or print your Authorization Code, also called the Locator ID. <!-- fact_02 --> The agent uses it to find your application at the appointment.

Fill it in together, with your passports open beside you, so names and dates match your ID exactly. A mismatch is far easier to fix at a desk in Sydney than at a counter in Honolulu.

### Step 2: Book your appointment with an agent

The main licensing office is on Oahu, in Honolulu. It is open Monday to Friday, excluding Hawaii State holidays, and appointments can be booked online or by email. <!-- fact_208 --> Agents on Hawaii Island, Kauai, Maui and Molokai are reached by phone, and private agents in the community are also listed by the Department. <!-- fact_10 --> <!-- fact_208 -->

If you are marrying on a neighbour island, book an agent there. It saves an inter-island flight in the busiest week of your trip.

## Pick up your licence together, in person

This is the step that shapes your itinerary. You meet the agent no sooner than 30 days before the ceremony date. The agent reviews your application, verifies your photo IDs and any documents needed, and issues the licence. <!-- fact_03 -->

You must appear together, in person. Proxies are not allowed. <!-- fact_03 --> If one of you lands a day later than the other, the appointment waits for the second flight.

### What to bring

- **Photo ID for both of you.** A valid government-issued photo ID, such as a passport, for each applicant. <!-- fact_04 -->
- **Proof of age.** The legal age to marry is 18 and proof of age is required. <!-- fact_07 --> Younger applicants need a certified copy of their birth certificate; check with the agent. <!-- fact_07 -->
- **Divorce decree or death certificate, if recent.** If either of you was married before, bring a certified divorce decree or death certificate when the divorce was finalised, or the death occurred, less than 30 days before you pick up the licence. <!-- fact_08 -->
- **Certified translations.** Any document not in English must come with a certified translation. <!-- fact_08 -->
- **Name change papers, if relevant.** A certified copy of a legal name change is required where it applies. <!-- fact_09 -->

Agents may ask for more documentation depending on your circumstances, at their discretion. <!-- fact_09 --> Carry certified copies of anything that tells the story of your names and past marriages, even where the list does not strictly require it.

The licence list does not include a [Certificate of No Impediment](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/what-is-a-certificate-of-no-impediment-and-who-issues-it). <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_07 --> <!-- fact_08 --> <!-- fact_09 --> If an agent asks for proof that neither of you is already married, Australia's registries of births, deaths and marriages can issue a statement that there is no record of an earlier marriage. <!-- fact_102 --> DFAT can issue a CNI in Australia as well. <!-- fact_100 -->

### Timing the appointment around your arrival

Two 30-day rules work together. The agent cannot issue the licence more than 30 days before the ceremony, and the ceremony must take place within 30 days of picking it up. <!-- fact_03 --> <!-- fact_05 --> There is no waiting period once it is issued, so you can marry the next day if you choose. <!-- fact_05 -->

For couples flying in from Australia, that means one practical thing: see the agent in Hawaii, in the first days after you land. Leave a buffer before the rehearsal and the welcome dinner, and keep the appointment away from any day with an early inter-island flight.

Two more rules are firm. The licence is valid only in the State of Hawaii, so it cannot travel with you to a ceremony elsewhere in the United States. If the ceremony does not take place within the 30 days, the licence is null and void. <!-- fact_05 -->

## Choose a state-licensed officiant

Every person who performs a marriage in Hawaii must be licensed by the State of Hawaii Department of Health before performing it. <!-- fact_11 --> That includes a celebrant you love at home, a family friend and a minister from your own parish.

If you want someone from Australia to marry you, they apply for a Hawaii performer licence. Performers register online at marriage.ehawaii.gov. <!-- fact_11 --> Start early, and do not print their name on the order of service until the licence is confirmed.

If you book a local officiant, ask to see their current State of Hawaii performer licence before you pay a deposit. <!-- fact_11 --> It is a simple question, and a good officiant expects it.

Use the conversation to ask how and when the marriage is registered after the ceremony, and what they need from you on the day.

## Your ceremony, recording and certified copies

On the day, your licence is the document in play. It permits the marriage, and the ceremony with your licensed officiant performs it. <!-- fact_207 --> <!-- fact_11 --> Hand the licence to your officiant before the guests are seated, so nothing is hunted for at the last minute.

Ask your officiant how and when the marriage is registered with the State, so you know when to place your order for copies.

### Order certified copies

The marriage certificate is the document that shows a legal marriage has been performed. <!-- fact_207 --> Certified copies can be ordered online, by mail or in person, and online ordering is the fast route. You need a government-issued ID, and a fee applies. <!-- fact_200 --> The Department's [birth and marriage certificates page](https://health.hawaii.gov/vitalrecords/birth-marriage-certificates/) has the order form.

Certified copies are issued only to someone with a direct and tangible interest in the record, which includes the registrant and the registrant's spouse. <!-- fact_202 --> Either of you can order.

Processing usually takes up to four to six weeks, and delays can occur. <!-- fact_203 --> Order more than one copy. Name changes, banks and passports all ask for proof, and a spare saves a second wait.

## Getting your certificate ready for Australia

You cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia. In most cases it is legally recognised anyway, provided it was valid under the law where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law had it happened here. <!-- fact_104 --> Our guide on [registering an overseas marriage at home](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/do-you-have-to-register-an-overseas-marriage-at-home) explains why there is nothing to lodge.

A foreign marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage. It may not work as proof of a name change, so check with your state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages first. <!-- fact_104 --> For peace of mind, a lawyer can confirm the certificate is valid in Australia. <!-- fact_104 --> [Smartraveller's guide to getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) covers the Australian side in full, and our page on [your overseas marriage certificate](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate) covers how to use it.

### Apostille

An Apostille certifies a public document for use in another country. Whether you need one depends on who will receive the certificate, so ask that authority before you order. <!-- fact_106 --> Our explainer, [what is an Apostille and does my paperwork need one](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/what-is-an-apostille-and-does-my-paperwork-need-one), walks through the decision.

If you do need one on your Hawaii certificate, apostille or authentication requests to the Department are mail-in only. <!-- fact_201 --> Build that postage into your timeline, alongside the usual processing time for copies. <!-- fact_203 -->

### Registering or recognising your marriage at home

Once Australia recognises your Hawaii marriage, you cannot marry again in Australia. You may hold another type of ceremony, such as a confirmation of vows or a recommitment ceremony. <!-- fact_105 --> Many couples do exactly that, with a long lunch at home for the guests who could not fly.

## Permits for beach and outdoor ceremonies

Sand is public in a way a lawn is not. A beach wedding on unencumbered State shoreline needs a right-of-entry permit through the Department of Land and Natural Resources' Wiki Permits system, and the permitted beach sites are listed by DLNR. <!-- fact_204 -->

The shoreline is generally under State jurisdiction even where there is a county park behind it, so an event on the sand needs that permit. A few beaches are managed by other agencies, such as the counties, and you seek authorisation from that agency instead. <!-- fact_205 -->

The applicant submits a Certificate of Liability Insurance with the application, and the ceremony must follow DLNR's General Terms and Conditions for Commercial Activity. <!-- fact_206 --> The [DLNR commercial activities page](https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/ld/commercial-activities/) sets out the process and the site list.

Ceremonies on resort or private grounds are a different matter. Ask the venue directly whether its lawn or terrace needs any permit, and who holds it.

## When to marry in Hawaii

<!-- IMAGE SLOT when-to-marry: Landscape 3:2. A named venue's ceremony or reception space photographed in a recorded month, late afternoon or sunset light, sea and sky visible. No people needed. Month taken recorded in the register or EXIF. -->

Hawaii's statewide climate is mild all year, with moderate humidity, persistent north-easterly trade winds, big differences in rainfall over short distances and infrequent severe storms. <!-- fact_117 --> For most of the islands there are only two seasons: summer from May to October and winter from October to April. <!-- fact_117 -->

Statewide, August and September are the warmest months and February and March the coolest. <!-- fact_118 --> Winter brings more frequent cloud and rainstorms, with the heaviest rain from winter storms between October and April. <!-- fact_118 --> The sea follows a gentle curve, coolest in late February and March and warmest in late September or early October. <!-- fact_119 --> Day length runs from about 11 hours to about 13.5 hours. <!-- fact_119 -->

Where you stand matters as much as when. Windward coasts and slopes get far more cloud and rain, while leeward areas tend to be sunny and dry. <!-- fact_118 --> For a ceremony timed to sunset, a leeward lawn is the more settled bet.

Storms are infrequent, but they happen. If you are planning a Hawaii trip, monitor the forecast and check directly with your airline, accommodation and activity providers for updates that may affect your plans. <!-- fact_113 -->

Each island suits a different kind of wedding. The licence process is the same on all of them, but the agent you meet and the flights you book are not. <!-- fact_10 -->

### Oahu

Most travellers reach Oahu through Daniel K. Inouye International Airport in Honolulu, the main gateway to Hawaii. <!-- fact_121 --> It is a year-round destination, with big winter waves on the North Shore and the warmest water in summer. Its resort areas include Waikiki, Kahala, the North Shore and Ko Olina on the Leeward Coast. <!-- fact_121 --> It is also home to the main licensing office. <!-- fact_208 -->

### Maui

Most visitors fly into Kahului Airport. <!-- fact_122 --> South Maui, around Wailea and Kihei, holds the oceanside hotels, and West Maui's Kaanapali Beach is known for its resorts and sunsets. <!-- fact_122 --> It suits a wedding weekend built around one resort and a long evening on the terrace.

### Kauai

Kauai, the Garden Island, is known for the Napali Coast and Waimea Canyon. Its main airport is Lihue, with most travellers connecting through Honolulu. <!-- fact_123 --> Summer brings calmer ocean conditions and winter bigger waves, which matters for anything planned near the water. <!-- fact_123 -->

### Hawaii Island

The largest of the islands, Hawaii Island is set apart by its size, its range of climates and Hawaii Volcanoes National Park. <!-- fact_124 --> Travellers fly into Kona or Hilo, and the luxury resorts sit along the Kona-Kohala Coast. <!-- fact_124 -->

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you need witnesses to get married in Hawaii?

The licence steps centre on the two of you, your licence agent and a state-licensed officiant. <!-- fact_03 --> <!-- fact_11 --> Ask your officiant what the ceremony itself requires on the day, including witnesses, and how the marriage is registered afterwards.

### Can a friend from Australia officiate our wedding in Hawaii?

Yes, if they hold a State of Hawaii performer licence. Every officiant must be licensed by the State of Hawaii Department of Health before performing a marriage, and performers register online at marriage.ehawaii.gov. <!-- fact_11 -->

### How far ahead can we get our Hawaii marriage licence?

You can complete the online application before you travel, but you meet the agent no sooner than 30 days before the ceremony. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_03 --> The ceremony must then take place within 30 days of pick-up, or the licence is void. <!-- fact_05 -->

### Is a Hawaii marriage recognised in Australia?

In most cases, yes. An overseas marriage is legally recognised in Australia if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. <!-- fact_104 --> You cannot register it in Australia, and your Hawaii certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage. <!-- fact_104 -->

### Do we need a permit to marry on a Hawaii beach?

On unencumbered State shoreline, yes: a right-of-entry permit through DLNR's Wiki Permits system, with a Certificate of Liability Insurance. <!-- fact_204 --> <!-- fact_206 --> A few beaches are managed by other agencies, such as the counties, and they authorise those instead. <!-- fact_205 -->

### Can we have a symbolic ceremony in Hawaii and marry legally at home?

Yes. A symbolic ceremony is not a legal marriage, so the Hawaii licence applies only if you want the ceremony to be legally binding. <!-- fact_01 --> If you marry legally in Hawaii instead, you cannot marry again in Australia, though you can hold a recommitment ceremony. <!-- fact_105 -->
