Paros wedding venues sit in three parts of the island: Naoussa and the bays of the north coast, Parikia and the hills above its gulf in the west, and Golden Beach on the east coast. The right one depends on your guest count, the ceremony you want (chapel, beach or terrace) and whether you would rather your guests sleep under the same roof.

This guide covers both halves of the question: where to marry, and how to get married on Paros. ## Choosing an area of Paros

Paros lies at the centre of the Cyclades, and the island's character shifts from coast to coast. <!-- fact_307 --> Start with the setting you want, then think about where guests will sleep and how they will get around.

**Naoussa and the north.** Naoussa has a harbour and an organised marina, with summer day cruises to Mykonos, Tinos and Delos. <!-- fact_312 --> Astir of Paros and Petra Farm sit beside and above Kolymbithres bay, Anemoi looks over Naoussa bay, and Monastiri is within Paros Park. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_400 --> <!-- fact_16 --> <!-- fact_14 --> This is the area for a chapel, a garden and a harbour village for the days around the wedding.

**Parikia and the west.** Parikia is the main port, and the island's ferries arrive here from Piraeus and Rafina all year. <!-- fact_312 --> The venues on this side look out over the gulf and the Bay of Parikia. <!-- fact_100 --> <!-- fact_411 --> It suits couples who want a view over the water and a base close to the port where ferry guests arrive.

**Golden Beach and the east coast.** Golden Beach (Chryssi Akti) is a sandy beach on the bus loop that runs on to Drios. <!-- fact_200 --> <!-- fact_313 --> Here you choose between a beachfront hotel with its own chapel and an estate in the hills above. <!-- fact_200 --> <!-- fact_405 -->

**The inland villages.** Lefkes and Marpissa sit on the island's bus loops. <!-- fact_313 --> None of the venues on this list is inland, but the villages make a quiet base for the days before and after.

Antiparos is about 10 minutes across the water from Pounta, with ferries every 30 minutes all year. <!-- fact_312 -->

Decide early whether the ceremony on Paros will be your legal marriage or a celebration of one. That choice decides your paperwork, your timeline and, in some cases, which venues work for you.

### Civil, Greek Orthodox and symbolic ceremonies

There are three broad paths: a wedding in one of the island's churches or chapels, a civil marriage through the Municipality of Paros, or a symbolic ceremony at a venue. <!-- fact_300 --> <!-- fact_301 --> <!-- fact_406 --> Confirm with the municipality and the Australian Embassy in Athens which path makes you legally married, and on what documents, before you book.

Paros has no shortage of churches. [Weddings on Paros](https://imagine.paros.gr/en/wedding-honeymoon/417-wedding-honeymoon.html) are held at country chapels and at Panagia Ekatontapiliani, with guests arriving by boat, horse-drawn carriage or on foot with musicians. <!-- fact_300 --> Favourite churches include:

- Panagia Ekatontapiliani
- Agia Anna in Pandroso
- Agios Fokas at the entrance to the port of Parikia
- Agios Ioannis in Kolymbithres
- Zoodochou Pigis in Lageri
- Agios Nikolaos in Piso Livadi
- Agios Nikolaos in Pyrgaki <!-- fact_300 -->

If you hope to marry in a Greek Orthodox church, raise it with the church itself before you plan the day around it. Several venues also have chapels of their own, but they do not publish which ceremony types they hold, so ask each one directly. <!-- fact_404 --> <!-- fact_19 --> <!-- fact_401 -->

For a symbolic ceremony, The Secret View offers one in its own words: "a romantic symbolic ceremony with a view". <!-- fact_406 -->

### A civil ceremony with the Municipality of Paros

The [Municipality of Paros](https://www.paros.gr/) has a Civil Marriages section within its Registry Office, in the municipality's own offices. <!-- fact_301 --> Ask the municipality for the steps, documents, ceremony location and lead times that apply to foreign couples. <!-- fact_301 -->

Before you set a date, check your documents with the Australian Embassy in Athens as well, including any fees. Allow time for any documents that need an authentication or apostille. <!-- fact_302 -->

### Steps for Australian couples

As at November 2026, [some countries ask you](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) to prove you are free to marry, most often with a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) and registry certificates. <!-- fact_302 --> Local authorities may also ask for divorce papers, a late spouse's death certificate, and your original birth certificate and passport. <!-- fact_302 -->

**Proving you are free to marry.** The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade can issue a CNI in Australia, but some local authorities accept only ones issued in their own country. <!-- fact_302 --> Ask the Greek embassy or consulate:

- whether you need one
- whether you can get it in Australia
- whether it needs an authentication or apostille. <!-- fact_302 -->

**In Athens.** The [Australian Embassy in Athens](https://greece.embassy.gov.au/athn/notarial.html) witnesses CNI applications, issues CNIs, witnesses notices of intended marriage and legalises documents by authentication or apostille. <!-- fact_304 --> All notarial services need an appointment, Monday to Friday, 9am to 12.30pm, though apostille and CNI documents can be sent by post. <!-- fact_304 -->

Apostilled documents and CNIs are ready within two working days unless same-day collection is arranged. <!-- fact_304 --> The Embassy gives no legal advice, honorary consuls cannot provide notarial services, and fees apply. <!-- fact_304 -->

**Back home.** An overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia, but in most cases it is legally recognised if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. <!-- fact_303 --> A foreign marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of marriage but may not work as proof of a name change, and a lawyer can confirm your certificate is valid in Australia. <!-- fact_303 -->

The full picture for marrying in Greece as Australians is in [our guide to Greece wedding venues](https://weddedwonderland.com/destination-weddings/greece-wedding-venues#getting-legally-married-in-greece-as-australians-in-brief). For the customs around the day itself, read our piece on [Greek wedding traditions](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/greek-wedding-traditions).

## Getting to Paros

Guests can fly or take the ferry. Flights from Athens International Airport to Paros take about 45 minutes, and from Thessaloniki about 1 hour 15 minutes. <!-- fact_307 --> Paros Airport (PAS) is 9 km southwest of Parikia. <!-- fact_311 -->

**By sea.** The island's main port is at Parikia, reached by ferry from Piraeus and Rafina all year round. <!-- fact_312 --> As at November 2026, [ferries and large catamarans](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) carry most visitors to the Greek islands, and services can be delayed or cancelled in bad weather, mainly because of strong winds, so reconfirm departures. <!-- fact_310 --> Expect crowds and heat at the busy ports from May to October, and stairs to carry luggage up and down. <!-- fact_310 -->

**Around the island.** Public buses link the villages with Parikia and Naoussa all year, from a station next to the port. <!-- fact_313 --> Arrange a coach or taxi for guests in advance. <!-- fact_310 -->

**Entry.** Australians can visit the Schengen area visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period, and must register biometrics under the Entry/Exit System on arrival. <!-- fact_310 --> Build that into the arrival day for older guests.

The island also has [marinas at Naoussa and Piso Livadi](https://imagine.paros.gr/en/gettingtoparos/ports-aiport.html). <!-- fact_312 --> For more on the island itself, see [Visit Greece](https://www.visitgreece.gr/en/islands/cyclades/paros).

**Temperature.** On [Naxos](http://oldportal.emy.gr/emy/en/climatology/climatology_city?perifereia=South%20Aegean&poli=Naxos), the mean monthly maximum is 22.1°C in May, 25.9°C in June, 27.1°C in July, 27.0°C in August, 25.1°C in September and 21.9°C in October. <!-- fact_305 -->

**Rain.** Naxos averages 3.6 days of rain in May, 1.1 in June, 0.4 in July, 0.3 in August, 1.9 in September and 5.5 in October. <!-- fact_306 --> A covered space still earns its place on the plan.

**Wind.** At Naxos the prevailing wind blows from the north in every month. <!-- fact_306 --> On Paros, the Piso Livadi marina in the south is protected from the strong north winds, and only strong westerlies cause trouble at Parikia port. <!-- fact_312 --> Ask each venue how an exposed terrace or beach ceremony works on a breezy afternoon.

**Heat and wildfire.** As at November 2026, heatwaves are frequent in Greece over the summer months and can cause heat exhaustion and heat stroke. <!-- fact_308 --> Wildfires occur in the extended summer from June to September, including near tourist areas on the islands, and alerts arrive by mobile phone from 112. <!-- fact_308 --> For a summer date, plan shade, a later ceremony and a covered fallback.

**Swimming.** Lifeguards work at many Greek beaches from June to September, and sea conditions can change quickly, so choose patrolled beaches for guests where you can. <!-- fact_309 -->

May and October sit either side of the warmest months at the Naxos station. <!-- fact_305 --> Whichever month you choose, confirm that the venue and its ceremony spaces are open on your date.

## Your booking checklist

Ask every venue on your shortlist these questions, and get the answers in writing:

- **Capacity:** the seated number for your ceremony space and your reception space, on your layout.
- **Ceremony type:** whether the chapel or terrace hosts a religious, civil or symbolic ceremony.
- **Rooms:** how many rooms your guests can hold on your dates, and any minimum stay.
- **Exclusive use:** whether the wedding spaces, or the whole property, can be closed to other guests.
- **Access:** whether there is a step-free route between guest rooms, the ceremony and the reception, for guests with limited mobility.
- **Getting there:** the drive time from Paros Airport and Parikia port, and, for venues without rooms, where guests usually stay.
- **Music:** what time amplified music must stop, and where an after-party can move.
- **Weather:** the plan for a windy afternoon, a rare shower or a heatwave.
- **Suppliers:** whether you may bring your own, and any rules for set-up and pack-down times.
- **Extras:** whether fireworks, a live band or a boat arrival are possible on your date.
