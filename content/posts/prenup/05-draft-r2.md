## What is a prenup in Australia?

"Prenup" is the everyday word. The legal term is a financial agreement, and the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia describes it as a contract between two or more parties made under Part VIIIA of the Family Law Act 1975 for marriages, or Division 4 of Part VIIIAB for de facto relationships. <!-- fact_01 --> Moneysmart, from ASIC, puts it simply: a binding financial agreement, also known as a prenup. <!-- fact_22 -->

Legal Aid NSW describes a binding financial agreement as a contract between two people who are, or were, married or in a de facto relationship. <!-- fact_11 --> What it does is set out how money and assets will be divided if they separate. <!-- fact_11 --> If it is binding, the agreement ousts the Court's jurisdiction over financial or property proceedings between you, either generally or on the specific issues it covers. <!-- fact_02 --> Prepared and entered into properly, it can be a way to contract out of court proceedings. <!-- fact_02 -->

The same framework covers agreements made at any stage. For a marriage, the agreement can be made before the marriage (section 90B), during it (section 90C) or after it (section 90D). <!-- fact_04 --> De facto couples have matching provisions: before (section 90UB), during (section 90UC) or after (section 90UD) the relationship. <!-- fact_05 -->

You don't go to court to make one, but once made it is legally enforceable. <!-- fact_12 --> The court's own [financial agreements page](https://www.fcfcoa.gov.au/fl/fp/financial-agreements) is the plainest starting point, and it describes the law in this area as very complex. <!-- fact_09 -->

## Prenup requirements: what makes a binding financial agreement binding

As at November 2026, the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia states that a financial agreement must meet technical requirements set out in section 90G of the Family Law Act for marriages (section 90UJ for de facto relationships) to be binding. <!-- fact_06 --> The Act's own heading for section 90G is "When financial agreements are binding". <!-- fact_24 -->

The Court also states that each party must be provided with independent legal advice about prescribed matters by an Australian legal practitioner before entering into the agreement, and that this is mandatory. <!-- fact_07 --> Its separation guidance puts it the other way round: you cannot enter into a financial agreement without each first getting independent legal advice from an Australian lawyer. <!-- fact_10 -->

Legal Aid NSW sets out the formal requirements for a binding financial agreement to be legally valid. <!-- fact_13 --> The agreement must:

1. Be in writing.
2. Say which part of the Family Law Act it is made under.
3. Deal with property, financial resources, spousal maintenance or related matters.
4. Not deal with issues covered by an earlier agreement that is still in force.
5. Be signed by all parties.
6. Be made after both parties have received independent legal advice.
7. Include a separation declaration, if necessary.
8. Not have been terminated or set aside by a court. <!-- fact_13 -->

The full list sits on the [Legal Aid NSW binding financial agreements page](https://www.legalaid.nsw.gov.au/my-problem-is-about/my-family-or-relationship/finance-and-property/property-settlement-agreements/binding-financial-agreements).

### In writing and signed by both partners

A financial agreement is a written document, and it does not need court approval. <!-- fact_17 --> For it to be binding, you both sign it. <!-- fact_13 --> <!-- fact_17 -->

### Independent legal advice for each partner, with a signed statement

The Court requires each of you to receive independent legal advice from an Australian legal practitioner before you enter into the agreement. <!-- fact_07 --> Victoria Legal Aid adds that both parties must have their own lawyers, and that the lawyer must give a signed statement confirming the advice was provided. <!-- fact_17 -->

Moneysmart says that for the agreement to be binding, you both have to sign it and have sought legal and financial advice before signing. <!-- fact_23 -->

### Honest financial disclosure

Legal Aid NSW lists fraud, such as hiding important financial information, among the grounds on which a court can set an agreement aside. <!-- fact_15 -->

### No unfair or unconscionable conduct

Legal Aid NSW lists unconscionable conduct as a ground for setting an agreement aside. <!-- fact_15 --> Victoria Legal Aid's version is that one person acted in an unfair way. <!-- fact_19 -->

### Why some prenups are set aside

The Court has power to set aside financial agreements in some circumstances, set out in section 90K for marriages and section 90UM for de facto relationships. <!-- fact_08 --> Legal Aid NSW notes it can do so only in limited circumstances. <!-- fact_14 -->

Legal Aid NSW lists grounds that include: fraud (such as hiding important financial information); an agreement made to defeat a creditor; one made to cheat a de facto partner of one of the parties; the agreement being void or unenforceable; it no longer being practical to carry out all or part of it; a material change of circumstances affecting a child that causes hardship; unconscionable conduct; a payment flag on a party's superannuation; and dealing with a superannuation interest that cannot be split under the law. <!-- fact_15 -->

[Victoria Legal Aid](https://www.legalaid.vic.gov.au/agreement-about-dividing-property) says financial agreements are legally binding unless it can be proved that: there was fraud; the agreement is not practical; it did not follow the formal requirements under the Family Law Act; there has been a major and unexpected change affecting a child; one person acted in an unfair way; one person did not follow the agreement; or property covered was taken by the government because of criminal activity. <!-- fact_19 -->

### One partner has property or super to protect

[Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/family-and-relationships/relationships-and-money) says that if you have assets to protect, such as property or super, you can ask your partner to sign a binding financial agreement. <!-- fact_22 --> It sets out how assets and money are divided if the relationship breaks down, and what financial support each of you gets. <!-- fact_22 -->

### Expected inheritances

Legal Aid NSW says an agreement can protect an inheritance a partner may receive in future. <!-- fact_21 -->

### Blended families and children from a previous relationship

Legal Aid NSW adds that an agreement can protect property for children from a previous relationship. <!-- fact_21 -->

### Couples who want certainty

Legal Aid NSW also says an agreement can reduce the risk of future disputes and deal with property and maintenance in one document. <!-- fact_21 --> It weighs that against the other side: once you make one, you usually cannot go to court later to divide property. <!-- fact_14 -->

### Couples living or marrying across countries

If either of you has signed, or is thinking of signing, an agreement outside Australia, read the overseas section below. Legal Aid NSW says an agreement made overseas is not automatically recognised in Australia and must meet the formal requirements to be legally valid here. <!-- fact_16 -->

## What a prenup can and cannot cover

An agreement can cover the field, meaning all aspects of your financial relationship, or deal with only some issues, such as spousal maintenance. <!-- fact_03 --> Victoria Legal Aid names how property is divided and ongoing spousal maintenance. <!-- fact_18 --> Legal Aid NSW adds that it can also deal with child maintenance. <!-- fact_11 -->

What it cannot do is just as useful to know. Legal Aid NSW says an agreement won't stop a court making property settlement orders in a case involving a bankruptcy trustee, and cannot be used to hide or protect property from bankruptcy. <!-- fact_25 --> An agreement also cannot deal with issues already covered by an earlier agreement that is still in force. <!-- fact_13 -->

If one of you dies, Legal Aid NSW says the agreement stays in force unless it specifically says otherwise. <!-- fact_14 --> The Act gives this its own section, 90H, "Effect of death of party to financial agreement". <!-- fact_24 --> Ask your lawyer how your agreement sits with the rest of your affairs.

The agreement is also not the whole of your financial picture. Moneysmart frames it around how assets and money are divided if the relationship breaks down, and what financial support each of you gets. <!-- fact_22 --> Anything beyond separation, such as how you share accounts day to day, is a conversation for your own advisers.

## How to get a prenup and when to sign before the wedding

There is no single form to lodge. You don't go to court to make an agreement. <!-- fact_12 --> The work happens between you and two separate lawyers, and how much time your agreement needs is a question for them.

### Step 1: Talk about it as a couple

Have the conversation as a couple first, then each speak with a lawyer. How much lead time your agreement needs is a question for your lawyers, so raise it at the first meeting.

Legal Aid NSW sets out what to understand before you make one. You usually cannot go to court later to divide property, the agreement stays in force even if one of you dies unless it says otherwise, and a court can set it aside only in limited circumstances. <!-- fact_14 --> Those three points shape the conversation worth having before either lawyer drafts a word.

### Step 2: Each partner chooses their own family lawyer

Both parties must have their own lawyers. <!-- fact_17 --> Each of you must be advised by an Australian legal practitioner before entering into the agreement. <!-- fact_07 -->

### Step 3: Be open about your finances

Legal Aid NSW lists fraud, such as hiding important financial information, as a ground for setting an agreement aside. <!-- fact_15 --> Moneysmart says you both have to have sought legal and financial advice before signing. <!-- fact_23 -->

### Step 4: Draft and review with your lawyers

The agreement must say which part of the Family Law Act it is made under and deal with property, financial resources, spousal maintenance or related matters. <!-- fact_13 --> Your lawyers advise on the agreement itself; the advice must come before you enter into it. <!-- fact_07 -->

### Step 5: Sign and receive your lawyer's signed statement

You both sign the agreement, and each lawyer gives a signed statement confirming the advice was provided. <!-- fact_17 --> From there it is legally enforceable without any court step. <!-- fact_12 -->

## Prenups for destination weddings and international couples

Couples marrying abroad often ask whether the wedding's location changes anything. As at November 2026, Legal Aid NSW says a financial agreement made overseas won't be automatically recognised in Australia, even if the agreement says otherwise. <!-- fact_16 --> It must meet the Australian formal requirements to be legally valid here. <!-- fact_16 --> If it is not valid, it cannot be enforced in Australia, but you may still be able to apply for property settlement orders. <!-- fact_16 -->

If you plan to sign in Australia and then marry overseas, ask your lawyer how the overseas ceremony affects your agreement. The same goes for assets or family in another country.

## Prenup after marriage: is it too late?

No. For a married couple, an agreement can be made during the marriage (section 90C) or after it (section 90D). <!-- fact_04 --> Legal Aid NSW describes three types: before a marriage or de facto relationship begins, during it, or after separation or divorce. <!-- fact_12 --> The same requirements apply, including independent legal advice for each of you before signing. <!-- fact_07 -->

An agreement you already have can also be revisited. The Act deals with ending one in section 90J, "Termination of financial agreement". <!-- fact_24 --> Victoria Legal Aid says both of you must get legal advice again and sign a new written agreement to change or end it. <!-- fact_20 --> If your circumstances have shifted since you signed, a new house, a child, a move overseas, raise it with your lawyers rather than assuming the old document still fits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are prenups legally binding in Australia?

Yes, if the agreement meets the technical requirements of section 90G of the Family Law Act, according to the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia. <!-- fact_06 --> Once made it is legally enforceable, and a court can set it aside only in limited circumstances. <!-- fact_12 --> <!-- fact_14 -->

### What is a prenup called in Australia?

A binding financial agreement, or financial agreement, made under Part VIIIA of the Family Law Act 1975 for married couples. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_22 -->

### How long does a prenup last in Australia?

Until it is ended or set aside. Victoria Legal Aid says that to change or end an agreement, both parties must get legal advice again and sign a new written agreement, called a termination agreement. <!-- fact_20 --> Legal Aid NSW says it can be changed or ended by a new agreement with a terminating clause, a termination agreement, or abandonment. <!-- fact_20 -->

### Can you do your own prenup without a lawyer?

No. The Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia says you cannot enter into a financial agreement without each first getting independent legal advice from an Australian lawyer. <!-- fact_10 -->

### What happens to a prenup if one partner dies?

Legal Aid NSW says the agreement stays in force even if one party dies, unless it specifically says otherwise. <!-- fact_14 -->

### Can we sign a prenup after the wedding?

Yes. An agreement can be made during the marriage or after it. <!-- fact_04 -->
