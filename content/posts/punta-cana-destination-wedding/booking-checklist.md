# Booking checklist: Punta Cana destination wedding

A working sheet for couples planning a Punta Cana wedding. These checks sit with the couple, the Dominican authorities and the venue, so they don't block publishing. The article has no `[[VERIFY]]` markers in the copy and no prices. The publishing blockers are the 4 image slots, the Dominican Republic category check and the URL check after saving (see `12-cms-report.md`).

All items are as at November 2026. Reconfirm each one for your own date before relying on it. The registry booking, your documents and their timing are your responsibility as a couple; take your own legal advice.

## Part 1: Dominican civil registry checklist (legal wedding only)

*General information as at November 2026, not legal advice.*

Sources: the Junta Central Electoral (JCE) help centre, [civil marriage requirements](https://ayuda.jce.gob.do/kb/a27/cuales-son-los-requisitos-para-un-matrimonio-civil.aspx) (read 24 November 2026; page modified 1 July 2025), and the Dominican Consulate General in the Netherlands, [what a foreigner needs to marry in the Dominican Republic](https://nld.mirex.gob.do/consular/amsterdam/que-necesita-un-extranjero-para-contraer-matrimonio-en-la-republica-dominicana/) (read 24 November 2026).

| Step | What the sources say | Done |
|---|---|---|
| Choose a registry office | Choose an Oficialía del Estado Civil (usually one per municipality), request its requirements and book a date for the civil judge (consulate). | [ ] |
| Documents, each of you | Birth certificate, passport and a certificate of single status issued in your country of origin (JCE). The consulate's list also names a recent divorce certificate if divorced, a passport photocopy and an apostilled sworn declaration of single status. | [ ] |
| Apostille | Every document must be legalised and apostilled in its country of origin (JCE). Order documents early and allow time for each step. | [ ] |
| Translation | Any document not in Spanish must be translated by a judicial interpreter and legalised by the Dominican Attorney General's Office (Procuraduría General de la República). In some cases the Dominican consulate can translate it, and it is then legalised at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (JCE). | [ ] |
| Two adult witnesses | At least two witnesses, relatives or not, who are adults, can sign and, if foreigners, carry a valid passport (JCE). Ask two guests to bring their passports to the ceremony. | [ ] |
| Separation-of-property deed (if wanted) | If you want to keep assets separate, an authentic deed must be executed before the wedding, registered, notified and given to the civil registry officer (JCE). Without one, the marriage is declared under community of property (consulate). Take your own legal advice before you book. | [ ] |
| Lodge documents when the date is booked | Documents are lodged at the civil registry office when you book the wedding date (JCE). | [ ] |
| The judge travels to the venue | By prior arrangement, the civil judge can travel to a place you nominate, which is why many marriages are held in hotels (consulate). Confirm the arrangement in writing with the registry office and tell the venue. | [ ] |
| Attend in person | Both of you must attend the ceremony in person (consulate). | [ ] |
| Fee | The exact price is given by the registry office you choose (consulate). Ask it directly. | [ ] |
| After the wedding | The registry issues a marriage certificate; for use abroad it is recommended that it be apostilled at the Dominican Ministry of Foreign Affairs (consulate). | [ ] |

## Part 2: open questions to check with the Dominican authorities

*General information as at November 2026, not legal advice.*

None of the sources read answers these. Ask the Dominican Republic's embassy or consulate accredited to Australia and the Oficialía you choose, in writing, before you order documents or book flights.

- [ ] **Single-status certificate from Australia.** Which Australian document serves as the certificate of single status: a Single Status or No Record Result certificate from your state registry of births, deaths and marriages, or a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage from DFAT (both described by [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas), page last updated 18 August 2026)? Does it need an apostille, and how recent must it be? Do any diplomatic missions in the Dominican Republic take single-status declarations from Australians (the JCE says some missions do for their own citizens)?
- [ ] **Notice period.** Is there a notice (edict) period for Australians? The JCE page only states one for Italian citizens (posted at least three days before the wedding).
- [ ] **Days in country.** How many days must you be in the Dominican Republic before the wedding, if any?
- [ ] **Witnesses or padrinos.** The JCE asks for at least two witnesses; the consulate's list names two witnesses and padrinos (minimum one). Which applies at your Oficialía, and can the same people act as both?
- [ ] **Name change, with your state registry in Australia.** Will your state registry accept the Dominican marriage certificate for a name change, and does it need translating for use in Australia? The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) notes some states and territories don't accept overseas marriage certificates for a name change.
- [ ] **Validity in Australia.** Smartraveller suggests talking to a lawyer to confirm your marriage certificate is valid in Australia. Overseas marriages cannot be registered in Australia (Attorney-General's Department).

Marrying legally at home first and holding a symbolic ceremony in Punta Cana avoids the Dominican civil process entirely. Decide which path you are taking before you hold a date.

## Part 3: questions for every venue (in writing)

- [ ] **Sole use.** How many weddings does the property hold on your date, and do any share your ceremony site, reception space or photo spots? What does sole use of each space cover, for how many hours, and can it extend to the whole property across your celebration?
- [ ] **Beach rules.** What is allowed on the sand: ceremony location, set-up and pack-down times, music, flowers and how close guests can sit to the water? Can other hotel guests use that stretch of beach during your ceremony?
- [ ] **Photos and posting.** What is the venue's policy on guests and staff photographing or posting your wedding?
- [ ] **Heat and shade.** What time does the sun leave your ceremony site, and what shade sits over the guests' seats?
- [ ] **Access for older guests.** How do older guests reach the ceremony on sand, how far is it from their rooms, and where can they sit out of the heat between events? Do paths, ramps and seating suit a guest using a walking frame or wheelchair?
- [ ] **Wet weather space.** Ask to see the covered option, not just hear about it. Does it hold your full guest count with seating? When is the call to move inside made, and who makes it? Get it confirmed in writing, especially for a date between 1 June and 30 November (the Atlantic hurricane season, [US National Hurricane Center](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/)).
- [ ] **The judge (legal wedding only).** When does the civil judge arrive, how long does the civil part take, and where does it sit in the day's programme?
- [ ] **Contracts.** What language and currency is the contract written in?
- [ ] **Deposits and refunds.** How are deposits paid, and what is refunded if plans change, including for a storm or hurricane?
- [ ] **The venue contact.** Who is the venue's contact on the day, and how do you reach them out of hours?
- [ ] Anything the venue doesn't publish (inclusions, set-up times, minimum nights): your Wedded Advisor confirms inclusions in writing before you commit.

## Part 4: guest travel checklist (Smartraveller)

From [Smartraveller's Dominican Republic advice](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/americas/dominican-republic), read 24 November 2026 (page "Updated: 25 February 2026", "Still current at: 25 November 2026"). Send this with the save the date, and ask guests to recheck Smartraveller close to travel, because entry and exit conditions can change at short notice.

- [ ] **Advice level.** Exercise a high degree of caution in the Dominican Republic due to the threat of violent crime.
- [ ] **Tourist card.** Every guest must buy a tourist card on arrival (valid for 30 days).
- [ ] **Entry and exit form.** To enter and to exit, complete the electronic entry and exit form (linked from Smartraveller at eticket.migracion.gob.do).
- [ ] **Latest entry details and passport validity.** Contact the nearest Dominican Republic embassy or consulate for the latest entry details, and ask about passport validity (Smartraveller gives only general guidance on this).
- [ ] **Children.** Exit requirements apply to children under 18 travelling alone, with one parent or with another adult; families ask the embassy or consulate before travel.
- [ ] **Photo ID.** Always carry photo identification, such as a certified copy of your passport.
- [ ] **Transfers.** Use official airport taxis, ride share, pre-booked taxis or hotel transport, not unofficial taxis, and travel to the airport in daylight hours.
- [ ] **Money.** Australian dollars can't be exchanged for Dominican pesos; bring US dollars and exchange them at banks or official exchange offices.
- [ ] **Insurance.** Comprehensive travel insurance covering all medical costs overseas, including evacuation; the Australian Government won't pay these costs. Read the policy for storm and hurricane cover.
- [ ] **Storms.** Tropical storms and hurricanes can happen outside the official season. If one hits, travellers may get stuck, flights could be delayed or cancelled, and flights out may fill quickly.
- [ ] **Earthquakes and tsunamis.** Near the coast, move to high ground immediately if advised or on natural warning signs, without waiting for official alerts.
- [ ] **The water.** Many beaches have strong rips, and some hotels, resorts and public beaches have no lifeguards.
- [ ] **Mosquitoes.** Dengue and malaria are common in the wet season (May to November) and Zika is widespread; pregnant guests talk to their doctor before travelling.
- [ ] **Consular help.** The Embassy of Canada in Santo Domingo provides consular help for Australians; consular help is also available from the Australian Embassy in Mexico.
