A Punta Cana destination wedding can be legally binding if you follow the Dominican civil process, or you can marry legally in Australia first and hold a symbolic ceremony on the sand. Either way, the hurricane season, the drier months and your guests' long journey from Australia should shape the date before anything else does.

Punta Cana tends to arrive on the shortlist through a photograph: white chairs, pale sand, water the colour of glass. This guide covers what sits behind that picture, from the paperwork to the questions that keep your day private. ## Why couples choose Punta Cana for a destination wedding

Start with where your guests will stay. When the ceremony, reception and guest rooms sit within one property, that choice shapes everything from the guest experience to how private your day feels.

The appeal is ease. When guests stay where you marry, the celebration can unfold over a long weekend rather than a single evening.

### Resort, private estate or beach club

A large resort gives you rooms, kitchens, staff and back-up spaces under one roof. A private estate or villa trades some of that convenience for sole use of the setting. A beach club sits between the two. The right choice depends on how many guests you are bringing and how much of the property you want to yourselves.

### Who Punta Cana suits, and who it does not

It suits couples who want warm water, an unhurried programme over several days and guests who are happy to stay in one place. It asks more of couples with older relatives or young families travelling from Australia, because the journey is long and the heat is real.

## Getting legally married in the Dominican Republic or holding a symbolic ceremony

*General information as at November 2026, not legal advice.*

You have two paths. Marry legally in the Dominican Republic through its civil registry, or marry legally in Australia and hold a symbolic ceremony in Punta Cana. As [a Dominican consulate's guidance for foreigners](https://nld.mirex.gob.do/consular/amsterdam/que-necesita-un-extranjero-para-contraer-matrimonio-en-la-republica-dominicana/) explains, Dominican civil registry offices assist any citizen of the world under Dominican law, including marriages between two foreigners.<!-- fact_024 --> For how other countries compare, see [where you can legally marry abroad](/destination-weddings/weddings-abroad-where-can-you-legally-get-married).

### Documents the civil registry asks foreigners for

The Junta Central Electoral, which runs the civil registry, lists [the civil marriage requirements](https://ayuda.jce.gob.do/kb/a27/cuales-son-los-requisitos-para-un-matrimonio-civil.aspx). Foreigners provide a birth certificate, a passport and a certificate of single status issued in their country of origin.<!-- fact_016 --> Documents are lodged at the civil registry office when you book the wedding date.<!-- fact_021 -->

As [Smartraveller's guide to getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) explains, your state registry of births, deaths and marriages in Australia can issue a Single Status or No Record Result certificate, and DFAT can issue a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage.<!-- fact_005 --><!-- fact_006 --> Which of these the Dominican registry accepts is a question to check with the Dominican authorities before you order anything.

If you plan to keep your assets separate, a separation of property deed must be executed before the wedding and given to the civil registry officer.<!-- fact_022 --> Without one, the marriage is declared under community of property.<!-- fact_028 --> Take your own legal advice on this before you book.

### Apostille, translation and legalisation

All documents a foreigner presents must be legalised and apostilled in their country of origin.<!-- fact_017 --> Any document not in Spanish must be translated by a judicial interpreter and legalised by the Dominican Attorney General's Office; in some cases the translation can be done at a Dominican consulate and legalised at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.<!-- fact_018 -->

Allow time for each step: the apostille, the translation and its legalisation. Order documents early, and confirm the exact chain with the registry office you choose.

### The civil judge, witnesses and the day itself

The same consulate guidance sets out the steps: choose a civil registry office, request its requirements and book a date for the civil judge to perform the marriage.<!-- fact_024 --> By prior arrangement, the civil judge can travel to a place the couple nominate, so many marriages can be held in hotels.<!-- fact_025 --> Both of you must attend in person.<!-- fact_026 -->

The registry asks for at least two witnesses, relatives or not, who are adults, can sign and carry a valid passport if they are foreigners.<!-- fact_020 --> The consulate's list also names padrinos, a minimum of one, alongside two witnesses.<!-- fact_026 --> Ask the registry which applies, and ask two guests to bring their passports to the ceremony.

### After the wedding and recognition in Australia

The registry issues a marriage certificate, and for use abroad it is recommended that it be apostilled at the Dominican Ministry of Foreign Affairs.<!-- fact_027 --> The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) is clear that overseas marriages cannot be registered in Australia, or performed by an Australian-authorised marriage celebrant.<!-- fact_007 -->

An overseas marriage is generally recognised in Australia if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid here. Some states and territories don't accept an overseas certificate for a name change.<!-- fact_007 --> Smartraveller suggests talking to a lawyer to confirm your certificate is valid in Australia.<!-- fact_001 -->

### Legal at home, symbolic in Punta Cana

You can sign the legal papers at home with a celebrant, then hold a ceremony in Punta Cana that carries all the meaning and none of the paperwork. You write the vows, choose who speaks and set the hour by the light, not by the judge's diary. Read [what a symbolic ceremony is and how to marry legally at home](/expert-advice/symbolic-ceremony) before you decide.

**Check with the Dominican authorities before you book:**

- Which Australian document serves as the certificate of single status, and whether it needs an apostille.
- Any notice period, and how many days you must be in the country before the wedding.
- Whether witnesses, padrinos or both are needed.
- With your state registry: whether your Dominican certificate needs translating for use in Australia, and whether it will be accepted for a name change.

## When to plan a Punta Cana wedding

The World Meteorological Organization's [climate normals for Punta Cana](https://worldweather.wmo.int/en/city.html?cityId=596), supplied by the Dominican national weather service ONAMET, give a clear picture of the year.<!-- fact_101 --> They are averages for the 30 years from 1961 to 1990, and a rain day is a day with at least 1 mm of rain.<!-- fact_102 -->

### Drier months and heat

February and March are the driest months, each averaging 54.2 mm of rain over fewer than seven rain days.<!-- fact_116 --> October is the wettest, at 152.1 mm over 11.4 rain days.<!-- fact_112 -->

Mean daily highs move within a narrow band, from 27.6°C in February to 30.9°C in September.<!-- fact_115 --> There is no cool season to wait for, so ceremony time and shade matter in every month.

### Hurricane season and how to plan around it

The [US National Hurricane Center](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/) gives the Atlantic hurricane season, whose basin includes the Caribbean Sea, as 1 June to 30 November.<!-- fact_117 --> As at November 2026, [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/americas/dominican-republic) notes that tropical storms and hurricanes can happen in other months, and that the wet season runs from May to November.<!-- fact_124 -->

A date in those months is possible, but it needs a covered ceremony space confirmed in writing and travel insurance your guests have read closely. If a hurricane or severe storm hits, travellers may get stuck, flights could be delayed or cancelled, and flights out may fill quickly.<!-- fact_124 -->

## Getting your guests to Punta Cana from Australia

For guests in Sydney, Melbourne or Perth, this is a long journey, and the earlier they know, the more choice they have. Our [destination wedding timeline from 18 months out](/destination-weddings/destination-wedding-timeline) sets out when to send save the dates and travel details.

### Entry, tourist card and travel advice

As at November 2026, [Smartraveller's advice for the Dominican Republic](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/americas/dominican-republic) is to exercise a high degree of caution, due to the threat of violent crime.<!-- fact_120 --> It says every guest must buy a tourist card on arrival and complete the electronic entry and exit form.<!-- fact_121 --><!-- fact_122 -->

Entry and exit conditions can change at short notice, so guests should contact the nearest Dominican embassy or consulate for the latest details.<!-- fact_121 --> Ask about passport validity at the same time. Exit requirements apply to children under 18 travelling alone, with one parent or with another adult, so families should ask about them before travel.<!-- fact_123 --> Smartraveller advises carrying photo identification, such as a certified copy of your passport.<!-- fact_011 -->

Australia has no embassy in the Dominican Republic. The Embassy of Canada in Santo Domingo provides consular help for Australians, and consular help is also available from the Australian Embassy in Mexico.<!-- fact_130 -->

### Safety, money and health

- **Transfers.** Use official airport taxis, ride share, pre-booked taxis or hotel transport rather than unofficial taxis, and travel to the airport in daylight hours.<!-- fact_128 -->
- **Money.** Australian dollars can't be exchanged for Dominican pesos, so bring US dollars.<!-- fact_129 -->
- **Insurance.** Choose comprehensive cover for all medical costs overseas, including evacuation, because the Australian Government won't pay these costs.<!-- fact_131 -->
- **The water.** Many beaches have strong rips, and some hotels and resorts have no lifeguards.<!-- fact_127 -->
- **Mosquitoes.** Mosquito-borne illnesses, including dengue and malaria, are common in the wet season from May to November, and Zika is widespread, so pregnant guests should talk to their doctor before they travel.<!-- fact_126 -->

Put all of this in a guest travel note that goes out with the save the date, not the invitation. ## Planning your Punta Cana destination wedding

Whichever venue you choose, the difference between a private celebration and a busy one comes down to what you ask before you sign. These are the questions to put to any venue, in writing.

### Other weddings that day and sole use

Ask how many weddings the property holds on your date, and whether any share your ceremony site, reception space or photo spots. If you want the setting to yourselves, ask what sole use of each space covers, and for how many hours.

### Beach ceremony rules

Ask what the venue allows on the sand: the ceremony location, set-up and pack-down times, music, flowers and how close guests can sit to the water. Ask whether other hotel guests can use that stretch of beach during your ceremony.

### Heat, shade and older guests

Ask what time the sun leaves your ceremony site and what shade sits over the guests' seats. Ask how older guests reach the ceremony on sand, how far it is from their rooms and where they can sit out of the heat between events.

### A covered wet weather option

Ask to see the covered option, not just hear about it. Confirm it holds your full guest count with seating, when the call to move inside is made and who makes it.

### The judge and the registry

If you are marrying legally in the Dominican Republic, ask when the civil judge arrives, how long the civil part takes and where it sits in the day's programme. The registry booking, documents and timing remain your responsibility, so build them into your plan from the start.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are you legally married if you get married in the Dominican Republic?

Yes, if the marriage is performed through a Dominican civil registry office under Dominican law, which assists marriages between two foreigners.<!-- fact_024 --> Australia generally recognises an overseas marriage that was valid where it took place and would have been valid here, though it cannot be registered in Australia.<!-- fact_007 --> As at November 2026; this is general information, not legal advice.

### Can we have a symbolic ceremony in Punta Cana and marry legally at home?

Yes. You marry legally in Australia with a celebrant, then hold a symbolic ceremony in Punta Cana without the Dominican civil process. It gives you full control of the ceremony's timing and wording.

### When should we hold a wedding in Punta Cana?

On the WMO's 1961 to 1990 climate normals for Punta Cana, supplied by ONAMET, February and March are the driest months.<!-- fact_116 --> October is the wettest month.<!-- fact_112 --> Weigh the weather against your guests' school holidays and leave, then hold your date.

### When is hurricane season in Punta Cana?

The US National Hurricane Center gives the Atlantic hurricane season as 1 June to 30 November.<!-- fact_117 --> As at November 2026, Smartraveller notes that tropical storms and hurricanes can happen in other months, so a covered option and good travel insurance matter in any month.<!-- fact_124 -->

### What do Australian guests need to enter the Dominican Republic?

As at November 2026, guests buy a tourist card on arrival and complete the electronic entry and exit form.<!-- fact_121 --><!-- fact_122 --> Rules can change at short notice, so they should check with the nearest Dominican embassy or consulate before booking.<!-- fact_121 -->

### Do Punta Cana resorts offer wedding packages?

Resort wedding offers differ in what they include, from ceremony set-up to the number of events and guests covered. Compare them line by line, ask what happens to the space if another wedding is booked that day, and get every inclusion in writing.
