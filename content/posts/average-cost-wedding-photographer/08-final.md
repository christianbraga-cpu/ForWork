Every couple who looks up the average cost wedding photographer quotes come to is asking a fair question: what should we expect to spend? We have chosen not to publish a figure, because a single average says very little about the quote you will receive for a wedding like yours.

That number blends a short registry signing with a celebration over several days abroad, one photographer with a full team, a digital gallery with a hand-bound album. What follows is what actually shapes a quote, what a package should spell out in writing, what tends to sit outside it, and where you stand before you sign. If you read one section, make it the quote comparison near the end.

## Key takeaways

- Coverage, team, travel, albums and image rights shape a photographer's quote far more than any national average, so ask every photographer to quote against the same written brief.
- Compare quotes line by line on what is written down: hours, people, deliverables, delivery timing, licence and extras.
- In Australia, a couple who commissions and pays for wedding photos owns the copyright by default under [section 35(5)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s35.html) of the Copyright Act 1968, but the agreement can exclude or modify that rule. <!-- fact_102 --> <!-- fact_104 --> <!-- fact_101 -->
- Under the [consumer guarantees](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees), a photographer must use an acceptable level of care and skill, and those basic rights can't be taken away by anything the business says or does. <!-- fact_200 --> <!-- fact_203 -->

## Why there is no single average cost for a wedding photographer

In our view, the question behind an average is really what you will get for what you spend, and an average can't answer it. It doesn't know who will photograph your day, which moments they will cover, what you will hold at the end or when it will arrive. Start with the day you are planning, write it down, and ask each photographer to quote against that same brief.

## What shapes a wedding photographer's quote

<!-- IMAGE SLOT cost-factors: Real, licensed candid of two photographers covering different angles of a ceremony or reception (lead and second shooter), guests and couple in frame, evening or reception light to suggest a long coverage day. Consenting couple, guests and photographers. No logos, no price lists or numbers. Never AI. -->

A photographer's quote is a sum of decisions, most of them yours. Change one and the figure moves.

### Hours of coverage and the shape of your day

Coverage is the time a photographer spends with you. Two quotes that look alike can hide very different hours, so read this line first.

Before you ask for prices, sketch the day: getting ready, the ceremony, portraits, the reception, the moment you want the camera to stop. Then [plan your wedding day timeline](/expert-advice/wedding-day-timeline) and share it, so each photographer quotes against the same hours. Ask how overtime is charged if the evening runs late.

### One photographer or a team

A second shooter covers what one person physically can't: both sets of preparations, guests' faces during the vows, the room while you are outside for portraits.

Ask whether a second photographer is included, optional or not offered, and for how much of the day. If an assistant is listed, ask whether they shoot or only carry equipment. For a celebration across several events, ask how many photographers cover each one and who leads the team.

### Travel and accommodation

Ask whether travel within the photographer's home area is included. Beyond it, flights, accommodation, car hire and meals can appear as a separate line or be folded into the package. Ask which, and whether the figure is fixed or charged at cost afterwards.

### An engagement shoot before the day

An engagement or pre-wedding session gives you time in front of the camera before the day itself. Ask whether it is part of the package, an add-on or not offered, and where it can take place.

### Albums and prints

Albums vary from printed books to hand-bound linen or leather heirlooms, and the choice of paper, size and page count changes the price. Ask whether an album is included, who designs it, how many rounds of changes you get and when it will arrive.

### Editing and delivery timing

Every delivered image has been selected and edited. Ask how many edited images you can expect, whether retouching of individual portraits is included, and the date by which the full gallery will be delivered. Get that date in writing.

### Licence and usage of the images

Who owns the photos, and what each party may do with them, belongs in the contract. If you want to share images online, print them at home or give them to a magazine, say so at booking. If you would rather the photographer didn't use any image in a portfolio, on social media or in the press, ask for that limit in writing too. The rights section below sets out the legal default.

### Season, day of week and demand for your date

Popular dates are held by whoever signs first. Ask whether pricing differs between peak and quieter months, or between a Saturday and a weekday, and whether a date hold requires a deposit.

### Experience and style

Portfolio, years behind the camera and editorial style all shape the fee. Ask to see full galleries from weddings like yours, not only a highlights reel.

## What wedding photographer packages include

Ignore the package name and read the contents. A well-written package states each of these in plain words:

- **Coverage:** the start and end time, or the number of hours, and the events covered.
- **People:** the lead photographer by name, and whether a second shooter or assistant is included.
- **Pre-wedding session:** included or not, and where it can be held.
- **Online gallery:** how images are delivered, how long the gallery stays online, and whether guests can view or download.
- **Edited images:** the expected number, the editing style and whether retouching is included.
- **Delivery timing:** the date for a preview set and the date for the full gallery.
- **Album and prints:** the product, its specifications and the number of design revisions.
- **Usage and copyright:** who owns the images and what each of you may do with them.
- **Backup:** what happens if the photographer is ill or can't travel on the day.

If your venue offers a package that bundles photography, the same list applies. ## Extras that sit outside the headline package

The package price is the start of the conversation, not the end. These are the lines that can arrive as extras, so ask about each before you compare totals:

- Overtime beyond the agreed hours.
- Additional events, such as welcome drinks, a rehearsal dinner or a morning-after shoot.
- Travel, accommodation and meals outside the photographer's home area.
- Location or permit fees for photographing in public places, gardens or heritage sites.
- Album upgrades, parent albums, extra prints and framing.
- Rush editing for a faster gallery.
- Raw, unedited files, where a photographer releases them at all.

## Photographing a destination wedding

<!-- IMAGE SLOT destination-coverage: Real, licensed portrait of a couple at a named destination (Italian countryside, coastal Greece or Bali clifftop), landscape dominant, golden hour, relaxed editorial pose. A wedding abroad changes the quote in ways a local one doesn't. Whether you fly a photographer with you or book one who lives near the venue, settle these questions in writing:

- **Travel:** who books and pays for flights, transfers and car hire, and in which currency the quote is written.
- **Accommodation:** how many nights, where, and whether it is booked by you or by the photographer.
- **Multi-day coverage:** which events are covered across the weekend, and how each day is priced.
- **Venue rules:** ask the venue whether it limits where photographers can stand during the ceremony, whether flash is allowed in a church or chapel, and whether drones are permitted.
- **Location rules:** ask whether a permit is needed to photograph on a public beach, in a piazza or at a heritage site, and who arranges it.
- **Backup:** who replaces the photographer if a flight is cancelled or they fall ill, and whether that person is named in the contract.

When an overseas business sells products directly to consumers in Australia, it must follow the [Australian Consumer Law](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees), including the consumer guarantees. <!-- fact_206 --> In practice, it can be difficult to get a repair, replacement or refund from an overseas business if there's a problem with the product or service, and you should ask the consumer protection agency where the seller is based whether it can help. <!-- fact_206 -->

## Your rights and the contract

This is general information about Australian law as at November 2026, not legal advice. The detail of your own agreement matters, so get advice on your own contract before you sign anything you are unsure of.

### Consumer guarantees for services

Under the [consumer guarantees](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees), service providers must carry out all services using an acceptable level of care and skill. <!-- fact_200 --> Services, and any resulting products, must be [fit for any stated purpose](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees) when you tell the provider what you want the service for, <!-- fact_201 --> so say it plainly at booking: an album for your parents, prints for the hallway, a feature in a magazine. These [basic rights can't be taken away](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/consumer-rights-and-guarantees) by anything a business says or does. <!-- fact_203 -->

When a service has a [major problem](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel), you can choose to cancel the contract and get a refund, which may not be a full refund because you pay a reasonable amount for any work done so far and as expected, or keep the contract and pay a lower price that takes the problem into account. <!-- fact_204 --> If you got what you asked for but [simply changed your mind](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel), you are not entitled to a repair, replacement or refund under the consumer guarantees, though a business with a change of mind policy must follow it. <!-- fact_205 -->

### Who owns the copyright in your wedding photos

The general rule in [section 35(2)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s35.html) of the [Copyright Act 1968](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1968A00063/latest/text) is that, subject to the rest of the section, the author of an artistic work owns the copyright in it. <!-- fact_100 --> Commissioned photographs for a private or domestic purpose are treated differently. <!-- fact_102 --> Under [section 35(5)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s35.html), where:

> "a person makes, for valuable consideration, an agreement with another person for the taking of a photograph for a private or domestic purpose" and "the work is made in pursuance of the agreement", "the first-mentioned person is the owner of any copyright subsisting in the work by virtue of this Part". <!-- fact_102 -->

The same subsection carries a proviso:

> "but, if at the time the agreement was made that person made known, expressly or by implication, to the author of the work the purpose for which the work was required, the author is entitled to restrain the doing, otherwise than for that purpose, of any act comprised in the copyright in the work." <!-- fact_103 -->

And [section 35(7)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s35.html) is specific about weddings:

> "private or domestic purpose" includes a portrait of family members, a wedding party or children. <!-- fact_104 -->

The default can be changed. Under [section 35(3)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s35.html), the operation of the commissioned-photograph rule "may be excluded or modified by agreement". <!-- fact_101 --> A contract can do exactly that, so read the usage and copyright clause before you sign, and ask for what you need (printing, sharing, an album, publication) in writing.

If you are engaging a photographer overseas or under a contract governed by another country's law, ask how ownership and usage are handled, and have the answer written into the agreement.

### Deposits, cancellations and getting it in writing

Ask for the deposit and cancellation terms in writing, and check they answer each of these questions:

- How much is the deposit, when is it due and in which currency is it paid?
- When are the remaining payments due?
- What happens to money already paid if you cancel?
- What happens if the photographer cancels?
- Can you postpone, and on what terms?
- What happens if travel is disrupted on a wedding abroad?

## Questions to ask before you book a wedding photographer

Take this list into every conversation, and ask for the answers in the written quote rather than in a phone call:

- Who exactly will photograph our wedding, and is their name in the contract?
- What are the start and end times of coverage, and how is overtime charged?
- Is a second shooter included, and for which parts of the day?
- How many edited images should we expect, and by what date?
- How is the gallery delivered, and for how long does it stay online?
- Is an album included, and what are its specifications?
- Who owns the copyright, and what may each of us do with the images?
- May you use any of our images in a portfolio, on social media or in the press without our written consent?
- What travel, accommodation and permit costs sit outside the package?
- How do the deposit, payment schedule and cancellation terms work?
- Who covers the day if you are ill or can't travel?
- Can we see several complete galleries from weddings like ours?

Fold the answers into your [wedding planning checklist](/expert-advice/wedding-planning-checklist) so photography sits alongside the venue, the celebrant and every other booking.

## How to compare quotes in writing

Two quotes only compare once they describe the same wedding. Send every photographer the same brief: date, venue, guest numbers, timeline, events and the products you want.

When the quotes come back, lay them side by side and work through them line by line:

1. **Match the hours.** Same start, same finish, same events.
2. **Match the people.** One photographer or two, and who they are.
3. **Match the deliverables.** Gallery, edited image count, album, prints.
4. **Match the timing.** Preview date, full gallery date, album date.
5. **Match the rights.** Ownership, licence and any limits on use.
6. **List the extras.** Travel, accommodation, permits, overtime, upgrades.
7. **Read the terms.** Deposit, payment schedule, cancellation and postponement.

Only then look at the totals. A lower headline price with fewer hours and a slower delivery date is a different product, not a saving. Decide which images matter most, and protect those hours first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why don't you give an average cost for a wedding photographer?

Because a national average blends very different weddings, and a figure built from other people's days can't tell you what yours will cost. Send several photographers the same written brief, then use the comparison steps above to read their quotes side by side.

### How much should I pay for a photographer?

We don't suggest a figure, because the right spend depends on the hours, people and products your day needs. Decide which moments matter, what coverage and team that takes and what you want to hold at the end, then compare quotes that match on those points.

### What do wedding photographer packages include?

A clear package states coverage hours, the photographers involved, any pre-wedding session, the online gallery, the expected number of edited images, delivery dates, album and print specifications, usage and copyright, and a backup plan. Anything not written down should be treated as not included.

### Who owns the copyright in our wedding photos?

As at November 2026, under [section 35(5)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s35.html) of the Copyright Act 1968, a person who pays for a photograph taken for a private or domestic purpose owns the copyright, and section 35(7) names a wedding party as a private or domestic purpose. <!-- fact_102 --> <!-- fact_104 --> The agreement can exclude or modify that rule, so read the usage and copyright clause before you sign. <!-- fact_101 -->

### How far ahead should we book a wedding photographer?

As soon as your date and venue are confirmed, because a photographer can only be in one place on any given day. For a wedding abroad, allow time to settle travel and accommodation in the contract too.
