The walk down the aisle is the shortest part of the ceremony and the one everyone remembers. The music sets its pace and mood.

These 40 wedding processional songs are grouped by style rather than ranked: classical, church and hymns, instrumental, and modern. Each comes with a practical note on how it plays on the aisle and who it suits, so you can hand a shortlist to a string quartet, a pianist or your celebrant.

## Key takeaways

- Choose the song for the length of your aisle and the pace of your walk, and favour pieces like Pachelbel's Canon in D <!-- fact_500 --> that musicians can stretch or shorten around your arrival.
- Separate songs for the wedding party and the couple, such as Handel's The Arrival of the Queen of Sheba <!-- fact_504 --> then Wagner's Bridal Chorus, <!-- fact_700 --> give the final entrance its own moment and a clear cue for guests to stand.
- Classical pieces and hymns suit live players and church settings, while modern songs such as Ed Sheeran's Perfect <!-- fact_100 --> work well as instrumental arrangements or recordings.
- For a destination ceremony, ask the venue and musicians early what a quote covers, who runs the sound and what the back-up is, and get the terms in writing.

## What are wedding processional songs?

The processional is the music played as the wedding party and the couple walk in at the start of the ceremony. Some couples use one piece for everyone; many use one for the wedding party and a second for their own entrance.

## How do you choose a song to walk down the aisle to?

Start with the setting. A chapel organ, a garden string trio and a clifftop guitarist each favour different music. Then think about pace: a slow piece suits a long aisle and a formal walk, while a brisker one keeps a large wedding party moving.

Instrumental versions carry well outdoors and leave space for the moment. Songs with vocals suit the couple's entrance if you are happy for guests to hear the words as they watch you.

### Who walks first, and does each group need its own song?

In many ceremonies, the officiant and one partner wait at the front, the wedding party walks in, and the couple, or the person being walked in, arrives last. Your officiant will confirm the order.

You don't need a separate song for every group. A common shape is one piece for parents, grandparents and the wedding party, then a change of music for the couple. The change cues guests to stand, so make the second piece sound clearly different.

### How long should a processional song be?

Long enough to cover the walk, which depends on your aisle and the number of people walking. Time it at the rehearsal. Live musicians can repeat a section while the wedding party walks and move to the ending as the couple reaches the front; with a recording, ask your celebrant or sound person to fade at a natural phrase rather than cutting mid-line.

### Live or recorded?

Live music follows the room: a quartet or pianist can slow down, repeat or finish on cue. Recorded music gives you the exact version you love, and needs a reliable sound system, someone to press play and a back-up copy.

Many couples use live players for the ceremony and recordings for the reception.

## Which classical pieces suit a wedding processional?

Classical pieces are easy to arrange for quartet, organ, harp or piano, and simple to time to the walk.

### 1. Canon in D, Johann Pachelbel

The Canon and Gigue in D major is by Johann Pachelbel. <!-- fact_500 --> The Canon's repeating bass line means musicians can stretch or shorten it without anyone noticing, which is why it handles a long or uneven procession so well.

*Suits:* the wedding party or the couple, in a garden or chapel.

### 2. Air (from Orchestral Suite No. 3), Johann Sebastian Bach

The Air comes from Bach's Suite No. 3 in D major, BWV 1068. <!-- fact_501 --> It is slow and unhurried and gives a formal entrance real calm.

*Suits:* the couple, for a slow, formal walk.

### 3. Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring, Johann Sebastian Bach

This chorale is part of Bach's Cantata BWV 147, Herz und Mund und Tat und Leben. <!-- fact_502 --> Its flowing triplet rhythm moves steadily without rushing, so a wedding party can walk naturally to it.

*Suits:* the wedding party, in a church or chapel.

### 4. Bridal Chorus (from Lohengrin), Richard Wagner

The Bridal Chorus comes from Wagner's opera Lohengrin, <!-- fact_700 --> first performed in 1850 at Weimar. <!-- fact_400 --> Guests know it from the first bar, a clear signal that the couple is arriving.

*Suits:* the couple, for a traditional entrance.

### 5. Prince of Denmark's March, Jeremiah Clarke

Also known as the Trumpet Voluntary, <!-- fact_701 --> this march is by Jeremiah Clarke, an English organist and composer of mainly religious music. <!-- fact_503 --> It is stately and bright, and it sounds its fullest on organ and trumpet.

*Suits:* the couple, for a grand entrance with organ or brass.

### 6. The Arrival of the Queen of Sheba (from Solomon), George Frideric Handel

This is an Act III number in Handel's Solomon. <!-- fact_504 --> It is quick and lively, for a wedding party who want to walk with energy.

*Suits:* the wedding party, for a brisk, upbeat walk.

### 7. Spring (from The Four Seasons), Antonio Vivaldi

Spring is Concerto No. 1 of Vivaldi's The Four Seasons, a group of four violin concertos published in 1725. <!-- fact_401 --> The opening movement is bright and sunny.

*Suits:* the wedding party, for outdoor and daytime ceremonies.

### 8. Clair de lune, Claude Debussy

Clair de lune is the third movement of Debussy's Suite bergamasque, a piano suite published in 1905. <!-- fact_402 --> It is soft and spacious, and it sounds lovely on solo piano or harp in a small room or at dusk.

*Suits:* the couple, for intimate and sunset ceremonies.

### 9. Salut d'Amour, Edward Elgar

Elgar composed Salut d'amour for violin and piano in 1888 and dedicated it to Caroline Alice Elgar. <!-- fact_404 --> It is short, warm and melodic, and it sits naturally with a string trio or quartet.

*Suits:* the couple, with a string trio or quartet.

### 10. Rondeau (from Suite de symphonies), Jean-Joseph Mouret

The Rondeau is the first movement of Suite 1 of Jean-Joseph Mouret's Symphonies de Fanfares. <!-- fact_505 --> Its fanfare opening announces an entrance clearly, and it works on trumpet and organ or as a brass ensemble piece.

*Suits:* the wedding party or the couple, for a fanfare-style entrance.

## What can you walk down the aisle to in a church?

Hymns and sacred pieces sit comfortably in a church service, and many can be played by the organist without rehearsal. Ask your officiant or music director early whether a particular piece, or any secular song, suits your service.

### 11. Ave Maria, Franz Schubert

Schubert composed his Ave Maria for voice and piano in 1825. <!-- fact_403 --> With a singer it becomes a focal point; on cello or violin it gives a slow, gentle walk.

*Suits:* the couple, in a church ceremony, with voice or strings.

### 12. Ave Maria, Charles Gounod

Gounod revised this setting of the Ave Maria text into its present form in 1859. <!-- fact_506 --> Its rippling accompaniment keeps it moving, so it carries a walk a little more easily than Schubert's version.

*Suits:* the couple, in a church ceremony.

### 13. Trumpet Tune, Henry Purcell

Purcell's Trumpet Tune in C major dates from 1696. <!-- fact_507 --> It is short, bright and ceremonial, and it is made for organ and trumpet.

*Suits:* the couple, with organ and trumpet.

### 14. Panis Angelicus, César Franck

Panis Angelicus is an extract from Franck's Messe solennelle, 1860. <!-- fact_508 --> It is calm and lyrical, for voice or strings.

*Suits:* the wedding party, in Catholic ceremonies; ask your officiant or music director.

### 15. Amazing Grace, words by John Newton

John Newton published the hymn in 1779 in the Olney Hymns. <!-- fact_05 --> It is sung to the tune New Britain, an Appalachian folk tune. <!-- fact_06 --> It works on bagpipes, guitar or organ.

*Suits:* the wedding party, in a church or outdoors.

### 16. Be Thou My Vision, sung to Slane

Slane is an old Irish folk tune, <!-- fact_03 --> and the English text is by Mary E. Byrne and Eleanor Hull. <!-- fact_01 --> The tune has a gentle, rolling lilt that suits a solo instrument such as a tin whistle, harp or violin.

*Suits:* the wedding party, for couples with Irish or Celtic heritage.

### 17. Morning Has Broken, words by Eleanor Farjeon

Eleanor Farjeon wrote the words in 1931, <!-- fact_509 --> and the hymn is sung to the tune Bunessan. <!-- fact_510 --> It is light, open and unhurried, and it belongs to the first half of the day.

*Suits:* the couple, for morning and garden ceremonies.

### 18. Love Divine, All Loves Excelling

A hymn many congregations know, <!-- fact_702 --> it has a broad, singable melody. Suggest it to your music director if you want guests to sing as the wedding party enters.

*Suits:* the wedding party, as a hymn the congregation can sing.

### 19. Joyful, Joyful, We Adore Thee, Ludwig van Beethoven

Henry van Dyke wrote these words in 1907 <!-- fact_07 --> to be sung to Beethoven's Hymn to Joy, adapted from the final movement of his Ninth Symphony. <!-- fact_08 --> It is bright and celebratory.

*Suits:* the couple, for an uplifting church entrance.

### 20. The Lord's My Shepherd, sung to Crimond

The tune Crimond is by Jessie Seymour Irvine. <!-- fact_511 --> It is steady and dignified, for organ or congregation.

*Suits:* the wedding party, in Scottish or Presbyterian ceremonies.

## How does a destination ceremony change your processional music?

Abroad, music becomes logistics. Send your shortlist to the venue early and ask these questions.

- **Live musicians:** Can the venue recommend local musicians, and can they learn your pieces from sheet music or a recording?
- **The musician's quote:** What does it include: rehearsal, travel, set-up time and how long they play? Is that confirmed in writing?
- **Deposit and cancellation:** What deposit is due, when, and what are the cancellation terms? Are both set out in writing?
- **Who runs the sound:** Who supplies and operates the sound system at the ceremony, and who on the venue side is responsible for the music on the day?
- **If something fails:** What happens if a musician can't play, the power drops or the playback fails?
- **Sound outdoors:** Will acoustic instruments carry across your ceremony space, or will you need amplification?
- **Power:** Is there power at the ceremony spot for a keyboard, speakers or a microphone? Is a generator or battery system available, and who manages it?
- **Aisle length and terrain:** How long is the walk on a beach, lawn or clifftop path, and is there a step-free route for older guests?
- **Music permissions:** Does the venue have the permissions it needs for live or recorded music at your ceremony, and are there any sound limits or curfews?
- **Back-up plan:** If the ceremony moves indoors for weather, where do the musicians set up, and does the processional still work?

Add sound limits, power at the ceremony site and music permissions to your booking checklist. Your Wedded Advisor confirms the inclusions in writing before you commit.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do you call the song when the bride walks down the aisle?

It is the processional. If the wedding party and the couple walk to different music, the couple's piece is often called the entrance or bridal entrance song. The music for walking out together at the end is the recessional.

### What is the correct order for a wedding processional?

In many ceremonies, the officiant and one partner wait at the front, then parents and grandparents are seated, the wedding party walks in, and the couple, or the person being walked in, enters last. Confirm the order with your officiant.

### What are some love songs to walk down the aisle to?

From the modern list, A Thousand Years, Can't Help Falling in Love, Make You Feel My Love and At Last are slow and romantic. For classical, try Salut d'amour or Clair de lune.

### What are some modern wedding processional songs?

Perfect by Ed Sheeran, Turning Page by Sleeping at Last and I Choose You by Sara Bareilles are good starting points.

### What songs suit the groom's entrance?

A groom or partner who walks in separately often chooses something shorter and more upbeat than the main processional, such as Here Comes the Sun or the Rondeau by Mouret.

### What are good bridal entrance songs?

For a traditional entrance, the Bridal Chorus from Lohengrin or the Prince of Denmark's March. For something gentler, Canon in D, Nuvole Bianche or Clair de lune. For a modern choice, A Thousand Years or At Last.
