## What these weddings share

The settings differ, yet the same few questions sit behind every one of these weddings. Each point below ends in questions to put to a venue before you sign, and the answers belong in writing. Take them to every site visit, and to your first conversation with [Wedded Concierge](/wonderland-concierge?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=content&utm_campaign=australia-real-wedding&utm_content=primary).

### Guests travelling from interstate and overseas

Two of these couples had guests flying in from interstate or overseas, and distance shapes the whole weekend, not only the ceremony.

- How do guests reach the venue from the nearest airport, and how long is the trip?
- Where can guests stay nearby, and is there accommodation on the property?
- Is there a drop-off point for guests who arrive by coach or car?
- Can the venue host a welcome drink the night before or a long lunch the day after?

### A ceremony outdoors and a covered plan

A clifftop, a view over the vines and a great old tree all ask the same thing of a venue: somewhere to go if the weather turns.

- Where is the covered option, and can every guest be seated there for the ceremony and for dinner?
- Who decides to move under cover, and how many hours before the ceremony?
- Is that space on the same property, and can we see it set up during our visit?

### Styling that lets the setting lead

Some of these couples kept their styling spare, and Helen and Trent dressed their marquee themselves. Either way, the venue's rules need to be clear early.

- What can we bring in ourselves, and what has to come from the venue's own suppliers?
- What time can our stylist or our family start setting up, and when must everything be cleared?
- Can anything be hung from trees, beams or a marquee frame?
- Are candles or other open flames allowed inside and outdoors?

### Keeping the guest list close

Several of these weddings were small by design, and a smaller guest list asks different things of a space.

- Is there a minimum guest number for the space we want?
- Can a smaller room or a corner of the grounds be set aside so a small group does not feel lost?
- Is the venue ours alone for the day, or will other events or guests be nearby?
- Can the ceremony and the reception take place in different spaces on the same property?

## Planning a wedding in Australia from here

Once a setting feels right, three things come next: the paperwork, the season and the guests who travel.

### The legal steps

As at November 2026, the Marriage Act 1961 and the Marriage Regulations 2017 set the rules for marrying in Australia. <!-- fact_01 --> What follows is general information, not legal advice.

The first step is a Notice of Intended Marriage, the form that tells an authorised marriage celebrant you plan to marry. It reaches your celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding, and you can complete it while living overseas. <!-- fact_02 --> If the date is less than 1 month away, talk to your celebrant, since you may still be able to marry if a prescribed authority approves it. <!-- fact_02 --> For each stage of the form, read our [Notice of Intended Marriage step by step](/expert-advice/notice-of-intended-marriage-explained-step-by-step) guide.

An authorised marriage celebrant must perform the ceremony for the marriage to be legal. There are four types: Commonwealth-registered marriage celebrants, Commonwealth-registered religious marriage celebrants, ministers of religion from a recognised denomination, and state and territory officers. <!-- fact_04 --> The Attorney-General's Department keeps a [register of Commonwealth-registered celebrants](https://marriage.ag.gov.au/commonwealthcelebrants/all) that you can search by name and filter by state, so check your celebrant's entry early. <!-- fact_05 --> For the detail on each type, see [who can legally marry you in Australia](/expert-advice/who-can-perform-a-marriage).

On the day, 3 marriage certificates are signed by the two of you, your celebrant and 2 witnesses over 18. The certificate you take home that day is ceremonial, and within 14 days your celebrant submits the paperwork to the registry of births, deaths and marriages in the state or territory where you married. <!-- fact_03 --> Leave room for the signing in the running order.

### Seasons and weather

Ask each venue about its own season rather than the country's, because a reef island and a southern vineyard can sit in very different weather in the same month.

On an island in the Whitsundays, summer brings the wet months. [Bureau of Meteorology climate records](https://www.bom.gov.au/climate/averages/tables/cw_033106.shtml) for an island airport there show January and February far wetter, with many more days of rain, than late winter and early spring. <!-- fact_06 -->

Plan for sun as well as rain. Australian [sun safety advice](https://www.cancer.org.au/cancer-information/causes-and-prevention/sun-safety/be-sunsmart) recommends protection once UV reaches 3 or above, and since UV can be neither seen nor felt, check the forecast for your location on the SunSmart app or the Bureau of Meteorology website. <!-- fact_07 -->

Trees and built shade cut UV, yet some still reaches guests by reflection, so treat shade as one layer alongside covering clothing, hats, sunglasses and SPF 50 or SPF50+ sunscreen. <!-- fact_08 --> Sunscreen goes on at least 20 minutes before heading outside, and again every two hours or after a swim or heavy sweating. <!-- fact_09 -->

- Which months does the venue suggest for an outdoor ceremony, and what is the fallback in the wettest ones?
- What shade covers the ceremony seating at the hour you plan to marry, and can a sunshade or tent be added?
- Who watches the forecast, and by what time on the day is the call made?
- Can we set out water and sunscreen for guests near the ceremony?

### Can we marry legally in Australia if we live overseas?

Yes. The Notice of Intended Marriage can be completed from overseas, and it still goes to an authorised marriage celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. <!-- fact_02 --> This is general information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

### Who can legally marry us in Australia?

An authorised marriage celebrant: a Commonwealth-registered celebrant, a Commonwealth-registered religious celebrant, a minister of religion from a recognised denomination, or a state or territory officer. <!-- fact_04 -->

### When should we lodge our Notice of Intended Marriage?

At least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. If the date is closer than 1 month, talk to your celebrant, since a prescribed authority may approve the marriage going ahead. <!-- fact_02 -->

### What should we ask an Australian venue about the weather?

Ask where the covered option is and whether every guest can be seated there, who makes the call to move and by what time, and what shade sits over the ceremony seating at the hour you marry.
