# Mauritius civil marriage checklist

The documents and steps from "Mauritius Wedding Legal Requirements and Steps", in order, plus questions to put to the Central Civil Status Office and your resort. Requirements come from the Civil Status Division, the Prime Minister's Office (Apostille), Smartraveller and Mauritius Meteorological Services; check their pages before you rely on any step. A fee applies to a wedding at a hotel or other premises (there is no payment for a ceremony at the Office): ask the Office for the current amount. The article has no `[[VERIFY]]` markers in the copy and no prices (nothing for Nic to sign off).

As at November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Before you leave Australia

- [ ] Decide on the civil ceremony before a Civil Status Officer (the legal route for two non-citizens), and whether to add a religious or symbolic ceremony.
- [ ] Read the Civil Status Division's Marriage of two Non Citizens page together. (Couples holding a Mauritian residence or work permit follow a different page.)
- [ ] Order fresh birth certificates from your state or territory registry once the date is fixed, so each is no more than 3 months old when you apply.
- [ ] Complete the coversheet.
- [ ] Make two photocopies of each birth certificate.
- [ ] Make two photocopies of each passport (photo page and the pages with your details).
- [ ] Add divorce, widowhood or change of name documents if they apply to either of you, and ask the Office which must be legalised.
- [ ] If either of you cannot produce a civil status document, arrange an affidavit (sworn in your country of residence or in a form the Registrar of Civil Status approves).
- [ ] Send the set to the Central Civil Status Office in Port Louis 6 to 8 weeks before the wedding; it must arrive at least one month before the date.
- [ ] Choose your two witnesses and make sure both will be on the island on the day.
- [ ] Book flights so you land with time for the Port Louis visit, the 24-hour publication and a spare day.
- [ ] Check Smartraveller's Mauritius advice and subscribe to updates for your dates.
- [ ] Check visa rules: under 60 days, a visa is provided on arrival; other types of visit need a visa before you travel.
- [ ] Complete the Mauritius All-in-One Travel Digital Form online before you fly (or on arrival).
- [ ] Check whether anyone needs a yellow fever certificate (arriving from or transiting a country with yellow fever transmission risk), including guests.
- [ ] Check travel insurance covers cyclone disruption if your date falls in November to May; ask guests to check theirs.
- [ ] If any document needs attention on the island, request an Australian High Commission appointment by email before you leave.
- [ ] Recheck entry conditions in the week you leave.
- [ ] Pack the original documents in your hand luggage.

## In Mauritius

- [ ] Visit the Central Civil Status Office in Port Louis together, early in the trip, with your original documents for verification.
- [ ] Receive your Certificate of Authority (certifying each of you is a non-resident).
- [ ] Allow the 24-hour publication before the ceremony.
- [ ] Pay the fee at the Central Civil Status Office if the wedding is at a hotel or other premises.
- [ ] Arrange the Civil Status Officer's transport to and from the venue if requested (car and driver on the run sheet).
- [ ] Marry before the Civil Status Officer with your two witnesses present.
- [ ] Ask the Office for certified copies of the marriage certificate before you fly home.
- [ ] If you may need an Apostille, keep a spare day after the wedding and lodge the original with the Prime Minister's Office (Apostille Office) before you leave.

## After the wedding

- [ ] Keep the original marriage certificate safe: only originals, or copies certified by a notary and the Supreme Court, can take an Apostille.
- [ ] Ask whoever will receive the certificate whether they need an Apostille.
- [ ] Before using the certificate for a name change, check with your state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages.
- [ ] Nothing to register in Australia. A recommitment ceremony at home is possible; a second marriage is not.

## Questions for the Central Civil Status Office

- [ ] Has our application and document set arrived, and is anything missing?
- [ ] Which of our documents (for example a divorce, widowhood or name change record) must be legalised or translated?
- [ ] Which days and hours should we come in to have our originals verified?
- [ ] How many working days should we allow between landing and the ceremony?
- [ ] What is the current fee for a ceremony at a hotel or other premises, and how is it paid?
- [ ] What identification should our two witnesses bring?
- [ ] Will the Officer ask us to choose a matrimonial regime, and which options apply to two non-citizens?
- [ ] How can we add a religious ceremony alongside the civil marriage?
- [ ] How and when can we obtain certified copies of the marriage certificate before we fly home?

## Questions for the resort

- [ ] Does the Civil Status Officer attend on site, and where on your grounds does the civil part take place?
- [ ] Does a ceremony on the beach itself need any permission, and who arranges it?
- [ ] What time can the Officer be booked for, and how does that sit with the light and the heat in our month?
- [ ] Who arranges the Officer's transport if it is requested, and who meets them on arrival?
- [ ] What is the covered alternative if rain or wind arrives, and when is that call made?
- [ ] What exactly is included for the civil formalities, and what sits outside it?
- [ ] Where can the witnesses stand, sit and sign without leaving the ceremony setting?
- [ ] Can the ceremony space, or the whole property, be booked for exclusive use and closed to the public?
- [ ] How does your security work alongside a private security team, and how is guest privacy handled on the day?
- [ ] What deposit is required, is it refundable, in which currency is it paid, and is the contract in English?
- [ ] Is there step-free access to the ceremony spot for older guests?
- [ ] Which coast does the ceremony site face, and where is the sun at our ceremony time in our month?
- [ ] How does the wind sit on that coast in our month, and how sheltered is the ceremony spot?
- [ ] How long is the drive to the Central Civil Status Office in Port Louis for our document visit?
- [ ] Which inclusions will you confirm in writing before we commit?
