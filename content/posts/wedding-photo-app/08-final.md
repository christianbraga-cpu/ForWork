Your photographer will capture the vows, the portraits and the first dance. Your guests will capture everything else: the aunt who cried at the aisle, the groomsmen at the bar, the view from the back table as the sun went down. A wedding photo app gathers those pictures and videos in one private place, so they don't scatter across a hundred phones and group chats.

We recommend no specific apps. Here are the main types of tool, what to check, privacy and ownership, and making it work at a venue overseas.

## Key takeaways

- A wedding photo app collects your guests' candid photos and videos in one private album, alongside your professional photographer's gallery rather than instead of it.
- Check any option for its total cost and what is included, browser uploads with no install, full resolution, video, moderation, a full download at the end, how long photos are kept and offline uploads.
- Ask guests before their photos are shared publicly, check with parents before sharing images of children, and remove any photo a guest asks you to take down.
- At a destination wedding, ask the venue about Wi-Fi and data early, and choose a set-up that lets guests upload later from home.

## What a wedding photo app does

It is a shared album you control. Guests add what they shoot, you decide who sees it, and at the end you download the lot. It collects rather than plans: for guest lists, budgets and seating, see our guide to [wedding planning apps](/expert-advice/best-wedding-planning-apps).

### How guests add photos and videos

A guest scans a QR code on a table card or taps a link on your wedding website, lands on the album and uploads from their camera roll. Some tools need an app and an account; others open in the phone's browser. The fewer steps between a guest and the upload button, the more photos you will receive.

### Guest photos and your photographer's gallery

Guest photos are a second layer, never a replacement. Your photographer brings composition, light and a plan; your guests bring the angles and in-jokes no professional could know about. Read [what a wedding photographer package includes](/expert-advice/average-cost-wedding-photographer) so you know exactly what their gallery will cover, and let the guest album fill the gaps around it.

## The main types of guest photo tool

Each type suits a different kind of wedding, and some couples use two together.

### Shared album apps

A dedicated app where guests join your event, upload and browse everyone else's photos. It suits app-confident guests and a celebration that runs over a weekend. The trade-off is the install: some guests will not bother.

### QR code upload pages with no install

A web page guests reach by scanning a code, with nothing to download and no account. It is the simplest route for a mixed or international guest list. The trade-off is that browsing can be more basic than in a full app.

### Disposable camera style apps

These borrow the charm of the cardboard camera on every table. Guests are given a limited number of shots, and the photos stay hidden until a later reveal. The trade-off is fewer photos, and some guests will simply use their own camera.

### Live slideshow displays

Uploads appear on a screen at the reception as they arrive, which makes a lively talking point. The trade-off is control: anything uploaded may appear in front of everyone, so check that you can review uploads before they reach the screen.

### Your photographer's own gallery

Your photographer's delivered gallery may have room for guest uploads, keeping everything in one place. Ask whether it is included, how long it stays open and whether guest uploads download separately.

## Guests who would rather not download an app

Older guests may find a new app a hurdle, and international guests on roaming plans may not want to download anything. A browser upload page removes that friction. If you prefer an app, offer the browser link as well, so every guest can still contribute.

## What to look for before you choose

Use these checks to compare any option, and ask the provider directly.

### No download needed for guests

Can a guest upload from the phone's browser, with no app and no account? If not, what does a guest share to sign up?

### Full resolution and video

Are photos kept at original resolution or compressed on upload? Is video supported, at what clip length and in what quality?

### Moderation and who can see the album

Who can view the album: people with the link, guests you approve, or anyone? Can the link be forwarded, and can you stop guests downloading other people's photos? Can you hide or delete a single photo quickly, and review uploads before they reach a slideshow?

### Downloading everything at the end

Can you download the full collection in one go, at full resolution, with videos included, and does it keep the name of the guest who uploaded each file?

### What it costs and what is included

Ask for the total cost, exactly what it includes, and whether full resolution downloads, video or longer storage cost extra.

### How long photos are kept

Ask how long the album stays online and what happens afterwards, then download and back up well before any deadline.

### Offline uploads when signal is patchy

Can photos taken with no signal send automatically once the phone reconnects? On a clifftop or a boat, check this first.

## Privacy, consent and who owns guest photos

This section is general information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

### Asking guests before you share

Australia's eSafety Commissioner advises that you should always [ask for consent before sharing a photo or video](https://www.esafety.gov.au/young-people/consent-sharing-photos-videos). <!-- fact_01 --> Genuine consent means the person understands what they are agreeing to and has not been pressured into it <!-- fact_03 -->, and the advice applies to pictures from a phone, tablet, smart glasses or any other device. <!-- fact_04 -->

Consent can change. If someone who said yes changes their mind, delete the photo from where it was posted or edit them out of the shot. <!-- fact_05 --> If a guest asks you to remove a photo, do it, no questions asked. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_07 -->

Once something is shared online, you can lose control of how others share or use it <!-- fact_06 -->, which is the strongest argument for a private, invite-only album. A short photo policy from the two of you gives guests a clear rule.

Children need particular care. eSafety advises parents to [check with other parents before posting, sharing or tagging images that include their children](https://www.esafety.gov.au/parents/issues-and-advice/privacy-child) <!-- fact_21 -->, to share only with people they know and trust, and to use privacy settings to limit who sees them. <!-- fact_22 -->

The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner treats photos and videos as [personal information under the Privacy Act 1988](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/social-media-and-online-privacy/photos-and-videos) when a person's identity is clear or could reasonably be worked out. <!-- fact_10 --> The Act does not cover individuals, so it does not apply to photos taken by someone acting in a personal capacity <!-- fact_11 -->, though state or territory laws may apply in some situations. <!-- fact_12 -->

If a photo is posted without permission, the [OAIC suggests](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/social-media-and-online-privacy/photos-and-videos) first asking the person who posted it to take it down, then the site's administrator. <!-- fact_13 --> A complaint to the OAIC is possible where the host is an organisation or agency covered by the Act. <!-- fact_14 -->

A line on your welcome sign does a lot of work: please ask before sharing anyone else's photo publicly, and we will remove any picture on request. For a fully private celebration, the line can simply ask guests not to post anything at all.

### Copyright in photos your guests take

Under [section 35(2) of the Copyright Act 1968](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s35.html), the author of an artistic work is, by default, the owner of its copyright <!-- fact_15 -->; for a photo, that is generally the person who took it. The full Act is on the [Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1968A00063/latest/text).

[Section 35(5)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s35.html) covers commissioned photos. Where someone agrees, for valuable consideration, to have a photograph taken for a private or domestic purpose, which includes a portrait of a wedding party, that person owns the copyright. <!-- fact_16 --> <!-- fact_18 --> The photographer can restrain uses outside a purpose made known at the time <!-- fact_17 -->, and the rule can be changed by agreement. <!-- fact_19 --> It turns on a paid agreement, which describes the photographer you hire, not a guest shooting on their own phone. <!-- fact_20 -->

In practice, ask a guest before you print, frame or post their photo, and read the usage and copyright clause in your photographer's contract before you sign.

### Questions to ask the venue about internet and data

Ask whether guest Wi-Fi reaches every space you are using, from the ceremony site to the garden, whether it copes with many guests uploading at once, and whether the password can go on your cards. Ask how mobile signal holds up across the grounds, and how venue staff and suppliers will be briefed on your photo policy.

### Offline uploads and guests in other time zones

If signal is uncertain, choose a tool that queues photos offline, or tell guests that uploading from the hotel or from home is welcome. A wedding photo sharing app that stays open for weeks suits a guest list spread across continents: friends flying home to London or Singapore can add pictures after they land.

### Celebrations over several days

A welcome dinner, the wedding and a farewell lunch are three sets of memories. Ask whether uploads can sort into an album per event, or label the QR cards by day.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest way to collect wedding photos from guests?

A shared album with a QR code on every table and a link on your wedding website, mentioned again at the reception and left open for a few weeks afterwards.

### Do guests need to download an app to share wedding photos?

Not necessarily. Look for a tool that lets guests upload from the phone's browser after scanning a QR code, with nothing to install.

### Can guests upload videos as well as photos?

It depends on the tool, so check before you commit that video is supported, kept in original quality and not cut short.

### Who owns the photos guests take at a wedding?

As general information as at November 2026, the Copyright Act's default rule is that the author of a work owns its copyright, which for a photo is generally the person who took it. <!-- fact_15 --> Ask a guest before you reuse their photo, and check your photographer's contract for your professional images.

### How do you share a QR code for wedding photos?

Print it on table cards and an entrance sign, add it to your website and invitation, and have the MC mention it.

### Is a disposable camera app a good idea for a wedding?

It can be, for a playful mood. Limited shots and a delayed reveal make guests choose their moments, though you will receive fewer photos.
