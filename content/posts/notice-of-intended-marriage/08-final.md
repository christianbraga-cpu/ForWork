A Notice of Intended Marriage, or NOIM, is the written notice every couple marrying in Australia gives to their authorised marriage celebrant before the wedding. As at November 2026, it must reach your celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the day. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_03 --> Without it, the marriage cannot go ahead. <!-- fact_100 -->

If you plan to marry legally in another country instead, the NOIM does not come into it: you follow that country's rules. <!-- fact_13 --> <!-- fact_17 -->

This guide takes the form step by step, then answers the question destination couples ask most: which paperwork applies to us?

## Key takeaways

- The NOIM is the written notice section 42 of the Marriage Act 1961 requires, and you give it to the authorised celebrant who will marry you. <!-- fact_100 -->
- It must reach your celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before your wedding. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_03 -->
- Since 12 June 2024 it can be witnessed in person or by audio-visual link, though not online if you sign overseas. <!-- fact_101 --> <!-- fact_102 -->
- Marry legally in Australia and celebrate abroad, and you need a NOIM; marry legally abroad, and you follow that country's rules instead. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_13 -->

## What is a Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM)?

<!-- IMAGE SLOT couple-signing-celebrant: Landscape 3:2. The NOIM is a form published by the Attorney-General's Department. Section 42 of the Marriage Act 1961 says a marriage shall not be solemnised unless written notice of the intended marriage, on this form, is given to the authorised celebrant solemnising it. <!-- fact_100 --> The form downloads as a PDF or a Word document. <!-- fact_100 -->

The rules for marrying in Australia sit in the Marriage Act 1961 and the Marriage Regulations 2017. <!-- fact_01 --> To marry here you must give the notice to an authorised marriage celebrant within the time window, and be married by an authorised marriage celebrant. <!-- fact_02 --> You must also not already be married, not be marrying a close relative, be at least 18 (unless a court has approved a marriage where one person is 16 to 18), and understand what marriage means and freely agree to it. <!-- fact_10 -->

You do not need to be an Australian citizen or permanent resident to marry in Australia. <!-- fact_09 --> If one of you wants to live here after the wedding, look into a visa with the Department of Home Affairs. <!-- fact_09 -->

The NOIM is the first document your celebrant will ask for, so the choice of celebrant comes first. Our guide on [how to choose a wedding celebrant](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/how-to-choose-a-wedding-celebrant) covers what to look for. The Attorney-General's Department keeps the current form on its [Notice of Intended Marriage page](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/publications/notice-intended-marriage).

## When do you need to lodge your NOIM?

<!-- IMAGE SLOT timing-calendar: Landscape 3:2 or 4:3. Still life of a linen-bound paper calendar or planner opened to a month view, a few dates lightly circled in ink or marked with small dried florals, a fountain pen and a cup of coffee. No readable brand or personal data. -->

As at November 2026, the timing rule is simple and fixed. <!-- fact_02 -->

### The earliest you can lodge

Your celebrant can receive the NOIM up to 18 months before your wedding, and no earlier. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_03 --> If you book a venue two summers ahead, your date may be secured before the paperwork can begin.

### The latest you can lodge

The notice must be with your celebrant at least one month before the wedding. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_03 --> Treat that as a floor, not a target. Your celebrant also needs to meet each of you separately and in person before the ceremony, and that meeting takes diary time on both sides. <!-- fact_06 --> <!-- fact_07 -->

### What happens if you miss the window

If there is less than one month until your wedding, talk to your authorised celebrant. You may still be able to marry if a prescribed authority approves it. <!-- fact_05 --> The full rules are under "Can the one-month notice be shortened?" below.

## How to fill out the NOIM form step by step

The form itself is short. It comes down to four steps: form, witness, celebrant, documents. For a fuller picture of the celebrant's role, read [what a celebrant does, at home and abroad](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/what-does-a-celebrant-do-and-do-you-need-one-abroad).

### Step 1: Get the official form and complete your details

Download the current NOIM from the Attorney-General's Department, in PDF or Word format. <!-- fact_100 --> The form was updated on 1 September 2021. <!-- fact_08 --> Use the official version only, so every field matches what your celebrant expects. Complete your own details, and ask your authorised celebrant about any field you are unsure of. <!-- fact_03 -->

### Step 2: Sign in front of an authorised witness

Your signatures must be witnessed, in person or remotely by audio-visual link. <!-- fact_101 --> Who can witness depends on where you sign: see "Who can witness a Notice of Intended Marriage?" below. <!-- fact_102 -->

### Step 3: Give it to your authorised celebrant

The completed, witnessed NOIM goes to the authorised celebrant who will marry you, within the one month to 18 month window. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_100 --> If you change celebrants later, the NOIM can be transferred to another authorised celebrant, including at your request. <!-- fact_07 -->

### Step 4: Provide your documents

Your celebrant will also need evidence of birth, identity and, where it applies, the end of an earlier marriage. The list is set out under "What documents do you need?" below. <!-- fact_06 -->

## Who can witness a Notice of Intended Marriage?

Where you are when you sign decides who can witness. As at November 2026, the requirements for authorised witnesses are unchanged. <!-- fact_102 --> In Australia, a remote witness must also be in Australia and can be a celebrant. Overseas, the witness must also be overseas, cannot be a celebrant, and must witness in person. <!-- fact_101 --> <!-- fact_102 -->

The full list of who qualifies is on the form, in the "Signatures of parties and witnesses" section. <!-- fact_103 --> If you are unsure whether someone qualifies, ask your celebrant before you book the appointment.

### Witnessing in Australia

If you are in Australia, your NOIM can be witnessed in person or remotely by audio-visual link. <!-- fact_101 --> A remote witness can be an authorised celebrant, provided they are in Australia too. <!-- fact_102 -->

### Witnessing when you are overseas

A NOIM for a person or couple outside Australia cannot be witnessed online. <!-- fact_102 --> Plan for an in-person appointment with an authorised witness where you live.

The Attorney-General's Department sets out these rules on its [Notice of Intended Marriage page](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/publications/notice-intended-marriage).

## What documents do you need?

As at November 2026, the Attorney-General's Department lists what your authorised celebrant will need alongside the NOIM: <!-- fact_06 -->

- Evidence of your date and place of birth, such as a birth certificate or passport. <!-- fact_06 -->
- Evidence of your identity, such as a driver's licence or passport. <!-- fact_06 -->
- Proof that any previous marriage has ended. <!-- fact_06 -->
- A separate, in-person meeting with each of you. <!-- fact_06 -->

Your celebrant will tell you how to present them.

Keep the originals together in one folder. Later, you will need your official marriage certificate to change your name or prove you are married. <!-- fact_12 -->

## Lodging a NOIM when you live overseas

As at November 2026, you can complete the NOIM while you are overseas if you plan to marry in Australia. <!-- fact_04 -->

The witnessing rule is the part to plan around: see "Witnessing when you are overseas" above. <!-- fact_102 --> Check the list of authorised witnesses on the form before you make the appointment. <!-- fact_103 -->

Once it is signed and witnessed, the NOIM still has to reach your authorised celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. <!-- fact_02 --> Ask your celebrant how they would like to receive it, and leave room for the in-person meetings once you land. <!-- fact_06 -->

Supporting documents issued overseas raise their own questions. Our guide on [when your paperwork needs an apostille](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/what-is-an-apostille-and-does-my-paperwork-need-one) explains the term, and your celebrant will tell you what they accept.

## Can the one-month notice be shortened?

If there is less than one month until your wedding, you may still marry if a prescribed authority approves it. <!-- fact_05 --> As at November 2026, the grounds the Attorney-General's Department lists are: <!-- fact_05 -->

- employment-related or travel commitments
- wedding or celebration arrangements
- medical reasons
- legal proceedings
- an error in giving notice

A prescribed authority is a government officer, such as a registrar of a local court or of a state registry of births, deaths and marriages, appointed by the Attorney-General. Among other things, a prescribed authority can authorise a celebrant to proceed where the minimum one-month notice has not been given. <!-- fact_104 -->

The [list of prescribed authorities](https://marriage.ag.gov.au/stateofficers/authorities) is searchable by state and territory. <!-- fact_104 --> Approval is never automatic, so start with your celebrant and treat a shortening of time as a fallback, not a plan.

## Does the NOIM differ by state?

The NOIM is one form, published by the Attorney-General's Department under the Marriage Act 1961. <!-- fact_100 --> After the wedding, your celebrant must lodge the marriage paperwork with the registry of births, deaths and marriages in the state or territory where you married, within 14 days. <!-- fact_11 -->

For any local step in the state or territory where you will marry, ask your celebrant.

## NOIM or marrying abroad: which paperwork applies?

For a destination wedding, the NOIM question comes down to where the legal marriage happens. There are two routes.

### Option A: marry legally in Australia, celebrate abroad

You hold a small legal ceremony in Australia with an authorised celebrant, and the NOIM applies exactly as above. <!-- fact_02 --> On the day, you, your celebrant and two witnesses over 18 sign three marriage certificates, and the celebrant gives you a ceremonial certificate. <!-- fact_11 --> Ask your celebrant where the legal ceremony can take place. Your celebrant then lodges the paperwork with the registry within 14 days. <!-- fact_11 --> Apply to the registry for your official marriage certificate, which you need to change your name or prove you are married; your celebrant can do this for you, generally at a cost. <!-- fact_12 -->

The celebration abroad can then be whatever you want it to be. Once your marriage is recognised, you cannot marry again in Australia, but you can hold another kind of ceremony, such as a confirmation of vows or a recommitment ceremony. <!-- fact_22 -->

You might choose a religious celebrant for the legal ceremony at home. In the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Australia, for example, every priest is also a registered civil celebrant who may perform the civil marriage only together with the religious service on the same day, and the NOIM is completed in the priest's presence at least one calendar month before the wedding. <!-- fact_24 --> <!-- fact_25 -->

### Option B: marry legally abroad

An Australian authorised celebrant cannot perform a marriage overseas, and an overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia. <!-- fact_13 --> The NOIM is not part of this route. Instead, you follow the destination's own rules.

**What you may need.** Some countries ask you to prove you are not already married. <!-- fact_17 -->

- A Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI). <!-- fact_17 -->
- Certificates from a registry of births, deaths and marriages. <!-- fact_17 -->
- Divorce papers or a deceased spouse's death certificate, where relevant. <!-- fact_18 -->
- Your original birth certificate and passport. <!-- fact_18 -->
- Some countries add further rules, such as a residence period. <!-- fact_17 -->

**Who to ask.** The destination's embassy or consulate. <!-- fact_19 -->

**What to check.** Whether you need a CNI, where to get it, and whether it needs an authentication or apostille. DFAT can issue a CNI in Australia, but some local authorities accept only CNIs issued in their own country. <!-- fact_19 -->

Smartraveller's [Getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) page is the official starting point.

In most cases, a marriage abroad is recognised in Australia if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. <!-- fact_20 --> A foreign marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, but you may not be able to use it for a name change, so check with your local registry. <!-- fact_21 --> Read more on [whether you have to register an overseas marriage](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/do-you-have-to-register-an-overseas-marriage-at-home) and on [getting your overseas marriage certificate](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate). ## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a Notice of Intended Marriage last?

As at November 2026, your NOIM must be with your celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before your wedding date. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_03 --> If your date moves, ask your celebrant whether your NOIM still stands.

### Who can witness a NOIM?

It depends where you sign, and the full list is on the form, so ask your celebrant if you are unsure. <!-- fact_102 --> <!-- fact_103 -->

### Can we lodge a NOIM if we live overseas?

Yes, if you plan to marry in Australia, with an in-person witness who is also overseas and is not a celebrant. <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_102 -->

### Can we get married with less than one month's notice?

Talk to your celebrant: you may be able to marry if a prescribed authority approves it, on grounds such as employment or travel commitments, celebration arrangements, medical reasons, legal proceedings or an error in giving notice. <!-- fact_05 -->

### Do we need a NOIM if we are getting married overseas?

No. The NOIM is for marriages solemnised in Australia by an authorised celebrant, and Australian celebrants cannot perform overseas marriages. <!-- fact_100 --> <!-- fact_13 --> Abroad, the local rules apply, and some countries ask for a Certificate of No Impediment. <!-- fact_17 -->

### Can we change celebrants after lodging our NOIM?

Yes. A NOIM can be transferred to another authorised celebrant, including at the couple's request. <!-- fact_07 -->
