In Australia, a celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination must say a short statement called the monitum before you marry. It is set out in [section 46(1)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html) of the Marriage Act 1961 and opens "I am duly authorised by law to solemnise marriages according to law", or words to that effect. <!-- fact_18 --> With a celebrant who is not a minister of religion, each of you then says the [section 45(2)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html) vow, or words to that effect, before the celebrant and [at least 2 witnesses over 18](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s44.html). <!-- fact_22 --> <!-- fact_17 -->

Everything else is yours. Those words sit inside an original wedding ceremony script Australia's celebrants can work from, with every part marked legally required or optional. ## Key takeaways

- A celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination says the [section 46(1) monitum](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html), or words to that effect, before you marry, in front of your witnesses. <!-- fact_18 -->
- With a celebrant who is not a minister of religion, each of you says the [section 45(2) vow](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html), or words to that effect, and your own vows can sit beside it. <!-- fact_22 -->
- An authorised celebrant must be physically present with the two of you and 2 witnesses over 18, and all of you [sign 3 marriage certificates](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) on the day. <!-- fact_08 --> <!-- fact_20 -->
- An Australian celebrant [cannot perform a legal marriage overseas](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married), so a celebration abroad pairs with a legal ceremony at home or follows the destination's own law. <!-- fact_12 -->

General information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

Marriage in Australia is governed by the [Marriage Act 1961](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text), administered by the Attorney-General's Department. Section 45 sets the form of ceremony, and section 46 the words some celebrants say to explain the nature of marriage. <!-- fact_01 -->

To [get married in Australia](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married), you must use specific words during the ceremony, give a notice of intended marriage to an authorised marriage celebrant at least 1 month and no more than 18 months before the wedding, and be married by an authorised marriage celebrant. <!-- fact_06 --> The celebrant must also meet each of you separately and in person beforehand, and must be physically present to solemnise the marriage, together with the two of you and 2 witnesses. <!-- fact_08 -->

### The monitum the celebrant says

The monitum is the short statement a celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination makes before you marry. Under [section 46(1)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html) of the Marriage Act 1961, a celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination says these words to the two of you, in the presence of the witnesses: <!-- fact_18 -->

> "I am duly authorised by law to solemnise marriages according to law.
>
> "Before you are joined in marriage in my presence and in the presence of these witnesses, I am to remind you of the solemn and binding nature of the relationship into which you are now about to enter.
>
> "Marriage, according to law in Australia, is the union of 2 people to the exclusion of all others, voluntarily entered into for life."; or words to that effect.
>
> Marriage Act 1961 (Cth), section 46(1)

### The vow each of you says

[Section 45(2)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html) of the Marriage Act 1961 covers a marriage solemnised by a celebrant who is not a minister of religion. It is sufficient if each of you says to the other, in the presence of the celebrant and the witnesses, the words: <!-- fact_22 -->

> "I call upon the persons here present to witness that I, A.B. (or C.D.), take thee, C.D. (or A.B.), to be my lawful wedded wife (or husband, or spouse)"; or words to that effect.
>
> Marriage Act 1961 (Cth), section 45(2)

A.B. and C.D. stand for your names, and each of you chooses wife, husband or spouse.

### Who has to say them, civil celebrant or minister of religion

The Act draws a line between celebrants. Under [section 45(1)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html), where the celebrant is a minister of religion, the marriage may be solemnised according to any form and ceremony recognised as sufficient by their religious body or organisation. <!-- fact_02 --> The section 46(1) monitum applies to celebrants who are not ministers of religion of a recognised denomination. <!-- fact_18 -->

[Section 46(2)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html) adds one more case. The Minister may exempt a person authorised under subsection 39(2) from the monitum, where satisfied that their form of ceremony sufficiently states the nature and obligations of marriage. <!-- fact_19 -->

If a minister is marrying you, ask which words their ceremony uses. For the kinds of celebrant who can marry you, see our guide to [who can legally marry you](/expert-advice/who-can-perform-a-marriage).

## The Australian wedding ceremony script, word for word

General information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

This script is written for a civil celebrant. The legal words are set in place; the rest you can keep, cut or rewrite with your celebrant. For the shape of the day around it, read [what happens in a wedding ceremony](/expert-advice/what-happens-in-a-wedding-ceremony).

Names in square brackets are placeholders. Your 2 witnesses need to be present from the monitum to the signing. <!-- fact_17 --> <!-- fact_18 --> <!-- fact_22 --> <!-- fact_20 -->

### Welcome

**Optional.**

Celebrant: "Good afternoon, and welcome. [Name] and [Name] have asked you here because you have shaped the people they are, and they want you beside them as they marry."

If you would like an Acknowledgement of Country, ask your celebrant to open with one before the welcome.

### The monitum

**Legally required** for a celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination, unless they hold a section 46(2) exemption. <!-- fact_18 --> <!-- fact_19 -->

Celebrant, to the couple, in the presence of the witnesses:

"I am duly authorised by law to solemnise marriages according to law.

"Before you are joined in marriage in my presence and in the presence of these witnesses, I am to remind you of the solemn and binding nature of the relationship into which you are now about to enter.

"Marriage, according to law in Australia, is the union of 2 people to the exclusion of all others, voluntarily entered into for life."

### A reading

**Optional.**

Celebrant: "[Name] and [Name] have asked [reader's name] to share a few words that matter to them."

### The vows, legal and personal

**Legally required:** the section 45(2) words, or words to that effect, with a celebrant who is not a minister of religion. <!-- fact_22 -->
**Optional:** your personal vows.

Celebrant: "[Name] and [Name] have written their own promises to each other. After them, each will say the legal vow."

Personal vows, first partner: "[Your own words.]"

Personal vows, second partner: "[Your own words.]"

Legal vow, first partner, to the second: "I call upon the persons here present to witness that I, [full name], take thee, [full name], to be my lawful wedded [wife, husband or spouse]."

Legal vow, second partner, to the first: "I call upon the persons here present to witness that I, [full name], take thee, [full name], to be my lawful wedded [wife, husband or spouse]."

### Ring exchange

**Optional.**

Celebrant: "[Name] and [Name] will now exchange rings."

Each partner, placing the ring: "[Name], I give you this ring as a sign of the promises I made today. Wear it, and know that I meant every word."

### Declaration

**Optional.**

Celebrant: "You have made your promises before the people you love most. It is my great pleasure to declare you married." Then, if you like, the kiss.

### Signing the certificates

**Legally required.** On the day, [3 marriage certificates must be signed](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) by the two of you, your authorised celebrant and 2 witnesses over 18. <!-- fact_20 -->

Celebrant: "While [Name] and [Name] sign their marriage certificates with their witnesses, please stay seated and enjoy the music."

### Presentation of the couple

**Optional.**

Celebrant: "Family and friends, please stand and welcome, for the first time as a married couple, [Name] and [Name]."

### A short legal script

For a small legal ceremony before a larger celebration, this is the core with nothing added. Your celebrant, the two of you and 2 witnesses are present throughout. <!-- fact_08 -->

1. **Optional.** Celebrant: "Welcome. We are here for the marriage of [Name] and [Name]."
2. **Legally required.** The celebrant says the section 46(1) monitum, as quoted above, unless exempt. <!-- fact_18 -->
3. **Legally required.** Each of you says the section 45(2) vow, with your full names and your choice of wife, husband or spouse. <!-- fact_22 -->
4. **Legally required.** The two of you, the celebrant and your witnesses sign the 3 certificates. <!-- fact_20 -->

## Legal wedding vows in Australia and how to add your own

General information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

The legal vow is one sentence. Under [section 45(2)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html), each of you names yourself and your partner and chooses to call them your lawful wedded wife, husband or spouse. <!-- fact_22 --> Your own vows sit beside it, before or after.

Two ways to arrange it:

- **Personal vows first, legal vow last.** The ceremony builds to the words that marry you.
- **Legal vow first, personal vows after.** You finish on your own words.

Read your personal vows aloud to your celebrant before the day, so they can confirm the legal line is intact around them. For examples, see our [short wedding vows](/expert-advice/short-wedding-vows); for the method, read [how to write your own wedding vows](/how-to-write-wedding-vows).

## Witnesses, the signing and the paperwork on the day

<!-- IMAGE SLOT signing: Licensed photo at a real Australian wedding of the couple signing at a dressed signing table with the celebrant and two adult witnesses beside them. Documents must not be legible, and no government crests, logos or certificate numbers visible (shoot from an angle or at shallow depth of field). Natural light, uncluttered table. General information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

[Section 44](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s44.html) of the Act says a marriage shall not be solemnised unless at least 2 people who are, or appear to the celebrant to be, over the age of 18 are present as witnesses. <!-- fact_17 --> Choose two people who will stand close by throughout, not anyone with a job to do. If an older relative is one of them, ask your celebrant to set a chair at the signing table.

On the day, [3 marriage certificates are signed](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) by the two of you, your celebrant and your 2 witnesses. <!-- fact_20 --> The [Form 15 Certificate of Marriage](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/resources-marriage-celebrants/your-responsibilities-marriage-celebrant) must be handed to you immediately after the ceremony. <!-- fact_15 -->

[Within 14 days of the marriage](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married), your celebrant must submit the marriage paperwork to the registry of births, deaths and marriages in the state or territory where you married. <!-- fact_20 --> For name changes or proof of marriage, you will need the official marriage certificate from that registry, which your celebrant can apply for on your behalf. <!-- fact_13 -->

Build a pause for the signing into the run sheet, and ask your celebrant to check every name and detail before anyone leaves. [Errors on the certificates](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/resources-marriage-celebrants/your-responsibilities-marriage-celebrant) can be corrected only while the two of you and your witnesses are still present; once the marriage is registered, only the registry's authorised officer can correct them. <!-- fact_16 -->

## Marrying overseas or holding a symbolic ceremony

General information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

An Australian script does not carry across borders. The Attorney-General's Department states that an overseas marriage [cannot be performed by an Australian-authorised marriage celebrant](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) or registered in Australia. <!-- fact_12 --> An overseas marriage is generally recognised in Australia if it was valid in that country and would have been valid here. <!-- fact_12 -->

That leaves two routes: marry under the destination's own law, or marry legally in Australia with the short script above and hold a symbolic ceremony abroad. If you marry under the destination's law, check its legal requirements with that country's authorities before you book. Our guide to [a legal wedding abroad or a symbolic ceremony](/expert-advice/should-you-marry-legally-abroad-or-have-a-symbolic-ceremony) weighs the two.

If you are overseas now, you can [complete the notice of intended marriage from abroad](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) when you plan to marry in Australia. <!-- fact_07 --> Your celebrant still needs to meet each of you separately and in person, <!-- fact_08 --> so book those meetings into the trip home. <!-- fact_21 -->

## Frequently asked questions

General information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

### What does a celebrant legally have to say in Australia?

A celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination says the [section 46(1) monitum](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html) before you marry, beginning "I am duly authorised by law to solemnise marriages according to law", or words to that effect. <!-- fact_18 --> With a celebrant who is not a minister of religion, each of you then says the [section 45(2) vow](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html) to the other, or words to that effect. <!-- fact_22 -->

### What is the monitum in a wedding ceremony?

It is the statement in [section 46(1)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html) of the Marriage Act 1961 that a celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination says to you, in front of your witnesses, before you marry. In it, the celebrant states they are duly authorised by law to solemnise marriages, reminds you of the solemn and binding nature of the relationship you are about to enter, and says what marriage is according to law in Australia. The full wording is quoted above. <!-- fact_18 -->

### Can we change the legal vows?

Both the [vow](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html) and the [monitum](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html) allow "words to that effect". <!-- fact_18 --> <!-- fact_22 --> Ask your celebrant before you change a word, and add your personal vows beside the legal line rather than in place of it.

### How many witnesses do we need at an Australian wedding?

[At least 2](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s44.html), who are or appear to the celebrant to be over 18. <!-- fact_17 --> They are present for the ceremony and [sign the 3 marriage certificates](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) with you and your celebrant. <!-- fact_20 -->

### Does a religious ceremony need the monitum?

[Section 46(1)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html) applies to celebrants who are not ministers of religion of a recognised denomination. <!-- fact_18 --> Under [section 45(1)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html), a minister of religion may solemnise a marriage according to any form and ceremony recognised as sufficient by their religious body or organisation. <!-- fact_02 --> Ask your minister which words their ceremony uses.

### Can our Australian celebrant marry us overseas?

No. An overseas marriage [cannot be performed by an Australian-authorised marriage celebrant](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married). <!-- fact_12 --> Couples marry under the destination's law, or marry legally at home and hold a symbolic ceremony abroad.
