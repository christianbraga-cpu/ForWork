# Australian wedding ceremony checklist

General information as at November 2026, not legal advice. Your authorised celebrant confirms the requirements for your ceremony.

## The legal steps on the day
- [ ] **The monitum.** A celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination says the section 46(1) words to the two of you, in front of your witnesses, before you marry, "or words to that effect" (unless they hold a section 46(2) exemption). Source: Marriage Act 1961, s46 (AustLII, read 23 November 2026).
- [ ] **The vows.** With a celebrant who is not a minister of religion, each of you says the section 45(2) vow to the other, in front of the celebrant and witnesses, "or words to that effect", with your full names and your choice of wife, husband or spouse. A minister of religion may use the form and ceremony of their religious body (s45(1)). Source: Marriage Act 1961, s45 (AustLII, read 23 November 2026).
- [ ] **2 witnesses over 18.** At least 2 people who are, or appear to the celebrant to be, over 18 are present as witnesses, from the monitum to the signing. Your celebrant must be physically present with the two of you and your 2 witnesses. Sources: Marriage Act 1961, s44; Attorney-General's Department "Get married" page (read 23 November 2026).
- [ ] **3 certificates signed.** On the day, 3 marriage certificates are signed by the two of you, your celebrant and your 2 witnesses. The Form 15 Certificate of Marriage is handed to you immediately after the ceremony. Sources: Attorney-General's Department "Get married" and "Your responsibilities as a marriage celebrant" pages.
- [ ] **Check every detail before anyone leaves.** Ask your celebrant to check every name and detail on the certificates while the two of you and your witnesses are still present. Errors can be corrected only then; once the marriage is registered, only the registry's authorised officer can correct them. Source: Attorney-General's Department "Your responsibilities as a marriage celebrant" page.
- [ ] **Lodging within 14 days.** Your celebrant must submit the marriage paperwork to the registry of births, deaths and marriages in the state or territory where you married, within 14 days of the marriage. For a name change or proof of marriage, apply for the official marriage certificate from that registry; your celebrant can apply on your behalf. Source: Attorney-General's Department "Get married" page.

## Questions to ask your celebrant
- [ ] **Wording.** Which exact monitum and vow wording will you use? Do you keep to the Act's text, or use "words to that effect"? Do you hold a section 46(2) exemption?
- [ ] **Personal vows beside the legal line.** Can we read our personal vows to you before the day, so you can confirm the legal line is intact? Do our vows go before or after the legal vow?
- [ ] **Acknowledgement of Country (if wanted).** Can you open the ceremony with an Acknowledgement of Country before the welcome?
- [ ] **The signing table.** Where will the signing table be, how long should we allow for the signing in the run sheet, and can you set a chair for an older witness? Who are our 2 witnesses, and do you need their full names and ages beforehand?
- [ ] **The overseas route.** If we are celebrating abroad, can you marry us legally in Australia with the short legal script before or after? (An Australian-authorised celebrant cannot perform a marriage overseas.) If we are overseas now, how do we give you the notice of intended marriage, and when can we book the separate in-person meetings with each of us on the trip home?
