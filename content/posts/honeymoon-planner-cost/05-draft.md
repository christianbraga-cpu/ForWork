The honeymoon planner cost you pay depends on two things: how that planner charges, and the trip you ask them to build. A number on its own tells you very little. The useful questions are what the fee covers, how it is worked out and whether it is agreed in writing before any work begins.

This guide sets out what you are paying a planner for, what shapes the fee, why the fee and the trip are two separate costs, and the questions to put to any planner before you agree. It closes with how the fee is set when you plan with Wedded Wonderland.

## Key takeaways

- The planner's fee and the cost of the trip itself are two separate things, so ask about each on its own terms.
- Ask any planner how they charge and whether they receive commissions from suppliers, and get the answer in writing.
- Season, length, distance, style of stay, private or shared experiences and the number of stops shape what the trip costs.
- With Wedded Wonderland, the fee is agreed in writing before any work begins.

## What you are paying a planner for

A planner's fee pays for their time and judgement. The trip itself (flights, stays, dinners, a boat for a day) is paid to the suppliers who provide it. Keep the two apart in your head from the first conversation and every quote becomes easier to read.

The time is real. Someone has to compare options, read each supplier's terms, line up flight arrivals with check-in times, hold dates while you decide and confirm the small requests that make a stay feel considered: the late check-out, the dietary note, the quiet table on the last night. On a honeymoon with several stops, each booking brings its own deposit, its own deadline and its own fine print.

Judgement is the other half. A planner who knows a region can tell you which island suits a slow week, which city works for three nights rather than five and where a transfer will eat half a day. That advice shapes the trip long before anything is booked.

What the fee does not buy is a different set of consumer rights. Australian consumer guarantees apply whether you book directly with an airline or tour operator, through a travel agent, or through a booking platform or package provider. <!-- fact_02 --> You give nothing up by handing the trip to someone else.

## What shapes the fee

A planner sets their own fee, so the only reliable figure is the one they put in writing for you. What you can do is understand what they will want to know before they quote, and ask how each point changes their number.

### How complex the itinerary is

A single stay with flights either side asks less of a planner than a journey across three countries with internal flights, a train and a private guide in each city. Length plays in too. Read our guide to [how long a honeymoon should be](https://weddedwonderland.com/how-long) before you settle on dates, because the number of nights you choose sets the number of moving parts.

### How much is booked and managed for you

Some couples want ideas and a shortlist, then book themselves. Others want every flight, stay, transfer and dinner booked and held in one itinerary. Ask the planner where their work starts and stops, whether changes after booking are part of the fee and whether anything is charged separately.

### Timing and how far ahead you start

Starting early gives a planner room to compare, hold and adjust. Starting late can narrow the choices and compress the work into a few weeks. Our guide on [when to go on honeymoon](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/a-guide-on-when-to-go-on-honeymoon) covers seasons by region, which helps you choose dates before you enquire.

## The fee and the trip are two different costs

When couples ask what a honeymoon planner costs, they are often asking two questions at once. One is the planner's fee. The other is the honeymoon: the flights, the stays, the experiences and everything paid to suppliers along the way. They move independently, and a clear quote shows each one separately.

If your real question is what the trip itself will cost in a given destination, start with our [guide to which destination suits your spend](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/luxury-honeymoon-budget-which-destination-suits-yours). It is the better place for that conversation.

### What moves the cost of the trip

These are the levers a planner will ask about first. Knowing your answers before you enquire makes the first conversation faster and the first quote closer to what you want.

- **Season.** The same stay can be a different proposition in peak months and in the shoulder weeks either side.
- **Length.** More nights means more of everything: accommodation, meals, experiences and days to fill.
- **Distance and flights.** How far you fly, how many legs it takes and the cabin you choose all count.
- **Style of stay.** A villa with its own pool and a room in a city hotel are different things to book.
- **Private or shared.** A private boat, guide or dinner is a different booking from a shared tour or a set menu.
- **Number of stops.** Each extra stop adds a transfer, a check-in and often a flight.

None of these is right or wrong. They are simply where your choices show up on the quote.

## How planners may charge, as questions to ask

Planners charge in different ways, and the only answer that matters is the one your planner gives you. Ask these questions plainly, and ask for the answers in writing.

- Do you charge a flat fee for planning?
- Is any part of your fee a percentage of the cost of the trip?
- Do you receive commissions from the suppliers you book, and will you tell us which?
- Is your fee a mix of these, and when is each part payable?
- What happens to your fee if we change our plans, or cancel?
- Is the quote in Australian dollars?

A good planner answers each of these without hesitation. If an answer is vague, ask again, and ask for it to be confirmed in writing before you pay anything.

### What the fee should cover, in writing

Before you commit, ask to see these set out on paper:

- the scope of the planning, and where it ends;
- what will be booked on your behalf;
- what is included in each booking;
- when each payment falls due, to the planner and to each supplier;
- the change and cancellation terms for the planner and for every supplier;
- who to contact while you are travelling.

This matters because two sets of terms apply. When you book through a travel agent or intermediary, both the intermediary's terms and the provider's terms apply, and agents are usually bound by the provider's terms. <!-- fact_05 --> If any terms are unclear or missing, ask the business to confirm them in writing, and note that different terms may apply to different travel periods. <!-- fact_06 --> The [ACCC's guidance on travel delays and cancellations](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/travel-delays-and-cancellations) sets out what you are entitled to if a provider delays or cancels.

### Paying in Australian dollars or another currency

A quote in Australian dollars lets you compare like with like. When a supplier asks to be paid in its own currency, the cost to you depends on the conversion. Providers choose the exchange rate they use and charge their own fees, and the total depends on how much you send, where and how. <!-- fact_19 -->

When you compare ways to pay, look at the exchange rate and whether it is the rate you actually get, whether it is guaranteed, the transfer fees and any margin on the conversion, and whether the receiver pays fees too. <!-- fact_20 --> Money transfer services in Australia must be registered with AUSTRAC, so check that before you use one. <!-- fact_21 --> [Moneysmart's guide to sending money overseas](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/sending-money-overseas) walks through each point.

### Protecting what you pay

As at November 2026, a few steps protect the money you put into a honeymoon.

Buy travel insurance early. Anything known before you buy a policy will not be covered if it directly affects your trip, and the earlier you buy, the more likely you are to be covered if something happens before you leave. <!-- fact_14 --> Read the Product Disclosure Statement before you buy, so you know what is covered and what is not. <!-- fact_10 --> [Smartraveller's travel insurance guidance](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) covers the rest.

Ask about insolvency. Travel provider or agent insolvency is commonly left out of cancellation cover, and only some agents hold insolvency insurance, so ask your planner directly. <!-- fact_17 -->

Think about how you pay. Some banks allow a chargeback, reversing a disputed card transaction, if you do not receive what you paid for, and you should not accept contract terms that ask you to give up chargeback rights. <!-- fact_18 -->

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a honeymoon planner cost?

Each planner sets their own fee, and it depends on how they charge and on the trip you want. Ask whether it is a flat fee, a percentage of the trip, supplier commissions or a mix, what it covers and when it is payable, and get all of it in writing before any work begins.

### Do travel agents charge fees?

Ask the agent directly. Ask whether there is a planning or booking fee, whether they receive commissions from suppliers, whether any fee is refundable and what happens to it if your plans change. Then ask for the answers in writing.

### Is the planner's fee part of the honeymoon cost?

It is part of what you spend on the honeymoon, but it is a separate cost from the trip. Ask for the planner's fee and the cost of flights, stays and experiences to be shown separately, so you can see each one clearly.

### What should a planner's fee include?

The scope of the planning, what will be booked for you, the inclusions in each booking, payment dates, change and cancellation terms, and who to contact while you travel. If any of these is missing from what the planner sends you, ask for it in writing.

### Will the quote be in Australian dollars?

Ask before you compare. A quote in Australian dollars is simpler to set beside another. If a supplier is paid in another currency, ask what exchange rate applies, whether it is guaranteed and what fees or margins sit on top.
