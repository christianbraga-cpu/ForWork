## Key takeaways

- The planner's fee and the cost of the trip itself are two separate things, so ask about each on its own terms.
- Ask any planner whether they charge a flat fee, a percentage of the trip, supplier commissions or a mix, and get the answer in writing.
- Season, length, distance, style of stay, exclusive use, privacy, private or shared experiences and the number of stops shape what the trip costs.
- Before you pay anything, have the scope, inclusions, payment dates and cancellation terms set out in writing.

## What you are paying a planner for

A planner's fee pays for their time and judgement. The trip itself (flights, stays, dinners, a boat for a day) is the cost of what the suppliers provide. Keep the two apart in your head from the first conversation and every quote becomes easier to read.

The time is real. A honeymoon means comparing options, reading each supplier's terms and lining up flight arrivals with check-in times. On a honeymoon with several stops, each booking brings its own deposit, its own deadline and its own fine print.

Judgement is the other half. Ask a planner how well they know the region you have in mind: which island suits a slow week, which city suits a short stay and where a long journey between stops would eat into your time.

What the fee does not buy is a different set of consumer rights. Australian consumer guarantees for travel services apply whether you book directly with an airline or tour operator, through a travel agent, or through a booking platform or package provider.

## What shapes the fee

A planner sets their own fee, so the only reliable figure is the one they put in writing for you. What you can do is understand what they will want to know before they quote, and ask how each point changes their number.

### How complex the itinerary is

A single stay with flights either side asks less of a planner than a journey across three countries with internal flights, a train and a private guide in each city. Length plays in too. Read our guide to [how long a honeymoon should be](https://weddedwonderland.com/how-long) before you settle on dates, because the number of nights you choose sets the number of moving parts.

### How much is booked and managed for you

Some couples want ideas and a shortlist, then book themselves. Others want every flight, stay, transfer and dinner booked and set out in one itinerary. Ask the planner where their work starts and stops, whether changes after booking are part of the fee and whether anything is charged separately.

### Timing and how far ahead you start

Starting early gives you and a planner room to compare and adjust. Starting late can narrow the choices. Our guide on [when to go on honeymoon](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/a-guide-on-when-to-go-on-honeymoon) covers seasons by region, which helps you choose dates before you enquire.

## The fee and the trip are two different costs

When couples ask what a honeymoon planner costs, they are often asking two questions at once. One is the planner's fee. The other is the honeymoon: the flights, the stays, the experiences and everything paid to suppliers along the way. They move independently, and a clear quote shows each one separately.

If your real question is what the trip itself will cost in a given destination, start with our [guide to which destination suits your spend](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/luxury-honeymoon-budget-which-destination-suits-yours). It is the better place for that conversation.

### What moves the cost of the trip

These are the levers a planner will ask about first. Knowing your answers before you enquire makes the first conversation faster and the first quote closer to what you want.

- **Season.** The same stay can be a different proposition in peak months and in the shoulder weeks either side.
- **Length.** More nights means more of everything: accommodation, meals, experiences and days to fill.
- **Distance and flights.** How far you fly, how many legs it takes and the cabin you choose all count.
- **Style of stay.** A villa with its own pool and a room in a city hotel are different things to book.
- **Exclusive use.** Ask what changes when you take a whole villa or venue to yourselves rather than sharing it with other guests.
- **Privacy.** Ask how privacy is arranged, from a secluded suite to a private dining space, and where that shows on the quote.
- **Private or shared.** A private boat, guide or dinner is a different booking from a shared tour or a set menu.
- **Number of stops.** Each extra stop adds a check-in and often a flight.

None of these is right or wrong. They are simply where your choices show up on the quote. For ideas, browse our [honeymoon packages](https://weddedwonderland.com/destination-honeymoon-packages).

## How planners may charge, as questions to ask

Planners charge in different ways, and the only answer that matters is the one your planner gives you. Ask these questions plainly, and ask for the answers in writing.

- Do you charge a flat fee for planning?
- Is any part of your fee a percentage of the cost of the trip?
- Do you receive commissions from the suppliers you book, and will you tell us which?
- Does any supplier price you quote include a mark-up, and can we see the supplier's own price?
- Is your fee a mix of these, and when is each part payable?
- If you are also planning our wedding, is the honeymoon quoted with the wedding planning or separately?
- What happens to your fee if we change our plans, or cancel?
- Is the quote in Australian dollars?

A good planner answers each of these without hesitation. If an answer is vague, ask again, and ask for it to be confirmed in writing before you pay anything.

### What the fee should cover, in writing

Before you commit, ask to see these set out on paper:

- the scope of the planning, and where it ends;
- what will be booked on your behalf;
- what is included in each booking;
- when each payment falls due, to the planner and to each supplier;
- the change and cancellation terms for the planner and for every supplier;
- who to contact while you are travelling.

This matters because two sets of terms apply. When you book through a travel agent or intermediary, both the intermediary's terms and the provider's terms apply, and agents are usually bound by the provider's terms. If any terms are unclear or missing, ask the business to confirm them in writing, and note that different terms may apply to different travel periods. The [ACCC's guidance on travel delays and cancellations](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/travel-delays-and-cancellations) sets out what you are entitled to if a provider delays or cancels.

### Paying in Australian dollars or another currency

A quote in Australian dollars lets you compare like with like. If a supplier is paid in its own currency, providers choose the exchange rate they use and charge their own fees, and the total depends on how much you send, where and how. Before you pay, check:

- the exchange rate, whether it is the rate you actually get and whether it is guaranteed;
- the transfer fees, any margin on the conversion and whether the receiver pays fees too;
- that the money transfer service is registered with AUSTRAC, as it must be in Australia.

[Moneysmart's guide to sending money overseas](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/sending-money-overseas) walks through each point.

### Protecting what you pay

As at November 2026, three steps protect the money you put into a honeymoon:

- **Insure early.** Anything known before you buy a policy will not be covered if it directly affects your trip, and the earlier you buy, the more likely you are to be covered if something happens before you leave. Read the Product Disclosure Statement first, so you know what is covered. [Smartraveller's travel insurance guidance](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) covers the rest.
- **Ask about insolvency.** Travel provider or agent insolvency is commonly left out of cancellation cover, and only some agents hold insolvency insurance, so ask your planner directly.
- **Keep chargeback rights.** Some banks allow a chargeback if you do not receive what you paid for, so do not accept contract terms that ask you to give them up.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a honeymoon planner cost?

There is no single price, because planners charge in different ways and the trip shapes the work. Compare what each fee covers. Ask each planner whether it is a flat fee, a percentage of the trip, supplier commissions or a mix, and when it is payable, and get all of it in writing before any work begins.

### Do travel agents charge fees?

Ask the agent directly. Ask whether there is a planning or booking fee, whether they receive commissions from suppliers, whether any fee is refundable and what happens to it if your plans change. Then ask for the answers in writing.

### Is the planner's fee part of the honeymoon cost?

It is part of what you spend on the honeymoon, but it is a separate cost from the trip. Ask for the planner's fee and the cost of flights, stays and experiences to be shown separately, so you can see each one clearly.

### What should a planner's fee include?

The scope of the planning, what will be booked for you, the inclusions in each booking, payment dates, change and cancellation terms, and who to contact while you travel. If any of these is missing from what the planner sends you, ask for it in writing.

### Will the quote be in Australian dollars?

Ask before you compare. A quote in Australian dollars is simpler to set beside another. If a supplier is paid in another currency, ask what exchange rate applies, whether it is guaranteed and what fees or margins sit on top.
