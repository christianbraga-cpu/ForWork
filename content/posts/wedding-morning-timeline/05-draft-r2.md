# Wedding Morning Timeline for the Getting Ready Hours

A wedding morning timeline is built backwards from the ceremony: fix the time you leave, then the time you must be dressed, then when the photographer arrives, and start the first hair and makeup chair early enough to finish everyone before that. For a 4pm ceremony, for example, that can mean hair and makeup from 9am with a first look or from 10am without one.

For the rest of the day, see [how to build your full wedding day timeline](/expert-advice/wedding-day-timeline).

## Key takeaways

- Start from the ceremony time and work backwards through travel, photos and dressing to find when the first chair must begin.
- The number of people in the chairs, and the pace your stylist and artist give you at the trial, set the start time.
- The person getting married sits last or close to last, so their hair and makeup are freshest for the photos.
- Put the buffer before dressing and before you leave for the ceremony, not spread thinly across the morning.

## How a wedding morning timeline works

The ceremony is the one fixed point. Work back from it in this order:

1. **The ceremony.** Guests arrive before it, and you are out of sight before they do.
2. **Travel.** From where you dress to where you marry, allowing for a slow lift or a long walk.
3. **Photos before the ceremony.** Portraits with a first look, or getting-ready photos only without one.
4. **Dressed and ready.** Dress or suit on, jewellery in place, final touches done.
5. **The chairs.** Hair and makeup for everyone, finished before dressing begins.

The start of the first chair is the last number you work out, not the first.

## Getting ready photos and when the photographer arrives

### Detail photos while the last chair finishes

Book the photographer to arrive while the final chair is still in progress. That leaves time for the details (rings, invitation suite, shoes, fragrance, florals) and candid frames of the room before anyone is dressed. In our 4pm example, that is 12.30pm with a first look and 1.30pm without one.

### Give dressing its own block

Gowns with buttons, corsetry or a long train need two people who have practised with them, and the photographer will want to be in the room. Give dressing its own block on the timeline, with the final touch-ups straight after.

## The order of the hair and makeup chairs

With one stylist and one artist, two chairs run side by side: while one person is in hair, another is in makeup, then they swap.

### Who goes first and who goes last

Start with anyone who has to leave early, anyone with the simplest look, and anyone whose hair needs time to set. Children sit mid-morning, once fed and settled.

The person getting married finishes last or close to last, so their makeup is freshest for the photos and they are not sitting fully styled for hours. Some teams prefer them second to last, so that if the morning runs over, a member of the wedding party waits rather than the couple. Ask your stylist and artist which order suits them.

### Stylists and artists for the number of people

Add up who needs hair, who needs makeup and who needs both, then ask your stylist and artist to work back from your dressed-by time. If their answer starts earlier than anyone would like, the choice is an earlier start, or a second stylist or artist so more chairs run at once.

## How long bridal hair and makeup takes

It depends on the look, the hair and the person. Skin preparation, lashes, a veil or a hairpiece each add to the chair, and a low bun on fine hair is a different job from curls on long, thick hair. Plan around your own team's figures, not a general one.

### Why your trial sets the pace

Time your trial, then ask your stylist and artist how long they will allow for you and for each person in the wedding party on the day, and whether that differs from the trial. Get their figures in writing and build the morning on them. ## Dressing, final touches and the buffer

A buffer belongs at the handovers, where one part of the morning waits on another:

- **Before the dress goes on.** For example, 15 minutes between the last chair and dressing. This is the block that runs late first.
- **Before you leave for the ceremony.** Time to find the bouquet and wait for the lift.
- **Around a first look.** A few quiet minutes either side of it.

Ask whether your artist stays for touch-ups or leaves a small kit, and give the lipstick and powder to one person who will be beside you.

## Your partner's morning

Your partner's side needs its own timeline for grooming, dressing and photos. With one photographer, the sides are photographed one after the other, so agree the order. Hand the rings to a named person early.

## Setting up the getting ready space

- **Light.** A large window with soft daylight, with the chairs beside it.
- **Room to work.** Space for two chairs, the kit, a rail for outfits and a corner where the photographer can step back.
- **Food and water.** Breakfast on arrival, something easy to eat later, and water by every chair.
- **One timekeeper.** A friend who holds the schedule, calls the next person and keeps the room calm.

**With a first look, for example:**

- **9am.** Hair and makeup begins.
- **12.30pm.** The photographer arrives for details.
- **1pm.** Getting-ready photos while the last touches are done.
- **1.45pm.** Dressed and ready, with a quiet quarter hour to eat something.
- **2pm.** First look, then couple portraits close to where you are getting ready.
- **2.45pm.** Wedding party photos.
- **3.15pm.** Any family photos you are happy to take before the ceremony.
- **3.30pm.** The couple goes out of sight. Guests arrive and are seated.
- **4pm.** The ceremony begins.

**Without a first look, for example:**

- **10am.** Hair and makeup begins.
- **1.30pm.** The photographer arrives for details and getting-ready photos, each side separately.
- **2.30pm.** Dressed. Individual portraits and photos with each half of the wedding party.
- **3.30pm.** Out of sight. Guests arrive and are seated.
- **4pm.** The ceremony begins.

## How the morning moves for earlier and later ceremonies

As examples:

- **A 2pm ceremony.** Hair and makeup from 7.30am, first look at 12pm, guests arriving at 1.30pm. Eat breakfast before the first chair.
- **A 3pm ceremony.** Hair and makeup from 8.30am, first look at 1pm if you want one, guests arriving at 2.30pm.
- **A 5pm ceremony.** Hair and makeup from 11am, first look at 3pm, guests arriving at 4.30pm. Plan a proper lunch so the morning does not drift.

## Destination wedding mornings

Away from home, more of the morning belongs to the venue. Ask:

- **Heat and humidity.** Will the suite be cooled from the first chair, and where will you wait once dressed? Talk to your artist about [keeping makeup in place in humid weather](/style/how-to-make-wedding-makeup-last-in-humid-weather). For an Australian venue, check the [Bureau of Meteorology forecast](https://www.bom.gov.au/) in the days before.
- **The suite or villa as base.** What time can you get in on the morning, and is there room for everyone, their outfits and the photographer?
- **Distance to the ceremony site.** How long is the walk or drive, and does it involve stairs, sand or a slope in a long gown?
- **The light.** For daylight getting-ready photos or an outdoor first look, check the sun at your venue on your date. [Geoscience Australia's sunrise, sunset and twilight calculator](https://geodesyapps.ga.gov.au/sunrise) works these times out using the National Gazetteer of Australia. <!-- fact_01 --> You can search a place name or enter a latitude and longitude, including places outside Australia, choose a time zone, and see a single day or a whole year. <!-- fact_02 -->

## When the ceremony is in the morning

The method is the same, with less room in it. Take the 2pm example, where the first chair begins at 7.30am. Move that ceremony to 11am with the same people, the same team and a first look, and every time in the morning moves three hours earlier with it.

Rather than starting that early, change the shape of the morning and agree it with your team well ahead: a second stylist or artist so more chairs run at once, fewer people in the chairs or simpler looks, and portraits after the ceremony instead of a first look, which in our 4pm examples lets the chairs start an hour later. Check sunrise with the calculator above, as the first chairs may need lamps.

## Frequently asked questions

### How early do bridesmaids get ready?

Early enough for every chair to finish before dressing. Bridesmaids sit before the person getting married, so the first chair of the morning may be theirs: 9am in our 4pm example with a first look, or 10am without one.

### What time should hair and makeup start for a 4pm ceremony?

In our examples, 9am with a first look and 10am without one. Your own start depends on the number of people getting ready, so ask your stylist and artist to work back from your dressed-by time.

### Should the bride go first or last in hair and makeup?

Last, or close to last, so hair and makeup are freshest for the photos. Some teams place the person getting married second to last, so an overrun falls on someone else.

### What time should the photographer arrive on the wedding morning?

While the last chair is still in progress, leaving time for details before dressing. In our 4pm examples, that is 12.30pm with a first look and 1.30pm without one.

### How much buffer should you leave on the wedding morning?

Put it at the handovers: before the dress goes on, before you leave and around a first look. For example, leave 15 minutes between the last chair and dressing.
