Bali wedding venues fall into four areas: the clifftops of the Bukit and Uluwatu, the beaches of Nusa Dua and Jimbaran, the west coast at Seminyak, and the river valleys around Ubud. The right one depends on how many guests are flying in, the ceremony you want and the setting you want behind you as you say your vows.

Every venue here is a luxury property that hosts weddings and names its wedding spaces or packages, and each is set out on the same terms so you can compare them fairly. The list runs by area, not by rank. For the wider picture, from paperwork to travel, start with [planning a wedding in Bali](https://weddedwonderland.com/destinations/asia/bali).

## Key takeaways

- Choose the area first and the venue second, because a clifftop chapel, a Nusa Dua ballroom and an Ubud river lawn suit very different guest lists.
- Published capacities range from a 16-guest hidden room in Seminyak to garden and ballroom spaces for well over a thousand in Nusa Dua and at AYANA, so match the space to your numbers before you fall for the view.
- Only Samabe and COMO Uma Ubud publish a full resort buy-out, so ask every other venue in writing whether the property can be closed to other guests.
- Bali's wet season usually runs from around October to April, and Smartraveller advises a high degree of caution in Indonesia, so build weather and travel fallbacks into your plans.

## How do these Bali venues compare like for like?

Every venue here hosts weddings. Three questions then separate them: what kind of place it is, where the ceremony and dinner happen, and how many people it holds and houses.

### Clifftop resort, beach resort, private villa or valley retreat

Clifftop resorts such as AYANA and Bvlgari keep the ocean below you and spread the day across several named spaces. <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_10 --> Beach resorts such as Grand Hyatt and The St. Regis pair the sand with a ballroom, a useful pairing in a wet-season month. <!-- fact_110 --> <!-- fact_407 -->

Private-villa formats fold everything into one address. The Edge holds the ceremony at a villa and houses the couple in a one-bedroom and a five-bedroom villa, and Mandapa's three-bedroom pool villas host preparations, ceremony and cocktails. <!-- fact_601 --> <!-- fact_602 --> <!-- fact_508 --> In Ubud, the setting is valley and paddy rather than sea. <!-- fact_202 --> <!-- fact_503 -->

### Ceremony on site, and where dinner goes

Most venues here hold the ceremony in one space and the reception in another. AYANA publishes both figures for every space, and they differ: Tresna Chapel takes 70 for a ceremony but 50 for a reception. <!-- fact_06 --> <!-- fact_04 --> Plan the walk or the shuttle between spaces as part of the day.

### Rooms, buy-outs and your own suppliers

Few venues publish a buy-out. Samabe lists a buy-out of the entire resort, COMO Uma Ubud a resort buy-out for up to 100, and COMO Uma's Kemiri restaurant can be hired privately. <!-- fact_702 --> <!-- fact_201 --> <!-- fact_203 --> For every other venue, ask whether the wedding spaces, or the whole property, can be closed to other guests.

If you want the day to feel less like a resort package, Samabe offers customisable event packages with the freedom to bring your own preferred vendors. <!-- fact_704 --> Ask every venue the same: can we bring our own planner, stylist, florist and photographer, and is there an outside-vendor fee?

### Money and contracts: questions to ask each venue

Ask each venue for a written quote, then ask:

- Whose name is on the contract, and in what currency is it written and paid?
- How much deposit is due, when, and at what point does it become non-refundable?
- Is there a minimum spend or venue hire fee, and are service charge and tax included?
- What does the cancellation clause cover, including weather or volcanic disruption?
- When should travel insurance be in place relative to paying deposits, and what does the venue's own policy cover?

### Security and privacy: questions for any couple

- Can the ceremony space, or the property, be closed to other guests?
- How does security work during weddings, and alongside any security a family brings?
- What are the rules on drones, outside photographers and guests posting on social media?

Anything this guide marks "Not published", from capacity to a buy-out, needs an answer in writing before you commit, and your Wedded Advisor confirms inclusions in writing before you sign, through [Wedded Concierge](https://weddedwonderland.com/wonderland-concierge?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=content&utm_campaign=bali-wedding-venues&utm_content=primary).

## Can foreigners legally marry in Bali?

Yes, with conditions, and the legal steps sit apart from choosing a venue. As at November 2026, the [Australian Embassy in Indonesia](https://indonesia.embassy.gov.au/) sets out these points as a general guide. <!-- fact_300 -->

- **Religion:** Persons of any nationality may generally marry in Indonesia if they hold a religion recognised by the Indonesian Government, listed as Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism or Christian, and both parties must hold the same religion. <!-- fact_317 -->
- **Certificate of No Impediment:** Australians need one from the Embassy in Jakarta or a Consulate-General; the Consulate-General in Bali covers Bali. Civil Registry Offices do not normally accept one issued more than three months before. <!-- fact_303 --> <!-- fact_317 -->
- **Notice and registration:** For non-Muslim marriages, lodge a Notice of Intention to Marry with the Civil Registry Office at least 10 days before the wedding, and register the marriage there afterwards for it to be legal. <!-- fact_304 -->
- **Documents:** The CNI, passports, birth certificates, proof that any previous marriage has ended, and four 4x6 cm photos; requirements vary by province. <!-- fact_304 -->
- **At home:** A marriage legally performed overseas that would have been legal in Australia is generally recognised, and you cannot register it in Australia. <!-- fact_305 --> <!-- fact_306 -->

You cannot marry at the Embassy or a Consulate-General. <!-- fact_303 --> For specific requirements, including any fees, contact the nearest Indonesian Embassy or Consulate-General, or the Civil Registry Office (Catatan Sipil) where you will marry, and talk to a lawyer to confirm validity. <!-- fact_300 --> <!-- fact_306 --> If you want a symbolic ceremony in Bali, plan the legal marriage separately; The Edge's blessing, for one, is non-legal. <!-- fact_603 -->
