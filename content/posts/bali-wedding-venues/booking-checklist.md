# Booking checklist: Bali wedding venues

These details are not published by the venues, or the published figures conflict, so they don't block publishing. The Wedded Advisor confirms each one in writing with the venue for the couple's dates before anyone commits. The article has no `[[VERIFY]]` markers in the copy; the only publishing blockers are image credits and licences (6 slots). No prices or amounts are stated here or in the article.

- **The Mulia glass chapel capacity.** The figure found is a package label, not a room capacity. Ask for the real ceremony capacity and a seating plan, the chapel's correct name (floating glass chapel or Harmony Chapel), and whether chapel weddings continue after December 2026.
- **The Apurva Kempinski Bali, Kimaya Chapel.** The page text and the capacity table give different figures (20 and 50 theatre). Confirm the ceremony capacity, and the reception spaces that pair with the chapel.
- **The St. Regis Bali Amphitheatre.** The wedding text and the capacity chart give different theatre figures (80 and 150). Confirm the ceremony figure.

- **Mandapa Temple Lawn and Ayung Riverside (Kubu Riverside).** The venue section and package text give different figures (Temple Lawn 100 seated vs up to 150; Riverside 100 seated vs up to 30). Confirm both.
- **Bvlgari Resort Bali.** Ceremony capacity, the chapel, reception spaces, villa count and exclusive use are not published on the pages read.
- **Jumeirah Bali.** The page read is a couple story. Request the official list of ceremony spaces, capacities, villa count and exclusive use.
- **The Edge Bali.** Location and outlook are not published. The blessing is non-legal: confirm what the couple must arrange separately for a legal marriage.
- **Raffles Bali.** Capacity beyond the Presidential Villa (30) is not published; with 32 villas, ask whether the guest list can fill the resort.

## General (ask every venue, in writing)

- Ceremony type: legal ceremony, religious ceremony or symbolic blessing only, and whether the venue can host the officiant and Civil Registry formalities
- Exclusive use: the wedding spaces only, one wedding per day, or the whole property; from what time; whether a buy-out is offered and the minimum nights
- Deposits: amount due to secure the date, when it is due, and the point at which it becomes non-refundable
- Currency: whose name is on the contract, and the currency it is written and paid in
- Minimum spend or venue hire fee, and whether service charge and tax are included
- Cancellation and postponement terms, including weather, flooding and volcanic disruption
- Insurance: when the couple's travel or event insurance should be in place relative to paying deposits, and the venue's own cover
- Step-free access: a step-free route or buggy between arrival, rooms, ceremony and dinner spaces
- Security and privacy: closing the space or property to other guests, security alongside any a family brings, rules on drones, outside photographers and guests posting on social media
- Wet-season plan: a named indoor or covered fallback for every outdoor ceremony and reception space, and when the call is made
- Outside suppliers: planner, stylist, florist and photographer allowed, and any outside-vendor fee
- Music: the time amplified music must stop outdoors and indoors
- Guest transport: airport transfers, journey time at the guests' arrival time, moves between ceremony and reception, and what happens if roads flood

## Legal marriage: questions for the Australian Embassy and the Civil Registry

Ask the Australian Embassy in Jakarta or the Consulate-General in Bali:

- How do we apply for a Certificate of No Impediment, and how long does it take?
- Which office issues it for a Bali wedding, and what documents and appointments are needed?
- Do we need to book an appointment, and what are the current fees?
- Confirm the marriage will generally be recognised in Australia, and what record we should keep, since it cannot be registered in Australia.

Ask the Civil Registry Office (Catatan Sipil) where the wedding will take place, and the Indonesian Embassy or Consulate-General:

- Do both parties need to hold the same recognised religion, and what proof of religion is accepted?
- How far before the wedding must the Notice of Intention to Marry be lodged (the Embassy guide says at least 10 days), and in person or by an agent?
- Will you accept a CNI issued more than three months before the wedding?
- Which documents are needed (CNI, passports, birth certificates, proof that any previous marriage has ended, photos), and do they need translation, legalisation or apostille?
- Who performs the religious ceremony, and who attends to register the marriage afterwards?
- Can registration happen at the venue or only at your office, and how long until the marriage certificate is issued?
- What are your current fees?
- Talk to a lawyer to confirm the marriage's validity.
