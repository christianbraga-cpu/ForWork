# Wedding Guest List Template for Google Sheets and Excel

A wedding guest list template is a spreadsheet with one row per guest, grouped by household, that holds contact details, invitation dates, replies for each event, meals and needs in one place. Ours downloads as an Excel file with no sign-up, opens in Google Sheets as well, and carries the travel columns a destination wedding needs: flights, arrival and departure, accommodation and visa letters.

## Key takeaways

- The template downloads as an Excel file with no sign-up and opens in Excel or imports straight into Google Sheets.
- Give every guest their own row and tie the people who share an invitation together with one Household ID.
- Eight destination columns track where guests are flying from, their flights, dates, accommodation, transport and visa letters.

## Columns in the wedding guest list template

Each group heading in row 4 of the Guest list tab matches a section below, with Notes as the last column. Column names are exactly as they appear in row 5.

### Guest

- **Household ID.** One code for everyone who shares an invitation, such as H001. It lets the Summary tab count households as well as people, which is the number your stationer needs.
- **First name** and **Last name.** Kept apart so you can sort by family name and find anyone quickly.
- **Envelope name.** How the invitation is addressed, which is not always how you say someone's name. Write it once per household.
- **Side.** Partner A, Partner B or Both. Useful for counting, never for seating rules.
- **List (optional).** A or B, for couples who choose to invite in rounds. If you are sending every invitation at once, leave it empty.
- **Age group.** Adult, Teen, Child or Infant. The Summary tab gives children and infants a count of their own, which matters for meals and seats.

### Contact

- **Email** and **Phone.** For the messages that come after the invitation: travel updates, a change of time, a reminder.
- **Street**, **City**, **State or region**, **Postcode** and **Country.** Split into five columns so the list sorts cleanly into address labels and works for guests in any country.

### Invitations

- **Save the date sent** and **Invitation sent.** Both take a date, not a tick. When a cousin says nothing ever arrived, you know exactly when it went, and the Summary tab counts every date entered.

### Events

- **Welcome dinner**, **Ceremony**, **Reception** and **Recovery brunch.** One column per event, because not every guest is invited to everything. Each cell takes Not invited, Invited, Attending or Declined.

Invited means the invitation has gone and no reply has come in yet. For deadlines and follow-ups, our guide to [managing wedding RSVPs](/expert-advice/wedding-rsvp) covers the process from invitation to final numbers.

### Needs

- **Plus one name.** A name, not a yes. If the plus-one is a person you will seat and feed, they need a name before the place card does.
- **Meal choice.** The course or menu option the guest picked.
- **Dietary needs and allergies.** Kept apart from meal choice on purpose. A preference changes a plate; an allergy changes how the kitchen works.
- **Access needs.** Steps, seating, quiet space, anything that helps a guest enjoy the day.
- **Table.** Filled in last, once replies settle. Our [wedding seating chart](/expert-advice/wedding-seating-chart) guide takes it from there.

### After the day

- **Gift** and **Thank you sent.** What arrived and from whom, then Yes once the note has gone. Months later, this is the column you will be grateful for.

### Notes

- **Notes.** Anything that doesn't fit elsewhere: a guest's role in the wedding party, a name pronunciation, a reminder to call rather than email.

## Destination wedding columns

The Destination travel group is what a local reception list never needs. When guests are crossing borders for you, the questions change from "are you coming?" to "how, when and where are you staying?"

- **Travelling from.** City and country. It shows at a glance how far each household is coming and which guests share a route.
- **Flights booked.** Yes or No. The Summary tab counts the yeses, and filtering the column for No shows who still has a seat to find.
- **Arrival date** and **Departure date.** Together they tell you who will be there for the welcome dinner and who is staying on for the recovery brunch.
- **Accommodation.** Where each guest is staying, in their own words: a hotel, a villa, with family.
- **Transport to venue needed.** Yes or No, so you have the number of people who will need a way to reach the venue once you start planning that part of the weekend.
- **Visa letter needed** and **Visa letter sent.** Mark Yes when a guest asks you for a letter for their visa application, then Yes again once you have sent it. The Summary tab counts the letters still to send.

Visa rules belong to each guest. Every guest should get the right visa for the countries they are visiting or transiting through, and check with the embassy or consulate of the destination before they travel, as [Smartraveller advises](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) (general information as at November 2026). <!-- fact_04 --> Send that reminder with the save the date, not the invitation.

The welcome dinner has its own column under Events because its numbers are separate from the wedding. A guest landing the morning of the ceremony can be Attending for one and Declined for the other.

## Filling it in by household

Start with households, then people. Give each household its ID, add one row per person under it, and write the envelope name on the first row only.

Settle the plus-one and children rule once, before the first row, and type it into the cell for it on the How to use tab so anyone helping works to the same rule. The reasoning behind those rules, and how to trim a list that has grown, lives in the same how-to guide linked at the top of this page.

## Privacy and sharing with helpers

A guest list is a small database of other people's lives. Names, addresses, phone numbers and dates of birth all count as [personal information](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/your-personal-information/what-is-personal-information), and so does sensitive information. <!-- fact_01 --> Dietary and access columns can reveal a good deal about someone's health, so treat them with care.

The Privacy Act doesn't cover individuals acting in a personal capacity, but [state or territory laws](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/social-media-and-online-privacy/photos-and-videos) may still apply in some situations. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_03 --> This is general information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

A few habits keep the file in good hands:

- Share it only with the people who need it, and give view-only access to anyone who doesn't need to edit.
- For a parent chasing one side of the family, copy the names and reply columns into a separate file rather than handing over every address.
- The template has no passport column, and it doesn't need one. If a visa letter calls for passport details, keep them out of the shared file.
- After the thank you notes have gone, delete the file or move it somewhere only the two of you can open.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you add your wedding party to your guest list?

Yes. Everyone who will be at an event needs a row, wedding party included, because they eat, sit and travel like every other guest. Note their role in the Notes column.

### How do you make a wedding guest list in Excel?

Put one guest per row, with a Household ID to group families, and give each event its own column. Add dropdowns for replies with data validation, freeze the header row and count replies with a formula. The template above has all of this built in, so you can start typing.

### What should you use for a wedding guest list?

A spreadsheet suits the early months, when names are still being added and removed. Once replies are coming in, a planning app that collects them for you saves the retyping. You can use both: the spreadsheet for drafting, the app once invitations go out.

### Should a guest list have one row per person or per household?

One row per person, grouped by a Household ID. You count meals, seats and flights by person, and invitations and envelopes by household. The template does both.

### Can I use this template in Google Sheets and Excel?

Yes. It is an Excel file, so it opens in Excel as it is. For Google Sheets, open a spreadsheet, choose File, then Import, and select the file, then check a dropdown to make sure it came across. <!-- fact_05 -->

### What extra columns does a destination wedding guest list need?

Travelling from, flights booked, arrival and departure dates, accommodation, transport to the venue, and visa letter needed and sent. A separate welcome dinner column under Events helps too, because its numbers differ from the wedding's.
