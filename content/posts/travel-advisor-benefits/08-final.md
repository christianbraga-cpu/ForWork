You can book a flight in minutes. A destination wedding is something else: flights, villas, a venue, a photographer, guests arriving from three cities and deposits due in two currencies. The real travel advisor benefits sit there. A good advisor is one person keeping all of it in step, with quotes in Australian dollars, the fee agreed in writing before any work begins, and every inclusion confirmed in writing before you pay. Each piece is possible on your own. Together, they are harder and slower to arrange alone, and the work lands on you at the busiest point of the year.

This guide covers what an advisor does, the six things they make easier, what you can comfortably book yourself, and the questions to ask any advisor before you sign.

## Key takeaways

- A good travel advisor gives you one point of contact across flights, stays and the wedding itself.
- Ask for quotes in Australian dollars and for every inclusion in writing before you commit.
- Plenty of couples book their own flights and stays, but the harder parts are guests, many suppliers and payments in several currencies.
- Before you agree, ask any advisor about their fee in writing, inclusions, cancellation terms, insolvency cover and travel insurance.

## What does a travel advisor actually do?

A travel advisor plans and books travel on your behalf. For a honeymoon, that might mean flights, two or three stays and a few private experiences stitched into one itinerary. For a destination wedding, the list grows: the venue's dates, accommodation for you and your guests, transport between them and the suppliers who make the day work.

The value is in coordination. Each booking has its own deposit, its own deadline and its own terms. An advisor holds those threads as your single point of contact, so they line up.

What an advisor does not change is your legal footing, which the terms section below explains.

## Six ways an advisor makes a wedding trip easier

Each of these is harder or slower to arrange alone. They are the jobs that take the most hours when you do them yourself, done by someone whose job it is.

### One point of contact from flights to the ceremony

When you book alone, you are the switchboard. The airline, the hotel, the venue coordinator and the florist each hold one piece, and only you see the whole picture.

A good advisor becomes that single contact. Instead of six separate email threads, you have one person to ask about every booking, from flights to the ceremony.

If the ceremony moves by a day, you tell one person, not six. The person planning your travel, vendors and payments hears it once, from you, rather than in six separate emails.

### Quotes in Australian dollars

Overseas suppliers quote in their own currency, and the number you see is rarely the number that leaves your account. Providers choose the exchange rate they use and charge fees, and the cost depends on how much you send, where and how. <!-- fact_19 -->

Ask a good advisor to quote in AUD, so you can compare the venue, the villa and the flights side by side on one plan. It turns a spreadsheet of euros, dollars and rupiah into one figure you can actually plan around.

Ask what exchange rate an AUD quote uses, and whether you can see each supplier's price in its own currency alongside it. Rates, fees and margins all shape the final figure. <!-- fact_20 -->

### Inclusions confirmed in writing before you commit

"Wedding package" can mean almost anything. One venue's package covers the ceremony set-up and a coordinator on the day. Another covers neither.

A good advisor confirms exactly what is included, in writing, before you pay a deposit. That habit lines up with official advice: check the terms of the service, and of your agent if you use one, before you book, and if anything is unclear, ask the provider to confirm it in writing. <!-- fact_06 -->

### Someone working in your time zone

Planning a wedding in Europe from Sydney or Perth means waking to replies sent at midnight your time, and answering them before the other side has gone to bed. A week of back and forth can cover one small question.

An advisor working in your time zone means you talk at a reasonable hour, rather than waiting on replies sent while you sleep.

### Venue, travel, vendors and payments planned together

These four pieces depend on each other. The venue fixes the date. The date sets flights and room nights. The suppliers follow the venue, and the payments follow everything.

Planned separately, a deposit can fall due before the dates are firm, or a supplier can be confirmed for a venue that has since changed. Planned together, each decision is made with the others in view.

### Both sets of terms, read before you pay

When you book through an agent, both the agent's terms and each provider's terms apply. Agents are usually bound by the provider's terms, so legally they may only need to pass on the remedy the provider offers. <!-- fact_05 --> A good advisor walks you through both sets before you book, and confirms anything unclear in writing. <!-- fact_06 -->

Booking through an advisor does not give you stronger rights, and it does not take any away. The consumer guarantees are the same whether you book direct, through an agent or through a booking platform. <!-- fact_02 --> As at November 2026, the ACCC says those guarantees give you rights if a travel service is delayed or cancelled. <!-- fact_01 --> They cover travel within Australia, travel departing Australia with international businesses, and travel to Australia booked through a provider's Australian website. <!-- fact_01 --> The [ACCC's guidance on travel delays and cancellations](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/travel-delays-and-cancellations) sets this out in full.

Providers do not have to offer a refund or replacement if you change your mind or miss a service for reasons unrelated to it. In those cases, it generally depends on the booking's terms. <!-- fact_04 -->

In short: your rights are the same either way, and whether you get money back usually depends on who makes the change and the terms you agreed to. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_16 -->

## What you can book yourself, and where it gets harder

Many couples book their own flights and their own honeymoon stays, and do it well. If the trip is just the two of you, a handful of bookings and one currency, you may not need an advisor at all.

The weight shifts when the trip becomes a wedding. A few questions help you see where you sit:

- **Who is tracking your guests?** Arrival dates, room allocations, dietary notes and the cousin who changes flights twice.
- **What about the guests who need more care?** Picture guests flying in from three cities and two grandparents who need an easy walk to the ceremony. Who checks each arrival time, how does everyone get from the airport to the venue, and do the rooms and the venue suit older or less mobile guests?
- **How many suppliers are involved?** Each one has its own deposit date, cancellation terms and contact person. Do you have a single view of all of them?
- **How many currencies are you paying in?** Each transfer has a rate, a fee and possibly a margin, and the advertised rate may not be the rate you get. <!-- fact_20 -->
- **Who reads the fine print?** Travel agents and booking sites have their own terms, which are the first port of call for a refund or credit, and those terms remain subject to Australian Consumer Law. <!-- fact_15 -->
- **What happens if plans change?** Whether you get money back usually comes down to who makes the change. If the provider cancels, you are more likely, though not always, to be eligible for a refund or credit. If you change plans, it may be treated as change of mind. <!-- fact_16 -->

If most of those answers are "me", that is where one point of contact for venue, travel, vendors and payments helps most.

## Questions to ask before you hire an advisor

These apply to any advisor, whoever you speak with. Ask them early, and ask for the answers in writing.

1. **How is your fee charged, and will you put it in writing before you start?** You should know what you are paying for before any work begins.
2. **Do you receive commissions or mark-ups from the suppliers you book, and will you tell us?** It helps to know how everyone in the chain is paid.
3. **What exactly is included in each quote?** Ask for inclusions in writing for every booking, not a summary.
4. **What are your terms, and the terms of each provider?** Both apply when you book through an agent, so ask to see both, including what happens if a provider cancels, if you cancel, or if the booking cannot proceed for reasons outside anyone's control. <!-- fact_05 --> <!-- fact_06 -->
5. **Do you hold insolvency insurance?** Travel provider or agent insolvency is commonly excluded from cancellation cover, and only a few insurers cover it. Insolvency insurance is not compulsory for agents, so only some hold it. <!-- fact_17 -->
6. **How are overseas payments made?** Ask about the exchange rate, whether it is guaranteed, and any transfer fees. <!-- fact_20 --> Money transfer services in Australia must be registered with AUSTRAC, so check that any transfer business involved is registered. If a transfer goes wrong, contact the bank or business first, then AFCA if they are a member. <!-- fact_21 --> [Moneysmart's guide to sending money overseas](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/sending-money-overseas) explains what to compare.
7. **Can I pay by credit card?** Some banks allow a chargeback if you do not receive what you paid for, and you should not accept contract terms that ask you to give up chargeback rights. <!-- fact_18 -->
8. **Do you belong to an industry accreditation scheme, and what does it require?** Which scheme is it, what does membership ask of them, and who would you go to with a complaint?
9. **When should we buy travel insurance?** The earlier you buy it, the more likely you are to be covered if something unexpected happens before the trip, and anything known before you buy will not be covered if it directly affects your travel. <!-- fact_14 --> The right policy is as essential as a passport, and the Australian Government cannot pay your bills overseas. <!-- fact_09 --> Read the Product Disclosure Statement, choose a policy that covers every country on the route including stopovers, and declare all pre-existing conditions upfront. <!-- fact_10 --> <!-- fact_11 --> <!-- fact_12 --> If you rely on credit card insurance, confirm what it covers and how to activate it, and do not assume it is already active. <!-- fact_13 --> The [Smartraveller travel insurance guide](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) covers the rest.

## When the trip is a wedding and a honeymoon

A destination wedding is rarely one trip. It is the wedding week, with guests arriving and leaving on their own schedules, and then the honeymoon, often in a different country with a different set of bookings. The benefits above multiply: more suppliers, more currencies, more dates that need to agree with one another.

It helps to plan both halves at once. The wedding fixes the dates and the first base. The honeymoon picks up from there, ideally with flights booked as one journey rather than as separate trips stitched together later.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a travel advisor do?

A travel advisor plans and books travel on your behalf, and coordinates the separate bookings so dates, deposits and terms line up. For a destination wedding, that can extend to the venue, guest accommodation and suppliers.

### Is a travel advisor worth it for a destination wedding?

It depends on how much there is to hold together. If you have guests, several suppliers and payments in more than one currency, an advisor saves you the coordination. If it is a simple trip for two, you may manage comfortably yourself. Your consumer guarantees are the same either way. <!-- fact_02 -->

### Can I book some things myself and use an advisor for the rest?

Yes. Many couples book their own flights or honeymoon stays and hand over the wedding pieces. Tell your advisor what you have booked, so dates and deposits can be planned around it.

### Do travel advisors quote in Australian dollars?

Ask before you start. Overseas suppliers charge in their own currency, and the exchange rate and fees vary between providers. <!-- fact_19 --> A quote in AUD, with the inclusions in writing, lets you compare options on the same footing.

### What should I ask before hiring a travel advisor?

Ask how the fee is charged and whether it is in writing, what each quote includes, what the cancellation and change terms are for the advisor and each provider, whether they hold insolvency insurance, and how overseas payments are made. <!-- fact_05 --> <!-- fact_17 --> <!-- fact_20 --> Then arrange travel insurance early. <!-- fact_14 -->
