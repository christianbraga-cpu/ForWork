Getting married in Italy as an Australian takes two documents, two countries and a sequence that has to run in the right order. Most guides online are written for British or American couples and talk about a Certificate of No Impediment, which Italy will not accept from you.

This is the Australian version, as at October 2026: what to prepare at home, what to sign in Rome or Milan, what happens at the town hall, and where the marriage stands once you are home. For other nationalities, see [our general legal guide for couples of every nationality](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/getting-married-in-italy-the-complete-legal-guide-for-destination-couples).

## Key takeaways

- Australians need two documents to marry in Italy: an Atto Notorio from the Italian consulate for your state, and a Nulla Osta sworn in person at the Australian Embassy in Rome or the Consulate-General in Milan. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_17 -->
- Order certificates and apostilles early, because DFAT publishes no set turnaround and the Atto Notorio, signed with four unrelated witnesses, cannot be issued more than six months before the wedding. <!-- fact_24 --> <!-- fact_06 --> <!-- fact_07 -->
- In Italy the order is fixed: sign the Nulla Osta, legalise it at a Prefettura, then lodge your declaration at the comune before the ceremony. <!-- fact_26 --> <!-- fact_28 -->
- An Italian marriage cannot be registered in Australia but is generally recognised here, so bring home the marriage certificate with an Apostille. <!-- fact_34 --> <!-- fact_38 -->

## Can Australians legally get married in Italy?

Yes, and you do not need to live there. The comune of Bagno a Ripoli, outside Florence, states that foreign citizens may marry there whether they live in Italy or abroad, in a civil, Catholic or other religious ceremony. <!-- fact_30 -->

## The two documents Australians need: the Atto Notorio and the Nulla Osta

The Embassy names two main documents for Australian citizens, the Atto Notorio and the Nulla Osta, and tells couples to ask the marriage office of their town for the full list. <!-- fact_02 -->

### What the Atto Notorio is and where to get it

The Atto Notorio is a sworn statement, made before witnesses, that nothing in Italian law stops you marrying. Get it before you leave, from the Italian consulate for the state where you live. <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_06 -->

In Sydney, both of you attend with four witnesses who are not related to either of you, though they can be related to each other. Each Australian signs their own Atto Notorio, and you can share witnesses. <!-- fact_06 --> Each consulate adds its own rules:

- **Melbourne** books through Prenot@mi, the consulate's online booking portal, and asks for proof of address in Victoria or Tasmania. <!-- fact_10 -->
- **Brisbane** wants first contact at least one month before you fly, and witnesses who have known you for at least 12 months. <!-- fact_13 -->
- **Adelaide** needs your details at least two weeks before the appointment. <!-- fact_14 -->
- **Perth** takes requests by email, and witnesses must not share your surname. <!-- fact_15 -->

If only one of you is Australian, the documents are for the Australian partner only, but four witnesses still attend. If you both also hold EU citizenship, no Atto Notorio is needed. <!-- fact_10 -->

Living outside Australia? The Embassy points you to the Italian consulate in the state where you reside, and Melbourne asks for proof of a Victorian or Tasmanian address. <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_10 -->

Not published: whether an Italian consulate in another country will issue the Atto Notorio for an Australian living there.

If you cannot get it in Australia, a Tribunale Civile (civil court) in Italy can issue it by appointment, with two adult witnesses and an interpreter if needed; neither of you can act as witness or interpreter. <!-- fact_05 --> So can the marriage office of your comune. <!-- fact_04 --> The Sydney consulate's [Atto Notorio page](https://conssydney.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-italiano/stato-civile/getting-married-in-italy-2/atto-notorio/) shows how booking works.

### What the Nulla Osta is and who signs it

The Nulla Osta is a sworn declaration that there is no impediment to your marriage under Australian law. Each Australian partner signs it in person before a consular officer at the Embassy in Rome or the Consulate-General in Milan. <!-- fact_17 --> <!-- fact_16 --> It is by appointment only, and the Embassy does not provide translations. <!-- fact_22 -->

Not published: how far ahead Rome and Milan release appointments, and whether a non-Australian partner attends. Your Wedded Advisor confirms both in writing before you book flights.

### Why a Certificate of No Impediment will not work in Italy

DFAT issues Certificates of No Impediment for some countries, which is why the term fills so many guides. For Italy, the Embassy is direct: "Nulla Ostas sworn outside of Italy and Certificates of No Impediment are not valid for use in Italy." <!-- fact_18 -->

### How long each document is valid

Both last six months. The Atto Notorio runs from the day you sign it, so Sydney's consulate will not issue it more than six months before the wedding. <!-- fact_07 --> The Nulla Osta is also valid for six months. <!-- fact_21 -->

## What to prepare before you leave Australia

At home, the order that works is certificate, then an official Italian translation by a NAATI-certified translator, then a Notary Public's notarisation of the translation, then DFAT apostilles on the original and the translation. <!-- fact_08 --> <!-- fact_24 -->

### Birth certificate and single status certificate

The consulates in Sydney and Adelaide say your comune will need your original birth certificate and a Single Status Certificate from your state's Registry of Births, Deaths and Marriages, each with a NAATI Italian translation and a DFAT Apostille. <!-- fact_08 --> <!-- fact_14 --> Queensland residents provide a Letter of No Record of Marriage instead, and Northern Territory residents a Letter of No Impediment. <!-- fact_13 -->

Treat this as your consulate's list, plus whatever your comune asks for.

### DFAT apostilles and NAATI translations

An Apostille is DFAT's certificate that lets an Australian public document be accepted abroad. DFAT applies it only to original Australian public documents, so a translation must first be notarised by an Australian Notary Public. <!-- fact_24 --> On Sydney's list, two Australians with two certificates and two translations each can need eight apostilles. <!-- fact_08 -->

Lodge at any Australian Passport Office by appointment, where someone can attend on your behalf, or by registered mail. Processing times vary, with delays at the end of the school year and in holiday peaks. <!-- fact_24 --> Smartraveller explains [how DFAT apostilles Australian documents](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised).

### If you have been married before

The officer witnessing your Nulla Osta must see the original divorce certificate, or the death certificate of a late spouse; photocopies are not accepted. <!-- fact_19 --> A divorced woman marrying again within 300 days of her divorce needs permission from an Italian magistrate. <!-- fact_20 -->

## Step by step once you arrive in Italy

On the ground the order is Rome or Milan, then the Prefettura, then the comune. Rome's Prefettura legalisation office opens four mornings a week, and some comuni want your declaration three days before the wedding, so allow a working week rather than arriving two days out. <!-- fact_26 --> <!-- fact_30 -->

### Sign the Nulla Osta in Rome or Milan

Bring your Australian passport and any original divorce or death certificates to the appointment. <!-- fact_17 --> <!-- fact_19 -->

### Legalise it at the Prefettura

Next, the Nulla Osta is legalised at the Ufficio Legalizzazioni of any Prefettura. Buy a revenue stamp (marca da bollo) at a tobacconist (tabaccaio) first. Rome's office opens Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday, 9am to 12pm. <!-- fact_26 --> The comune of Montespertoli in Tuscany lists this step as required for Australians. <!-- fact_27 -->

### Lodge your Declaration of Intent at the comune

Take both documents to the Ufficio Matrimoni (marriage office), with someone who can translate. You get one appointment to lodge your Declaration of Intent to Marry and one for the ceremony, and the Embassy says these "may fall on the same day". <!-- fact_28 -->

Not every comune agrees. Bagno a Ripoli and Montespertoli ask non-resident foreign couples to sign their sworn declaration at least three days before the wedding. <!-- fact_30 --> <!-- fact_33 -->

### The ceremony and your interpreter

If either of you does not speak Italian perfectly, those comuni require a translator-interpreter at the request and at the ceremony. <!-- fact_33 --> Bagno a Ripoli does not provide one, so you bring your own. <!-- fact_31 --> Bagno a Ripoli also wants two witnesses, one per partner, named at least 15 days ahead. <!-- fact_32 -->

### Ask for an apostille on your marriage certificate

You should receive the marriage certificate right after the ceremony. Ask the Prefettura of the city where you married to add an Apostille, so the certificate is valid once you are back in Australia. <!-- fact_34 -->

Not published: interpreter rules and declaration lead times for most comuni.

For getting married in Italy from Australia, the dates that matter are set by the documents.

## Civil, Catholic or symbolic: which ceremony makes you legally married?

For most Australian couples, the legal route is a civil ceremony before the comune or a Catholic ceremony with civil effect. A symbolic ceremony without those steps is not a legal marriage. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_28 --> <!-- fact_36 -->

### Civil ceremony at the town hall or an approved venue

In Bagno a Ripoli, a civil ceremony can be held in the town hall council chamber or at external locations on the comune's own list, with the cost varying by location. <!-- fact_32 -->

Not published: which private villas your comune approves for civil ceremonies. Your Wedded Advisor confirms in writing whether your venue qualifies before you sign with it.

### Catholic wedding with civil effect

A concordat marriage is celebrated by a Catholic priest and takes civil effect once transcribed in the civil register, after publications at the parish and the comune. The priest sends the act to the registrar within five days, and the civil effects run from the day of the ceremony. <!-- fact_36 -->

A civil wedding works differently: where both partners are foreign and neither lives in Italy, Bagno a Ripoli takes a sworn declaration instead of banns. <!-- fact_30 --> Not published: what parish and comune publications make public.

### Marry legally in Australia, celebrate in Italy

You can also sign the legal papers at home. You give a Notice of Intended Marriage to an authorised celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before, and it can be completed from overseas; an Australian celebrant cannot marry you abroad. <!-- fact_37 --> The trade-off is fewer steps in Italy, but the legal wedding happens at home before an authorised celebrant and two witnesses, and the day in Italy is symbolic. <!-- fact_37 --> Weigh [whether to marry legally abroad or hold a symbolic ceremony](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/should-you-marry-legally-abroad-or-have-a-symbolic-ceremony) before you commit.

## Is an Italian marriage recognised in Australia?

In most cases, yes. Smartraveller puts it plainly: "You can't register an overseas marriage in Australia. But in most cases, your marriage is legally recognised here." <!-- fact_38 --> Part VA of the Marriage Act 1961 recognises foreign marriages as valid, with exceptions such as a party already being married or not truly consenting. <!-- fact_40 -->

One catch if you are changing your name. In NSW and Victoria, an overseas marriage certificate cannot be used to take your spouse's surname, so you apply for a change of name instead. That does not mean the marriage is not recognised. <!-- fact_41 --> <!-- fact_42 -->

## Same-sex couples: civil unions in Italy

Italy's legal option for same-sex couples is the civil union (unione civile), created by Law 76 of 20 May 2016. Two adults form it by declaration before the civil registrar, with two witnesses. <!-- fact_44 --> It is a civil union, not a marriage. [[VERIFY: Italy offers same-sex couples a civil union, not marriage; confirm against Law 76/2016 and D.Lgs. 7/2017]]

## What the paperwork costs

Most official steps carry a fee, and amounts move with exchange rates or annual indexation.

- **Atto Notorio:** a consular fee applies, set by each consulate's tariff. Sydney takes EFTPOS or cash, Melbourne takes cards, and Brisbane advises the fee after reviewing your documents and accepts cash only. <!-- fact_09 --> <!-- fact_11 --> <!-- fact_13 -->
- **DFAT apostilles:** a fee applies per document, rising with CPI each 1 January. See [DFAT's current notarial fees](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services#fees). <!-- fact_23 -->
- **Nulla Osta:** a consular fee applies per Australian citizen, by card only. <!-- fact_21 -->
- **Prefettura:** a fee applies as a revenue stamp (marca da bollo), bought at a tabaccaio before you go. <!-- fact_26 -->
- **Comune:** a fee applies for the marriage hall and varies by location. <!-- fact_28 --> In Bagno a Ripoli, the sworn declaration itself has no cost. <!-- fact_30 -->

## Frequently asked questions

### Can Australians get legally married in Italy?

Yes, with an Atto Notorio and a Nulla Osta, in a civil ceremony at the comune or a Catholic ceremony with civil effect. <!-- fact_02 --> <!-- fact_36 --> You cannot marry at the Australian Embassy. <!-- fact_01 -->

### Am I legally married in Australia if I married overseas?

In most cases, yes, if the marriage was valid where it took place and would have been valid in Australia. <!-- fact_38 -->

### Do we need to register our Italian marriage in Australia?

No. Overseas marriages cannot be registered here; keep the Italian certificate as evidence. <!-- fact_41 -->

### How long does it take to get a Nulla Osta in Italy?

It is signed at your appointment in Rome or Milan, then legalised at a Prefettura. <!-- fact_17 --> <!-- fact_26 --> Appointment lead times are not published, so book before you fly.

### How long is a Nulla Osta valid for?

Six months, the same as the Atto Notorio. <!-- fact_07 --> <!-- fact_21 -->

### Can we use an Australian Certificate of No Impediment in Italy?

No. The Embassy says Certificates of No Impediment are not valid for use in Italy. <!-- fact_18 -->

### How early should we arrive in Italy before the wedding?

It depends on the comune: the Embassy says the declaration and ceremony may fall on the same day, but some comuni want the declaration three days before. <!-- fact_28 --> <!-- fact_30 --> Add time for Rome or Milan and the Prefettura.

### Can we marry legally in Australia and have a symbolic ceremony in Italy?

Yes. Lodge a Notice of Intended Marriage with an authorised celebrant at least one month before the Australian ceremony. <!-- fact_37 -->
