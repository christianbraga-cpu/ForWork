A good wedding hashtag is short, yours alone and easy for every guest to spell. You don't need a wedding hashtag generator to find one. Start with your names, add a little wordplay, your date or your destination, then search the tag before it goes anywhere near your stationery.

Below is that method, ideas by style built from invented couples, and what a list of tags can't settle: one tag across a weekend abroad, and guests' photos shared the way you intend.

## Key takeaways

- Build the tag from your names, surnames or initials, then add wordplay, your date or your destination until it sounds like the two of you.
- Before anything is printed, search the tag, read it aloud, check where the words join and capitalise each word.
- One tag for the whole weekend keeps the ask simple, while a short suffix for each event sorts the photos by occasion.
- A hashtag gathers public posts for anyone to see, so pair it with a private album and ask guests to check before they share photos of other people or their children.

## What makes a good wedding hashtag

A tag earns its place on the welcome sign when it is:

- **Short.** Easy to type on a phone, glass in the other hand.
- **Yours.** Built from your names or story, so guests' photos are less likely to sit among strangers'.
- **Easy to spell.** A guest who hears it once from the MC should type it correctly first time.
- **Readable.** A capital at the start of each word.

Clever comes last. A witty tag that guests misspell gathers nothing.

### Start with your names, surnames and initials

Write down every version of your names: first names, nicknames, surnames, a shared surname if you will have one, and initials. Sam and Alex Rivera have #SamAndAlexRivera, #TheRiveras and #SamAndAlex to work with before a single pun.

Initials suit a couple who like a monogram: Jordan and Casey Bell could use #JCBell. Try your names in both orders too, since one order may read more easily aloud.

### Add wordplay that sounds like you

Play with the surname: puns, rhymes, alliteration, or a phrase it already lives inside. A couple keeping their own surnames can join them, as in #HartAndStone, or let one word carry both.

If the two of you are dry rather than giddy, the tag should be too.

### Add your date or year

A year makes a common name distinctive. #TheBells could belong to any Bell family; #TheBells2027 narrows the field. A month and year, such as #RiveraJune2027, works when the year alone still returns other weddings.

### Add your destination

For a wedding abroad, the place is a natural way to set a tag apart. A town, an island or a region pairs well with a surname; there are patterns in the destination ideas below.

### Test it before you print

Run every shortlisted tag through the same checks:

1. **Search it** on the platforms your guests use. If someone else's photos already sit under it, choose again or add your year.
2. **Read it aloud**, as your MC will, and listen for anything that sounds wrong.
3. **Check where the names join.** Write the tag in lower case and read it slowly, since two names run together can spell a word neither of you meant.
4. **Capitalise each word.** #JordanAndCaseyBell is far easier to read than #jordanandcaseybell.
5. **Keep to letters and numbers.** As at November 2026, [Instagram's help page on hashtags](https://help.instagram.com/351460621611097) says numbers are allowed, but spaces and special characters such as $ or % won't work. <!-- fact_18 -->

## Wedding hashtag generator ideas by style

Every tag here is built from invented couples (Sam and Alex Rivera, Jordan and Casey Bell, Priya and Tom Hart, Mia and Leo Stone). Swap in your own names, then run the tests above.

### Surname puns

- **#HeartToHart:** for the Harts, warm and easy to say.
- **#HartAndSoul:** suits a couple whose wedding has a live band.
- **#BellOfTheBall:** for Jordan and Casey Bell, if there is a dance floor at the centre of the night.
- **#SetInStone:** for Mia and Leo Stone, plainly romantic and very short.
- **#RiveraRiviera:** for Sam and Alex Rivera marrying on the Côte d'Azur or the Italian coast.

### Classic and romantic

- **#SamAndAlexRivera:** the full names, clear for older guests.
- **#ForeverRivera:** a single idea, simple to remember.
- **#TheStonesSayIDo:** for a ceremony that is the centre of the weekend.
- **#PriyaAndTomHart:** classic, and easy for the MC to announce.

### Funny wedding hashtags

- **#TwoBellsOneRing:** a pun that lands for guests who know the couple.
- **#HartsOffTheMarket:** cheeky, for a crowd that enjoys a joke.
- **#RiveraReunionTour:** for a wedding where family flies in from everywhere.

Keep a funny tag kind: your grandparents will see it on a sign.

### Destination and travel hashtags

Patterns that work for a wedding abroad:

- **Surname plus place:** #RiverasInRavello, #BellsInBali, #HartsInHvar. Alliteration makes them easy to remember.
- **First names plus place:** #SamAndAlexInSantorini, for a couple whose surname is long or shared with a big family.
- **The venue town or island:** #StonesOnSkiathos, when the island is the story.
- **A weekend in:** #AWeekendWithTheBells or #RavelloWithTheRiveras, for celebrations that stretch over several days.
- **A word from the local language:** #SiToTheRiveras for Italy, kept without accents so it types cleanly.

Check that guests can spell the town without looking it up. For places worth building a tag around, browse [our digital magazine](/magazine?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=content&utm_campaign=5-tips-to-creating-a-personalised-wedding-hashtag&utm_content=magnet).

### Year and date hashtags

- **#JordanAndCasey2027:** first names plus year, short and distinctive.
- **#TheHarts2027:** for a couple taking one surname.
- **#RiveraJune2027:** month and year, when the year alone isn't enough.
- **#StonesSince2027:** works on the wedding day and every anniversary after.

The suffix ties each event back to the main tag, with photos sorted by occasion.

### Where to display it

Put the tag where guests already look:

- **Your wedding website**, beside the travel and timing details covered in [what to include on your wedding website](/expert-advice/wedding-website).
- **The invitation or welcome note**, so guests know it before they board the plane.
- **The welcome sign** at the reception entrance.
- **Table cards and place cards.** The seating plan behind them can live in [our planning app for seating charts and RSVPs](/wonderland-studio).
- **The photo booth template**, so every printed strip carries it.
- **A word from the MC**, once at the start of the reception.

If you would rather guests held off posting until after the ceremony, ask your celebrant to say so. That choice is yours.

### One tag for the weekend or one per event

One tag is the simpler ask, and every photo lands in one place. A tag for each event suits a weekend with different guest lists; if you choose this, print the right tag at each event rather than listing them all on the invitation.

Guests who forget on the night can catch up later. As at November 2026, Instagram's help page on hashtags says you can add a hashtag to a post you've already uploaded by editing the caption or adding it in a comment. <!-- fact_19 --> It also says you can only tag your own posts, so you can't add your tag to a guest's photo for them. <!-- fact_19 -->

### Public hashtags and private albums

A hashtag is public by design. Under Instagram's rules, a post from a public account that carries a hashtag appears on that hashtag's page <!-- fact_15 -->, and tapping the tag shows all the photos and videos people have uploaded with it. <!-- fact_17 --> When people with private profiles tag posts, those posts won't appear publicly on hashtag pages. <!-- fact_16 -->

So a tag suits photos you are happy for anyone to see. For everything else, a private shared album keeps the pictures among your guests. Our guide to [collecting guest photos in a private album](/wedding-tools/wedding-photo-app) covers the options. You can use both: a tag for the public moments, an album for the rest.

### Guest privacy and children's photos

This is general information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

Australia's eSafety Commissioner advises that you should always [ask for consent before sharing a photo or video](https://www.esafety.gov.au/young-people/consent-sharing-photos-videos). <!-- fact_01 --> If someone asks you to remove one, do it, no questions asked. <!-- fact_02 --> Consent can also be reversed: someone who said yes can change their mind, and the photo should then be deleted or the person edited out of the shot. <!-- fact_04 -->

Once something is shared online, you can lose control of how others use it. <!-- fact_05 --> Guests who prefer not to be identified can set their own accounts to review the photos they are tagged in, or stop anyone tagging them. <!-- fact_07 -->

Children need particular care. [Check with other parents before posting, sharing or tagging images or videos that include their children](https://www.esafety.gov.au/parents/issues-and-advice/privacy-child) <!-- fact_12 -->, share only with people you know and trust, and use privacy settings to limit who can see them. <!-- fact_13 --> If a child's photo is posted without permission, the first step is to ask the person who posted it to delete it. <!-- fact_14 -->

Photos and videos are [personal information under the Privacy Act 1988](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/social-media-and-online-privacy/photos-and-videos) when a person's identity is clear or could reasonably be worked out. <!-- fact_08 --> The Act doesn't cover individuals, so it doesn't apply to a photo taken by someone acting in a personal capacity. <!-- fact_09 --> If a photo is posted without permission, first ask the person who posted it to take it down; if they refuse, or you don't know who posted it, ask the site's administrator to remove it. <!-- fact_11 -->

Print a short line beside the tag on your welcome sign: please ask before posting anyone else, check with parents before sharing children, and take a photo down if someone asks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you make a hashtag for your wedding?

Start with your names, surnames or initials, add wordplay, your year or your destination, then test it: search it, read it aloud, check where the words join and capitalise each word.

### Are wedding hashtags still a thing?

Yes, as one option. A tag gives guests one place for public photos; for more privacy, pair it with a private album or skip it.

### What if our wedding hashtag is already taken?

Add your year, your month, your destination or your first names until a search shows nobody else's photos under it.

### Should we use one hashtag for the whole wedding weekend?

One tag keeps things simple, and a short suffix for each event sorts the photos if the occasions feel separate. ### Will guests with private accounts show up under our hashtag?

As at November 2026, Instagram's rules say that when people with private profiles tag posts, those posts won't appear publicly on hashtag pages. <!-- fact_16 -->

### Can we ask guests not to post photos of children?

Yes. Checking with other parents before posting, sharing or tagging images or videos that include their children is the official advice for parents in Australia <!-- fact_12 -->, so a line on your welcome sign asking guests to do the same is a reasonable request.
