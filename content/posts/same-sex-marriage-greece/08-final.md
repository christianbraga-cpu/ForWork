## Is same-sex marriage legal in Greece?

Yes, as at November 2026. The law is Law 5089/2024, titled "Equality in civil marriage, amendment of the Civil Code and other provisions". It was voted on 15 February 2024 and published in the Government Gazette on 16 February 2024. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_03 --> It came into force on publication. <!-- fact_02 -->

The heart of it is one sentence in the Greek Civil Code. Article 1350(1) now begins: "Marriage is contracted between two persons of different or the same sex. Marriage requires the agreement of the intending spouses." <!-- fact_05 --> You can read the [official text of Law 5089/2024](https://www.e-efka.gov.gr/sites/default/files/2024-05/5089-24.pdf) in Greek.

A few weeks later, the Ministry of Interior wrote to every municipality, registry office and Greek consulate. Its circular confirms that a marriage between two persons of the same sex is valid in the Greek legal order and is entered in the civil registry like any other marriage. <!-- fact_06 -->

What matters for your plans is the word civil. The marriage is made before a registrar under the Civil Code. Any church or religious element sits outside it. <!-- fact_07 -->

The short answer is that the civil route is the one open to you, and it runs through the municipality where you marry. The detail is set by the municipality and its registry office, so confirm each step locally.

### Residency rules for overseas couples

Law 5089/2024 states no residency or nationality condition of its own, and neither the law nor the Ministry of Interior circular sets out how a foreign national's freedom to marry is assessed. <!-- fact_15 --> That part is handled by the municipality and its registry office, which may set its own requirements.

So the question to put to the municipality is direct: what do two Australian citizens, neither of them resident in Greece, need to give you, and by when? Ask the same of the Australian Embassy in Athens, including any fees.

For the trip itself, Greece is in the Schengen area. Australians can visit for short stays without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period, and on arrival you register fingerprints and a photo under the European Entry/Exit System. <!-- fact_105 -->

### Civil ceremony at the town hall

A civil ceremony is led by a municipal registrar. On Rhodes, the Town Hall ceremony takes place every Wednesday and Friday, two adult witnesses over 18 must be present, and the couple returns to the registry office the next working day to sign and have the marriage certificate drawn up. <!-- fact_24 -->

The town hall is not the only setting. The Municipality of Rhodes, for one, names outdoor ceremony locations served by its registrars, from beaches to chapels and parks. <!-- fact_19 --> <!-- fact_20 --> More on that below.

Build your timeline around the registry, not the venue. Hold the next working day free, and keep your departure flights well clear of it.

### Documents you will usually need

Requirements are set locally. The Municipality of Rhodes, for example, lists the following for a civil marriage of foreign nationals: <!-- fact_16 -->

- a joint application from the couple
- a marriage licence or certificate of no impediment to marriage for each of you, issued by your consular or other public authority, naming the person you will marry and valid for six months
- a copy of each passport
- a form with your details as newlyweds
- the municipal fee

Documents issued by foreign authorities must be recent originals, officially translated into Greek by a Greek lawyer, the Greek Embassy abroad or the Greek Ministry of Foreign Affairs. For countries outside the European Union, they also need an Apostille. <!-- fact_17 --> Details missing from your documents can be recorded by a solemn declaration on the municipality's template. <!-- fact_18 -->

Other municipalities may ask for different documents. Confirm the current list with the municipality where you marry and with the Australian Embassy in Athens, including any fees.

### What Australians arrange

These steps are for Australian citizens. If either of you holds another passport, ask your own embassy in Athens what it issues in place of a CNI.

The key document is the Certificate of No Impediment, or CNI. The [Australian Embassy in Athens](https://greece.embassy.gov.au/athn/notarial.html) witnesses CNI application forms and issues CNIs, and it can also legalise documents by apostille. <!-- fact_112 -->

At the embassy: <!-- fact_112 -->

- Every notarial service needs an appointment, Monday to Friday, 9:00am to 12:30pm.
- Apostille and CNI documents can be submitted by post.
- Apostilled documents and CNIs are ready for collection within two working days, unless same-day collection is arranged in advance, or they can be returned by mail.
- A fee applies.

The embassy does not give legal advice, and you are responsible for knowing which service you need. <!-- fact_113 --> DFAT can also issue a CNI in Australia, but some local authorities only accept CNIs issued in their own country, so ask first whether yours must come from Athens and whether it needs an apostille. <!-- fact_115 -->

The civil paperwork sits with you, the municipality where you marry and the Australian Embassy in Athens. You may wish to ask a legal adviser to check your documents.

Local authorities may also ask for your original birth certificate and passport, and for divorce papers or a late spouse's death certificate if either of you has been married before. <!-- fact_107 -->

## Will your Greek marriage be recognised in Australia?

As at November 2026, yes in most cases. Since 9 December 2017 the Marriage Act 1961 has defined marriage as "the union of 2 people to the exclusion of all others, voluntarily entered into for life", and same-sex marriages that took place overseas are generally recognised in Australia. <!-- fact_109 --> Recognised same-sex couples are treated the same as other married couples. <!-- fact_111 -->

You cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia. It is generally recognised if it was valid under Greek law when it took place and would have been valid under Australian law had it taken place here. <!-- fact_116 --> Australia does not recognise an overseas marriage where either person is already married, too young, too closely related or forced to marry. <!-- fact_108 -->

Your Greek marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, but may not work as proof of a change of name, so check that with your state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages. You may wish to ask a lawyer to confirm that the certificate is valid in Australia. <!-- fact_116 -->

One more point for planning. If Australia recognises your marriage, you cannot marry again at home, but you can hold another kind of ceremony, such as a confirmation of vows or a recommitment ceremony. <!-- fact_110 --> Read the [Attorney-General's Department guidance on marriage equality](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/marriage-equality-australia) for the detail.

## What the law covers, and what it does not

Law 5089/2024 extends marriage to same-sex couples and strengthens protection from discrimination. <!-- fact_04 --> Beyond the wedding itself, a few points are useful to know.

- **Adoption.** The adoption provisions of the Civil Code now apply to married same-sex couples. <!-- fact_09 -->
- **Assisted reproduction.** The law does not amend the Civil Code provisions on medically assisted reproduction. <!-- fact_08 -->
- **Parentage recorded abroad.** A parent-child relationship recorded in another country's public documents or court decision is recognised in Greece regardless of the parents' sex. <!-- fact_11 -->
- **Children's surnames.** Couples declare their future children's surname before the wedding, to a notary or to the officiant, who must ask for it. Without a declaration, children take both surnames, in alphabetical order. <!-- fact_13 -->

The surname declaration is easy to miss in the rush of a wedding week. Ask the registrar about it when you lodge your documents.

## Can same-sex couples marry in a Greek Orthodox church?

The law is a civil reform. It leaves the Orthodox Church and other religions in Greece free to set the conditions of their own religious ceremonies. <!-- fact_07 --> The Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Australia describes its sacrament of marriage as the union of a man and a woman. <!-- fact_120 -->

A symbolic ceremony at your venue can follow the civil wedding, as set out below. For background on the Church's own service, read [what a Greek Orthodox wedding involves](https://weddedwonderland.com/destination-weddings/greek-orthodox-wedding).

There are three ways to shape the day, and each suits a different couple.

**The legal ceremony in Greece.** You marry before the registrar, then celebrate. You might keep the civil ceremony small, with the two witnesses and close family, and hold the larger celebration that evening or the next day.

**Legal at home, celebration in Greece.** You marry in Australia first, then hold a symbolic ceremony at your Greek venue with no registry paperwork on the island. The day is written by you, led by a celebrant you choose, wherever the venue allows.

**Both, in the one week.** A civil ceremony at the town hall or a municipal setting, then a symbolic ceremony with vows, readings and music at the venue. It gives guests a ceremony to witness and you a legal marriage in the country you chose.

Whichever you choose, remember the rule on recognition. If your Greek marriage is recognised, a later ceremony in Australia is a recommitment or confirmation of vows, not a second marriage. <!-- fact_110 -->
