# Booking checklist: same-sex marriage in Greece

These details sit with the couple, the authorities and the venue, so they don't block publishing. The Wedded Advisor plans the celebration around the civil ceremony and confirms venue details in writing for the couple's dates; the civil paperwork stays with the couple, the municipality where they marry and the Australian Embassy in Athens. The article has no `[[VERIFY]]` markers in the copy and no prices (nothing for Nic to sign off); the only publishing blockers are image credits and licences (6 slots).

All items are as at November 2026. Reconfirm each one for the couple's date before relying on it.

## Legal: the municipality where you marry (ask the municipality and its registry office, in writing)

- Which municipality and registrar cover the chosen island and venue (the island decides the municipality, and the municipality decides the paperwork and ceremony settings).
- Its current document list for two foreign nationals. Example: the Municipality of Rhodes lists a joint application, a marriage licence or certificate of no impediment for each partner naming the other and valid for six months, a copy of each passport, a newlyweds' details form and the municipal fee.
- Residence or nationality conditions: Law 5089/2024 states none of its own and does not set out how a foreign national's freedom to marry is assessed, so ask directly what two non-resident Australian citizens must provide, and by when.
- Translation and Apostille: documents from foreign authorities must be recent originals, officially translated into Greek (a Greek lawyer, the Greek Embassy abroad or the Greek Ministry of Foreign Affairs); documents from outside the European Union also need an Apostille. Confirm what counts as "recent".
- Missing details: whether a solemn declaration on the municipality's template can record them.
- Registrar: ceremony days (Rhodes Town Hall: Wednesday and Friday), approved outdoor locations served by a registrar, whether the registrar can officiate privately at or near the venue, and who else can be present.
- Witnesses: two adults over 18 (as Rhodes states); confirm for the chosen municipality.
- After the ceremony: the return visit to sign and draw up the marriage certificate (Rhodes: the next working day). Keep departure flights clear.
- Children's surname declaration: made before the wedding to a notary or the officiant; ask the registrar when lodging documents.
- Current municipal fees.

## Legal: Australian Embassy in Athens

- Certificate of No Impediment (CNI): the Embassy witnesses CNI application forms and issues CNIs; confirm whether the municipality needs the CNI from Athens or accepts one issued by DFAT in Australia, and whether it needs an Apostille.
- Appointment needed for every notarial service (Monday to Friday, 9:00am to 12:30pm); Apostille and CNI documents can be submitted by post; collection within two working days unless same-day is arranged, or returned by mail; a fee applies.
- The Embassy does not give legal advice; consider a legal adviser to check the documents.
- Partners with another passport: ask that country's embassy in Athens what it issues in place of a CNI.
- Previously married partners: divorce papers or a late spouse's death certificate; originals of birth certificate and passport if asked.
- Travel: Schengen short stay without a visa up to 90 days in any 180 days; Entry/Exit System registration on arrival. A passport with "X" in the sex field: ask Greece and every transit country whether they accept it.

## Legal: recognition in Australia

- An overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia; it is generally recognised if valid under Greek law when it took place and valid under Australian law had it taken place here.
- Check with the state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages whether the Greek certificate works as proof of a change of name.
- Consider a lawyer to confirm the certificate is valid in Australia.
- A recognised couple cannot marry again in Australia; a later ceremony is a recommitment or confirmation of vows.

## Venue questions (ask the venue, in writing)

- Have you hosted same-sex weddings before, and will you put that in writing?
- Private civil ceremony location: can the civil ceremony take place on the grounds or at a municipal location nearby on the same day as the celebration, and on which days does the registrar officiate there?
- Who leads a symbolic ceremony on your grounds, and can we choose our own celebrant?
- Privacy: how private is the ceremony space, can we take the whole venue or a section, and how do you manage guest photography, the photographer's position and social media on the day?
- Step-free access to the ceremony spot and the celebration, and how older guests reach them.
- Rain plan (and heat plan) for any outdoor ceremony or dinner.
- Music: amplified music and curfew times, and any limits near guest rooms or neighbours.
- Where guests stay, and how close those rooms are to the celebration.
