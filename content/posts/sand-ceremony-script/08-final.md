Two small carafes, one glass vessel, and a pause in the ceremony where nobody has to speak. A sand ceremony asks very little of you on the day, which is exactly why the words around it matter.

This sand ceremony script gives your celebrant something to read word for word: an introduction for your guests, the direction for the pour and the closing words. Below it is original wording in several tones, a fill-in template, what to set on the table, and the questions to ask before you pour sand on a beach.

## Key takeaways

- Every script works in three parts: the celebrant introduces the ritual, you pour, and the celebrant closes the moment.
- A sand ceremony is symbolic, so it sits beside the legal words of your ceremony and never takes their place.
- The pour fits neatly after the vows and rings and before the pronouncement, and your celebrant sets the final order.
- On a beach or abroad, ask the venue or local council about wind, sealing the vessel and whether sand may be taken from the shore.

## Sand ceremony script, word for word

A sand ceremony is a short ritual in which the two of you, and sometimes your family, pour separate colours of sand into one vessel so the layers settle together. It is symbolic, and it sits alongside the legal part of your ceremony rather than replacing it.

The script below is our classic version. Directions are in brackets, and every word is ready to read. If you want the whole ceremony around it, our [full wedding ceremony script with the legal lines](/expert-advice/wedding-ceremony-script) sets out each section in order.

### Introducing the sand ceremony to guests

**CELEBRANT:** [Name] and [Name] have chosen to mark this moment with sand. Each of them holds a different colour, gathered for today. In a moment they will pour them into one vessel, and what settles there will be a record of this afternoon that they can keep on a shelf at home and look at for years.

### Pouring the sand

**CELEBRANT:** [Name], [Name], when you are ready.

[The couple pour their sand together, or take turns. The celebrant waits in silence until the pour is finished. A musician may play softly underneath.]

**CELEBRANT:** Take your time. There is no right way to do this, only yours.

[Optional: the couple pour a final layer together from both carafes at once.]

### Closing line

**CELEBRANT:** Look at what you have made. Two colours, one vessel, and every layer still there to see. May your life together always have room for you both.

[The celebrant moves on to the next part of the ceremony.]

Read aloud at an unhurried pace, this whole sand ceremony wedding script takes a couple of minutes with the pour. If your own promises follow straight after, our guide on [how to write your own wedding vows](/how-to-write-wedding-vows) will help you match the tone.

## Where the sand ceremony goes in your ceremony

Place the sand ceremony after the vows and the exchange of rings, just before the celebrant pronounces them married.

You can also pour straight after the welcome, so the ritual sets the tone. What matters is that the pour never interrupts the legal lines, so your celebrant can say them clearly.

## Sample sand ceremony wording for every kind of couple

Each version keeps the same three parts, so you can mix the introduction from one with the closing line of another.

### A short version for a simple ceremony

**CELEBRANT:** [Name] and [Name] will now pour two colours of sand into one vessel, as a small, lasting reminder of today.

[The couple pour.]

**CELEBRANT:** Two colours, now one. Keep it close.

This suits a pared-back ceremony. Pair it with our [simple wedding ceremony script](/expert-advice/simple-wedding-ceremony-script) and, if you want promises to match, [short wedding vows](/expert-advice/short-wedding-vows).

### A romantic version

**CELEBRANT:** Some things are easier to show than to say. [Name] and [Name] have spent [number] years learning each other's habits, the coffee order, the way one of them hums when they are nervous. Today they will pour that history into one vessel.

[The couple pour slowly, together.]

**CELEBRANT:** Every layer you see is a day you have already shared. The empty space above it is every day still to come.

### A secular version

**CELEBRANT:** This ceremony is about two people choosing each other, willingly and on purpose. [Name] and [Name] will mark that choice with sand: one colour each, poured into one vessel.

[The couple pour.]

**CELEBRANT:** Nothing here was decided by chance. It was poured by your own hands, in the order you chose, and it will look like nothing else in the world.

### For a ceremony of faith

**CELEBRANT:** [Name] and [Name] bring their faith into this marriage, and they ask that it shape the life they build. As they pour their sand together, we hold them in our prayers.

[The couple pour. The celebrant or a family member may offer a short prayer the couple have chosen.]

**CELEBRANT:** May the love you have promised today be steady, patient and generous, and may you always find your way back to it together.

If a minister of religion is marrying you, ask where a sand moment can sit within their rite before you write anything down.

### With children or a blended family

<!-- IMAGE SLOT family-pour: Real wedding. Couple with one or two children each pouring a separate colour into the shared vessel. Hands and vessel as the focus; children photographed from behind, side or hands-only unless the register records written parental consent for identifiable children. Outdoor ceremony, soft daylight, warm and candid. Landscape or 4:5. Alt: Couple and their children pouring coloured sand into one vessel together at a wedding ceremony -->

**CELEBRANT:** Today is not only about [Name] and [Name]. It is about [Child's name] and [Child's name] too, and the family the four of them already are. Each of them holds a colour of their own.

[Each child pours first, then the couple pour together over the top. For very young children, a parent can steady the carafe.]

**CELEBRANT:** [Child's name], [Child's name], look at what you made with your mum and dad. Every colour is still there, and every one belongs. This is your family, and it is in the same vessel now.

Adjust "mum and dad" to whatever your children call you both.

### With parents or both families

**CELEBRANT:** [Name] and [Name] did not arrive here alone. Their parents, [Names] and [Names], have been invited to begin this pour, one colour from each family.

[Each set of parents pours a thin layer. If a parent would rather stay seated, bring the carafe and vessel to them. The couple then pour their own colours together.]

**CELEBRANT:** Two families poured the first layer. [Name] and [Name] poured the rest. From today, this is one family's story, written in sand.

### For a beach or destination ceremony

**CELEBRANT:** We are standing [on this beach / in this garden] in [place], a long way from where many of you woke up last week. [Name] and [Name] wanted something from this day to travel home with them, so today they will pour it into a vessel they can seal and carry.

[The couple pour. The celebrant or a planner seals the lid straight after.]

**CELEBRANT:** Wherever you live, this will remind you of the light, the salt air and the people who travelled to stand here with you.

### For a vow renewal

**CELEBRANT:** [Number] years ago, [Name] and [Name] made their promises. Today they make them again, with [children, grandchildren, friends] watching. They will pour two colours of sand, and this time they know exactly what they are pouring into.

[The couple pour, slowly.]

**CELEBRANT:** Not a beginning this time, but a continuation. May the next layer be as rich as the ones before.

For more ways to mark the day, see our [vow renewal ideas](/expert-advice/vow-renewal-ideas).

### A template to fill in

Copy this, fill in the blanks and send it to your celebrant.

> **CELEBRANT:** [Name] and [Name] have chosen sand to mark this moment because ________. Each of them holds a colour that stands for ________. They will now pour them into one vessel.
>
> [Who pours, and in what order: ________.]
>
> **CELEBRANT:** ________. [One closing line about what the mix of colours means to you.]

Keep each blank to a sentence. The pour is the moment, not the speech.

## What you need for a sand ceremony

A unity sand ceremony needs very little, but choose each piece with the photographs in mind.

- **One keepsake vessel.** Clear glass shows the layers. A narrow neck slows the pour and keeps it neat.
- **One small carafe per pourer.** Two for the couple, plus one for each child or family taking part. Carafes with a lip pour more cleanly than jars.
- **Sand in contrasting colours.** Choose shades that read clearly side by side: ivory and sea blue, blush and charcoal. Fill each carafe so the vessel ends up nearly full.
- **A small table.** Draped in linen, at waist height, placed so guests can see your hands and you are not turning your back on them.
- **A funnel and a lid.** The funnel stays out of sight in case a pour goes wide. The lid, or a cork and wax seal, closes the vessel once you are done.

Do a full run at the rehearsal with the actual vessel and carafes. For a large guest list, ask your photographer where to stand so the pour is captured close up.

### Keeping the sand afterwards

The vessel is now your keepsake. Seal it the same day so the layers do not shift on the way to the reception. Set it on the guestbook table for the evening, then take it home with you.

At home, a shelf out of direct sun keeps the layers sharp. A small engraved plate with your names and the date finishes it.

## A sand ceremony at a beach or destination wedding

<!-- IMAGE SLOT beach-destination: Real destination wedding at a named venue the register or partner supply agreement can prove (location verified by register, EXIF GPS or source page, never appearance). Sand ceremony table set on or beside the beach with the ocean behind. Alt: Sand ceremony table on the beach with the ocean behind during a destination wedding -->

On a beach, a gentle breeze can lift a fine stream of sand straight off the table. Before you settle on the setting, put these questions to the venue or, for a public beach, the local council.

- **Wind.** Is there a sheltered spot on the beach or a lawn behind it? At what time of day does the breeze pick up there? Can the table sit with its back to the wind?
- **Sealing the vessel.** Who seals the lid straight after the pour, and where is the vessel kept safe until you leave?
- **Taking sand from the beach.** Are you allowed to scoop a handful from the shore into your vessel, or should you bring your own coloured sand?
- **Leaving nothing behind.** If any sand spills, who clears it, and is coloured sand acceptable on that beach at all?
- **Ceremony approval.** Does a ceremony on this beach need a booking or approval, and who arranges it?
- **Access.** Can guests using a walking frame or wheelchair reach the ceremony spot, and is there seating close to the table?
- **Privacy.** Can the ceremony space be kept private, and who keeps the public at a distance?

Keep the table low and heavy, or weight its legs. If you plan to wear something flowing, our edit of [beach wedding dresses](/style-advice/beach-wedding-dresses) has silhouettes that cope with a sea breeze.

## A sand ceremony and the legal words

A sand ceremony is symbolic. The legal part of your wedding is spoken, and in Australia the words come from the [Marriage Act 1961](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text).

As at November 2026, section 45 of the Act is headed "Form of ceremony" and section 46 is headed "Certain authorised celebrants to explain nature of marriage relationship". <!-- fact_01 -->

When your celebrant is not a minister of religion, it is sufficient for each of you to say to the other, in the presence of the celebrant and the witnesses, the words in [section 45(2)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html):

> "I call upon the persons here present to witness that I, A.B. (or C.D.), take thee, C.D. (or A.B.), to be my lawful wedded wife (or husband, or spouse)"; or words to that effect. <!-- fact_101 -->

Before the marriage is solemnised, a celebrant who is not a minister of religion of a recognised denomination shall say these words to you, in the presence of the witnesses, under [section 46(1)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html):

> "I am duly authorised by law to solemnise marriages according to law. Before you are joined in marriage in my presence and in the presence of these witnesses, I am to remind you of the solemn and binding nature of the relationship into which you are now about to enter. Marriage, according to law in Australia, is the union of 2 people to the exclusion of all others, voluntarily entered into for life."; or words to that effect. <!-- fact_102 -->

Under [section 46(2)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s46.html), the Minister may exempt certain celebrants authorised under subsection 39(2) from section 46(1) where satisfied that their form of ceremony sufficiently states the nature and obligations of marriage. <!-- fact_103 --> Under [section 45(1)](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ma196185/s45.html), when a minister of religion marries you, the marriage may be solemnised according to any form and ceremony recognised as sufficient for the purpose by the religious body or organisation of which they are a minister. <!-- fact_100 --> Either way, ask your celebrant which legal lines they will say, and build the pour around them.

If you marry overseas, [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) notes that you can't register an overseas marriage in Australia. To be recognised here, the marriage must have been valid under the law of the country where it happened, and valid under Australian law had it taken place in Australia. Talk to a lawyer to confirm your overseas marriage certificate is valid in Australia. <!-- fact_104 -->

## Frequently asked questions

### What do you say at a sand ceremony?

Your celebrant introduces the ritual, invites you to pour, then closes the moment with a line or two. The couple need say nothing at all. The scripts above give each part word for word.

### Do you do the sand ceremony before or after the vows?

The pour fits neatly after the vows and rings, just before the pronouncement, or straight after the welcome. Agree the order with your celebrant, and keep the pour clear of the legal lines.

### Is the sand ceremony biblical?

A sand ceremony is a symbolic ritual you choose, and it sits as comfortably in a civil ceremony as in a religious one. If a minister of religion is marrying you, ask where a sand moment can sit within their rite.

### What do you need for a sand ceremony?

One clear keepsake vessel, a small carafe for each person pouring, sand in contrasting colours, a small table, a funnel and a lid to seal the vessel afterwards.

### How do you include children in a sand ceremony?

Give each child their own colour and a carafe small enough to hold in one hand. Let them pour first, then pour your colours over the top, and have the celebrant speak to them by name.

### How long does a sand ceremony take?

The classic script above takes a couple of minutes with the pour. A family pour with several carafes runs a little longer. For the full running time of your day, see [how long a wedding ceremony runs](/expert-advice/how-long-is-a-wedding-ceremony).
