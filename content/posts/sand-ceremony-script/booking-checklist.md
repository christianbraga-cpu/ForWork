# Sand ceremony planning checklist

Use this while you plan the sand ceremony with your celebrant and venue. Get the answers that matter on the day in writing.

## Vessels
- [ ] One keepsake vessel in clear glass, so the layers show
- [ ] A narrow neck to slow the pour and keep it neat
- [ ] A lid, or a cork and wax seal, to close the vessel once you are done
- [ ] A funnel kept out of sight in case a pour goes wide
- [ ] One small carafe per pourer: two for the two of you, plus one for each child or family taking part
- [ ] Carafes with a lip, which pour more cleanly than jars
- [ ] For children, a carafe small enough to hold in one hand

## Sand colours
- [ ] Choose shades that read clearly side by side (for example ivory and sea blue, or blush and charcoal)
- [ ] One colour for each person or family pouring
- [ ] Fill each carafe so the vessel ends up nearly full
- [ ] On a beach, decide whether you bring your own coloured sand or ask first about scooping a handful from the shore

## Table
- [ ] A small table at waist height, draped in linen
- [ ] Placed so guests can see your hands and you are not turning your back on them
- [ ] On a beach, keep the table low and heavy, or weight its legs
- [ ] Ask your photographer where to stand so the pour is captured close up

## Who pours
- [ ] The two of you, together or taking turns
- [ ] Children: each pours their own colour first, then you pour over the top; a parent can steady the carafe for very young children
- [ ] Parents or both families: each set pours a thin layer first; if a parent would rather stay seated, bring the carafe and vessel to them
- [ ] Optional final layer poured together from both carafes at once
- [ ] Choose your wording and fill in the template, then send it to your celebrant

## Where it sits in the order
- [ ] After the vows and the exchange of rings, just before the pronouncement, or straight after the welcome
- [ ] The pour never interrupts the legal lines
- [ ] Your celebrant sets the final order
- [ ] Optional: a musician plays softly under the pour

## Rehearsal run
- [ ] Do a full run at the rehearsal with the actual vessel and carafes
- [ ] Practise the order of who pours, and the handover of carafes
- [ ] Check the table position with your photographer

## Sealing and keepsake
- [ ] Agree who seals the lid straight after the pour (the celebrant or a planner)
- [ ] Seal it the same day so the layers do not shift on the way to the reception
- [ ] Agree where the vessel is kept safe until you leave
- [ ] Set it on the guestbook table for the evening, then take it home with you
- [ ] At home, a shelf out of direct sun keeps the layers sharp
- [ ] Optional: a small engraved plate with your names and the date

## Questions for your celebrant
- [ ] Which legal lines will you say, and how do we build the pour around them?
- [ ] Where do you suggest the sand ceremony sits in our order: after the vows and rings, or straight after the welcome?
- [ ] Will you read our chosen wording as written, and do you want it before the day?
- [ ] If a minister of religion is marrying us, where can a sand moment sit within your rite?
- [ ] Abroad: is this ceremony our legal marriage or a symbolic one, and who can read our script on the day?
- [ ] Will you seal the vessel straight after the pour, or should our planner?
- [ ] Can we include our children or parents in the pour, and will you speak to the children by name?
- [ ] Can we run the pour at the rehearsal with the actual vessel and carafes?

## Questions for the venue or local council
For a public beach, ask the local council.
- [ ] **Wind.** Is there a sheltered spot on the beach or a lawn behind it? At what time of day does the breeze pick up there? Can the table sit with its back to the wind?
- [ ] **Sealing the vessel.** Who seals the lid straight after the pour, and where is the vessel kept safe until we leave?
- [ ] **Taking sand from the beach.** Are we allowed to scoop a handful from the shore into our vessel, or should we bring our own coloured sand?
- [ ] **Leaving nothing behind.** If any sand spills, who clears it, and is coloured sand acceptable on that beach at all?
- [ ] **Ceremony approval.** Does a ceremony on this beach need a booking or approval, and who arranges it?
- [ ] **Access.** Can guests using a walking frame or wheelchair reach the ceremony spot, and is there seating close to the table?
- [ ] **Privacy.** Can the ceremony space be kept private, and who keeps the public at a distance?
