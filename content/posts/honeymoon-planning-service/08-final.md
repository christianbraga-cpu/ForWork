A honeymoon planning service is one person who plans and books your honeymoon for you: the flights, the stays, the days in between, all held in a single itinerary. The good ones agree their fee and confirm what is included in writing before you commit to anything.

For a couple already planning a wedding, that matters. The honeymoon tends to arrive in the busiest months of the year, when every spare evening goes to guest lists and suppliers. This guide covers what a planner does, how it works with Wedded Wonderland, how the honeymoon fits around a destination wedding, what to decide before you enquire and what to ask any planner before you agree.

## Key takeaways

- A honeymoon planning service plans and books the whole trip for you, so you deal with one person instead of every airline, stay and supplier.
- Your Australian consumer rights stay the same whether you book directly or through a planner.
- Planning the honeymoon alongside a destination wedding keeps the contacts, the deadlines and the currencies in one plan instead of two.
- Decide your dates, length, style and must-haves first, then ask any planner about fees, inclusions, terms, insurance, exchange rates and commissions.

## What a planner does for your honeymoon

A honeymoon planner takes the trip off your hands. You describe the shape of it: how long you have, the kind of places you both want, the one thing you will not go without. The planner turns that into an itinerary and books it on your behalf.

For a two-week honeymoon, that might be a long-haul flight, two or three stays, a private dinner, a boat for a day and the connections between them. Each piece comes with its own deposit, its own deadline and its own terms. The work is in keeping those pieces in step, so the deadlines and terms line up.

What a planner does not change is your footing as a consumer. Where Australian consumer guarantees cover a booking, they apply whether you book directly with an airline or tour operator, through a travel agent, or through a booking platform or package provider. <!-- fact_02 --> You give up none of your rights by handing the trip to someone else.

Where a planner earns their place is time. Comparing options, reading terms, matching flight times to check-in times and confirming the small things (a dietary note, a table on the last night) are hours of work. A planner does them so you don't have to. If your trip is a single stay, in one currency, with flexible dates, you may be happy booking it yourselves.

## Planning the honeymoon alongside your wedding

A destination wedding and a honeymoon share more than a calendar. They often share a continent, a currency and a set of flights. Planning them as two separate projects means two sets of contacts, two sets of deadlines and two lots of quotes in foreign currencies.

Planned together, the honeymoon becomes the second chapter of the same trip. The wedding week ends, the guests fly home and the two of you move on to the next stay without starting a new conversation with someone new.

If you are leaving the morning after, settle that morning before the wedding week begins. Decide what time you will say goodbye to the last guests, whether you need a later check-out, who packs the wedding clothes and keepsakes and how they get home, how you will reach the next stay and how long the journey takes, and which documents travel in your hand luggage.

### Straight after, or later

Some couples leave the morning after the wedding. Others wait for a quieter season, or for the leave to build up again. Both are sound. The questions to weigh are the weather where you are going, how tired you expect to be and whether you would rather the honeymoon feel like an escape from the wedding or a continuation of it. Our guide on [when to go on honeymoon](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/a-guide-on-when-to-go-on-honeymoon) works through the timing.

### The name on your tickets

If you are travelling soon after the wedding, book travel in the name that is in your passport. If your family name changes because of marriage, you can choose to get a new passport or keep using your current one. <!-- fact_33 -->

If you marry overseas, a foreign marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, but you may be unable to use it as proof of a name change. <!-- fact_34 --> Check with your local registry of births, deaths and marriages. Born in Australia and married overseas? You will always need a name change certificate for your passport. <!-- fact_33 --> Our guide to [changing your name after marriage](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/name-change-after-marriage) covers the steps in order.

## What to decide before you enquire

You do not need a finished plan to start a conversation. A few decisions, even loose ones, make the first conversation far more useful.

**Dates.** Fix the window you have, even if only roughly: the earliest you can leave, the latest you must be home and any days that cannot move.

**Length.** Ten days and three weeks are very different trips. Two stays can feel generous in one and rushed in the other. Our guide to [how long a honeymoon should be](https://weddedwonderland.com/how-long) helps you settle on a number.

**Style.** Do you want to be still, or to move? One stay with nothing on the schedule, or a sequence of cities, coastlines and mountains? Private and secluded, or close to restaurants and a little life in the evenings?

**Must-haves.** Each of you names one or two things the trip must include. A pool you never have to share. A night somewhere very remote. A long lunch with a view of the sea. These shape every choice that follows.

**Pace of payment.** Think about how you would like to pay and in what order, before the first deposit is due. It is easier to set this once than to decide it booking by booking.

## Questions to ask any planner before you agree

These are worth asking whoever you speak to, including us. The answers should come back clearly, and in writing. The points below are general information as at November 2026.

1. **What is the fee, and what does it cover?** Ask how it is worked out and when it is payable.

2. **What is included in each booking?** Ask for the inclusions of every stay, flight and experience in writing before you pay a deposit. If terms are unclear or cannot be found, ask the provider to confirm them in writing. <!-- fact_06 -->

3. **Whose terms apply?** When you book through an agent or intermediary, both the agent's terms and conditions and those of each airline, hotel or operator apply, so check both. <!-- fact_05 --> Your consumer rights are the same whether you book directly or through an agent. <!-- fact_02 --> The [ACCC's guide to travel delays and cancellations](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/travel-delays-and-cancellations) sets out what applies.

4. **When should we take out travel insurance?** Early. If a risk or event is known before you buy the policy, you will not be covered if it directly affects your travel, and the earlier you buy, the more likely you are to be covered. <!-- fact_14 --> Choose a policy that covers every country on the route, stopovers included. <!-- fact_11 --> [Smartraveller's travel insurance guidance](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) explains what to look for in the Product Disclosure Statement. <!-- fact_10 -->

5. **Do you hold insolvency insurance?** Agent insolvency is commonly excluded from cancellation cover, and insolvency insurance is not compulsory for agents, so only some have it. <!-- fact_17 --> Ask.

6. **What exchange rate sits behind an AUD quote?** Providers choose the exchange rate they use and charge fees. <!-- fact_19 --> Ask whether the rate is guaranteed, what fees or margin apply and whether the receiver pays anything at the other end. <!-- fact_20 --> [Moneysmart's guide to sending money overseas](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/sending-money-overseas) lists what to compare.

7. **Do you receive commission from suppliers?** A straightforward question deserves a straightforward answer. If so, ask whether it is disclosed to you, and whether it affects which suppliers are recommended.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a honeymoon planning service do?

It plans and books the honeymoon on your behalf: flights, stays, experiences and the connections between them, held in one itinerary. You deal with one person rather than every provider.

### How far ahead should we plan a honeymoon?

Work back from your dates rather than a rule. Ask yourselves how fixed your leave is, whether you are travelling in a popular season and whether the honeymoon follows straight on from a destination wedding. The more of those that apply, the earlier it pays to start. Our guide on when to go on honeymoon helps with the timing.

### How much does a honeymoon planner cost?

There is no single price, because planners charge in different ways. Ask each one what the fee is, what it covers and whether they also receive commissions from suppliers, and get the answers in writing. Comparing what each fee includes tells you more than comparing the numbers alone.

### Whose name should go on our tickets if we travel straight after the wedding?

Book travel in the name that is in your passport. If you change your family name because of marriage, you can get a new passport or keep using your current one. <!-- fact_33 -->
