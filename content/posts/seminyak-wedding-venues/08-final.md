## How do the venues compare like for like?

Every venue here hosts weddings. Four questions then separate them: is it on the sand, where do the ceremony and dinner happen, how many guests fit, and can your guests sleep on site.

### On the sand or around a pool

The five resorts describe themselves as beachfront. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_07 --> <!-- fact_11 --> <!-- fact_14 --> <!-- fact_201 --> The Elysian and Mrs Sippy centre the day on a pool instead of the sand. <!-- fact_207 --> <!-- fact_101 --> On a public beach, decide early how much of the view you want to share.

### Ceremony and dinner on the same site

Several venues name more than one wedding space at one address: Hotel Indigo, for example, names three ceremony settings and four wedding venues. <!-- fact_202 --> <!-- fact_203 --> Plan the walk between spaces, and the rain plan for each, as part of the day.

### Rooms for your guests

Hotel Indigo and The Seminyak Beach Resort both list rooms, suites and pool villas, and Hotel Indigo invites larger parties to ask about room blocks. <!-- fact_204 --> <!-- fact_18 --> At W Bali Seminyak, ask whether room blocks are held for your guests. Ask each one whether your spaces, or the property, can be closed to other guests.

### Money and contracts: questions to ask each venue

None of these seven venues publishes wedding pricing online, so ask each for a written quote, then ask:

- Whose name is on the contract, and in what currency is it written and paid?
- How much deposit is due, when, and at what point does it become non-refundable?
- If the venue cancels, changes ownership or cannot host on your date, how and when is your deposit returned, and is that written into the contract?
- Is there a minimum spend or venue hire fee, and are service charge and tax included?
- What exactly does the package include, and is every inclusion written into the contract?
- What does the cancellation clause cover, including weather, flooding or volcanic disruption?

### Access: questions about the beach and the evening

- Is the ceremony space on the resort's own grounds, or on the public beach in front of it?
- How do guests reach the sand, and is there a step-free route between the ceremony and dinner?
- What is the wet-weather backup for each space, and when is the call made?
- What are the noise and music curfews, and do they differ between the beachfront and indoor spaces?

### Insurance timing

- When should travel insurance be in place relative to your first deposit, and does it cover the wedding itself?
- What does the venue's own policy cover if the wedding is moved or cancelled?

### Security and privacy: questions for any couple

- How is the ceremony space screened from the public beach and from passers-by?
- Which areas are reserved for wedding guests only?
- How loud are the neighbouring beach clubs at your ceremony and dinner times?
- What are the rules on drones, outside photographers and guests posting on social media?
- Can your own security or close-protection team work alongside the venue's staff, and who is the venue's contact for that?

## How do guests get to Seminyak?

Guests fly into Bali and need a few steps done before they land. As at November 2026, travellers arriving at Indonesia's international entry points must complete an arrival card and apply for an e-Visa on Arrival, both on the All Indonesia website. <!-- fact_308 --> For a regular visa on arrival, an ordinary passport needs at least six months' validity from the planned entry date, plus a return or onward flight booking. <!-- fact_308 -->

The e-Visa on Arrival allows a 30-day stay, extendable once for up to 30 days at an immigration office. <!-- fact_308 --> Damaged passports have led to refused entry, so ask guests to check theirs early. <!-- fact_308 -->

Foreign tourists entering Bali also pay a tourist levy, separate from the visa, either online before travel or at counters at Bali's airport and seaport. <!-- fact_309 --> Volcanic activity can bring flight delays, cancellations or airport closures with little or no notice, so build a buffer day into the guest schedule. <!-- fact_310 --> The full advice sits with [Smartraveller Indonesia advice](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/asia/indonesia).

For moving guests between the airport, their rooms and the venue, ask each venue on your shortlist:

- Does the venue arrange guest transport, and for how many guests?
- How long is the journey from the airport at the time of day your guests land?
- If guests stay at a different address, how do they reach the ceremony and get home after dinner?
- Can the venue arrange a private, discreet arrival for guests?
- What happens to transport if roads flood in heavy rain? <!-- fact_314 -->

Smartraveller's overall advice for Indonesia is to exercise a high degree of caution, and Bali falls under that overall level. <!-- fact_307 --> A few hazards belong in the plan:

- **The sea:** Indonesia has a high level of earthquake activity that sometimes triggers tsunamis, and high wave events can happen on the coast. Australians have drowned in rough seas and rip currents at popular beaches, including in Bali. <!-- fact_311 -->
- **Volcanoes:** Mount Agung in Bali Province is listed among volcanoes to watch, and activity can escalate with little or no notice. <!-- fact_310 -->

## Can foreigners legally get married in Bali?

Yes, with conditions, and the legal marriage sits apart from booking a venue. As at November 2026, the [Australian Embassy in Indonesia](https://indonesia.embassy.gov.au/) sets out these points as a general guide. <!-- fact_300 -->

- **Religion:** Persons of any nationality may generally marry in Indonesia if they hold a religion recognised by the Indonesian Government, listed as Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism or Christian, and both parties must hold the same religion. <!-- fact_301 --> <!-- fact_302 --> <!-- fact_317 -->
- **Certificate of No Impediment:** Australians need one from the Embassy in Jakarta or a Consulate-General; the Consulate-General in Bali covers Bali, and its consular services are by appointment only. Civil Registry Offices do not normally accept one issued more than three months before. <!-- fact_303 --> <!-- fact_316 --> <!-- fact_317 -->
- **Notice and registration:** For non-Muslim marriages, lodge a Notice of Intention to Marry with the Civil Registry Office at least 10 days before the wedding, and register the marriage there afterwards for it to be legal. <!-- fact_304 -->
- **Documents:** The CNI, passports, birth certificates, proof that any previous marriage has ended, and four 4x6 cm photos; requirements vary by province. <!-- fact_304 -->
- **At home:** A marriage legally performed overseas that would have been legal in Australia is generally recognised, and you cannot register it in Australia. <!-- fact_305 --> <!-- fact_306 -->

You cannot marry at the Embassy or a Consulate-General. <!-- fact_303 --> For specific requirements, contact the nearest Indonesian Embassy or Consulate-General, or the Civil Registry Office (Catatan Sipil) where you will marry, and talk to a lawyer to confirm validity. <!-- fact_300 --> <!-- fact_306 --> If you want a symbolic ceremony on the beach, plan the legal marriage separately: Hotel Indigo's celebrant ceremony is non-legal, as are The Seminyak Beach Resort's packages. <!-- fact_205 --> <!-- fact_19 -->
