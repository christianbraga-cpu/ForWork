A wedding RSVP reminder is a short, personal note that brings in the replies you still need before your venue and caterer ask for final numbers. Send a gentle reminder before your reply date, a personal follow-up once it has passed, and a last call only if a name is still blank.

For a destination wedding, those replies carry more weight. Guests are arranging flights, leave and rooms, and each event across the weekend needs its own headcount. Below is ready-to-send wording for every channel in three tones, and a plain order for sending it.

## Key takeaways

- Time every reminder from your own reply date and the date your venue and caterer need final numbers, not from a fixed rule.
- Remind one guest or household at a time, and never send a group message that shows everyone's details.
- Match the channel to the guest: a text for friends, an email or printed card for a formal suite, a call for older relatives.
- Send three reminders at most, and keep one guest list so only the people who have not replied hear from you again.

## When to send a wedding RSVP reminder

Everything starts with your reply date. If you have not set one yet, our guide on [how to manage wedding RSVPs from invitation to final numbers](/expert-advice/wedding-rsvp) walks through choosing it and the full process around it.

Then look at the date your venue and caterer need final numbers. Your reminders sit in the gap between the two, so the earlier your suppliers need numbers, the earlier your reminders go out.

### Before your reply date

Send a gentle reminder while there is still comfortable time to answer. It reads as helpful rather than urgent, and it catches the guest who meant to reply and simply forgot.

### On the reply date

Do nothing dramatic. Check your list, note who is still outstanding and let the late replies that arrive that evening settle before you follow up.

### After the reply date

Follow up personally with each guest who has not answered. If that still leaves gaps close to your caterer's deadline, send a last call with a firm new date.

## The number of reminders to send

Plan on three steps at most: a gentle reminder, a personal follow-up and a last call. Each one goes only to guests who are still outstanding.

Beyond that, a fourth message starts to feel like pressure. A guest who has ignored three kind notes is better reached by a call from someone they know.

## Who to remind and how to reach them

Remind by household, not by individual. One message to the couple or family on the invitation is enough, addressed to the names you invited. If a guest's invitation included a plus-one, ask for that person's name in the same note; if it did not, say nothing about one.

Split the list between families. Each partner, or each set of parents, takes their own side, so every reminder comes from a familiar voice. Ask each helper to pass every answer back to you as it comes in, so your one guest list stays accurate.

Older relatives may prefer a call or a printed card to a link. Guests overseas need a little more thought: check their time zone before you call, and use the channel they already reply to you on.

Send each reminder to the one guest or household it is for. A group text or a shared email list shows everyone's names and numbers, and names, addresses and phone numbers are all examples of [personal information](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/your-personal-information/what-is-personal-information). Keep your list somewhere you trust, share it only with the people helping you, and ask them not to forward or screenshot it.

As general information as at November 2026, not legal advice: the [Privacy Act does not cover individuals acting in a personal capacity](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/social-media-and-online-privacy/photos-and-videos), and in some situations state or territory laws may also apply. Treat guest details with the same care you would want for your own.

## Wedding RSVP reminder text message wording

A text is the lightest touch, so keep it to a few lines and include the reply link or method again. Replace everything in square brackets.

### Formal

> Dear [Guest name], a gentle reminder that we would be grateful for your reply to our wedding invitation by [reply date]. You can respond at [RSVP web address]. With warm regards, [Your names].

### Warm

> Hi [Guest name], we are counting down to [month] and are hoping you can be there. Could you send your reply by [reply date]? Here is the link again: [RSVP web address]. Love, [Your names]

### Light

> [Guest name]! Quick nudge from the two of us: are you in? Reply by [reply date] at [RSVP web address] and we will save you a seat (and a glass).

If a guest may not have received the invitation, say so kindly: "We sent your invitation to [address] and want to make sure it reached you. Here is the link in case it went astray."

## RSVP reminder email wording

Email gives you room for the details guests need to plan: the reply link, the events and anything they must book.

### Subject lines

- Formal: "Your reply to [Your names]'s wedding invitation"
- Warm: "Hoping to see you in [month]"
- Light: "One small favour before [month]"

### Formal email

> Dear [Guest names],
>
> We hope this finds you well. As we finalise arrangements for our wedding on [wedding date], we would be grateful for your reply by [reply date].
>
> You can respond at [RSVP web address], or simply reply to this email. Please let us know of any dietary or access needs at the same time.
>
> With warm regards,
> [Your names]

### Warm email

> Hi [Guest names],
>
> Plans are coming together and we cannot wait to celebrate with you. When you have a moment, could you tell us whether you will be there? Our reply date is [reply date], and the link is [RSVP web address].
>
> If anything has changed or you are not sure yet, just tell us. We would rather hear "maybe" than nothing at all.
>
> Love,
> [Your names]

### Light email

> Hello [Guest names],
>
> This is your friendly reminder that a seat, a meal and a dance floor are waiting for your answer. Reply by [reply date] at [RSVP web address] and we will set a place for you.
>
> [Your names]

## RSVP reminder cards for a formal invitation suite

A printed reminder suits a formal suite and guests who replied to you by post in the past. Match the paper and type to your invitations, and repeat the reply method exactly as it appeared on the reply card. For the original reply card itself, see our [RSVP card and website wording examples](/expert-advice/wedding-rsvp-wording).

> Formal: The favour of a reply is kindly requested by [reply date]. Please respond at [RSVP web address] or by returning the enclosed card.

> Warm: We would so love to have you with us. Kindly let us know by [reply date] at [RSVP web address].

> Light: Still deciding? We are saving you a seat. Reply by [reply date] at [RSVP web address].

If the card carries a QR code to your RSVP page, print the web address beside it. Scammers can use [QR codes to hide harmful links](https://www.cyber.gov.au/guidance/learn-about-phishing-scams), and a guest cannot check where a QR code leads before scanning it. A typed address gives cautious guests another way in.

## Wedding website banner wording

A short banner at the top of your wedding website catches guests who visit for travel details and forget the reply. Our guide on [what to include on your wedding website](/expert-advice/wedding-website) covers the rest of the page.

- Formal: "Kindly reply by [reply date]. Respond here."
- Warm: "We are hoping to celebrate with you. Please reply by [reply date]."
- Light: "Not replied yet? There is still a seat with your name on it. Reply by [reply date]."

Take the banner down once your reply date has passed, so late visitors are not told they still have time.

## What to say when you call or see them in person

A call works when a message has not. Keep it short, warm and specific, and have your guest list open so you can note the answer straight away.

> "Hi [Guest name], it's [Your name]. Have you got a minute? We're giving the venue our numbers soon and we'd hate to leave you off. Are you able to come? If you're still sorting out flights or leave, that's fine, just tell me when you'll know."

For a formal tone, open with "I hope I'm not catching you at a bad time." For a lighter one, end with "And if it's a yes, tell me what you're drinking."

If you see a guest in person, ask the same question lightly and offer to note their answer for them on the spot.

## Reminding guests for a destination wedding

Guests travelling to a destination wedding are booking flights, arranging leave and choosing rooms. Your reminder should make those decisions easier, not just ask for a yes.

> Dear [Guest names], we are so looking forward to welcoming you to [destination]. Could you let us know by [reply date] whether you will join us? Your answer helps us confirm numbers with our venue, and the travel and accommodation details are at [RSVP web address]. Please also tell us about any dietary, mobility or access needs so we can plan around them.

When the celebration runs across several events, ask for a reply for each one. A guest may come to the ceremony and dinner but arrive too late for welcome drinks.

> Hi [Guest names], could you tell us which events you will join: [welcome event] on [date], the ceremony and dinner on [date], and [farewell event] on [date]? Please reply for each one at [RSVP web address] by [reply date].

## When the reply date has passed

### Last call wording

A last call is firm but kind. Give one clear new date, tied to your caterer's deadline, and make replying effortless.

> Formal: Dear [Guest name], our reply date has now passed and we are confirming final numbers with our venue. We would be grateful for your answer by [new date].

> Warm: Hi [Guest name], we are finalising numbers and would hate to miss you. Could you let us know by [new date] either way?

> Light: [Guest name], this is our very last nudge, we promise. Yes or no by [new date] and we are all set.

### Deciding whether to count a guest

If the new date passes with no answer, decide as a couple and tell your caterer which way you went. Counting a quiet guest as attending is the cautious choice for catering; counting them as declined protects a tight venue capacity. For more on planning numbers, read [how to plan for guests who say no](/wedding-tools/what-percentage-of-wedding-guests-rsvp-no).

## Keep one list so you only remind the right guests

Every reminder above depends on knowing exactly who has replied. Keep one guest list, by household, with each reply, plus-one name, dietary note and event choice recorded as it arrives.

When it is time to send reminders, filter for guests still outstanding and contact only them. Reminding someone who has already answered can quickly sour goodwill.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it rude to send an RSVP reminder for a wedding?

No. A kind, personal reminder helps guests who meant to reply and forgot. It becomes rude only when it is repeated too heavily or sent to a group.

### How do you politely remind guests to RSVP?

Write to each guest or household individually, thank them, give the reply date and repeat the link or reply method. Keep it to a few lines.

### How frequently should you remind guests to RSVP?

Three steps at most: a gentle reminder before your reply date, a personal follow-up after it and a last call if a name is still blank. Contact only guests who have not replied.

### What do you say when the RSVP deadline has passed?

Say that you are confirming final numbers, and ask for an answer by one clear new date. The last call wording above gives formal, warm and light versions.

### How do I say please RSVP as soon as possible?

Give a reason and a date rather than urgency alone: "We are confirming numbers with our venue, so could you let us know by [new date]?"

### Can family members send RSVP reminders for us?

Yes, and it works well. Split the list so each side's parents, a trusted sibling or a family assistant contacts the guests they know, and share only the names and details each helper needs.
