# Wedding RSVP website checklist

Use this while you compare RSVP websites and build your form. Ask the same questions of every tool on your shortlist, test it as a guest, and keep the answers with your wedding paperwork. Plans and features change, so check the tool's current terms on the day you decide.

## 1. Choose the kind of RSVP website
- [ ] All-in-one wedding website with RSVP: one link for your story, travel notes, schedule and the reply form. Check how far the RSVP questions can be shaped around your events.
- [ ] Standalone RSVP or form tool: suits couples who already have a site, or only need replies. Travel details and the schedule will live elsewhere.
- [ ] Invitation platform with RSVP built in: the invitation and the reply travel together. Check it goes beyond yes, no and a meal.
- [ ] Planning app with RSVP: replies feed the seating chart and guest list. Wonderland Studio, our planning app for seating charts and RSVPs, sits here.
- [ ] DIY form plus spreadsheet: full control, but every question, reminder and export is yours to set up.

## 2. Questions to ask any RSVP website

### No-cost tier or paid tier
- [ ] Guest and event limits: does our full guest list, and every event, fit under any cap?
- [ ] Custom questions: can we add our own, such as arrival dates or accommodation?
- [ ] The tool's branding: will guests see the tool's name or logo, and does removing it need a paid tier?
- [ ] Custom domain: can the site sit at a web address of our choosing, or only at the tool's own address?
- [ ] Password protection: can we lock the site so only invited guests can see it?
- [ ] Reminder sends: can we send reminders from the tool, and how many?
- [ ] Data export: can we download every answer in a format our caterer and venue can open?
- [ ] Ads shown to guests: will our guests see advertising on the page?
- [ ] If the answers that matter sit behind a paid tier, upgrade early rather than rebuild halfway through.

### Guests replying
- [ ] Do guests need to create an account or log in to reply? A sign-up step can make guests put the reply off.
- [ ] Can we set invitations by household, so a couple or family replies once for everyone named?
- [ ] Can we control plus-ones and children per invitation?
- [ ] Can we add answers ourselves for guests who reply by phone or email?

### Several events
- [ ] Can one reply cover every event (for example a welcome dinner, the wedding and a brunch the morning after), with a separate yes or no for each?
- [ ] Can we limit which events each household is invited to?
- [ ] Do our numbers show per event, not just per wedding?

### Languages and time zones
- [ ] Can the form show another language, or can we add a short translated note?
- [ ] Can the reply date be shown with a date and a time zone?

### Privacy settings
- [ ] Can the site and the replies be password protected?
- [ ] Who on our side can see the replies, and can we give family members or assistants limited access?
- [ ] Is the site hidden from search engines and public listings?
- [ ] Can we delete guest data after the wedding, and does the tool keep a copy?

### Export and handover
- [ ] Can replies flow into our seating chart without retyping?
- [ ] Can we export a clean list for the caterer, with each guest's event, meal and dietary note?
- [ ] Can we export arrival dates and accommodation for whoever is planning travel and rooms?

### QR codes
- [ ] Can we create a QR code for the RSVP page with a trusted, secure tool?
- [ ] Is the RSVP web address short and easy to type, so it can be printed beside the code?

## 3. Destination RSVP question set
Keep the form to what your planning needs. Every extra question is a reason to put the reply off.

- [ ] Name of each guest in the household, and whether each is attending
- [ ] A yes or no for each event
- [ ] Meal choice and dietary needs for each person, not each household (for example, "Any allergies or dietary needs we should share with the chef?")
- [ ] Plus-one name, where one is invited
- [ ] Children's names, where children are invited
- [ ] Arrival and departure dates
- [ ] Where they are staying
- [ ] Any access or mobility needs we should plan for
- [ ] A phone number or email for updates during the wedding week
- [ ] A short note field for anything else
- [ ] Reply date written with a date and a time zone

Keep private travel details off the form. Where a guest's travel plans should stay private, leave them off the RSVP and gather them directly. Ask only for the details your planning needs.

## 4. Before you send
- [ ] Settle the guest list by household, including who is invited to each event.
- [ ] Build the form with your events and the questions above.
- [ ] Turn on password protection and decide who can see the replies.
- [ ] Test it as a guest: reply as a couple with a child and as a guest who declines one event, and check each answer lands where you expect.
- [ ] Print the RSVP web address beside any QR code on invitations and save-the-dates.
- [ ] Test-scan the printed proof on more than one phone before the full print run.
- [ ] Offer a phone number or email for guests who would rather not reply online.

## 5. After the wedding
- [ ] Share the guest list only with the suppliers who need it.
- [ ] Delete the guest details you no longer need.
