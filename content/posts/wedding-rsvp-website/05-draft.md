# Wedding RSVP Website Guide to Collect Every Reply

A wedding RSVP website is the quiet workhorse of a destination wedding. It is where your aunt in Perth, your school friends in London and your colleague in Singapore all tell you the same things: whether they are coming, which nights they will be there, what they eat and when their flight lands.

Choose well and the replies arrive in one tidy list. This guide compares the five kinds of RSVP website, the questions worth asking before you pay for anything, and the details that matter when guests are crossing oceans to be there.

## Key takeaways

- Choose the kind of RSVP website by what your weekend needs, such as separate replies for each event and travel questions, rather than by the look of the template.
- Before paying for an upgrade, check guest and event limits, custom questions, exports, password protection and whether guests see the tool's branding or ads.
- Print the RSVP web address beside any QR code and test-scan the printed proof before the full print run.
- Ask guests only for the details your planning needs, keep the list private and delete what you no longer need after the wedding.

## What a wedding RSVP website does

At its simplest, an RSVP website replaces the reply card and the stamped envelope. A guest opens a link, finds their name, says yes or no, and the answer lands in a list you can see at any hour.

### What it replaces from the paper reply card

The reply card asked for a name, a yes or no and perhaps a meal choice. An online wedding RSVP asks the same things, then keeps going: a meal and dietary note for each person, the name of a plus-one, and the date and time each reply came in.

You see at a glance who has said yes, who has declined and who has yet to answer.

### What it gives you that a card cannot

The real gain is that answers arrive sorted, ready to filter by event, household or meal and pass to the people who need them.

If you already have a wedding site, or only want replies, a standalone RSVP form can be all you need. For everything else that belongs on the site itself, see [what to include on your wedding website](/expert-advice/wedding-website).

## The five kinds of wedding RSVP website compared

There is no single right tool, only the right kind for your guest list, judged on what a destination weekend asks of it.

### All in one wedding website with RSVP

One site holds your story, travel notes, schedule and the reply form. It suits couples who want guests to find everything in one place. The trade-off is that the RSVP is one feature among many, so check how far its questions can be shaped around your events.

### Standalone RSVP or form tool

A tool built only to collect replies. It suits couples who already have a site, or who want a form that does one job well. You will need somewhere else for travel details and the schedule, and a link between the two.

### Invitation platform with RSVP built in

The digital invitation and the reply travel together, so guests answer the moment they open it. It suits couples who are sending invitations online anyway. Check whether the RSVP can handle several events and travel questions, or whether it stops at yes, no and a meal. Our guide to [making a digital wedding invitation](/wedding-tools/how-to-make-a-digital-wedding-invitation) covers the invitation side.

### Planning app with RSVP

Here the replies feed straight into the rest of your planning, such as the seating chart and the guest list. It suits couples who would rather not move data from one tool to another. Wonderland Studio, our planning app for seating charts and RSVPs, sits in this group. For a wider view, see our [wedding planning apps compared](/expert-advice/best-wedding-planning-apps).

### DIY form plus spreadsheet

A general online form feeding a shared spreadsheet. It suits couples who like full control and do not mind building it themselves. The cost is your time: every question, reminder and export is yours to set up.

## What changes between paid tiers and those at no cost

Plans change often, so run the same questions on any tool you are considering. Each can decide whether a no-cost tier will see you through or a paid tier is worth it.

- **Guest and event limits.** Does your full list fit under any cap?
- **Custom questions.** Can you add your own, such as arrival dates or accommodation?
- **The tool's branding.** Will guests see the tool's name or logo, and does removing it need a paid tier?
- **Custom domain.** Can the site sit at a web address of your choosing, or only at the tool's own address?
- **Password protection.** Can you lock the site so only invited guests can see it?
- **Reminder sends.** Can you send reminders from the tool, and how many?
- **Data export.** Can you download every answer in a format your caterer and venue can open?
- **Ads shown to guests.** Will your guests see advertising on the page?

If the answers that matter sit behind a paid tier, upgrade early rather than rebuild halfway through.

## Features that matter for destination weddings and several events

A destination wedding is rarely one event. It is a welcome dinner, the ceremony and reception, and a slow brunch the morning after, with guests arriving from different countries.

### Separate RSVPs for each event

Look for a form where one reply covers every event, with a separate yes or no for each. A guest may be at the ceremony but land too late for the welcome dinner, and your numbers for each event should show it.

### Travel and accommodation questions

Ask where each guest is staying, whether they would like a room in your block, and their arrival and departure dates. Those answers shape room numbers and the welcome dinner. Your Wedded Advisor plans venue, travel, vendors and payments with you, so these answers have somewhere useful to go.

### Households, partners and children

Set each invitation by household, so a couple or a family replies once for everyone named. Decide in advance whether guests may bring a plus-one and whether children are invited, and let the form reflect it.

### Meal and dietary needs per person

Ask for meal choices and dietary needs for each person, not each household. Keep the question to what the caterer needs to cook safely, for example, "Any allergies or dietary needs we should share with the chef?"

### Time zones and languages

When guests live in other countries, write your reply date with a date and a time zone, so nobody in another time zone misses it by a day. If some family read more comfortably in another language, check whether the form can show it, or add a short translated note.

### Exporting numbers for your venue and caterer

The RSVP is the start of the seating plan, not the end of the job. Before you choose, check that the replies can flow into your seating chart without retyping, and that you can export a clean list for the caterer, with each guest's event, meal and dietary note. Allow in your planning for guests who decline; our piece on [the share of guests who RSVP no](/wedding-tools/what-percentage-of-wedding-guests-rsvp-no) is a useful read.

## QR code RSVPs and when to print the link too

A QR code on a printed invitation or details card lets guests reach your RSVP page with a phone camera. It is a graceful shortcut, with one caution.

Fake QR codes exist, and scammers use them to hide harmful links. <!-- fact_08 --> Because a QR code is an image rather than text, you can't check the link before scanning it. <!-- fact_09 --> The [Australian Cyber Security Centre](https://www.cyber.gov.au/guidance/learn-about-phishing-scams) advises going directly to official websites rather than following links in messages. <!-- fact_10 -->

So print the RSVP web address beside the code, short and easy to type, for guests who would rather not scan. Create the code with a trusted, secure tool <!-- fact_11 -->, then test-scan the printed proof, on more than one phone, before the full print run.

## What to ask on your RSVP form

Every extra question is a reason to put the reply off. Keep the form to what your planning needs:

- Name of each guest in the household, and whether each is attending
- A yes or no for each event
- Meal choice and dietary needs for each person
- Plus-one name, where one is invited
- Arrival and departure dates, and where they are staying
- A phone number or email for updates during the wedding week
- A short note field for anything else

## How to set up your RSVP website step by step

1. **Settle the guest list by household,** including who is invited to each event.
2. **Choose the kind of tool.** Use the table above, then run the list of tier questions on your shortlist.
3. **Build the form** with your events and the questions above.
4. **Set privacy.** Turn on password protection and decide who on your side can see the replies.
5. **Test it as a guest.** Reply as a couple with a child and as a guest who declines one event, and check each answer lands where you expect.
6. **Add the link and QR code to your invitations.** Print the web address beside the code and test-scan the proof.
7. **Send, then check replies against your guest list** as they arrive.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which type of wedding RSVP website suits a destination wedding?

One that handles several events in one reply and lets you add travel and accommodation questions. An all-in-one wedding website or a planning app with RSVP can both do this.

### Can you collect wedding RSVPs online without paying?

Yes, on a no-cost tier. Check guest and event limits, custom questions, export, branding and ads before you commit.

### Can guests RSVP without creating an account?

It depends on the tool, so check before you choose: a sign-up step can make guests put the reply off.

### Can one RSVP website handle several wedding events?

Some can, with a separate yes or no for each event in a single reply. Test it as a guest before you send.

### How do QR code RSVPs work on a printed invitation?

The code opens your RSVP page on the guest's phone. Print the web address beside it, create the code with a trusted tool and test-scan the proof before printing.

### Is it okay for wedding guests to RSVP online?

Yes. Offer a phone number or email for guests who would rather reply another way, and add their answers yourself.
