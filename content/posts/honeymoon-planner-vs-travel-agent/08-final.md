Honeymoon planner vs travel agent sounds like a choice between two kinds of business. The short answer: compare the service, not the title. The useful comparison is the service a particular business will actually provide, and this guide gives you the questions that show it.

Put the same questions to anyone you are considering, ask for the answers in writing and set them side by side. The questions below cover what they will do, how they are paid and what happens when plans change.

## Key takeaways

- Whichever title a business uses, compare what it will actually do.
- Ask every planner or agent the same questions, and get the answers in writing.
- Australian consumer guarantees, supplier terms, insurance timing and insolvency risk apply whichever label you choose.
- If the honeymoon follows a destination wedding, ask whether the same business can plan the wedding travel and the honeymoon as one plan.

Before you speak to anyone, agree between you what kind of help you want.

### How much of the planning do you want to hand over?

Some couples love comparing rooms late at night; others want to describe a feeling and receive a finished itinerary. Decide whether you want someone to design the trip, to book what you have already chosen, or to check your plan and take on the parts you would rather not. Planning it yourselves is a real option too, and your consumer rights do not depend on who makes the booking. <!-- fact_02 -->

### Is the honeymoon straight after the wedding, or later?

A honeymoon that leaves the morning after a destination wedding shares flights, dates and luggage with the wedding itself. One taken months later is a separate trip with its own timing. Know which before you ask anyone to quote.

### How many moving parts does the trip have?

A slow week in one place is a different job from several countries, internal flights and a boat between islands. Count the stops, flights, time zones and bookings that depend on each other: that is the coordination you are asking for.

## Questions about what they will actually do

Whatever a business calls itself, its answers to these questions are what you are comparing.

### Will you design the trip, or book what we choose?

Ask whether the business will suggest ideas or book your shortlist, and whether it will tell you when something on your list does not suit the season or the route.

### Who looks after flights, connections and the days in between?

Ask who books the flights, who checks that arrivals line up with check-in, and who arranges the days between stays. If the answer is "some of it", ask which parts stay with you.

### Do you work across suppliers, or with a set list?

Ask whether they can book any hotel or experience you find, or work with particular suppliers. Then ask whether they receive anything from the suppliers they recommend, and whether that is disclosed to you.

## Questions about how they are paid

A fee only makes sense once you know how it is worked out. Ask each business the same questions, and ask for the answers in writing:

- Is there a planning fee, and what does it cover?
- Is it a set amount, a share of the trip, or something else?
- Do you earn commission from the places you book, and is that shown to us?
- Does any supplier price you quote include a mark-up, and can we see the supplier's own price?
- When are deposits and the fee payable, and is any of it refundable if our plans change?
- Will the quote be in Australian dollars, with your fee shown separately from the trip?

## Questions about what happens when plans change

As at November 2026, Australian consumer guarantees give you rights if a travel service is delayed or cancelled. <!-- fact_01 --> They apply whether you book directly with an airline, cruise line or tour operator, through a travel agent, or through a booking platform or package provider. <!-- fact_02 -->

If you book through an intermediary, both its terms and the provider's apply, so read both sets before you pay. <!-- fact_05 -->

The guarantees do not require a refund or replacement if you change your mind or miss a service for reasons unrelated to it. In those cases, the booking's terms decide what you get back. <!-- fact_04 --> The [ACCC's guide to travel delays and cancellations](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/travel-delays-and-cancellations) sets out the detail.

### Who do we contact while we are away?

Ask for a name, a contact method and the hours, and whether you call the business, the airline or the hotel first if something goes wrong in the middle of the night at home.

### What happens if a flight or booking changes?

- If a provider changes or cancels something, who tells us, and who asks for the replacement or refund?
- What do your terms say if we cancel or change plans ourselves?
- Can we see each supplier's terms before we pay, and will you confirm anything unclear in writing? <!-- fact_06 -->

Whether you get money back usually comes down to who makes the change. You are more likely, though not always, to be eligible for a refund or credit if the provider cancels; if you cancel or change plans, it may be treated as change of mind. <!-- fact_16 -->

If timing matters, allow extra time between connections. <!-- fact_07 -->

### What does our travel insurance need to cover?

Anything known before you buy the policy is not covered if it directly affects your travel, and the earlier you buy, the more likely you are to be covered if something unexpected happens before you leave. <!-- fact_14 -->

Ask about insolvency too. Cover for a travel provider or agent going out of business is commonly excluded, and only a few insurers offer it. <!-- fact_17 --> Ask whoever you book through whether they hold insolvency insurance. <!-- fact_17 -->

For cover, stopovers and what to declare, see [Smartraveller's travel insurance guide](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance).

## When the honeymoon follows a destination wedding

When the honeymoon begins where the wedding ends, the two trips share dates, flights and luggage. The real question is one plan or two, and either can work if everyone knows where one trip ends and the next begins.

Ask any business you are considering:

- Can you plan the honeymoon together with our wedding travel, or only one of them?
- If only one, who checks that the dates and flights line up?
- Whose name goes on the honeymoon tickets?

On that last point, book travel in the name on your passport. <!-- fact_33 -->

## Frequently asked questions

### Do we need a travel agent for our honeymoon?

That is your choice. Consumer guarantees apply whether you book directly or through an agent or intermediary. <!-- fact_02 --> The choice rests on how much planning you want to hand over and how many moving parts the trip has.

### Can one business plan both our wedding travel and our honeymoon?

Ask directly: whether the same person plans both, and how dates and flights are checked between the two. Get the answer in writing, alongside the fee.

### Are our rights different if we book through an agent?

Consumer guarantees still apply whether you book with the provider, an agent or a booking platform. <!-- fact_02 --> What changes is the paperwork: the agent's terms and the provider's terms both apply. <!-- fact_05 -->

### What should we ask first?

Start with two questions: who will we deal with from first call to return, and what will you confirm in writing before we commit?
