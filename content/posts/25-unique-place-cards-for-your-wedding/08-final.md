A place card is the smallest piece of stationery at your wedding and the one every guest picks up. These ideas for wedding place cards are grouped by style, so you can shortlist in a minute: classic paper, natural and edible, keepsakes, glass and acrylic, local touches, and cards that sit on the napkin or plate.

After the ideas: how the cards work with your seating plan, how to keep them readable after dark, and what changes when they travel overseas.

## Key takeaways

- Shortlist from six style groups (classic paper, natural and edible, keepsakes, glass and acrylic, local touches, napkin or plate), then check your choice survives wind, heat and travel.
- Take every name from one source, your finished seating plan, and check spelling, titles and preferred names before anything is made.
- Edible place cards are food, so give your caterer the list of what is in them and of guests with allergies.
- For a destination wedding, decide early whether the cards are made at home and carried or made near the venue, and who sets them out on the day.

## Place Cards and Escort Cards

An escort card waits near the entrance and tells a guest which table they are at; a place card sits on the table and marks the exact seat. Our guide to [escort cards vs place cards](/wedding-tools/escort-cards-vs-place-cards) sets out how each works.

## Classic Stationery Place Cards

Paper takes any lettering, colour or fold.

### Calligraphy and hand lettering

A folded card with a guest's name in ink suits a formal dinner, a ballroom or a long table under a pergola. Ask for a lettering sample on the actual card stock, as some inks feather on textured paper.

### Tiles

A small ceramic or terracotta tile with a painted or printed name sits flat and stays put in a breeze. It suits a Mediterranean courtyard. Tiles are heavier than paper, so plan how they reach the venue.

### Marble and stone effect cards

A slice of marble, or a card printed to look like stone, looks cool and polished against white linen. Real stone is weighty and can chip in transit; a printed version keeps the look without the luggage.

### Bookmarks

A long, narrow card shaped like a bookmark lies across the plate. It suits a literary couple or a library venue. Keep the name near the top so it reads when the card lies flat.

## Natural and Edible Place Cards

Natural place cards bring the garden to the table and pair easily with your florals.

### Leaves

A broad leaf such as magnolia, fig or olive takes a name in white or gold ink. It suits a garden wedding. Leaves curl in heat, so letter them close to the day and keep them cool.

### Rosemary rings and herb sprigs

A small ring of rosemary tied around a folded napkin, with a name tag on twine, adds scent as well as colour; thyme or lavender work too. A ring holds the tag in place in a breeze.

### A piece of fruit

A lemon, pear or fig with a small tag on a ribbon suits a summer table or a coastal lunch. Choose fruit that sits steady on the plate and is in season where you are marrying.

### Macarons, biscuits and sweets

A biscuit iced with a name, a macaron in a small box, or a sweet wrapped with a tag doubles as dessert. Heat and humidity soften icing and chocolate, so check where the cards will wait before service.

Food Standards Australia New Zealand notes that a food allergy reaction can include anaphylaxis, and in Australia [the Food Standards Code requires](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/labelling/allergen-labelling) certain foods and substances to be declared when they are present as ingredients. <!-- fact_02 --> For food that doesn't carry a label, allergen declarations must be displayed in connection with the food or provided on request. <!-- fact_01 --> Give your caterer the list of what is in each edible card and of guests with allergies. For a wedding overseas, ask the venue how its kitchen handles allergens. This is general information rather than legal advice.

## Keepsake Place Cards That Double as Favours

<!-- IMAGE SLOT keepsake: Close detail of a keepsake place card: e.g. a small engraved wooden or ceramic tag, mini candle or tiny jar with a name tag, set on a dinner plate. Soft daylight, neutral palette. Placeholder name only. No people, no maker's mark or stationer branding. 4:5. -->

A keepsake place card marks the seat and is the favour, which keeps the table tidy. If you would like the seating itself to become a talking point, our [interactive seating chart ideas](/expert-advice/our-favourite-interactive-seating-charts) pair well with this group.

### Personalised glassware and shot glasses

A glass etched or printed with a guest's name marks the seat and gives them something to drink from all evening. Confirm the venue is happy to serve in them.

### Small stones and agate

A smooth river stone or a slice of agate takes a name in metallic ink. Agate suits a jewel-toned palette, pale stones a coastal setting. Both are heavy enough to ignore a gust of wind.

### Slate hearts and keys

A small slate heart takes chalk or paint pen lettering and suits a rustic or industrial venue. A vintage key with a tag on ribbon suits a manor house. Both pack easily.

### A small plant

A succulent or herb in a tiny pot, with a name on a marker stick, brings green to every setting. For a wedding overseas, source plants near the venue rather than packing them.

## Glass, Mirror and Acrylic Place Cards

<!-- IMAGE SLOT glass-acrylic: Clear acrylic or glass tile place card with white or gold lettering (placeholder name) standing at a place setting; backlit by candlelight or window light so the lettering reads. Crystal glassware, minimal florals. No people, no branding, no recognisable venue. Landscape 3:2. -->

Clear and reflective materials sit quietly on a busy table and catch candlelight. They suit a rooftop or a city ballroom.

### Laser cut acrylic names

A name cut from acrylic in a single flowing script can rest on the plate or hook over the rim of a glass. Clear acrylic disappears against white linen, so a tinted or frosted finish, or a contrasting lettering colour, keeps the name readable.

### Mirror cards

A small mirror tile with the name in white or gold lettering reflects the candles around it. Mirror marks easily, so ask whoever sets the tables to wear gloves or give each card a final polish.

### Names on wine glasses

Lettering applied directly to each wine glass removes the need for a card. Check with the venue first, as it may supply its own glassware and not allow lettering on it.

Far from home, the place cards can carry a little of the place itself, without filling a suitcase.

### Shells for a beach setting

A scallop or oyster shell with a name in fine ink, or a row of mussel shells holding small tags, belongs on a table by the sea. Check local rules before gathering shells from a beach, or ask your stylist to source them.

### Wine cork holders for a vineyard

A cork with a slit cut into the top holds a small card upright. It suits a winery wedding. Ask the winery whether it can supply corks from its own bottles.

### Local fruit or materials from the region

Lemons on the Italian coast, olive sprigs in Provence or frangipani in Bali tie the table to the view. Local ceramics or woven palm work as well. Your florist or stylist on the ground will know what is in season on your date.

## Place Cards on the Napkin or Plate

Sometimes the neatest card rests on the setting itself. These ideas suit a crowded table and fit any of the styles in our guide to [wedding table settings](/style-advice/22-amazing-table-settings-for-every-wedding-theme).

### A name tag tied to the napkin

A small tag tied with ribbon, twine or a sprig of greenery around a folded napkin suits an outdoor table, as the napkin holds the tag down.

### A card resting on the plate

A flat card laid on the charger plate, or tucked under the fold of a napkin, keeps sight lines across the table clear. It suits a long, narrow table where guests talk across the centre.

### A menu with the name printed

A menu printed with each guest's name at the top doubles as the place card at a plated dinner. Allow time for a final proof.

## How to Do Wedding Place Cards

### Spelling, titles and preferred names

Take every name from one source: your finished seating plan. Build the plan in [Wonderland Studio](/wonderland-studio), our planning app for seating charts and RSVPs, and send your stationer the names from there. Check titles and honorifics, preferred names and every surname, and agree early how senior family members are addressed. If you are still placing guests, read [how to make a wedding seating chart](/expert-advice/wedding-seating-chart) first.

### Legibility in evening light

Queensland guidance for events says signage needs to be [clear, logical, accurate and accessible](https://www.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0030/470865/queensland-government-accessible-events-guide.pdf). <!-- fact_03 --> The same guide suggests keeping placement, size and colour consistent, and limiting each sign to three or four pieces of information, because people tend to glance rather than read. <!-- fact_04 --> For place cards, that means a name in strong contrast and large enough for older relatives to read, the same position on every setting, and little else on the card. Test a sample by candlelight: if it isn't legible at a glance, choose a bolder ink or a plainer script.

### Outdoor wind and heat

On a terrace, paper cards fly. Weight them with a holder, tie them to the napkin, or choose a heavier material such as tile, stone or glass. In heat, keep anything natural or edible cool until the tables are set.

### When to order

Order or make the cards once RSVPs have closed and the seating plan is settled. Leave room for late changes and a proof, and keep a few blank cards and a matching pen for the unexpected plus one.

## Place Cards for a Destination Wedding

Place cards for a wedding overseas raise a few questions to settle before you choose a style.

**Make at home and carry, or make near the venue?** Making them at home lets you approve every card. Making them near the venue saves packing space and opens up local materials.

**Will the cards survive the journey?** Paper creases, stone adds weight and glass needs wrapping. Carry finished cards flat and boxed in your hand luggage.

**Will they survive the climate?** Humidity softens card stock, and heat wilts leaves and melts chocolate. Choose for the season at the venue.

**Who sets them out?** Someone needs the plan, the cards and time before guests arrive, and a long guest list may need a small team. Agree in writing whether that is the venue, your planner or your stylist, and whether it is included in what you pay them.

**Can local produce stand in for imported items?** Often it can, and it ties the table to the setting.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you need place cards if you have escort cards?

Not always. If guests choose their own seat at an assigned table, escort cards alone can be enough. For a plated dinner with meal choices, place cards help the team serve each guest correctly.

### What goes on a wedding place card?

The guest's name, written as they prefer to be addressed. Some couples add a meal choice symbol for the catering team.

### Who gets a place card at a wedding?

Every guest with an assigned seat, children and the head table included.

### When should you order or make wedding place cards?

After RSVPs close and the seating plan is settled, with room left for late changes and a proof.

### Can place cards double as wedding favours?

Yes. Glassware, small stones, slate hearts, plants and edible cards all mark the seat and serve as the favour.

### What can I use instead of place cards?

A name tag tied to the napkin, a card resting on the plate, a menu printed with each guest's name, or lettering on the wine glass all do the same job as wedding name cards.
