# Booking checklist: wedding place cards

Work through this once your seating plan is forming and you have a shortlist of place card ideas. Tick each item as it is settled, and ask for the stationer's and venue's answers in writing.

## Place card planning

**Style**

- [ ] Choose a style group: classic paper, natural and edible, keepsake, glass and acrylic, local touches, or on the napkin or plate.
- [ ] Check the choice suits the setting: indoors or outdoors, day or evening, and the season at the venue.

**Names**

- [ ] Take every name from one source: your finished seating plan.
- [ ] Check the spelling of every first name and surname.
- [ ] Check titles and honorifics (for example Dr, Mr and Mrs) and preferred names.
- [ ] Agree how senior family members are addressed.
- [ ] Confirm who gets a card: every guest with an assigned seat, children and the head table included.
- [ ] Keep a few blank cards and a matching pen for late changes.

**Legibility**

- [ ] The name is in strong contrast to the card or material it sits on.
- [ ] The name is large enough for older relatives to read from the chair.
- [ ] Little else is on the card (the name, plus a meal choice symbol only if the catering team needs it).
- [ ] Every card sits in the same position on each setting.
- [ ] Test one finished sample by candlelight or at the hour the meal is served.

**Edible cards and allergens**

- [ ] Give your caterer the list of what is in each edible card.
- [ ] Give your caterer the list of guests with allergies.
- [ ] Agree what goes on the setting of a guest with a relevant allergy (for example a non-edible card with the same look).
- [ ] For a wedding overseas, ask the venue how its kitchen handles allergens.
- [ ] Check where edible cards wait before service, away from heat and humidity.

This is general information as at November 2026, not legal advice.

**Outdoor wind and heat**

- [ ] Paper cards on a terrace are weighted, held in a holder or tied to the napkin.
- [ ] Or choose a heavier material such as tile, stone or glass.
- [ ] Anything natural or edible is kept cool until the tables are set.

**Travel (destination weddings)**

- [ ] Decide early: made at home and carried, or made near the venue.
- [ ] If carried, pack finished cards flat and boxed in hand luggage.
- [ ] Check local rules before gathering shells or other natural materials.

**Setting out on the day**

- [ ] Agree in writing who sets the cards out: the venue, your planner or your stylist.
- [ ] Confirm whether setting out is included in what you pay them.
- [ ] That person has the final seating plan, the cards and time before guests arrive.
- [ ] A long guest list may need a small team.

## Questions for the stationer

- [ ] Can we see a sample of our chosen idea, with lettering on the actual card stock or material?
- [ ] Please send a written, itemised quote, showing the deposit and the delivery date.
- [ ] Is a proof included before the full set is made, and how are late name changes handled?
- [ ] How will the cards be packed for delivery or for travel?
- [ ] We will share the final name list from our seating plan only once we have booked you.

## Questions for the venue

- [ ] Who sets out the place cards on the day, and is that included?
- [ ] When can the tables be set, and how long before guests arrive?
- [ ] Do you supply your own glassware? Can we use lettered or personalised glasses, or apply names to yours?
- [ ] Can we bring items in (stone, tiles, glass, plants, edible cards, fresh leaves or fruit), and are there any limits?
- [ ] Where can edible or natural cards be kept cool before service?
- [ ] For an outdoor table, what is the plan if the wind picks up?

## Order of decisions

1. Seating plan settled and RSVPs closed
2. Style group and idea chosen
3. Sample and written quote from the stationer
4. Booking, then the final name list sent
5. Proof checked against the seating plan
6. Who sets the cards out, agreed in writing with the venue
