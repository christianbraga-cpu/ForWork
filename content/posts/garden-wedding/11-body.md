## A garden wedding in brief

A garden wedding is a ceremony, a reception or both held outdoors among planted grounds, lawns or orchards. It works when the styling and the backup plan are decided together, because the garden that sets the mood also needs a plan for the weather.

Below are eight garden weddings from our own pages, styling ideas from the aisle to the stationery, and the questions to put to a venue before you fall for its lawn. For other couples and settings, browse [more real weddings from our pages](/blog/real-weddings).

## Key takeaways

- The eight real garden weddings below come from our own published stories, each linked to the full account.
- Let an existing path set the line of the aisle, keep aisle markers low, and design the lighting for the hour your reception ends, not the hour it begins.
- Before you book, confirm that the covered space holds every guest seated for both the ceremony and dinner, who makes the call to move under cover and by what time on the day.
- A garden wedding abroad adds questions about the garden's months, guest travel and who looks after the day on the venue's side.

## Garden wedding ideas for the ceremony

A garden already has structure, so the ceremony ideas that hold up use it rather than cover it.

### Aisles and arches

<!-- IMAGE SLOT floral-arch: A ceremony arch or framed focal point dressed in florals and greenery at the end of a grass or gravel aisle in a garden, with aisle markers (petals, low arrangements or lanterns) leading to it. Even daylight, the arch filling the upper frame. No people. Flowers described only by colour and form in alt and caption, never by variety or season unless sourced. Let the garden set the axis. An existing path, an avenue of trees or a gap in a hedge gives the aisle its line, and the arch goes where the eye already lands.

A timber or metal arch dressed asymmetrically with greenery reads as planted; a pair of large urns either side of an old tree does the same job with less build. Keep aisle markers low, such as petals or lanterns at the end of each row, so no one's view is blocked.

### Ceremony seating in the garden

Straight rows suit a formal lawn; curved rows suit a courtyard or an orchard and bring the back rows closer. Put the front rows, and the grandparents, where the shade falls at the hour of the ceremony, with every row on level ground.

The reception carries the garden through to the table, from the linen to the light.

One long table under trees turns dinner into a single conversation, and two or three parallel runs do the same for a larger guest list. A plain cloth in stone, sage or white lets the planting do the work; a printed linen, as at Valerie Lungu and Alex Gilka's wedding, becomes the centrepiece in its own right.

### Florals and greenery as design choices

Think in colour and form before you think in varieties. A tonal palette of white and green sits quietly against a garden; a single strong colour pulled from the planting around you ties the table to the view.

Loose, trailing arrangements look gathered from the beds; structured ones look deliberate against a clipped hedge. Low runs keep sight lines open, while a hanging installation, like the flower ceiling at Tina and Steven's wedding, moves the flowers overhead and leaves the table clear.

See [wedding centrepieces that suit a long garden table](/style-advice/wedding-centrepieces), and check [which wedding flowers are in season in Australia](/style-advice/fashionable-flowers-opt-for-in-season-or-order-imported) before you settle the palette.

### Lighting for dusk

Design the lighting for the hour your reception ends, not the hour it begins. Festoon lights strung between trees give the space a ceiling, and fairy lights threaded through branches keep the canopy visible after dark. Samira and Tom styled their reception with fairy lights.

Lanterns along paths look considered and show guests the way to the bathrooms. Candlelight on the table is the softest layer; its safety side sits in the planning section below.

### Lawn games and lounges

Croquet, as at Paige and Todd's reception, or quoits and bowls give guests something to do between the ceremony and dinner.

Rugs, low sofas and cushions under a tree turn a corner of the garden into a lounge, and suit [open seating at a relaxed garden reception](/expert-advice/wedding-without-seating-chart), where guests move between groups instead of staying at one table.

### Stationery and signage

Garden stationery has to survive a breeze, so weight menus under a napkin and choose a heavier card for anything that stands. Use the invitation to tell guests what the ground is like, and on the day, put up one clear sign showing where the covered space is.

## The garden party wedding feel

A garden party wedding is a theme, not a dress code. It leans towards daylight: an afternoon ceremony, a long lunch outdoors and lawn games. Mismatched china, printed linen and flowers that look picked rather than arranged all read as garden party.

## What to plan for with your venue

Plan a garden for its weather, its ground and its neighbours. Each point below ends in questions to ask before you book, and the answers belong in writing.

### Wet weather plan and covered space

<!-- IMAGE SLOT wet-weather-plan: A clear-span marquee or covered pavilion set beside or within a garden for a wedding: open or clear sides showing lawn and planting, tables or ceremony seating set inside, soft daylight or evening light inside. No people. No branding or hire company signage visible. Must not imply a specific venue offers this. Treat the backup plan as a second venue and see it on your site visit.

- Where is the covered space, and does it hold every guest seated for both the ceremony and dinner?
- Who makes the call to move under cover, and by what time on the day?
- Can a marquee be added if the indoor space is too small, and what does the venue require for one?

If a marquee is your covered space, read [planning a marquee as your covered space](/everything-you-need-to-know-about-planning-a-marquee-wedding) first.

### Heat, shade and sun

A daytime ceremony in Australia needs sun protection as well as shade. [Cancer Council's sun safety advice](https://www.cancer.org.au/cancer-information/causes-and-prevention/sun-safety/be-sunsmart) recommends sun protection whenever UV levels reach 3 or higher, and UV cannot be seen or felt, so check the UV for the location on the SunSmart app or the Bureau of Meteorology website.

Shade from trees or built structures reduces UV, but UV can still reach people by reflection, so pair shaded seating with covering clothing, hats, sunscreen and sunglasses. Set a sunscreen station out well before guests take their seats, since sunscreen goes on at least 20 minutes before going outside.

- What shade covers the ceremony area and the drinks lawn, and is it trees or a built structure?
- Where does the shade fall at the hour of the ceremony?
- Can a sunshade or marquee be added over the seating?
- Where can guests get drinking water outdoors?

### Ground and footwear

Heels sink into soft lawn and catch in gravel, and hire chairs rock on a slope.

- Is the ceremony on lawn, gravel or paving, and is any of it on a slope?
- Is the lawn watered close to the day, and how soft does it get?
- Can flooring be laid under the dance floor and the tables?

### Access for older guests and a route without steps

Walk the day as your oldest guest would.

- Is there a route without steps from arrival to the ceremony, the reception and the bathrooms?
- Is the surface firm underfoot the whole way?
- Is that route lit after dark?
- Is there a drop-off point close to the ceremony, and seating for guests who cannot stand for long?

### Insects

At dusk, a garden belongs to its insects as much as to your guests.

- Does the venue treat the gardens for insects, and how long before the event?
- Are there ponds or water features near the tables?
- Can citronella, fans or screens be used in the reception area?

### Noise and curfew

Sound carries across an open garden to the neighbours. Ask about limits rather than assume them.

- Is there a limit on amplified music outdoors, and at what time must it stop?
- What time must guests leave, and when must pack down be finished?

### Power and lighting

A candle is an open flame and can easily ignite anything combustible nearby, so keep the immediate area around it clear and well away from anything that can catch fire, such as paper and flammable decorations. Use sturdy holders that will not tip, made of material that cannot burn, and remember that any air movement past a candle can make the wax melt unevenly, and the candle may then become unstable and fall over. [Battery candles are a safe alternative](https://www.mfs.sa.gov.au/community-safety/safety-and-education/fact-sheets/candles2/mfs-fact-sheet-candles.pdf).

- Where are the outdoor power points, and what can they run at once?
- Is a generator needed, and where can it sit out of earshot?
- Are the paths and the car park lit after dark?
- Are naked flames allowed outdoors, and who puts them out at pack down?

### Garden protection rules

Ask for the garden's rules in writing before you plan the styling.

- Are confetti, petals or rice allowed, and which kinds?
- Can arches, signs or lanterns be staked into the lawn?
- Where can marquee pegs go, and where are the irrigation lines?
- Which areas are closed to guests, and can vehicles drive onto the lawn for set up?
- Can the gardens be closed to the public and to other guests for the whole event?

## Destination garden weddings

A garden abroad asks every question above, with distance added.

- Which months does the venue recommend for its garden, and what does the garden look like in yours?
- What is the covered backup, and is it on the same property?
- How do guests travel between the hotel and the garden, and how long does it take?
- Is that transfer suitable for guests with limited mobility, and where is the nearest medical help?
- Who is on the venue's side on the day, and do they stay until the end of the night?
- If something goes wrong on the day, who answers for it, and how do you reach them?
- Who holds your deposits, and is the contract in English with the currency of each payment stated?
- What does the garden allow for set up and pack down, and over how many hours?

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a garden wedding?

A ceremony, a reception or both held outdoors among planted grounds, lawns or orchards. It works when the styling and the wet weather plan are decided together.

### Can I have a wedding in my own garden?

Yes, and the same questions apply: covered space for every guest, power outdoors, a route without steps and lighting after dark.

### What is a garden party theme for a wedding?

A daylight theme built around an afternoon ceremony, a long lunch outdoors, lawn games and relaxed styling.

### What is the dress code for a garden party wedding?

Whatever the invitation sets, ideally with a note on what the ground is like underfoot. For outfit ideas, see [what guests wear to a garden wedding](/what-to-wear-to-a-garden-wedding).

### What happens if it rains at a garden wedding?

The wedding moves to the covered space agreed with the venue. Before you book, confirm where it is, that it holds every guest seated, and who makes the call and by what time.
