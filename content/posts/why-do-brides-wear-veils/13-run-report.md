## How the topic is handled
- History in brief, from the reference and museum facts: the Roman bride's flame-coloured veil; early Christian bridal veiling; Queen Victoria's 1840 Honiton lace veil, worn back rather than over her face; veils of the 1850s to 1902 worn at the back, kept in the family and trimmed with orange blossom. Royal veils are limited to Victoria.
- Meaning: the one folklore meaning a reference prints (luck from borrowing a happily married bride's veil), linked to the Something Old article. Popular claims (protection from spirits, hiding the bride) are not used.
- Faith: the Jewish badeken, tied to Jewish tradition with its own explanation. No Hindu or Muslim claims (no source); one line notes that many faiths and cultures have their own bridal head coverings.
- Today: who lifts the veil and when, the blusher, wearing it back, heirloom veils, a veil for wind and heat at a destination, keeping it in hand luggage and insuring it, a wedding-morning checklist (who holds it, where it waits, who returns a borrowed one), and choosing not to wear one.
