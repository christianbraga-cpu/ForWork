A name change after marriage in Australia is a choice, and it does not happen on its own: you update each record yourself. As at November 2026, if you married in Australia, NSW, Victoria and Queensland all let you do that with your official marriage certificate, without a formal change of name.

Marry overseas and the picture shifts. NSW and Victoria both say you will need to apply for a change of name. Below is the order to work in, what each state says, and the passport choice that matters when a honeymoon follows the wedding.

## Key takeaways

- If you married in Australia, NSW, Victoria and Queensland let you take your spouse's surname using your official marriage certificate, with no formal change of name.
- Agencies want the official certificate issued by the registry, not the ceremonial one signed on the day.
- Following the NSW order, get the official certificate first, then update your driver licence and Medicare, which gives you the proof to change your name almost everywhere else.
- If you married overseas, NSW and Victoria require an official change of name, and the Passport Office needs any document not in English translated in full.

## Does marriage automatically change your name in Australia?

No. Signing the register changes nothing on your licence, passport or bank account. In Queensland's words, you need to contact each organisation to change your name. Until you do, every record stays exactly as it was.

Changing is also optional, and it is not only for one partner. NSW describes taking your spouse's surname, or adding it to your own, as something you can do. In NSW, your partner can equally take yours. The full detail sits on the [NSW Registry's surname page](https://www.nsw.gov.au/family-and-relationships/name-changes-and-corrections/change-your-surname-after-marriage).

The one firm exception is an overseas wedding. Victoria says that if you married overseas, you can't take your spouse's family name automatically and need to apply to change your name. More on that below.

## Your options: take, keep, hyphenate or combine

What matters legally is which options work on the marriage certificate alone, and which need a formal application.

### Taking your partner's surname

This is the simplest route if you married in Australia. NSW, Victoria and Queensland all let you take your spouse's family name using your marriage certificate. In Victoria, you don't need to apply for a change of name: you give the relevant organisations a copy of your Australian marriage certificate.

### Keeping your name

Keeping your name asks nothing of you. There is no form to lodge and no record to update, because you are simply not changing anything.

### Hyphenated or double-barrelled surnames

In NSW you can combine your name and your partner's name with a hyphen on the strength of your marriage certificate. Victoria and Queensland both say that both partners can hyphenate their family names.

Anything beyond that needs paperwork. In NSW, for any other change of name, you need to apply to change your name. A double-barrelled name without the hyphen, or a newly blended surname you both adopt, falls into that category.

### Changing a first or middle name

In NSW, a first or middle name change is not one of the surname options your marriage certificate covers, so it goes through a formal application.

### When a partner takes your surname

In NSW, your partner can take your surname in exactly the way you could take theirs. Victoria's rule, taking your spouse's family name, applies to either of you.

The handsome certificate you sign at the ceremony is a keepsake. Victoria says the commemorative certificate you got on your wedding day isn't the official, legal marriage certificate, and most organisations will only accept the one the registry issues. NSW says ceremonial or commemorative certificates are not accepted. Centrelink does not accept them as a supporting document for a legal name change either.

The official certificate comes from the registry in the state or territory where you married. The Attorney-General's Department says your celebrant can apply for it on your behalf, generally at a cost, and that you will need it to change your name or prove you are married. Ask your celebrant early whether they will order it. The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) covers the wider legal steps.

### Step 2: Update your photo ID first (driver licence)

In NSW, take your official marriage certificate and current driver licence to a Service NSW centre. Once approved, a new licence is sent to you within 1 to 2 weeks. Outside NSW, check your state's transport agency for its process and documents.

### Step 3: Passport

The passport touches your travel plans, so it has its own section below. In short, after a marriage-related change of surname you can choose whether to replace it.

### Step 4: Medicare, ATO, Centrelink and electoral roll

- **Medicare.** NSW says to use your official marriage certificate to update your name at a Services Australia centre or over the phone.
- **Centrelink.** To update your legal name, bring your supporting documents to a service centre. Accepted documents include an Australian marriage certificate, for surname changes only, and a certificate of name change.
- **ATO.** Update your name with the ATO. NSW lists it among the federal agencies to tell.
- **Electoral roll.** The Australian Electoral Commission lets you update your enrolment details online. The form asks for an identity detail such as your driver licence number, passport number or Medicare card details.

The [Services Australia name page](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/how-to-update-your-name-with-centrelink?context=64107) lists every accepted document.

### Step 5: Banks, super and insurance

NSW lists banks and building societies, insurance and superannuation providers among the businesses to update. By this point you hold what you need: NSW says your marriage certificate, new driver licence and new Medicare card will provide the proof in most cases. Each business sets its own requirements, so ask each one what it needs before you visit.

### Step 6: Work, professional registrations and everything else

NSW includes mobile phone and internet providers and utility providers. NSW also lists state registries such as the Land Registry for homeowners.

Then come your employer, any professional registration body and your loyalty programmes. Ask each what evidence it accepts.

For couples with a honeymoon booked, this is the step to time with care. As at November 2026, the Australian Passport Office says that if you change your family name because of a change in your marital status, you can choose to get a new passport or keep using your current one. It recommends booking travel in the name that's in your passport.

**Marrying abroad with a honeymoon straight after**

1. Book flights in the name in your current passport. The Passport Office lets you keep using it after a marriage-related change of family name.
2. Travel on that passport, with tickets that match it.
3. If you later want the new name in your passport and you were born in Australia but married overseas, the Passport Office will need a name change certificate, so the formal change of name comes first.

If you are unsure how a change would affect a trip, ask the Passport Office before you book.

The documents depend on where you married. For a married name, the Passport Office links your Australian birth or citizenship certificate with a marriage certificate issued by an Australian registry, a registered relationship certificate, or a registry change of name certificate. If you were born in Australia and married overseas, it will always need a name change certificate.

It accepts foreign certificates only in limited cases, such as when you married overseas and became an Australian citizen before the marriage, and those certificates must also be legalised. Any document not in English needs to be translated in full and stamped by an accredited translator before you show it to them. The details are on the [Passport Office's change of name page](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender).

## State by state: NSW, Victoria, Queensland and Western Australia

As at November 2026, the registries differ mainly on overseas marriages and on couples born overseas.

### New South Wales

Married in Australia: your official marriage certificate is enough, with no fee for an official change of name. Married overseas: NSW says you will have to apply for an official change of name, and a fee applies.

### Victoria

Married in Australia: your certificate is enough. Born overseas and married in Australia: [Births, Deaths and Marriages Victoria](https://www.bdm.vic.gov.au/changing-your-name-after-marriage-separation-divorce) says most organisations will accept your primary identity document plus your Australian marriage certificate, though some may need you to change your name with BDM. Married overseas: you need to apply to change your name.

### Queensland

Married in Australia: your certificate is enough, used with each organisation. Born overseas and married in Australia: [Queensland's registry](https://www.qld.gov.au/family/name-changes/marriage-or-separation) says some organisations may need an official change of name document. Married overseas: you may not be able to take your partner's family name automatically, because some organisations don't accept overseas documents as proof of identity.

### Western Australia

[Western Australia's registry](https://www.wa.gov.au/service/justice/civil-law/marriages) says that if you were married overseas, you may be required to register a formal change of name before your married surname is accepted by some organisations. It recommends checking with the relevant authority for its specific requirements.

### Other states and territories

In South Australia, no registration is needed to take a partner's surname after marrying in Australia. If you married overseas, South Australia says to contact the relevant authority or business, such as your bank or the Passport Office, to see what evidence they need. In the ACT, if you change your name after marriage overseas, you may have difficulty using your foreign marriage certificate and may be asked to register a change of name. In Tasmania and the Northern Territory, check with the registry before you start.

## Can you go back to your maiden or previous name?

Yes, and how depends on how you changed it. Victoria says that if you married in Australia and took your spouse's name, you can go back to your previous family name without having separated or divorced, and you don't need to apply for a change of name with BDM. Most organisations will want your Australian marriage certificate, a standard birth certificate (not a birth extract) and, if relevant, your divorce certificate.

In NSW, if you changed your surname using your Australian marriage certificate, you can usually use proof of divorce to change other identification back to your pre-marriage name. If you changed it through a legal change of name application, you may need to apply to the registry for another legal change of name.

After an overseas marriage, the formal route works in reverse too. Victoria says you need to change your name with BDM to go back, and Queensland says the same where you previously changed your name with its registry.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I change my name before or after the wedding?

After. You need the official marriage certificate from the registry to change your name, and your celebrant can apply for it on your behalf.

### How long does a name change after marriage take?

It depends on your certificate and how many organisations you tell, so ask the registry for current timing when you order. In NSW, once approved, a new driver licence arrives within 1 to 2 weeks.

### How much does it cost to change your name after marriage?

If you married in Australia, NSW says you don't need to pay the fee for an official change of name. Your celebrant can order the official marriage certificate for you, generally at a cost. A formal change of name, which an overseas marriage needs in NSW, carries a registry fee.

### Can I travel on my honeymoon with my old passport?

The Passport Office says that after a marriage-related change of family name you can choose to get a new passport or keep using your current one, and recommends booking travel in the name that's in your passport. Check the name on your tickets matches the passport you will carry.

### Can I use an overseas marriage certificate to change my name?

Usually not on its own. NSW says an overseas marriage certificate cannot be used to change your surname, and Victoria says you need to apply to change your name.

### Can I just revert back to my maiden name?

Yes. In Victoria, if you married in Australia and took your spouse's name, you can go back without applying to BDM, using your marriage and birth certificates and, if relevant, your divorce certificate. If you changed your name formally, NSW says you may need to apply to the registry for another legal change of name, and Victoria says you need to change your name with BDM to go back after an overseas marriage.
