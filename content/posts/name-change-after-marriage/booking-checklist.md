# Name change checklist: after your marriage

These steps sit with the couple and the official agencies, so they don't block publishing. The Wedded Advisor plans the celebration; the marriage certificate, any change of name application and every record update stay with you, your celebrant, the registry and each organisation. The article has no `[[VERIFY]]` markers in the copy and no prices (nothing for Nic to sign off); the only publishing blockers are image credits and licences (5 slots).

All items are as at November 2026, per the state and territory registries of births, deaths and marriages (NSW Registry and Service NSW, Births, Deaths and Marriages Victoria, Queensland's registry, Western Australia's registry, South Australia and the ACT), the Australian Passport Office, Services Australia, the ATO list in NSW's guidance, the Australian Electoral Commission, the Attorney-General's Department and Smartraveller. Each state's rules are its own; reconfirm with the relevant agency before relying on them.

## Decide first

- [ ] Choose: take your partner's surname, keep yours, hyphenate, or another change (a partner can take yours too).
- [ ] Taking a surname or hyphenating after an Australian marriage: usually your official certificate is enough (NSW, Victoria, Queensland).
- [ ] Any other change (a double-barrelled name without a hyphen, a new blended surname, a first or middle name): in NSW this needs a formal change of name application. Check your state's registry.

## 1. Official marriage certificate

- [ ] Order the official certificate from the registry in the state or territory where you married. The ceremonial certificate signed on the day is not accepted.
- [ ] Ask your celebrant whether they will apply on your behalf, and what it costs (generally at a cost; amount not published in the article).
- [ ] Ask the registry for current processing times.

## 2. Your state's route

- [ ] NSW: married in Australia, use the certificate (no official change of name fee); married overseas, apply for an official change of name (a fee applies).
- [ ] Victoria: married in Australia, give organisations a copy of the certificate; born overseas, most organisations accept your primary identity document plus the certificate, some may need a BDM change of name; married overseas, apply to change your name.
- [ ] Queensland: married in Australia, use the certificate with each organisation; born overseas, some organisations may need an official change of name document; married overseas, you may not be able to take the name automatically.
- [ ] Western Australia: married overseas, you may need to register a formal change of name; check each authority's requirements.
- [ ] South Australia: no registration needed after an Australian marriage; married overseas, ask each authority or business what evidence it needs.
- [ ] ACT: married overseas, you may be asked to register a change of name.
- [ ] Tasmania and Northern Territory: check with the registry before you start.

## 3. Photo ID: driver licence

- [ ] Update your driver licence first, through your state's transport or licensing agency (in NSW, Service NSW with your official certificate and current licence; a new licence within 1 to 2 weeks once approved).

## 4. Passport

- [ ] Decide whether to replace it: after a marriage-related change of family name you can keep using your current passport.
- [ ] Book travel, including the honeymoon, in the name in the passport you will carry.
- [ ] Born in Australia and married overseas: a name change certificate is needed for a new passport, so the formal change of name comes first.
- [ ] Check the Passport Office's change of name page for the documents that link your birth or citizenship certificate to your new name.

## 5. Federal agencies

- [ ] Medicare: update with your official certificate at a Services Australia centre or by phone.
- [ ] Centrelink: bring supporting documents to a service centre (Australian marriage certificate for surname changes only, or a change of name certificate).
- [ ] ATO: update your name.
- [ ] Electoral roll: update your enrolment online with the AEC (have your licence number, passport number or Medicare details ready).

## 6. Banks and others to ask

- [ ] Banks and building societies, superannuation and insurance providers.
- [ ] Mobile phone, internet and utility providers.
- [ ] State registries such as the Land Registry (homeowners).
- [ ] Employer and payroll, professional registration bodies, loyalty programmes.
- [ ] Ask each what evidence it accepts before you visit; each sets its own requirements.

## Married overseas

- [ ] Remember you cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia, so there is no Australian marriage certificate to use.
- [ ] Follow your state's rules above: NSW and Victoria require a formal change of name; Queensland and Western Australia say some organisations may need one.
- [ ] Get the certificate issued where you married before you fly home.
- [ ] Translation: any document not in English must be translated in full and stamped by an accredited translator (Passport Office).
- [ ] Legalisation: check whether your certificate needs an apostille; foreign certificates the Passport Office accepts must be legalised.
- [ ] Smartraveller: a foreign certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, but may not be accepted as proof of a name change.

## Going back to a previous name

- [ ] Victoria: married in Australia and took your spouse's name, you can go back without applying to BDM (marriage certificate, standard birth certificate and, if relevant, divorce certificate).
- [ ] NSW: changed using the marriage certificate, you can usually use proof of divorce; changed through a legal application, you may need another legal change of name.
- [ ] After an overseas marriage: Victoria requires a BDM change of name to go back; Queensland the same where you changed your name with its registry.
