- Diversity: the article says once that customs differ by dialect group, region and family. Most detailed facts come from Singapore government heritage pages, so they are credited to Singapore's Chinese communities, not to all Chinese families; Cantonese or Teochew only where a source says so.
- In wedding order: betrothal gifts (guo da li, no amounts) and an auspicious date; the night before and the wedding morning (hair combing, door games); the tea ceremony; symbols (red, double happiness, dragon and phoenix, from the V&A and the British Museum); the bride's dress (qun kwa); the banquet and red envelopes (no amounts); the bride's return visit.
- UNESCO: the Chinese tea listing (2022) and Chinese paper-cut (2009) are linked to weddings only as far as their own pages name weddings among occasions.
- Family custom ("often told", one framing line per section): the names of the six rites, blessing phrases, lucky numbers, months to avoid, the banquet toast, the qipao, double happiness on doors.
- Culture, not politics: nothing on Hong Kong, Taiwan or other political questions. Travel and legal (as at November 2026): Smartraveller "Exercise a high degree of caution" overall, Gyirong County in Tibet "Reconsider your need to travel", risks stated as Smartraveller words them, the visa waiver to 31 December 2026; a marriage made in China can't be registered in Australia but is usually recognised if valid there and under Australian law; the Australian Embassy in Beijing. Registration in China is four questions to ask the registry and the embassy, including any fees.
- Chinese-Australian weddings at home: questions for older guests and how to fit the rites around the day.
