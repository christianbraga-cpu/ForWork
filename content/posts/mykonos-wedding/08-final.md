Mykonos is on your shortlist, and before you ask a single venue for a quote you need three things settled. A Mykonos wedding comes down to three decisions, and this guide takes them in this order: the area and kind of venue that suit your guest list; the season, read from official climate figures and current travel advice; and whether the ceremony is legal or symbolic, which starts the paperwork clock, so settle that one early. When you are ready for names, [our guide to Mykonos wedding venues](https://weddedwonderland.com/destination-weddings/mykonos-wedding-venues) has the full list, and our notes on [getting married in Greece](https://weddedwonderland.com/destinations/europe/greece) cover the wider country.

## Key takeaways

- Decide early whether your ceremony will be legal or symbolic, because that choice sets the paperwork and the timeline.
- Choose the area and the kind of venue first, then narrow to names using the full Mykonos venues guide.
- June to August are the driest months at Naxos, the nearest official weather station, with May and September milder, so plan ceremony times around summer heat and the north wind. <!-- fact_201 --> <!-- fact_202 --> <!-- fact_203 -->
- Ask every venue about deposits, cancellation, exclusive use and music times, and get the answers in writing before you commit.

Mykonos sits in the heart of the Cyclades and is known as the island of the winds. <!-- fact_119 --> Its name has long been shorthand for cosmopolitan, luxury holidays. <!-- fact_119 -->

The picture couples carry with them is a real one: paved lanes, white houses, small chapels, dry-stone walls and the windmills that serve as the island's trademark. <!-- fact_119 --> In Hora, the main town, the Little Venice neighbourhood has houses built right against the sea. <!-- fact_119 -->

For a wedding, that combination matters. You can marry in a whitewashed chapel or above a beach, hold the dinner on a terrace facing the water, and give guests a town to wander between events. [Visit Greece](https://www.visitgreece.gr/en/islands/cyclades/mykonos) is a good first read for anyone in the wedding party who has never been.

The setting decides how many people you can seat, how far guests walk, how the wind reaches you and where everyone sleeps. Settle the kind of place first, then the name.

### Areas of the island and what each suits

Think about the island in terms of the evening you want rather than a map.

**Hora and Little Venice** suit couples who want guests close to the life of the town, with houses built against the sea and the windmills above. <!-- fact_119 --> A venue within easy reach of town keeps free evenings simple for guests.

**The beaches** suit couples who picture a ceremony with the sand below and dinner by a pool. Mykonos Blu offers wedding experiences on Psarou beach with the sunset as a backdrop. <!-- fact_01 --> Royal Myconian lists a private spot on Elia Beach among its event settings. <!-- fact_12 --> Ftelia Bay Hotel sits on Ftelia Beach. <!-- fact_10 -->

**The sunset coast** suits a sunset ceremony. Saint John Hotel Villas & Spa is in Agios Ioannis, one of the island's celebrated sunset spots. <!-- fact_07 -->

### Resorts with chapels, beach settings and private villas

Mykonos wedding venues fall into a few types, each with its own trade-offs.

**Hotels with a private chapel.** Kivotos and Ftelia Bay Hotel each have a private chapel on their grounds, and Saint John offers vows at Agia Fotini Chapel. <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_10 --> <!-- fact_08 --> A chapel setting tells you nothing about the legal form of the ceremony, so ask each venue whether its chapel suits a civil or a symbolic ceremony.

**Beachfront resorts.** These give you the ceremony, the dinner and the guest rooms under one events team. Mykonos Blu describes itself as a Cycladic settlement on the waterfront, with 103 guest rooms. <!-- fact_01 -->

**Large resorts with several spaces.** For a long guest list, a resort with more than one terrace lets the welcome drinks, the ceremony and the dinner each have their own setting. Saint John lists several ceremony and reception spaces, all with sea views, and describes weddings of up to 800 guests. <!-- fact_08 --> <!-- fact_09 -->

**Private villas.** A villa gives the wedding party one address and a ceremony no one else walks through. Ask any villa whether it is permitted to host a wedding, how many guests it may hold for an event, and where the rest of your guests will sleep.

The month you choose sets the ceremony time, the fabrics, the shade you need and the fallback plan. There is no single right month, only the one that matches the wedding you have in mind.

A few patterns matter for a wedding. From June to August, rain all but disappears at Naxos, with under one rain day a month in July and August. <!-- fact_202 --> October is different at Naxos: rain rises to 38.6 mm across 5.5 days, and April sees 6.0 rain days. <!-- fact_202 --> At Naxos, May and September sit between, with mean maximums of 22.1°C and 25.1°C. <!-- fact_201 -->

The wind deserves as much attention as the temperature. At Naxos the prevailing wind blows from the north in every month, and the station's mean wind readings run higher from July to October than in May and June. <!-- fact_203 --> On an island known as the island of the winds, ask every venue where the ceremony sits relative to that north wind, and what shelter it has. <!-- fact_119 -->

### Wind, heat and travel advice

As at November 2026, [Smartraveller's advice for Greece](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) is to exercise normal safety precautions. <!-- fact_109 --> Within that, several points shape a Mykonos wedding date.

**Heat.** Heatwaves are frequent in Greece over the summer months and can cause heat exhaustion and heat stroke. <!-- fact_110 --> Plan water, hats, light fabrics, shade and air-conditioned rest for guests, and set a summer ceremony later in the day. <!-- fact_110 -->

**Wildfire.** Wildfires occur during the extended summer period, from June to September, including near tourist areas, and can start with little warning and travel quickly. <!-- fact_111 --> Keep a covered or indoor fallback in your plan and follow local authorities' advice on the day.

**Wind and ferries.** Ferry services can be delayed or cancelled in bad weather, mainly because of strong winds. <!-- fact_113 --> Guests arriving by sea should check the forecast and reconfirm departures with their travel provider. <!-- fact_113 -->

**Swimming.** Boat traffic, coastal hazards, strong currents and marine life all call for care, and sea conditions can change quickly. <!-- fact_112 --> Point guests to patrolled beaches. <!-- fact_112 -->

**The ground itself.** Greece is in an active seismic zone and experiences earthquakes and volcanic activity. <!-- fact_114 --> Keep travel insurance in place for everyone travelling, and check the advice again before you fly.

**Crowds.** The tourist season runs from May to October, with high temperatures and large crowds at popular seaports. <!-- fact_113 -->

Whichever month you choose, confirm that your venue and its ceremony spaces are open on your date.

This is the decision that sets your timeline, so make it before you fall for a terrace. The requirements below are as at November 2026, and every document question ends with the same advice: ask the authority that will receive it.

### Legal or symbolic ceremony

A legal marriage in Greece brings documents, appointments and lead times. A symbolic ceremony on the island, after a legal step at home, keeps the paperwork in Australia and frees the day itself for the ceremony everyone travels for.

You can sign the legal paperwork quietly in Australia, then hold the full ceremony on Mykonos with nothing left to file. A symbolic ceremony also leaves room for customs that matter to your families; read our notes on [Greek wedding traditions](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/greek-wedding-traditions) before you talk to a celebrant or venue.

Whichever you choose, ask every venue on your shortlist which ceremony types it hosts.

### Where an elopement fits

An elopement is either of the two above, only smaller. Legally, the steps are the same as for any marriage in Greece; symbolically, it can be the two of you and a photographer.

The island's smaller settings suit it. Kivotos describes its private chapel as ideal for an intimate wedding, and the Ocean Balcony at Mykonos Blu holds up to 60, so a very small party fits easily. <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_02 --> Kivotos also offers newlyweds their first days aboard the hotel's privately owned schooner. <!-- fact_06 -->

### Steps for Australian couples

If you are marrying legally in Australia and holding a symbolic ceremony on the island, you can skip ahead to [Getting your guests to Mykonos](#getting-your-guests-to-mykonos).

Some countries ask you to prove you are not already married, and each decides what proof it accepts. <!-- fact_100 --> The most common documents are a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) and certificates from a registry of births, deaths and marriages. <!-- fact_100 --> Those registry certificates can be a statement of no record of a previous marriage, a Single Status certificate or a No Record Result certificate. <!-- fact_100 -->

Local authorities may also ask for divorce papers, a late spouse's death certificate, and your original birth certificate and passport. <!-- fact_100 --> Regional and national laws can differ, so contact the destination's embassy or consulate for the specifics. <!-- fact_100 --> [Smartraveller's page on getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) sets out the Australian side.

The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade can issue a CNI in Australia, but some local authorities accept only CNIs issued in their own country. <!-- fact_101 --> Ask the Greek embassy or consulate:

- whether you need a CNI
- whether you can get it in Australia
- whether it also needs an authentication or apostille. <!-- fact_101 -->

**At the embassy in Athens.** The [Australian Embassy in Athens](https://greece.embassy.gov.au/athn/notarial.html) witnesses CNI applications, issues CNIs and witnesses notices of intended marriage. <!-- fact_104 --> All notarial services need an appointment, Monday to Friday, 9am to 12.30pm, though CNI and apostille documents can be sent by post. <!-- fact_104 --> They are ready for collection within two working days unless same-day collection is arranged in advance, or can be returned by mail, and a fee applies. <!-- fact_104 -->

The embassy gives no legal advice, and you are responsible for knowing which service you need. <!-- fact_105 --> It does not witness signatures on overseas documents, and honorary consuls are not authorised to provide notarial services. <!-- fact_105 --> If you plan to use the embassy in person, build a few Athens days into the itinerary before you travel on to the island.

**Legalising your documents.** Ask the Greek authority which documents it needs and which must be legalised, and whether that means an apostille or an authentication certificate. <!-- fact_106 --> Documents that are not original Australian public documents must first be notarised by an Australian Notary Public. <!-- fact_106 --> Ask too whether any document needs a translation, and who may make it.

The apostille or authentication certificate is applied directly to the document. <!-- fact_107 --> If you do not want your originals marked, have a notarised copy legalised instead, and check the Greek authority accepts it. <!-- fact_107 --> To lodge a request: <!-- fact_107 -->

- Complete the Document Legalisation Request Form.
- Sign it in front of an authorised witness.
- Lodge it by appointment at an Australian Passport Office, at many Australian embassies and consulates overseas, or by registered mail to the Authentications Section in Melbourne or Sydney.
- A fee applies.

[How to get your documents legalised](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised) has the details.

Processing times vary, with delays at the end of the school year, in peak holiday periods and for incomplete requests. <!-- fact_108 --> An Australian-issued apostille can be verified online. <!-- fact_108 --> If your wedding falls after the Australian summer holidays, lodge well before them.

### Civil marriage with the Municipality of Mykonos

For a civil marriage on the island, check the steps, documents, lead times and ceremony locations with the Municipality of Mykonos and the Australian Embassy in Athens, including any fees. Confirm the list in writing before you set your date, and keep copies of every document you send.

### Recognition back in Australia

You cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia, but in most cases it is legally recognised if it was valid under the law of the country where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law had it happened here. <!-- fact_102 --> Australia does not recognise an overseas marriage where either person is already married, is not old enough to marry, is too closely related to the other, or was forced to marry. <!-- fact_103 -->

A foreign marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, but it may not work as proof of a name change. <!-- fact_102 --> If either of you plans to change your name, check with your state registry of births, deaths and marriages before you fly, and talk to a lawyer to confirm the certificate is valid in Australia. <!-- fact_102 -->

Guests flying from Australia arrive tired, and some will be older or less steady on stairs. Build the travel plan around them, and leave a spare day before the wedding.

### Flying into Mykonos

[Mykonos Airport](https://www.jmk-airport.gr/en) has a Private Flights Lounge with VIP welcoming services, and accessible travel information for passengers with reduced mobility, worth sending to any guest who needs help on arrival. <!-- fact_118 --> It also lists taxis and a direct public bus service. <!-- fact_118 --> The flight from Athens International Airport to Mykonos takes about 45 minutes, and from Thessaloniki about 1 hour 10 minutes. <!-- fact_120 -->

**Entry.** Greece is in the Schengen area, and Australians can make short trips without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. <!-- fact_115 --> On arrival, Australian passport holders register fingerprints and a photo under the European Entry/Exit System, which is free and needs no action beforehand but may slow the border. <!-- fact_115 --> Count the wedding week inside the 90 days if you are adding a longer European trip.

### Arriving by ferry

Ferries and large catamarans carry most visitors to the Greek islands. <!-- fact_113 --> Some services lack suitable access for people with disabilities. <!-- fact_113 --> Services can be delayed or cancelled when the wind is strong, so guests should check the forecast and reconfirm departures. <!-- fact_113 -->

For guests with mobility needs or a tight connection, the flight is the steadier choice.

### Transfers and access to ask about

Ask each venue on your shortlist:

- Is there a step-free route between guest rooms, the getting-ready space, the ceremony and the reception?
- How far is the nearest point a car can reach, and how do guests with limited mobility get from there to the ceremony?
- How long is the drive from the airport, and how do guests usually travel between the venue and their accommodation in the evening?
- If guests stay in town, how do they get back after the reception?

A venue's rules shape the day as much as its view, and every one is easier to settle before you sign. Send each venue these questions.

**Music.** Can a DJ or live band play, where, and at what time must amplified music stop? Where can an after-party move once it does?

**Exclusive use.** Will the space, the venue or the whole property be closed to other guests, for how many days, and what does exclusive use cover?

**Deposits.** How much is the deposit, when is it due, and what is the payment schedule after that?

**Cancellation.** Which payments are refundable, and what happens if you need to move or cancel the date?

**Ceremony time.** What times are available on your date, and how long do you have the space for set-up and pack-down?

**Weather plan.** What is the plan for a strong north wind, a rare shower or a heatwave, and which covered space is held for you? <!-- fact_203 --> <!-- fact_110 -->

**Suppliers.** May you bring your own photographer, florist and musicians, and what are the rules on access and loading? Saint John works with experienced wedding planners for flowers, DJs, photography and fireworks, with additional charges. <!-- fact_09 -->

**Packages.** Which spaces, menus and services does each package include, and are they current for your year?

**Guest rooms.** How many rooms can your guests hold on your dates, and is there a minimum stay?

Get the answers in the contract or a written confirmation, not a chat thread.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you legally get married in Mykonos?

There are two routes: a civil marriage on the island, or a legal step in Australia followed by a symbolic ceremony on Mykonos. For a civil marriage, check the steps, documents and any fees with the Municipality of Mykonos and the Australian Embassy in Athens. In most cases an overseas marriage is recognised in Australia if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. <!-- fact_102 -->

### What documents do Australians need to get married in Greece?

The most common are a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage and certificates from a registry of births, deaths and marriages, and local authorities may also ask for divorce papers, a late spouse's death certificate, and your original birth certificate and passport. <!-- fact_100 --> Ask the Greek authority which documents it needs and which must carry an apostille or authentication. <!-- fact_106 -->

### Can we have a symbolic ceremony in Mykonos?

Yes. You can complete the legal step in Australia, then hold a symbolic ceremony on the island. A legal marriage in Greece is in most cases recognised in Australia, though it cannot be registered here; a legal step at home simply keeps the paperwork in Australia. <!-- fact_102 -->

### Is Mykonos a good place to elope?

It suits a small wedding well. Kivotos describes its private chapel as ideal for an intimate wedding, and newlyweds there may spend their first days aboard the hotel's privately owned schooner. <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_06 -->

### When is the wedding season in Mykonos?

The tourist season runs from May to October. <!-- fact_113 --> At Naxos, the nearest official weather station, June to August are driest, while October brings the most rain of those months and the north wind runs stronger from July to October. <!-- fact_202 --> <!-- fact_203 -->

### How do wedding guests get to Mykonos?

Guests can fly from Athens, about 45 minutes, or from Thessaloniki, about 1 hour 10 minutes. <!-- fact_120 --> They can also arrive by ferry or catamaran, which can be delayed or cancelled in strong winds. <!-- fact_113 -->
