In most cases, yes: Australia generally recognises a marriage that was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. <!-- fact_104 --> Getting married in Costa Rica is open to two Australians, because the Registro Civil celebrates civil marriages for foreign applicants who bring a valid passport, an apostilled birth certificate and an apostilled civil status certificate. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_03 --> <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_05 --> Costa Rican notaries also celebrate marriages and declare them to the Registro Civil online. <!-- fact_200 -->

## Can Australians marry legally in Costa Rica?

Yes. The Registro Civil sits within the Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones (TSE), and its Unidad de Matrimonios receives requests for civil marriages and celebrates them. <!-- fact_01 --> Its requirements set out what foreign applicants bring, which is where two Australians flying in for their wedding sit. <!-- fact_03 --> The TSE sets out the process in its [procedure sheet for a civil marriage](https://www.tse.go.cr/pdf/requisitosytramites/Celebracion-Matrimonio-Civil.pdf).

There is a second route. Costa Rican notaries celebrate marriages and must declare each one to the Registro Civil through the TSE's online declaration system. <!-- fact_200 -->

Australia applies its own test when you come home. It does not recognise an overseas marriage where either person is already married to someone else, is not old enough to marry, is too closely related to the other, or is forced to marry. <!-- fact_103 --> Outside those cases, a marriage that was valid where it took place, and would have been valid under Australian law had it happened here, is in most cases legally recognised. <!-- fact_104 -->

### Civil, Catholic or symbolic

A civil marriage, before the Registro Civil or a notary, is the legal route this guide follows. <!-- fact_01 --> <!-- fact_200 --> Catholic marriages celebrated in Costa Rican parishes are registered with the Registro Civil under the same procedure as marriages celebrated at notarial offices and consular offices. <!-- fact_13 --> If a church wedding matters to you, ask the parish early what it requires of two Australians.

A symbolic ceremony carries no legal weight on its own. You can sign the civil marriage quietly, then hold the ceremony your guests fly in for at the setting you love.

### The documents you gather at home

For a Registro Civil marriage, each foreign applicant brings the original and a legible copy of a valid passport in good condition. <!-- fact_03 --> Each also brings a birth certificate from their country of origin, apostilled and, because it is not in Spanish, with an official translation. <!-- fact_04 --> The birth certificate has no expiry unless it is in poor condition or carries a correction to substantial identity details. <!-- fact_04 -->

The second document is a civil status certificate, also apostilled and officially translated. <!-- fact_05 --> It is valid for the period it states, and if it states none, it must have been issued no more than one month before you present it. <!-- fact_05 --> That one-month window shapes your timeline more than anything else on the list, so order this certificate last.

Your state registry of births, deaths and marriages can issue a statement that there's no record you have been married before, a Single Status certificate or a No Record Result certificate, but only the Registro Civil or your notary can tell you which of these, if any, it accepts. <!-- fact_102 --> Before you order anything, put three questions to them:

- Which Australian document do you accept as the civil status certificate?
- What should a divorced or widowed partner bring?
- Will you accept a notarised copy, or do you need the original apostilled?

Your Australian documents are apostilled in Australia, the country that issued them, before you travel. <!-- fact_18 --> The apostille is applied directly to the document, so if you don't want your originals marked, have a notarised copy legalised instead, and check that it will be accepted. <!-- fact_107 --> Documents that are not original Australian public documents must first be notarised by an Australian Notary Public. <!-- fact_107 --> Read [what an apostille is and whether your paperwork needs one](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/what-is-an-apostille-and-does-my-paperwork-need-one).

You lodge a Document Legalisation Request Form, signed in front of an authorised witness, by appointment at an Australian Passport Office or by registered mail to the Authentications Section in Melbourne or Sydney, and a fee applies. <!-- fact_108 --> Processing times vary, with delays at the end of the school year and in peak holiday periods. <!-- fact_109 -->

Once your apostilled documents arrive in Costa Rica, they need no further approval from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to be recognised. <!-- fact_17 --> The Spanish translation is made by an official translator, found through the Ministry's website, and the translation itself needs no authentication. <!-- fact_17 -->

### Entry and travel

On an Australian passport you don't need a visa for a tourist visit of less than 90 days, though you might be refused entry if you can't show you'll return home or travel onwards. <!-- fact_112 --> Entry and exit conditions can change at short notice, so contact the Embassy of Costa Rica or your airline for the latest details before you fly. <!-- fact_112 -->

Many countries require at least 6 months' passport validity, including for transit. <!-- fact_114 --> Flying through the US means meeting US entry or transit requirements, and through Canada you need an eTA. <!-- fact_113 --> You also pay a departure tax to leave Costa Rica, which might be included in your airline ticket. <!-- fact_113 -->

Australia has a consulate in San José headed by an Honorary Consul, which provides limited consular assistance; full consular assistance comes from the Australian Embassy in Mexico City. <!-- fact_120 --> Seriously ill or injured travellers may need medical evacuation, so your travel insurance must cover it. <!-- fact_119 -->

Smartraveller rates Costa Rica at level 2, "Exercise a high degree of caution", due to the threat of violent crime. <!-- fact_110 --> It notes that carjackings and "express kidnappings" are increasingly common, and that criminals often target tourist areas, resorts, transport hubs and public transport. <!-- fact_110 --> <!-- fact_111 --> Its practical advice is to plan to arrive at or leave the airport during daylight hours. <!-- fact_111 --> Read the full [Smartraveller advice for Costa Rica](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/americas/costa-rica) before you book.

## Step by step: the Registro Civil or a Costa Rican notary

Two routes lead to the same register. The Registro Civil celebrates the marriage itself, at its central office or a regional office. <!-- fact_10 --> A notary celebrates the marriage and declares it to the Registro Civil online. <!-- fact_200 -->

### Lodging your request in person

The Registro Civil route starts with both of you presenting yourselves together, in person, and both signing the request. <!-- fact_02 --> The central office receives requests Monday to Friday from 7:00 to 10:00 am, with 10 places a day, and regional offices nationwide receive them from 7:00 am to 2:00 pm. <!-- fact_10 --> The request gives an email address for notifications and a mobile phone number. <!-- fact_09 -->

A notice of your marriage, called an edict, must be published in the official gazette, Diario Oficial La Gaceta, through the Imprenta Nacional platform. <!-- fact_08 --> You arrange the quotation and pay to publish it. <!-- fact_08 --> There is no charge for the registry service of celebrating the marriage. <!-- fact_12 -->

The ceremony follows on average 4 to 6 months later, depending on diary availability once the request and requirements are complete and the edict has been published. <!-- fact_11 --> Living in Australia, remember that you lodge the request together in person and the ceremony follows on average 4 to 6 months later, so ask the Registro Civil or a notary how this fits your travel.

The notary route is the other legal route, with the notary declaring the marriage to the Registro Civil online. <!-- fact_200 --> Ask any notary you consider how their timing works for you before you set a date.

### Choosing your officiant

At the Registro Civil, marriages are celebrated by appointment, Monday to Friday between 7:00 am and 2:00 pm, at the central office or the respective regional office. <!-- fact_10 -->

A notary is the second choice. The online declaration does not change or exempt notaries from the requirements of Costa Rica's Family Code for celebrating a marriage. <!-- fact_202 --> A notary must not celebrate marriages while suspended or barred by the Dirección Nacional de Notariado, and must have an enabled user in the TSE's system before the ceremony. <!-- fact_202 --> <!-- fact_14 --> The TSE explains the system on its [online marriage declaration page](https://www.tse.go.cr/matrimonioenlinea.html).

Questions for any notary you consider:

- Where may you hold the ceremony, and can it be at our resort?
- Which of our documents do you need, and in what form: apostilled, translated, originals or copies?
- Are you in good standing with the Dirección Nacional de Notariado, with an enabled user in the TSE's system?
- How will we receive copies of the registered certificate?

### Witnesses

You name two witnesses in the request: full name and identity document number, phone and email, exact address, profession or occupation, and current civil status. <!-- fact_06 --> A foreign witness attaches a complete copy of their document and sends it to the Unidad de Matrimonios email address. <!-- fact_06 -->

On the day, both witnesses attend with a valid identity document, which for a foreigner can be a passport. <!-- fact_07 --> They are sworn in during the ceremony and must know that you are both free to marry with no legal impediment. <!-- fact_07 --> Witnesses attend only on the day of the ceremony. <!-- fact_07 --> Choose two people who will be in Costa Rica on the date. If you marry before a notary, ask how many witnesses the notary needs and what they should bring.

### Signing and registration with the Civil Registry

On the day of the marriage, you and your witnesses attend with valid identity documents in good condition. <!-- fact_09 --> The celebrating authority completes, signs and seals the marriage declaration certificate. <!-- fact_14 --> Where one or both of you are foreign, a certified copy of your valid identity document goes with that declaration. <!-- fact_15 -->

With a notary, the declaration goes through the TSE's online system. <!-- fact_200 --> Automatic, immediate registration applies only when both spouses are Costa Rican by birth; when either is foreign, the notary uploads an image of that spouse's identity document so the identity can be verified and registration can continue. <!-- fact_201 -->

Registration of a marriage between foreigners, or between a Costa Rican and a foreigner, takes 25 working days from receipt at the Sección de Inscripciones, because it includes a study verifying the foreign party's identity. <!-- fact_16 --> There is no charge for registration. <!-- fact_16 --> Ask the Registro Civil how to request copies of the registered certificate.

### Apostille and translation

Every Costa Rican public document that is to have legal effect in another country must be authenticated. <!-- fact_19 --> For a country that is party to the Hague Apostille Convention, you receive an Apostille attached to the physical document, which is also digitised for online verification. <!-- fact_19 --> Documents issued by the TSE and documents made before a notary are among those the Ministry of Foreign Affairs authenticates. <!-- fact_19 --> The Apostille certifies only the signature, the signatory's capacity and the seal, not the content of the document. <!-- fact_18 -->

The Ministry's Departamento de Autenticaciones in San José works by appointment only, Monday to Friday, and returns documents within 1 to 10 working days. <!-- fact_20 --> Every document must show the issuing public institution and the name of the official who signs it, and the signature must be handwritten. <!-- fact_21 --> Appointments are booked online through the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs authentications service](https://www.rree.go.cr/?sec=servicios&cat=autenticaciones). <!-- fact_21 -->

The procedure is not personal, so a third party can lodge your documents without an authorisation. <!-- fact_20 --> Our guide to [your overseas marriage certificate](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate) covers what to check on it before you leave.

### Recognition and registering at home

You cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia, but in most cases it is legally recognised if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. <!-- fact_104 --> Your foreign marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, though it may not work as proof of a name change, so check with your state registry of births, deaths and marriages. <!-- fact_104 --> A lawyer can confirm the certificate is valid in Australia. <!-- fact_104 -->

Our guide to [whether you have to register an overseas marriage at home](https://weddedwonderland.com/expert-advice/do-you-have-to-register-an-overseas-marriage-at-home) sets out the detail, and Smartraveller's page on [getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) is worth reading in full.

If Australia recognises your marriage, you can't marry again at home, but you may hold another kind of ceremony, such as a recommitment ceremony, for family who could not travel. <!-- fact_105 -->

## Venues and planning

The questions to put to a resort are less about the view and more about control. Ask each one, in writing:

- **Deposits and contracts:** whose name is on the contract, in which language is it written, in which currency are payments made, and when do deposits become non-refundable?
- **Access:** which ceremony, dining and guest spaces are step-free?
- **Guest travel:** how do our guests travel safely between the airport, the resort and our events, and can arrivals and departures be planned in daylight?
- **Sole use:** can we take the spaces we need for the hours or days we need them, with no other events booked alongside?
- **Privacy:** how do you handle other guests, drones and outside photographers during our events?
- **Security:** how does your security work, and how would it work alongside any security we bring?

On currency, the local money is the Costa Rican colón; US dollars can be exchanged in Costa Rica but Australian money can't. <!-- fact_123 --> Nationwide strikes and roadblocks have occurred, so ask the resort what alternative transport it can arrange if roads close. <!-- fact_124 -->

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Costa Rica marriage recognised in Australia?

In most cases, yes. An overseas marriage is generally recognised in Australia if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. You cannot register it in Australia, and your Costa Rican certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage. <!-- fact_104 -->

### Who can legally marry us in Costa Rica?

The Registro Civil celebrates civil marriages by appointment at its central and regional offices. <!-- fact_10 --> Costa Rican notaries also celebrate marriages and declare them to the Registro Civil online. <!-- fact_200 -->

### Do we need our own witnesses?

Yes. You name two witnesses in the Registro Civil request, and both attend on the day, are sworn in, and must know you are free to marry. <!-- fact_06 --> <!-- fact_07 --> If you marry before a notary, ask how many witnesses the notary needs.

### Do our documents need to be translated into Spanish?

Yes. Your apostilled birth certificate and civil status certificate each need an official Spanish translation. <!-- fact_04 --> <!-- fact_05 --> Official translators are found through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs website, and the translation needs no further authentication. <!-- fact_17 -->

### How much does it cost to get married in Costa Rica?

There is no charge for the registry service of celebrating a civil marriage before the Registro Civil. <!-- fact_12 --> You pay to publish the notice in La Gaceta, and a fee applies to apostilling your Australian documents. <!-- fact_08 --> <!-- fact_108 --> Ask any notary and official translator for their fees in writing before you commit.
