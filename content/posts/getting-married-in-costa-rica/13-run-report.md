## Please note
- **Two legal routes, both from Costa Rican government pages (translated from Spanish):**
  - **Registro Civil (Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones):** both of you lodge the request together, in person, at the Unidad de Matrimonios; foreigners bring a passport, an apostilled birth certificate and an apostilled civil status certificate, with official Spanish translations; the civil status certificate has a limited validity; two witnesses are named and attend on the day; a notice is published in La Gaceta, the official gazette; the ceremony follows on average 4 to 6 months later.
  - **A notary:** notaries celebrate marriages and declare them to the Registro Civil through the TSE's online system; when a spouse is foreign, the notary uploads that spouse's ID; Family Code requirements still apply (I read this page myself).
  - **Registration:** 25 working days when a spouse is foreign.
  - **For Australia:** a Costa Rican certificate is apostilled by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, by appointment, in 1 to 10 working days.
- **Two trips:** readers asked whether the registry route means flying over twice. The article states the facts only (lodge together in person; ceremony on average 4 to 6 months later) and tells couples to ask the Registro Civil or a notary how this fits their travel. The final check cut a "plan around two visits" line that went beyond the sources.
