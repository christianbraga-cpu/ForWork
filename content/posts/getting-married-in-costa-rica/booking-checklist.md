# Costa Rica civil marriage checklist

The routes, documents and steps from "How to Get Married in Costa Rica Step by Step", in order, plus questions to put to the Registro Civil, your notary and your resort. Requirements come from the Tribunal Supremo de Elecciones (TSE) and its Registro Civil, Costa Rica's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Australian document legalisation guidance and Smartraveller; check their pages before you rely on any step. Costs apply to publishing the notice in La Gaceta, to legalising Australian documents, and to any notary and official translator: ask each for the current amount in writing. The article has no `[[VERIFY]]` markers in the copy and no prices (nothing for Nic to sign off).

As at November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Choose your route

- [ ] **Registro Civil:** the Registro Civil (Unidad de Matrimonios) receives your request and celebrates the marriage by appointment, Monday to Friday between 7:00 am and 2:00 pm, at its central office or a regional office. The ceremony follows on average 4 to 6 months after a complete request and the published notice.
- [ ] **Costa Rican notary:** a notary celebrates the marriage and declares it to the Registro Civil through the TSE's online system. Ask the notary how their timing, location and document requirements work for you.
- [ ] Decide whether to add a Catholic or symbolic ceremony. A symbolic ceremony carries no legal weight on its own; Catholic marriages in Costa Rican parishes are registered with the Registro Civil, so ask the parish early what it requires of two Australians.

## Before you leave Australia

- [ ] Read the TSE procedure sheet for a civil marriage and its online marriage declaration page together.
- [ ] Ask the Registro Civil or your notary which Australian document they accept as the civil status certificate (Single Status certificate or No Record Result certificate), what a divorced or widowed partner should bring, and whether a notarised copy is accepted or the original must be apostilled.
- [ ] Check both passports are valid, in good condition, and have at least 6 months' validity for travel and transit.
- [ ] Order both birth certificates.
- [ ] Order both civil status certificates last: unless a certificate states its own validity, it must be issued no more than one month before you present it.
- [ ] Have notarised by an Australian Notary Public any document that is not an original Australian public document.
- [ ] Lodge the Document Legalisation Request Form (signed in front of an authorised witness) for an apostille on each document, by appointment at an Australian Passport Office or by registered mail to the Authentications Section in Melbourne or Sydney; lodge early, as processing slows at the end of the school year and in peak holiday periods.
- [ ] Arrange an official Spanish translation of each apostilled document by an official translator found through the Costa Rican Ministry of Foreign Affairs website.
- [ ] Choose two witnesses who will be in Costa Rica on the date, and collect their full name, identity document number, phone, email, exact address, profession or occupation and current civil status.
- [ ] For a foreign witness, send a complete copy of their identity document to the Unidad de Matrimonios email address.
- [ ] Check Smartraveller's Costa Rica advice and subscribe to updates for your dates.
- [ ] Check entry conditions with the Embassy of Costa Rica or your airline; plan any US transit to meet US entry or transit requirements, and get an eTA for Canada.
- [ ] Take out travel insurance that covers medical evacuation, for the two of you, and suggest the same cover to your guests.
- [ ] See your doctor about dengue, chikungunya, Zika and malaria risk, especially for a wet-season date or a coast in Limón or Puntarenas.
- [ ] Note the Australian consulate in San José (Honorary Consul, limited assistance) and the Australian Embassy in Mexico City (full consular assistance).
- [ ] Plan airport arrivals and departures in daylight.
- [ ] Pack the original apostilled documents and translations in your hand luggage.

## In Costa Rica: Registro Civil route

- [ ] Present yourselves together, in person, and both sign the request (central office Monday to Friday 7:00 to 10:00 am, with 10 places a day; regional offices 7:00 am to 2:00 pm).
- [ ] Give an email address for notifications and a mobile phone number in the request.
- [ ] Name your two witnesses in the request.
- [ ] Arrange the quotation and pay to publish the edict in the Diario Oficial La Gaceta through the Imprenta Nacional platform.
- [ ] Wait for your appointment date once the request is complete and the edict is published.
- [ ] On the day, attend with valid identity documents in good condition; both witnesses attend with a valid identity document (a passport for a foreigner) and are sworn in.
- [ ] The celebrating authority completes, signs and seals the marriage declaration certificate, with a certified copy of each foreign spouse's identity document.

## In Costa Rica: notary route

- [ ] Confirm the notary is in good standing with the Dirección Nacional de Notariado and has an enabled user in the TSE's system before the ceremony.
- [ ] Give the notary the documents they ask for, in the form they ask for.
- [ ] Confirm the ceremony location and how many witnesses the notary needs.
- [ ] On the day, attend with valid identity documents; the notary declares the marriage online and uploads an image of each foreign spouse's identity document for verification.

## After the wedding

- [ ] Allow 25 working days for registration from receipt at the Sección de Inscripciones (a marriage with a foreign spouse includes an identity verification study).
- [ ] Ask the Registro Civil, or your notary, how to request copies of the registered certificate.
- [ ] Book an appointment online with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs Departamento de Autenticaciones in San José to apostille the certificate (by appointment only, Monday to Friday, returned within 1 to 10 working days). A third party can lodge it for you without an authorisation.
- [ ] Check the certificate shows the issuing institution and the name of the signing official, with a handwritten signature.
- [ ] Keep the original marriage certificate safe.
- [ ] Before using the certificate for a change of name, check with your state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages; a lawyer can confirm the certificate is valid in Australia.
- [ ] Nothing to register in Australia. A recommitment ceremony at home is possible; a second marriage is not.

## Questions for the Registro Civil

- [ ] Which Australian document do you accept as the civil status certificate?
- [ ] What should a divorced or widowed partner bring?
- [ ] Will you accept a notarised copy, or do you need the original apostilled?
- [ ] Has our request been received, and is anything missing?
- [ ] When will our appointment be, and at which office?
- [ ] What do our witnesses need to bring on the day?
- [ ] How do we request copies of the registered certificate, and when will they be ready?
- [ ] How does the Registro Civil route fit couples who live in Australia and travel for the wedding?

## Questions for the notary

- [ ] Where may you hold the ceremony, and can it be at our resort?
- [ ] Which of our documents do you need, and in what form: apostilled, translated, originals or copies?
- [ ] Are you in good standing with the Dirección Nacional de Notariado, with an enabled user in the TSE's system?
- [ ] How many witnesses do you need, and what should they bring?
- [ ] How does your timing work for us, from first contact to the ceremony?
- [ ] How will we receive copies of the registered certificate?
- [ ] What is your fee, and will you confirm it in writing before we commit?

## Questions for the resort

- [ ] Can the legal ceremony take place on your grounds with our notary, and where exactly?
- [ ] Whose name is on the contract, in which language is it written, in which currency are payments made, and when do deposits become non-refundable?
- [ ] Which ceremony, dining and guest spaces are step-free?
- [ ] How do our guests travel safely between the airport, the resort and our events, and can arrivals and departures be planned in daylight?
- [ ] Can we take the spaces we need for the hours or days we need them, with no other events booked alongside?
- [ ] How do you handle other guests, drones and outside photographers during our events?
- [ ] How does your security work, and how would it work alongside any security we bring?
- [ ] What alternative transport can you arrange if strikes or roadblocks close the roads?
- [ ] What is the covered alternative for the ceremony and the dinner if rain or a storm arrives, and when is that call made?
- [ ] What local advice do you give on currents, rip tides and wildlife for guests who want to swim?
- [ ] Which inclusions will you confirm in writing before we commit?
