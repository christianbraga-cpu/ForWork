# Greece Marriage Licence and the Eight-Day Wait Explained

[IMAGE: hero]

Somewhere in your planning, someone has said "eight days", and now it seems to decide when your guests should fly. The Greece marriage licence does carry an official eight-day timeline, but it belongs to couples who live in Greece. If you live in Australia, the route may look quite different. This guide explains what the licence is, what starts its clock, the town-hall route for couples who live abroad, which papers come from which Australian office, how Greek translations work since 2021, and what makes the marriage count when you come home.

Sources were checked on 12 November 2026. Town halls apply national rules in their own way, so confirm each requirement for your own passports with your town hall, DFAT and the Australian Embassy in Athens.

## What a Greek marriage licence is, and whether you need one

Greek law requires a licence from the mayor of each partner's last place of residence before a civil wedding or a wedding in the Greek Orthodox Church, says the [gov.gr civil licence entry](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_civil_marriage_licence). For foreign nationals who live in Greece, the [foreign-resident licence](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_marriage_licence_for_foreign_residents_in_Greece) is issued by their municipality, costs EUR 18 (about AUD 29 at the Reserve Bank rate of 12 November 2026) and is valid for six months.

[IMAGE: town-hall]

If you live in Australia, that changes the picture. A 4 January 2019 [Interior Ministry circular](https://www.ypes.gr/UserFiles/f0ff9297-f516-40ff-a70e-eca84e2ec9b9/1969-04012019.pdf) says a foreign document confirming you are free to marry counts as the licence when it also names the person you are marrying. In that case no Greek licence is issued.

Two town halls on Crete apply this in writing. [Chania](https://www.chania.gr/files/57/44617/civil_marriages_documents_for_foreigners_of_abroad_no5_(en).pdf) asks foreigners living abroad for their own country's licence naming the other partner, and will not accept one that leaves the partner out. [Heraklion](https://www.heraklion.gr/en/citizen/civil-marriages/marriage-foreigners.html) asks non-residents for a home-country licence, translated and apostilled. Neither list mentions a Greek licence, a newspaper notice or a week's wait.

What Australia issues for this purpose is a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) from DFAT. The [Australian Embassy in Athens](https://greece.embassy.gov.au/athn/marriages.html) says Greek municipal and religious bodies both require it. The open question is whether your CNI names your partner, so confirm that with DFAT and with your town hall before you plan around the shorter route.

Elsewhere, practice varies. The [Municipality of Athens](https://www.cityofathens.gr/en/service/issuance-of-civil-marriage-license/) publishes a route only for its residents, and we could not find an official page for Santorini (Thira) or Mykonos. Ask those town halls directly.

| Route | Legal in Greece? | What you need | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Civil | Yes | Mayor's licence, or for couples living abroad a foreign certificate naming your partner, where the town hall accepts it | Open to same-sex couples under Law 5089/2024 |
| Greek Orthodox | Yes | The same licence or its equivalent, plus the church's own requirements | Ask the parish priest early |
| Symbolic | No | Nothing legal in Greece; marry legally in Australia first | Full freedom of venue and wording |

## Is it really an eight-day wait?

Yes, on one official page. The gov.gr civil licence entry, updated 5 November 2026, lists a completion deadline of eight days: a seven-day wait for objections from third parties, then one day to grant the licence. The foreign-resident entry describes the same one-week wait with the licence granted straight after it, and Athens says its licence is issued seven days after your documents are submitted and verified. So the law sets one week, and the full process runs seven to eight days.

The detail that matters most is what starts the clock. The week runs from the day the town hall accepts a complete application. Staff first check that every document is there and that your newspaper notice has already been published; only then do you receive a protocol number. If anything is missing, the file is returned with instructions and the process starts again.

If a third party lodges a written objection, the mayor invites you to answer it and decides after hearing you. One more rule: if you do not marry within six months of the newspaper notice, the notice must be published again.

For couples living abroad whose town hall accepts a certificate naming the partner, there is no Greek licence and so no week to wait, on the Chania and Heraklion lists at least. If your town hall does run the licence for you, count eight days from the day your complete file is accepted, never from the day you land.

## The Australian documents and where each one comes from

Every Australian public document Greece receives needs an apostille (the Hague Convention certificate one member country uses to accept another's documents) and a Greek translation, says gov.gr. Your papers come from three places: DFAT, your state or territory Births, Deaths and Marriages registry, and the court or registry that recorded any earlier divorce or death.

[IMAGE: documents]

| Document | Issued by | Apostille | Greek translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CNI | DFAT: Passport Offices, missions overseas or by mail | Ask your town hall whether it wants one | Yes | AUD 181 per CNI (2026 fee); the Embassy says each non-Greek partner needs one |
| Birth certificate | Your state or territory BDM registry | DFAT, AUD 105 (2026 fee) | Yes | Athens wants documents issued within the last six months; Chania says "recent" |
| Divorce certificate or former partner's death certificate | The court or BDM registry | DFAT, AUD 105 | Yes | Only if you were married before |
| Passport | Australian Passport Office | No | No | Also shows you are lawfully in Greece |

For the CNI, at least one of you must be an Australian citizen or permanent resident. You sign the application in front of an authorised witness, such as a Justice of the Peace, and apply by mail or in person, paying [DFAT's fee](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services) when you submit. DFAT fees rise with CPI each 1 January, so a 2027 wedding will pay the 2027 rate.

Where the CNI is issued matters too. The Embassy says the acceptable place of issue (a DFAT office in Australia, the Embassy in Athens or another Australian mission) "can differ significantly between organisations", and that you can lodge the application in Australia for issue in Greece. [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-certificate-no-impediment-marriage) adds that DFAT cannot advise on another country's process, so ask your town hall, or your priest for an Orthodox wedding, before you apply.

Apostilles are booked at any Australian Passport Office, where someone can attend for you, or sent by registered post. DFAT publishes no fixed [processing time](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised) and warns of delays at the end of the school year and in peak holidays. Couples applying over an Australian summer for a June wedding should start early.

## Translations, Greek names and the newspaper notice

The Australian Embassy's page still says Greece accepts only translations by the Greek Foreign Ministry's Translation Section or a Greek consulate. That has changed. On 1 September 2021 the [Greek Foreign Ministry](https://www.mfa.gr/en/announcement-regarding-the-simplification-of-procedure-for-the-official-translation-of-documents/) replaced its in-house service, under Law 4781/2021, with a [Register of Certified Translators](https://mfa.gr/en/services/register-of-certified-translators). You choose a translator from the register, searchable at metafraseis.services.gov.gr, and contact them directly. Your documents must carry their apostille first.

Today's routes are a certified translator from that register or a Greek consulate. The Chania and Heraklion lists also name a Greek lawyer and graduates of the Ionian University's translation department, so ask your town hall which it accepts. No Greek official source we read mentions NAATI, the Australian accreditation, so do not assume an Australian translation will be accepted in Greece.

Names need care. Heraklion writes names on the application and in the newspaper in Greek letters, and Athens also prints a foreign partner's details in Latin characters. On the civil licence entry, details on the birth certificate, ID and notice must match, so check that every spelling agrees with your passport before translation.

[IMAGE: greek-script]

The newspaper notice is part of the resident's licence: gov.gr, Athens and Heraklion's resident list all require it, published in a local daily. It does not appear on the Chania or Heraklion lists for couples living abroad. Greek fees are paid in euros as an e-paravolo (an electronic fee code), by bank, card, web banking or Hellenic Post. ## Civil or Greek Orthodox: how the route changes

The licence rule is the same for both: a civil wedding and a wedding in the Orthodox Church each need the mayor's licence, or its equivalent for couples living abroad. The Embassy adds that religious bodies require the CNI just as town halls do.

[IMAGE: ceremony]

An Orthodox wedding brings the church's own conditions, which we have not seen set out on an official church page. Speak to the parish priest and the local Metropolis (the diocese) before you book anything, and confirm the CNI's place of issue with them, as the Embassy advises.

Same-sex couples can marry in a civil ceremony under Law 5089/2024. Australia's [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) notes that the right to marry here no longer depends on sex or gender.

If the paperwork does not suit your timeline, a symbolic ceremony in Greece after a legal marriage at home is an honest alternative. An Australian celebrant cannot legally marry you overseas, so the legal part happens in Australia.

## Your wedding week: town hall days, witnesses and registration

This is the part your guests never see. Here is an illustration, never a promise, built on the Chania and Heraklion lists for couples living abroad.

- **Months before:** Chania accepts documents by email, early enough to be checked and signed before a date and time are set. Lodge them as soon as your translations are back.
- **On arrival:** take your originals to the town hall. Chania does not accept photocopies. Bring your passports, which show you are lawfully in Greece, a condition the 2019 circular sets for every foreign couple.
- **Ceremony day:** Heraklion asks each partner to bring an adult witness with ID (a passport for foreigners), and a witness may be a relative. Its ceremonies are held in the Municipal Council Hall, and its page lists EUR 100 (about AUD 162) for Tuesday or Thursday at 12:00 and EUR 150 (about AUD 243) for Wednesday or Friday from 18:00. The fees come from a 2007 council decision, so confirm them with the town hall.
- **After the ceremony:** the marriage is registered at the registry office (lixiarcheio) of the municipality where it took place. Copies of the certificate are free, with a 10-day completion deadline. Digital copies need Greek tax-system log-ins, which visiting Australians may not have, so plan on asking in person.

[IMAGE: wedding-week]

Before you fly home, ask the town hall how to have the certificate apostilled, because the Australian Passport Office asks for foreign certificates, where it accepts them, to be legalised. Around those town-hall days sit your guests: arrivals, rooms near the venue, the welcome dinner. ## Back home: is a Greek marriage valid in Australia?

This is the question parents ask first. [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) says that in most cases an overseas marriage is legally recognised in Australia if it was valid where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. It is not recognised if either of you was already married, under age, too closely related or forced to marry. The rules sit in Part VA of the Marriage Act 1961, and Smartraveller suggests a lawyer if you want certainty.

You cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia: your Greek certificate is your proof. For the general steps, read [what to do with a marriage certificate issued overseas](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate).

Changing your name takes one extra step. You can keep travelling on your current passport. For a new one in your married name, the [Australian Passport Office](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender) always asks an Australian-born applicant who married overseas for a change of name certificate from their state or territory registry. Some states and territories do not accept overseas certificates for a name change, so check yours. [NSW](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/births-deaths-marriages/about-registry/using-our-services/proof-of-identity), for example, wants foreign documents translated in full by a NAATI-accredited translator: here, at home, NAATI is the right route.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get a marriage licence in Greece?

The gov.gr civil licence entry lists eight days: a seven-day objection period, then one day to grant. The week starts only once the town hall accepts a complete application. Couples living abroad may not need a Greek licence at all; ask your town hall.

### Do Australians need to live in Greece to get married there?

No. Chania and Heraklion publish document lists for foreigners living abroad, built on a foreign certificate that names your partner. Athens publishes only a residents' route, so ask the town hall where you plan to marry.

### Is a marriage in Greece legally recognised in Australia?

In most cases, yes, if it was valid in Greece and would have been valid under Australian law. You cannot register it in Australia; your Greek certificate is the proof.

### Can same-sex couples legally marry in Greece?

Yes. Law 5089/2024 provides for civil marriage between people of the same sex, and Australian law no longer determines the right to marry by sex or gender.

### How long can Australians stay in Greece without a visa?

Greece is in the Schengen area, and [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) says Australians can stay 90 days or less for a short stay without a visa in some cases. Register under the EU Entry/Exit System on arrival, and check ETIAS status before you book.
