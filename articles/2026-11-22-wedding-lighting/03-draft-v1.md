# Wedding Lighting Ideas for a Glowing Reception

[IMAGE: hero]

A reception abroad is lit twice. First by the sun, which sets on your date at whatever time it sets at the destination, not at home. Then by everything you have planned to take over from it. Wedding lighting decides how every surface, face and flower reads after dusk, and it is the hardest thing to fix on the night.

This guide builds a lighting plan in layers, then adapts it to a villa terrace, a beach or island, a vineyard and a ballroom. It covers timing around golden hour, what reads well on camera, the power and weather work nobody sees, and the fire rules to check before a single candle is ordered. Sources current as at November 2026.

## How wedding lighting layers work together

Good reception lighting is three layers doing three different jobs. Plan them in order, because each one depends on the one before.

- **Ambient light** sets the mood of the whole space. Festoons over the tables, dimmed chandeliers, warm uplight washing a wall or a row of trees, and candles in number all count. It is what makes the space feel finished from the moment guests walk in.
- **Accent light** picks out what you want people to look at: the centrepieces, the florals, the cake, the couple's table, a stone archway or an old olive tree.
- **Task light** lets people move and work safely: the bar, the buffet, paths, stairs, the band and the spot where speeches are given. It should be bright enough to use and quiet enough that nobody notices it.

[IMAGE: layers]

Start with ambient, because it decides whether the space reads as one room or a collection of bright patches. Then add accent, lighting the focal point first and the guest tables second. Fill in task light last, wherever a guest might trip, queue or squint.

Keep every source warm white. Cool white makes skin look grey and turns linen blue, and a mix of warm and cool sources looks unplanned in photographs. Ask for dimmers wherever possible, because the light you want at dinner is not the light you want at 11pm on the dance floor.

## Candles, festoons and chandeliers by venue

The statement pieces change with the setting. Exposed filament bulbs, paper lanterns lit from inside with LEDs, a candelabra on each table or a modern geometric chandelier can all work, but only where the venue's structure, wind and rules allow them.

### Villa terrace

On a villa terrace in [Italy](/destinations/europe/italy), string festoons or lanterns from the pergola, beams or trees that are already there rather than building a frame. Light the building itself with warm uplight on the stone, so the villa becomes the backdrop. On the tables, group tapers of two or three heights in clusters about an arm's length apart, rather than spacing single candles evenly. For more on how the candles, linen and florals fit together, see [how stylists build a table](/style-advice/wedding-table-decor).

### Beach or island

Wind is the deciding factor on a beach in [Bali](/destinations/asia/bali) or an island lawn. Use hurricane lanterns or candles enclosed in glass, and low, warm path lights that guide guests without glare. A headland is harder than a beach, because the wind comes side on and rarely drops, so tall hurricane glass is the safer choice there. Keep any loose flame off the sand unless the venue has approved it in writing.

[IMAGE: beach]

### Vineyard

Among the vines, festoons over long tables give the classic look, with trees and barrels uplit warm from below. If you are considering [Tuscany's vineyard venues](/expert-advice/12-best-tuscany-vineyard-wedding-venues), plan to keep every flame in glass and off dry grass, and line the paths with lanterns.

### Ballroom

A ballroom already has its light, usually too much of it. Dim the chandeliers, keep the perimeter dark, and use pin spots and candles to bring the ceiling down to table height. If the room is already gilded, do not answer it with more gold: put white light on the stage and tables so the gold and ivory both read. Where flame must be enclosed, tall hurricane glass or flameless pillars at two or three heights keep the gilding catching light. In [Dubai's hotel ballrooms](/destination-weddings/wedding-reception-in-dubai), Dubai Civil Defence's published rules do not address table candles, so plan on enclosed or flameless ones until your hotel confirms.

### Fairy lights and candles, with restraint

Fairy lights work best as one gentle layer, wound through a tree or hidden in greenery; too many and the space looks like a shopfront. With candles, mix heights and confirm the venue's rules before the florist designs around them. A mirrored runner, a straight line of glass cylinders or [floating candles](/trending-the-magical-ambiance-of-floating-candles) in shallow water all look deliberate. There are [more ways to style a room in candlelight](/planning-tips-for-candlelit-wedding-reception) and [table settings for every theme](/style-advice/22-amazing-table-settings-for-every-wedding-theme) in our other guides.

## Pin spotting and uplighting for tables and florals

A pin spot is a narrow beam aimed at a single object so it glows against the softer light of the room. Use one on each centrepiece, on the cake and on the couple's table. In a ballroom, a pin spot on each centrepiece is often the difference between florals that read and florals that disappear.

[IMAGE: pin-spot]

Uplighting is a light set at floor level and aimed upwards, onto a wall, a column, a tree or a facade. Choose one warm colour for every uplight. A rainbow of colours makes a heritage building look like a nightclub, and it fights the candles.

Bring the florist and the lighting supplier together before either finalises the plan, because tall arrangements cast shadows across faces and neighbouring tables. View a lit sample table after dark before you sign off, because tonal linen and matte ceramics can read flat under evening light. If the venue has stone, timber or a view that changes with the hour, our guide to [reading a venue by its stone and light](/style-advice/wedding-style-for-your-venue) shows how to work with it rather than over it.

## Planning around golden hour and sunset

Look up the sunset time for your wedding date at the destination, not at home, using the venue or the local meteorological service. Everything else in the running order hangs from that one time.

Book portraits for golden hour, the soft light before sunset, and have the ambient layer fully on before dusk, so guests never sit in half light while their eyes adjust. On a long, light summer evening in Europe, the candles will not read until late, so the reception has to look finished in daylight. In the tropics, do not assume a long dusk: check the time for your date and plan as though the light will go quickly.

[IMAGE: golden-hour]

A simple order of lighting cues, to fold into your [sample reception running order](/expert-advice/wedding-reception-timeline):

1. **Afternoon:** the lighting crew bumps in, rigs and tests every circuit, then walks every path before guests arrive.
2. **Golden hour:** portraits, with festoons off so they do not compete with the sun.
3. **Before dusk:** ambient layer fully on, candles lit if permitted.
4. **Dinner, speeches and first dance:** ambient dimmed, pin spots on the tables, soft even light on the speaker and the dance floor.
5. **Last song:** house lights up gently, paths lit for the walk out.

## Lighting that looks beautiful on camera

Your photographer and videographer will see the room through a lens that is less forgiving than the eye. A few choices make the difference.

- **Warm, even ambient light** flatters skin. Candlelight alone is too dim for faces, so pair it with a soft overhead layer.
- **No coloured washes on faces** during speeches, the first dance and the cake cutting. Save any colour for the dance floor later in the night.
- **Light the speeches spot and the first dance area** properly, so neither relies on flash.
- **Ask about flicker.** Some low quality LEDs and dimmers flicker on video even when the eye cannot see it. Ask the supplier to test their fittings on camera before the day.
- **Share the lighting plan** with the photographer and videographer well before the wedding, so they can bring the right gear and know when each cue happens.

## Power, generators and weather at outdoor venues

This is the part of outdoor wedding lighting no guest sees, and the part a paying parent should ask about first. A villa, beach club or vineyard was not necessarily built to run a band, a kitchen and a lighting rig at once.

Confirm the venue's power capacity in writing against the combined load of the lighting, the band and the catering, and where the outlets are. Where power is limited, plan a silent generator and a backup. [Energy Safe Victoria's generator guidance](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/community-safety/emergencies/using-generator-safely) says portable generators should never be used indoors or in enclosed areas, because they emit carbon monoxide you cannot see or smell. It also says to use heavy duty extension cords rated at least equal to the generator and to protect connection points from the weather. In practice, that means the generator sits outside and well away from any marquee or guest area, run by the hire company or a licensed electrician.

[IMAGE: behind-the-scenes]

For the lights themselves, [Energy Safe Victoria's guidance on decorative lights](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/community-safety/energy-safety-guides/home-safety/christmas-light-safety) says outdoor equipment needs an IP rating of at least IP23 (the IP number shows how weatherproof a light is), and that LED or extra low voltage lights are the safest options. The same guidance says not to run leads over walkways, to secure outdoor lights against wind and turn them off in poor weather, to keep hot flood and halogen lights away from anything that might catch fire, and to use a safety switch and power boards with overload protection. That is Victorian guidance written for homes; at venues in other states and overseas, the local rules apply, so ask your lighting supplier or the venue electrician to confirm them.

Then plan for weather: if rain arrives at 6pm or the wind picks up at dinner, which pieces come down, which move under cover, and who decides?

## Candle and open flame rules to check

Some hotels and country houses ban naked flame outright, and some historic venues include LED candles in their hire for exactly that reason. Ask whether open flame is allowed, allowed only in glass or not allowed at all, whether tapers are allowed, and whether anything can be hung from beams, walls or marquee poles. Get every answer in writing before you brief the florist.

**New South Wales.** The [NSW Rural Fire Service](https://www.rfs.nsw.gov.au/fire-information/fdr-and-tobans/total-fire-ban-rules) says that during a Total Fire Ban you cannot light, maintain or use a fire in the open. Its page does not name candles, so ask your venue in writing whether that covers table candles. Solid fuel barbecues and wood fired pizza ovens cannot be used outside during a ban, and permits are suspended. Exemptions are set for each ban and need at least 21 days to assess, so one cannot be arranged at short notice.

**Victoria.** [CFA counts anywhere that is not inside a permanent structure](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/warnings-and-restrictions/fire-bans-ratings-and-restrictions/can-i-or-cant-i) with a roof and fully enclosed walls as open air, and tents are not permanent structures, so a marquee does not count as indoors. Fire pits, braziers and chimineas are banned on Total Fire Ban days, and during the Fire Danger Period they are allowed only under strict conditions on wind, clearance and a person in attendance.

**Queensland.** The [Queensland Fire Department](https://www.fire.qld.gov.au/safety-education/using-fire-outdoors/fire-bans-and-restrictions/can-cant) says open flame fire pits and braziers are not permitted during a fire ban, and all permits to light fires are cancelled automatically, so a permit arranged weeks earlier can still fall on a ban day.

**Other states and territories.** Check with your venue and the local fire authority.

Sky lanterns, also called floating or flying lanterns, are [permanently banned from supply in Australia](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/business/find-banned-products/sky-lanterns-ban). For fireworks, [SafeWork NSW](https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/licences-and-registrations/licences/explosives-and-fireworks-licences/fireworks) requires a licence and limits use to an organised public event with a supporting letter from the local council, and in Queensland private fireworks are illegal and only licensed contractors may use them. For sparklers, check with your venue and the local fire authority.

**Overseas.** Local fire rules and the venue's insurance decide. Check with your venue or planner and local authorities, and plan enclosed candles or good flameless candles in the same holders as the fallback.

Wherever you marry, keep table candles unscented, in stable, non-combustible holders, and name one person with a snuffer. Use dripless tapers, weight the linen and put every flame in glass where air moves. Keep candles clear of florals, drapery and guests' sleeves, and use LED for anything hung overhead.

## Questions to ask your venue and lighting supplier

The fear with a large styling budget is paying for something that looks like every other wedding. The answer is a lighting plan drawn for this venue and the light at the hour you marry, not a standard package. Ask for a drawing that shows where every light goes and when each one comes on.

**Ask the venue**

- What is the power capacity, where are the outlets, and are generators allowed?
- Is open flame allowed, allowed only in glass, or not at all?
- Can anything be hung or rigged from the building, and what are the rigging points and the weight each holds? On a heritage building, what is off limits?
- What are the bump in and bump out times, and the noise and lighting curfew?
- Which lighting comes with the venue hire, and can the house lights be dimmed?

**Ask the lighting supplier**

- Will you draw a plan for this venue, timed to sunset on our date?
- Is every outdoor fitting weatherproof, and what is the wind and wet weather plan?
- Who will be on site through the night to adjust the lighting between dinner, speeches and dancing?
- What do you bring, and what do you expect the venue to supply?
- Will you test the fittings on camera with our photographer and videographer?

## Frequently asked questions

### What lighting is best for a wedding reception?

Layered warm white light. Start with ambient light for the whole space (festoons, dimmed chandeliers or uplighting), add accent light on tables, florals and the cake, then task light for paths, the bar and the speeches. Keep every source warm and on dimmers where you can.

### How much lighting do you need for an outdoor wedding?

Enough ambient light to see faces clearly after sunset, plus light on every path, step and the bar. The right amount depends on the size of the space and what the venue already has, so ask your lighting supplier for a plan drawn for the site and confirm the power supply with the venue.

### Can you have real candles at a wedding venue?

Only if the venue allows naked flame, so get its rule in writing. In New South Wales, the [NSW Rural Fire Service](https://www.rfs.nsw.gov.au/fire-information/fdr-and-tobans/total-fire-ban-rules) says you cannot light or use a fire in the open on a Total Fire Ban day, so ask your venue in writing whether that covers table candles. Overseas, check with your venue or planner and local authorities.

### When should wedding lighting be turned on?

Before dusk, so guests never sit in half light. Check the sunset time for your date at the destination, then have the ambient layer fully on before the sun goes down and the crew's test finished before guests arrive.

### What is pin spotting at a wedding?

Pin spotting is a narrow beam of light aimed at one object, such as a centrepiece, the cake or the couple's table, so it glows against the softer light of the room.
