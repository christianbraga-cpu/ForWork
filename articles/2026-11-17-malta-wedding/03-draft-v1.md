faq:
  - q: "Can foreigners get married in Malta?"
    a: "Yes, including two Australians with no Maltese connection. You do both need to be there: under the Marriage Act, marriage by proxy is possible only with the Registrar's written authority, where one partner is in Malta and there are grave reasons."
  - q: "Is a marriage in Malta legally recognised in Australia?"
    a: "Generally, yes, if it was valid in Malta and would have been valid here. Smartraveller lists the exceptions: either person already married, not old enough, too closely related or forced to marry. It also suggests a lawyer if you want certainty that an overseas certificate holds up in Australia."
  - q: "Can same-sex couples get married in Malta?"
    a: "Yes. Malta introduced marriage equality in July 2017 (Act XXIII of 2017), removing all legal distinction between same-sex and different-sex couples, and the Marriage Act describes civil marriage as between two consenting individuals. Religious ceremonies follow each faith's own rules, so ask the officiant directly."
  - q: "How far in advance do you need to apply to marry in Malta?"
    a: "Three months is the minimum lead time the Registry gives. Under the Marriage Act, the date must also fall inside the banns window: not before the sixth day after the banns come down, and not more than three months after they first go up, or they are published again."
  - q: "Can you get married on Gozo?"
    a: "Yes. Couples marrying on Gozo apply for their banns at the Marriage Registry in Victoria, the Public Registry's Gozo division, rather than in Malta. The same ceremony rules apply. Ask the Public Registry for the Gozo office's contact details before you send documents."

# Malta Wedding Legal Requirements and Paperwork

[IMAGE: hero]

Yes. A Malta wedding can be a legal one for two Australians who live at home. Malta's Public Registry (Identita) publishes a process for couples living abroad and sets no residency rule. You file forms and Australian documents with its Marriage Registry Section, then marry before a Registrar in Malta. Confirm the current requirements with the Public Registry and for your own passports.

You can marry legally in Malta, or marry in Australia and celebrate in Malta. We last reviewed these sources in November 2026.

## Malta wedding: can foreigners marry legally?

Yes. The Public Registry's [guideline for EU and non-EU citizens living abroad](https://identita.gov.mt/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Marriage-Registry-Requirements-for-EU-and-Non-EU-Nationals.pdf) sets out the documents and steps for couples who live outside Malta. It calls itself indicative, so treat it as your starting list and confirm the detail with the Marriage Registry Section. Gozo has its own Marriage Registry division (see the FAQ).

| Who it suits | Couples who want the legal moment and the celebration on the same day, in Malta | Couples who would rather keep every legal step at home |

## Residency and eligibility for marrying in Malta

The guideline for couples abroad states no residency requirement, but it does ask for copies of both passports and of a valid visa or Schengen entry stamp.

The [Marriage Act (Cap. 255)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/255/eng/pdf) sets the minimum age at 18. If either of you was married before, the Registry asks for proof of how that marriage ended (see the document table). If either of you holds, or is entitled to, a second nationality, Maltese-Australians included, capacity to marry must be shown for both. Same-sex couples can marry on the same terms (see the FAQ).

[Smartraveller's Malta page](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/malta) explains that Australians can visit the Schengen area, Malta included, without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180, so a Malta wedding and a European honeymoon share one allowance. Expect fingerprints and a photo at the border.

## Can't marry legally in Malta? Marry in Australia, celebrate in Malta

Malta's route is open to you, so this is a choice, not a fallback, and it takes the Maltese paperwork off your list.

The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) sets the steps. You give a NOIM to an authorised celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the legal ceremony. The celebrant must be physically present with you and two witnesses over 18 (no video-link weddings), and an Australian celebrant cannot marry you overseas. Within 14 days the celebrant lodges the paperwork with your state or territory registry. For the detail, see [how to marry legally in Australia first](/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements).

This route removes the RZ forms and notary, the apostilles, the three-month validity window and the six-week banns deadline, and gives you an Australian certificate for any name change.

## Documents from Australia

The Public Registry sets the final list and may ask for more case by case, so confirm yours with the Marriage Registry Section before ordering. Documents must be originals from a competent civil authority, and the Registry keeps them, so order spare certified copies.

| Document | Issued by | Apostille | Translation | Validity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RZ1 (request for banns) and RZ2 (sworn declaration) | You; RZ2 signed by both of you before a notary, solicitor or Commissioner for Oaths | Ask the Public Registry | Not needed if in English | Up to 3 months from endorsement |
| Full birth certificate (or adoption certificate) | Your state or territory registry | Yes, DFAT | Not needed if in English | Ask the Public Registry |
| Free-status evidence: DFAT CNI or third-party affidavit | DFAT (CNI) or a person over 18 who knows you well (affidavit) | Ask the Public Registry | Not needed if in English | Up to 3 months from issue |
| Previous marriage certificate with divorce, or death certificate, if relevant | The issuing court or registry | Yes, DFAT | Not needed if in English | Ask the Public Registry |

[IMAGE: documents]

The Registry asks for a Free Status Certificate, or, where a country does not issue one, an affidavit from someone over 18 who knows you well. Australia is not named either way, so ask the Public Registry whether to bring a DFAT Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) or an affidavit.

For a CNI, [DFAT's CNI guide](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-certificate-no-impediment-marriage) explains that at least one of you must be an Australian citizen or permanent resident and that you sign only before an authorised witness. The fee is AUD 181 (2026 fee), and [DFAT's notarial fees](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services) rise with CPI each 1 January. The Australian High Commission in Malta does not publish its own CNI process, so apply through DFAT in Australia or ask the High Commission.

## Translations and apostilles

An apostille is a single certificate, attached by the issuing country, that confirms a public document's signature and seal so another country accepts it without an embassy stamp. Malta and Australia are both parties to the Apostille Convention, according to the [Hague Conference status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41).

Identita's [policy on foreign public documents](https://identita.gov.mt/public-registry-sec-page-policy-for-the-recognition-of-foreign-public-documents/) says documents from outside the EU must be apostilled or fully legalised. The order: get the certificate from the issuing registry or court, have DFAT apostille it (AUD 105 per document, 2026 fee), then send it to the Marriage Registry Section. DFAT says [processing times vary](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised), so leave room. Whether an RZ2 signed before an Australian notary needs its own apostille: ask the Public Registry.

Documents must be in English or Maltese, so standard Australian certificates need no translation; a translation made outside Malta or the EU must itself be apostilled. Check that every name is spelt identically across passports, certificates and forms.

## Banns and timeline

Banns are the public notice of your intended marriage, posted at the Marriage Registry for at least eight consecutive days, not counting weekends and public holidays. Two dates anchor your file, from the [Marriage Registry FAQs](https://identita.gov.mt/public-registry-faqs-marriage-registry/) and the guideline for couples abroad:

- **At least three months before:** contact the Marriage Registry Section with the place, the date and the surname or surnames you will use after marriage.
- **No later than six weeks before:** your complete file must be in. Late files are rejected.

The forms and free-status evidence are valid for three months and must still be valid on the day, so prepare them between three months and six weeks out.

Once the paperwork is approved, a Registry officer confirms by email, you pay, and a celebrant is assigned. Your appointment with the Registrar is typically one or two days before the wedding, and you both bring original passports, so plan to arrive at least two days ahead.

As an illustration only (the Public Registry and your celebrant set the real timeline):

| When | Marry legally in Malta | Marry in Australia |
|---|---|---|
| 12 months out | Choose the route; check passports have 6 months' validity beyond the trip; email the Registry to confirm your document list | Choose your celebrant and give the NOIM (valid up to 18 months; at least one month before the legal ceremony) |
| 9 to 4 months out | Order full birth certificates and any divorce or death certificates; DFAT apostilles | Plan the celebration in Malta |
| At least 3 months out | Contact the Marriage Registry Section to start the banns | Meet your celebrant; fix the legal ceremony date |
| 3 months to 6 weeks out | Sign RZ1 and RZ2 before a notary; CNI or affidavit; apostilles as the Registry directs; file complete by 6 weeks | The legal ceremony in Australia, a few weeks before you fly |
| Wedding week | Registrar appointment 1 to 2 days before; civil ceremony | The celebration in Malta (no legal step) |

## The civil ceremony in Malta

Under the Marriage Act, a civil marriage is led by the Registrar or an officer the Registrar authorises, at the Marriage Registry, on a Maltese registered ship, or at another place open to the public that the Registrar accepts. A private villa is not automatically suitable.

You may propose your own words, readings or music by asking no later than seven days before. The act of marriage is lodged for registration straight after the ceremony.

You need at least two witnesses over 18, relatives included, and the Registry asks for copies of their ID (see [who can act as a witness and what they do](/expert-advice/wedding-witnesses)).

Registry fees (fee schedule published 2024), payable by card or exact cash:

| Ceremony | Registry fee |
|---|---|
| Civil marriage at the Marriage Registry | EUR 76.95 |
| Civil marriage at another location | EUR 102.60 |
| Religious marriage in a Roman Catholic church | EUR 51.30 |
| Marriage under another religious denomination | EUR 102.60 |

These cover the Registry only, not your venue, notary, apostilles or translations. Ask the Public Registry how to order copies of your marriage certificate after the wedding.

## Religious and symbolic ceremonies

A church wedding in Malta can carry civil effect, but the civil banns come first. For a Catholic marriage, ten days before you collect two copies of the Act of Marriage and the Certificate of Banns for the parish priest, who returns the signed act to the Public Registry. For church-side preparation, ask the parish as well as the Public Registry.

A symbolic ceremony has no legal effect. If you married in Australia first, your Malta celebration can be in a palazzo courtyard, at the hour and in the words you choose.

## Recognition and name change back in Australia

If you married in Australia, your celebrant has lodged the record with your registry, and that certificate is your record.

If you married in Malta, [Smartraveller's advice on marrying overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) says the marriage cannot be registered in Australia, but in most cases it is recognised if it was valid where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. The legal basis is Part VA of the Marriage Act 1961. The certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, though some states and territories do not accept overseas certificates for a change of name. See [what to do with a marriage certificate issued overseas](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate), and ask the Public Registry whether your Maltese certificate needs an apostille for Australian use.

You can keep travelling on your current passport; the [Australian Passport Office](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender) recommends booking travel in the name on it. For a passport in a married name, a spouse born in Australia who married overseas needs a change of name certificate from their state or territory registry. A foreign certificate is accepted only where no Australian equivalent exists, and it must be legalised.

By contrast, in NSW a person legally married in Australia can take their spouse's family name without registering a change of name. Check your own registry.

## FAQ

### Can foreigners get married in Malta?

Yes, including two Australians with no Maltese connection. You do both need to be there: under the Marriage Act, marriage by proxy is possible only with the Registrar's written authority, where one partner is in Malta and there are grave reasons.

### Is a marriage in Malta legally recognised in Australia?

Generally, yes, if it was valid in Malta and would have been valid here. Smartraveller lists the exceptions: either person already married, not old enough, too closely related or forced to marry. It also suggests a lawyer if you want certainty that an overseas certificate holds up in Australia.

### Can same-sex couples get married in Malta?

Yes. Malta introduced marriage equality in July 2017 (Act XXIII of 2017), removing all legal distinction between same-sex and different-sex couples, and the Marriage Act describes civil marriage as between two consenting individuals. Religious ceremonies follow each faith's own rules, so ask the officiant directly.

### How far in advance do you need to apply to marry in Malta?

Three months is the minimum lead time the Registry gives. Under the Marriage Act, the date must also fall inside the banns window: not before the sixth day after the banns come down, and not more than three months after they first go up, or they are published again.

### Can you get married on Gozo?

Yes. Couples marrying on Gozo apply for their banns at the Marriage Registry in Victoria, the Public Registry's Gozo division, rather than in Malta. The same ceremony rules apply. Ask the Public Registry for the Gozo office's contact details before you send documents.
