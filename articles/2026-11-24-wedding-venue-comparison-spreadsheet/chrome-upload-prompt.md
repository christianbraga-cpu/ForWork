## Article body

# Wedding Venue Comparison Spreadsheet and What to Compare

[IMAGE: wedding-venue-comparison-spreadsheet-couple-laptop-01.webp]

Three venue tours in, the details start to blur. Which one had the 10 PM music rule? Was the terrace quote with tax or without it? A wedding venue comparison spreadsheet settles that: every venue in the same columns, every answer in writing, so you compare like with like rather than whichever place had the best light on the day you visited.

Below is a blank template to copy, the four columns to settle first, how to read what a quote does and does not include, what Australian Consumer Law says about deposits (and where it stops), and what changes when the venue is overseas. Sources current as at November 2026.

## How to use a wedding venue comparison spreadsheet

The simplest way to compare wedding venues is to give each one a row and fill in the same columns for every venue. Then agree, together, which three or four columns will actually decide it.

1. **Start with numbers.** Have a guest count before you tour. [A working guest list](/wedding-guest-list-template) is enough; it does not need to be final.
2. **Shortlist three to five venues.** Enough to compare, few enough to tour properly. Start with [our venue search, filtered by location, capacity and venue type](/search?tab=venues), or a city page such as [Sydney wedding venues](/au/venues-in-sydney) or [venues in Dubai](/ae/venues-in-dubai).
3. **Fill every cell.** A blank cell is a question to send the venue, not a gap to guess.
4. **Rank your columns before you see prices.** If 120 guests seated is non-negotiable, a venue that seats 90 leaves the sheet, however good the view.
5. **Record the source.** Note whether each answer came from the contract, an email or the tour. Only the first two count.

**How to fill it in**

- **Minimum spend or hire:** which one it is, the amount, the currency and whether tax and service charges are inside it.
- **What is included:** tables, chairs, linen, lighting, staffing, styling, accommodation. Write "not included" rather than leaving it blank.
- **Catering:** in-house only, or outside caterers allowed, and at what fee.
- **Wet-weather plan:** the named covered space and its seated capacity.
- **Deposit and cancellation:** amount, due date, balance date, currency and what is refundable.
- **Notes:** where each answer came from, and the date.

## What to compare first

Four columns rule a venue in or out before anything else does. Settle these, then look at the rest.

**Capacity, per space.** A venue's headline number is rarely the number for your dinner. Take one Bali venue as an example. [Our listing for Nirjhara](/venue/nirjhara-bali-waterfall-weddings) gives a capacity of 150 seated overall, but The Garden seats 100 (150 standing), Ambu seats 65 (80 standing) and the Lounge seats 50 (65 standing). Its Resort Exclusivity offer, a two-night buyout of all 25 units, is for up to 60 guests. Compare the seated number for the space and the package you are actually being quoted.

[IMAGE: wedding-reception-room-long-tables-before-guests-01.webp]

**Dates.** Ask which of your dates are free, whether the venue will hold one, and for how long. A verbal "that should be fine" is not a hold.

**Exclusive use.** Ask what exactly is exclusive to you. At Nirjhara, the listing says music must end by 10 PM for non-buyout events, while no music curfew applies with an exclusive resort buyout. Exclusive use can change the whole evening, not just who else is on the property.

**Total cost, including minimum spend.** Two quotes that look similar can describe very different things. Before you compare totals, make sure each one is the same kind of number, which is the next section.

## Reading what is and is not included

**Minimum spend or venue hire.** Venue hire is a fee for the space. A minimum spend is a floor on what you spend with the venue, so ask what counts towards it. Our [Dubai wedding cost guide](/destination-weddings/dubai-wedding-cost), for example, suggests asking whether a quoted rate includes the municipality fee, service charge and VAT. Put "plus tax" or "tax included" in the cell, every time.

**Venue hire abroad.** Our [guide to what a wedding in Italy costs](/expert-advice/how-much-does-a-wedding-in-italy-cost) says exclusive hire in Tuscany for 60 to 80 guests across three nights typically starts at around EUR 27,000 to EUR 30,000 in 2026. That is venue hire, not the wedding total.

**Accommodation and room blocks.** Separate your own room from your guests' rooms. Our [destination wedding packages](/destination-wedding-packages) list guest accommodation as paid separately, usually at negotiated group rates, while the ceremony venue and styling, a dedicated planner and concierge, and the couple's accommodation are included in every package. The same page notes that specific inclusions vary by resort, so confirm each one in writing.

**The extras that hide in footnotes.** Our [Bali cost guide](/expert-advice/bali-wedding-cost-the-complete-2026-guide) lists generator hire, overtime, corkage, wet weather marquees, government taxes and service charges among the hidden costs. Add local fees such as community or permit charges, which vary by place; ask each venue to list them. Give each a line in your Notes column until the venue confirms it in writing.

**Catering and outside suppliers.** Some venues allow only their own kitchen. Nirjhara's listing, for example, says outside food and drinks are not permitted, and exceptions for food require prior approval. For decorators, [ask the venue in writing](/style-advice/wedding-style-for-your-venue) for load-in hours, rigging points and fabric fire ratings, and whether an outside decorator pays a fee or works beside the in-house team. If outside suppliers are welcome, you can [search planners, caterers and stylists among our trusted vendors](/search?tab=vendors).

## Deposits, cancellation and the contract

Fill the deposit column from the contract itself, not the brochure. This is general information, not legal advice.

Under the Australian Consumer Law, the ACCC says a business [must not accept payment](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/not-receiving-products-or-services-that-are-paid-for) for services it does not intend to supply, or knows (or should know) it cannot supply by the promised date. If a service has a major problem, you can [cancel the contract and get a refund](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel), which may not be full if work has been done, and terms and conditions cannot take that right away. A change of mind, or a third party stopping the venue from supplying, generally runs on the contract.

These are Australian rules; do not assume they cover a venue overseas. Get the terms in writing for every venue on your sheet:

- the deposit amount and when it is due
- the balance date and the currency of each payment
- what is refundable, and when, if you cancel or postpone
- what happens if the venue cannot host (closure, sale, a third party)
- any security deposit, and the deductions it covers: Nirjhara's listing asks for an IDR 10,000,000 security deposit per event, refundable after any applicable deductions

If a contract is in another language, ask for an English version or have it translated before you sign. Exchange rates move between deposit and balance, so note the currency of every payment and keep a buffer.

In [Wonderland Studio](/wonderland-studio) you can log deposits against your budget and pay a venue's invoice or payment link securely through Wedded Wonderland. Paying does not by itself confirm a booking, and if you pay in another currency, your bank or card provider may add its own foreign-transaction charge.

## What changes for a venue abroad

Five columns matter more once the venue is overseas.

**Legal ceremony on site.** Ask the venue whether marriages held there are legally registered, or whether couples marry legally elsewhere and celebrate on site. Then confirm the requirements for your own passports with the authority that registers the marriage. Start with [where you can legally marry abroad](/expert-advice/weddings-abroad-where-can-you-legally-get-married). Our packages page lists legal and documentation as paid separately and destination-dependent, so give it its own line in the budget.

**Curfews and noise.** Ask whether a music curfew is the venue's own rule or set by local authorities, and whether a buyout changes it, as it does at Nirjhara.

**Wet weather.** In Indonesia, Smartraveller says [weather conditions can change quickly during the wet season](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/asia/indonesia), usually around October to April, and that flooding, landslides and mudslides occur regularly in that season. Nirjhara's listing says ceremony and dinner venues depend on weather and guest numbers. Ask for the named covered space, its seated capacity, and who makes the call and by what time.

[IMAGE: bali-wedding-covered-pavilion-wet-weather-plan-01.webp]

**Access and transfers.** Ask how long the drive is from the nearest airport, whether coaches can reach the door, and how late the last transfer can leave after the music stops. Older guests will thank you for the answer.

**Local permits.** At Nirjhara, fireworks require hotel approval and valid police and village permits. Ask each venue what needs a permit and who applies for it, and get every answer in writing.

## What to email every venue on your shortlist

Send the same questions to every venue on your sheet, and paste the replies into the Notes column. For [a longer list for the venue tour itself](/20-essential-questions-to-ask-your-wedding-venue-before-booking), see our tour guide.

- Which spaces and hours are exclusive to us, and is there a guest cap on the package you quoted?
- Will you hold our date in writing, and for how long?
- Is this venue hire or a minimum spend, and does it include tax, service charges and local fees?
- Can we bring outside caterers, decorators or a planner, and is there a fee?
- What is the wet-weather plan, in which space, and who decides?
- What deposit is due, when is the balance due, in what currency, and what is refundable if we cancel or postpone?

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a wedding venue comparison spreadsheet include?

One row per venue, with identical columns for capacity, dates, exclusive use, price type, inclusions, catering, accommodation, the wet-weather plan, curfews, transfers and deposit terms. Abroad, add whether a legal ceremony can happen on site, and note every answer's source.

### How many venues should we compare at once?

We suggest three to five. Fewer gives you little to measure against; more, and the tours and follow-up emails start to blur. If a venue fails one of your deciding columns, take it off the sheet.

### What is the difference between a minimum spend and venue hire?

Venue hire pays for the space; a minimum spend is a floor on your spend there. Our Dubai cost guide, for example, says some luxury spaces set a minimum spend, from AED 60,000 at a desert resort to AED 120,000 for an outdoor amphitheatre (November 2026), and suggests asking whether a quoted rate includes the 7% municipality fee, 10% service charge and 5% VAT.

### Can we get our venue deposit back if we cancel?

If you cancel by choice, your contract decides. If an Australian venue has a major problem with its service, the ACCC says terms and conditions cannot take away your right to a remedy, but if a third party's actions stopped the venue from supplying, your right to a refund will generally depend on the terms of your agreement.

### Does Australian Consumer Law protect us with a venue overseas?

Do not assume it does. The ACCC guidance describes Australian law, and a venue abroad may contract under local law. Treat the written contract as your protection: deposit, balance dates, currency, postponement and what happens if the venue cannot host.
