# Wedding Venue Comparison Spreadsheet and What to Compare

[IMAGE: hero]

Three venue tours in, the details start to blur. Which one had the 10 PM music rule? Was the terrace quote with tax or without it? A wedding venue comparison spreadsheet settles that: every venue in the same columns, every answer in writing, so you compare like with like rather than whichever place had the best light on the day you visited.

Below is a blank template to copy, the four columns to settle first, how to read what a quote does and does not include, what Australian Consumer Law says about deposits (and where it stops), and what changes when the venue is overseas. Sources current as at November 2026.

## How to use a wedding venue comparison spreadsheet

Shortlist three to five venues, give each one a row, and fill in the same columns for every venue. Then agree, together, which three or four columns will actually decide it.

1. **Start with numbers.** Have a guest count before you tour. [A working guest list](/wedding-guest-list-template) is enough; it does not need to be final.
2. **Shortlist.** Three to five venues is enough to compare and few enough to tour properly. Start from [the venues we list](/venue), or by city, such as [Sydney](/au/venues-in-sydney) or [Dubai](/ae/venues-in-dubai).
3. **Fill every cell.** A blank cell is a question to send the venue, not a gap to guess.
4. **Rank your columns before you see prices.** If 120 guests seated is non-negotiable, a venue that seats 90 leaves the sheet, however good the view.
5. **Record the source.** Note whether each answer came from the contract, an email or the tour. Only the first two count.

## The wedding venue comparison spreadsheet

Copy this into a spreadsheet or print it landscape. One row per venue; add rows as your shortlist grows.

**How to fill it in**

- **Capacity (seated):** the seated number for the space you would use, not the venue's largest figure.
- **Exclusive use:** which spaces are yours, for which hours, and who else is on the property that day.
- **Minimum spend or hire:** which one it is, the amount, the currency and whether tax and service charges are inside it.
- **What is included:** tables, chairs, linen, lighting, staffing, styling, accommodation. Write "not included" rather than leaving it blank.
- **Catering:** in-house only, or outside caterers allowed, and at what fee.
- **Wet-weather plan:** the named covered space and its seated capacity.
- **Deposit and cancellation:** amount, due date, balance date, currency and what is refundable.
- **Notes:** where each answer came from, and the date.

## What to compare first

Four columns rule a venue in or out before anything else does. Settle these, then look at the rest.

**Capacity, per space.** A venue's headline number is rarely the number for your dinner. [Our listing for Nirjhara in Bali](/venue/nirjhara-bali-waterfall-weddings) gives a capacity of 150 seated overall, but The Garden seats 100 (150 standing), Ambu seats 65 (80 standing) and the Lounge seats 50 (65 standing). Its Resort Exclusivity offer, a two-night buyout of all 25 units, is for up to 60 guests. Compare the seated number for the space and the package you are actually being quoted.

[IMAGE: capacity]

**Dates.** Ask which of your dates are free, whether the venue will hold one, and for how long. A verbal "that should be fine" is not a hold.

**Exclusive use.** Ask what exactly is exclusive to you. At Nirjhara, the same listing says music must end by 10 PM for non-buyout events, while no music curfew applies with an exclusive resort buyout. Exclusive use can change the whole evening, not just who shares the pool.

**Total cost, including minimum spend.** Two quotes that look similar can describe very different things. Before you compare totals, make sure each one is the same kind of number, which is the next section.

## Reading what is and is not included

**Minimum spend or venue hire.** Venue hire is a fee for the space. A minimum spend is a floor on what you spend with the venue, so ask what counts toward it. Our [Dubai wedding cost guide](/destination-weddings/dubai-wedding-cost) says some luxury spaces set a minimum spend, from AED 60,000 at a desert resort to AED 120,000 for an outdoor amphitheatre (November 2026), and suggests asking whether a quoted rate includes the 7% municipality fee, 10% service charge and 5% VAT. [[NEEDS: AUD approximation and conversion date, Nic]]

**Venue hire abroad.** Our [guide to what a wedding in Italy costs](/expert-advice/how-much-does-a-wedding-in-italy-cost) says exclusive hire in Tuscany for 60 to 80 guests across three nights typically starts at around EUR 27,000 to EUR 30,000 in 2026, and that one Lake Garda villa offers a 2026 two-night exclusive package from EUR 24,000 to EUR 30,000, depending on the month. That is venue hire, not the wedding total. [[NEEDS: AUD approximation and conversion date, Nic]]

**Tax.** Our [Lake Como cost guide](/expert-advice/how-much-does-a-lake-como-wedding-cost) notes that Italian VAT is 22% on most services, so check whether each quote includes it. Put "plus tax" or "tax included" in the cell, every time.

**Accommodation and room blocks.** Separate your own room from your guests' rooms. Our [destination wedding packages](/destination-wedding-packages) list guest accommodation as paid separately, usually at negotiated group rates, while the ceremony venue and styling, a dedicated planner and concierge, and the couple's accommodation are included in every package.

**The extras that hide in footnotes.** Our [Bali cost guide](/expert-advice/bali-wedding-cost-the-complete-2026-guide) lists banjar (local community) fees, generator hire, overtime, corkage, wet weather marquees, government taxes and service charges among the hidden costs. Give each a line in your Notes column until the venue confirms it in writing.

**Catering and outside suppliers.** Some venues allow only their own kitchen. Nirjhara's listing says outside food and drinks are not permitted, and exceptions for food require prior approval. For decorators, [ask the venue in writing](/style-advice/wedding-style-for-your-venue) for load-in hours, rigging points and fabric fire ratings, and whether an outside decorator pays a fee or works beside the in-house team. If outside suppliers are welcome, you can [search planners, caterers and stylists in our Marketplace](/search?tab=vendors).

**Power at estates and villas.** A blank lawn often means hired power. Energy Safe Victoria says portable generators [should never be used indoors or in enclosed areas](https://www.energysafe.vic.gov.au/community-safety/emergencies/using-generator-safely), because they emit carbon monoxide, so ask where the generator will sit and who supplies it.

## Deposits, cancellation and the contract

Fill the deposit column from the contract itself, not the brochure. This is general information, not legal advice.

The ACCC says that under the Australian Consumer Law, businesses [must not accept payment](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/not-receiving-products-or-services-that-are-paid-for) for services they don't intend to supply, intend to supply differently from what was promised, or know (or should know) they won't be able to supply by the promised date or within a reasonable time. Where the actions of a third party prevented a business from supplying, the ACCC says it does not have to give refunds under the consumer guarantees, and your right to a refund will generally depend on the terms and conditions of your agreement.

When a service has a major problem, the ACCC says you can [cancel the contract and get a refund](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel), which may not be a full refund because you pay a reasonable amount for work already done, or keep the contract and pay a lower price. Businesses cannot use terms and conditions to take those rights away. But if you simply change your mind, the consumer guarantees do not entitle you to a refund, so a cancellation by choice runs on the contract.

These are Australian Consumer Law rules; do not assume they cover a venue overseas. Get the terms in writing for every venue on your sheet:

- the deposit amount and when it is due
- the balance date and the currency of each payment
- what is refundable, and when, if you cancel or postpone
- what happens if the venue cannot host (closure, sale, a third party)
- any security deposit, and the deductions it covers: Nirjhara's listing asks for an IDR 10,000,000 security deposit per event, refundable after any applicable deductions [[NEEDS: AUD approximation and conversion date, Nic]]

## What changes for a venue abroad

Five columns matter more once the venue is overseas.

**Legal ceremony on site.** Ask the venue whether marriages held there are legally registered, or whether couples marry legally elsewhere and celebrate on site. Then confirm the requirements for your own passports with the authority that registers the marriage. Start with [where you can legally marry abroad](/expert-advice/weddings-abroad-where-can-you-legally-get-married), or the [legal steps for marrying in Italy](/expert-advice/getting-married-in-italy-the-complete-legal-guide-for-destination-couples). Our packages page lists legal and documentation as paid separately and destination-dependent, so give it its own line in the budget.

**Curfews and noise.** Ask whether a music curfew is the venue's own rule or set by local authorities, and whether a buyout changes it, as it does at Nirjhara.

**Wet weather.** In Indonesia, Smartraveller says [weather conditions can change quickly during the wet season](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/asia/indonesia), usually around October to April, and that flooding, landslides and mudslides occur regularly in that season. Nirjhara's listing says ceremony and dinner venues depend on weather and guest numbers. Ask for the named covered space, its seated capacity, and who makes the call and by what time.

## Questions to ask every venue

Send the same questions to every venue on your sheet, and paste the replies into the Notes column. For [a longer list for the venue tour itself](/20-essential-questions-to-ask-your-wedding-venue-before-booking), see our tour guide.

- What exactly is exclusive to us: which spaces, for which hours, and who else is on the property that day?
- What is the seated capacity of each space we would use, and is there a guest cap on the package you quoted?
- Which of our dates are free, and will you hold one in writing? For how long?
- Is this a venue hire fee or a minimum spend, and what counts toward it?
- Does the quote include tax, service charges and any local fees?
- Can we bring outside caterers, decorators or a planner, and is there a fee?
- What are the noise, curfew and amplified-music rules, and are they yours or set locally?
- What is the wet-weather plan, in which space, for how many seated, and who decides?
- What are the load-in hours, and is open flame allowed?
- What deposit is due, when is the balance due, in what currency, and what is refundable if we cancel or postpone?
- Can a legally recognised ceremony take place here?

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a wedding venue comparison spreadsheet include?

One row per venue and the same columns for each: seated capacity per space, available dates, exclusive use, minimum spend or hire fee, what is included, catering rules, accommodation, ceremony space, the wet-weather plan, curfew and noise rules, access and transfers, deposit and cancellation terms and, for a venue abroad, whether a legal ceremony can be held on site. Leave a notes column for where each answer came from.

### How many venues should we compare at once?

We suggest three to five. Fewer, and you have little to measure against; more, and the tours and follow-up emails start to blur. If a venue fails one of your deciding columns, take it off the sheet rather than keeping it in reserve.

### What is the difference between a minimum spend and venue hire?

Venue hire is a fee for the space. A minimum spend is a floor on what you spend with the venue, so ask what counts toward it and whether tax and service charges sit inside or on top. Our Dubai cost guide, for example, gives minimum spends of AED 60,000 to AED 120,000 at some spaces and suggests asking whether a quoted rate includes the 7% municipality fee, 10% service charge and 5% VAT.

### Can we get our venue deposit back if we cancel?

If you cancel because you changed your mind, the ACCC says the consumer guarantees do not entitle you to a refund, so your contract terms decide it. If an Australian venue cannot supply what it promised, the Australian Consumer Law gives you rights that terms and conditions cannot take away. Read the deposit and cancellation clauses before you pay, and keep them in writing. This is general information, not legal advice.

### Does Australian Consumer Law protect us with a venue overseas?

Do not assume it does. The ACCC guidance in this article describes Australian Consumer Law, and a venue abroad may contract under local law. Treat the written contract as your protection: deposit, balance dates, currency, postponement and what happens if the venue cannot host.
