# AI Wedding Vows and How to Make Them Sound Like You

[IMAGE: hero]

AI wedding vows work best when the AI drafts and you decide. A general AI chat assistant is quick at structure: it can sort messy notes, suggest an order and flag lines that sound like everyone else's. What it cannot know is the joke only the two of you understand, or the promise your partner has been waiting to hear. Treat it as a drafting partner, not a ghostwriter. Here is how: start from your own stories, prompt it to keep your phrasing, cut the generic lines, time it aloud and leave the legal words alone, at home or abroad.

[KEY TAKEAWAYS]

## Start with your own stories

A chatbot's first draft is generic by design, because without your stories it can only reach for the phrases every vow already uses. So write the raw material before you open a chat. [Our vow-writing method](/planning/how-to-write-wedding-vows) says to "write a messy first draft from your own memories before you read any examples," and its five questions make a good warm-up.

Celebrant Josh Withers, talking to us about example vows found through a search engine, put it this way: "They're all awesome examples of how to party, but they're not you." We would say the same of a chatbot's first attempt.

Give yourself ten minutes with a voice memo or a page of bullets:

- **Memories.** Three specific moments, each with a place, a time and something that was said.
- **Habits.** One thing your partner does that nobody else would notice.
- **Promises.** Two to four you could keep on an ordinary Tuesday, not only on the day.
- **Inside language.** The nickname, running joke or phrase only the two of you use, with a note on whether it works in front of grandparents.

Before either of you starts, agree whether you will use AI and how much, plus a rough length and tone. Nobody should find out at the altar that the other vow came from a chatbot.

## Prompts that keep it personal

Paste your notes in first, then ask for help shaping them, not replacing them. Our [notes on prompting any AI assistant](/expert-advice/chatgpt-vs-ai-wedding-planner) suggest giving the basics up front, asking for options rather than answers, asking it to show its assumptions and asking what to check. Here is how that looks for vows, in four prompts we wrote for this guide.

> **Interview me first.** "Act as an editor, not an author. Ask me eight questions about my partner, one at a time. Do not write anything until I have answered them all."

> **Give me options.** "Using only my notes above, suggest three ways to order them into a vow of about 150 words. Show outlines, not a finished vow, and keep my phrases where you can."

> **Flag the clichés.** "Mark every line in my draft that a stranger could say about their own partner. Do not rewrite them. Tell me why each one is generic."

> **Show your assumptions.** "List anything in this draft you assumed about us that I did not tell you."

If it hands you a finished vow anyway, ask it to go back to the outline.

## Edit out the generic lines

Our vow guide's test is the one to run on every line: "If a stranger could say a line about their own partner, cut it." It names soulmate, partner in crime and other half as phrases that belong to everyone. We would add my everything, every step of the way, endless love and through thick and thin.

I vow or I promise? Either works, as long as the verb commits. "I will" and "I promise" hold up; "I'll try" does not.

### Our before and after example

This example is ours, written for this guide. The couple and their story are invented.

**Before, a typical first draft from a chatbot:**

> From the moment we met, I knew you were my soulmate. You are my best friend, my partner in crime and my other half. I promise to love you endlessly, to stand by you through every high and low, and to cherish you every step of the way.

**After, the same vow rebuilt from the couple's own notes:**

> On our second date you missed the last ferry home because you would not stop talking about your grandmother's lemon tree. You have planted three of your own since. I promise to get us to the ferry on time, to finally learn her recipe, and to tell you the truth when it is hard to hear. And I promise to choose us on the ordinary days, not only this one.

What changed: one real memory replaced three labels, a habit does the work "best friend" was trying to do, and the promises are small enough to keep in ten years. The improvement came from the notes, not from a cleverer prompt.

## Check the length out loud

Our guide says to "aim for about 150 words, a minute out loud, and agree the length with your partner," adding that "300 words is the most we would suggest." Give the assistant that target before it suggests anything, then read your draft aloud with a timer, because nerves change your pace. For lines that fit, browse [vows short enough for a minute](/expert-advice/short-wedding-vows), or [ideas written for a bride](/expert-advice/wedding-vows-for-her) and [ideas written for a groom](/expert-advice/wedding-vows-for-him).

## Your vows and the legal words

In Australia, your personal vows do not replace the words the law requires. The Attorney-General's Department says that to marry here you must [use specific words during the ceremony](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married), and those words are set out in [section 45(2) of the Marriage Act 1961](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text). Each of you says to the other, in front of your celebrant and witnesses:

> "I call upon the persons here present to witness that I, A.B. (or C.D.), take thee, C.D. (or A.B.), to be my lawful wedded wife (or husband, or spouse)"

The Act allows "words to that effect", and nothing in it stops you adding your own vows. So never let an AI rewrite the legal lines. Your own vows sit alongside them and must not contradict them, and your authorised celebrant confirms the final wording, which is also who the department points couples to for guidance. Send your celebrant the finished vow early, then read it on the day from a card or a vow book, never your phone, with a spare for your celebrant. To see where the legal lines fall, here is [a full ceremony order with the legal lines in place](/expert-advice/wedding-ceremony-script-australia), and some [classic wording to borrow from](/expert-advice/traditional-wedding-vows).

### If you marry abroad

The department is clear that [overseas marriages cannot be performed by an Australian-authorised marriage celebrant](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married), and they cannot be registered in Australia. An overseas marriage is generally recognised here if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid in Australia. So if your Australian celebrant leads a ceremony overseas, it is symbolic, and the legal step happens at home or under local law. Confirm the local wording and paperwork with the registrar in the country where you marry, or that country's embassy. Planning a ceremony for two? ## Keep it private

Your vow notes may be the most personal thing you write all year. Before you paste them into any chatbot, leave out full names, health details, family conflict and anything your partner would not want stored. Use initials, and add the real names back in your own document. Check the tool's data and history settings before you start, and read its privacy policy for how it handles what you type. That is our practical advice rather than a legal rule.

## Questions about AI wedding vows

### Is it wrong to use AI to write your wedding vows?

No, as long as the stories and promises are yours. Use AI to sort and tighten your own notes rather than invent feelings, agree with your partner first whether either of you will use it, and edit until no line could belong to a stranger.

### Should you tell your partner you used AI for your vows?

Yes, ideally before either of you starts. Agree together on AI, length and tone, so nobody is surprised at the ceremony and the two vows feel matched.

### How long should AI-assisted wedding vows be?

The same as any vow. Our vow guide suggests about 150 words, a minute out loud, with 300 words the most it would suggest. Give the assistant that target, then read the result aloud with a timer.

### Is it safe to put personal stories into an AI chatbot?

Be careful with what you paste. Leave out full names, health details, family conflict and anything your partner would not want stored, check the tool's data and history settings, and read its privacy policy. That is our practical advice, not a legal rule.
