# Honeymoon Itinerary Template and Two Week Sample

[IMAGE: hero]

A honeymoon itinerary is the one document that lets you stop planning. Two weeks away after months of wedding decisions sounds simple, until you are deciding on day three whether to change hotels or stay put. So settle the shape of the trip first: where you arrive and rest, where you move, and where you leave room to do nothing at all. The places follow easily after that.

Below is a printable template to fill in together, a two-week sample built by purpose rather than by hotel, and the passport, visa, insurance and health checks Australians need before flying. If you are leaving straight from a destination wedding, there is a section for that too. Sources current as at November 2026.

## How to use this honeymoon itinerary template

Plan the shape first, then the places. Decide how many bases you want, which days are for travel and which are for rest, and only then match destinations and stays to that frame.

Work through it in three passes.

1. **Shape.** Count the nights. Mark the arrival day, any travel day between bases and the last full day before you fly home.
2. **Bookings.** Fill in stays first, then flights and transfers, then private experiences, then restaurants. That is the booking order on our [seven-day trip planner](/travel-guides/weekly-itinerary-template), where we note that in peak periods sought-after rooms can be reserved six to twelve months ahead, private experiences two to three months ahead in high season, and restaurants often two to four weeks ahead. These are indicative windows, not fixed rules; lead times vary by destination, season and experience.
3. **Details.** Add confirmation numbers, meeting points and each driver's contact in the notes column, so neither of you has to search an inbox at the kerb.

If your trip is a single week in one place, the seven-day planner may be all you need. For two weeks and two bases, use the template below.

## The honeymoon itinerary template

Print one page per base, or keep it as a shared document. Our advice is to keep every travel arrangement in one document you can both open on your phones, with an offline copy saved before departure. Where useful, save each property's address in the local language as well.

**How to fill it in**

- **Base:** where you sleep that night, with the address.

## A two week sample honeymoon itinerary

This is a structure, not a booking. It assumes a long-haul flight from Australia, two bases and one travel day between them. Swap in your own places and keep the rhythm.

[IMAGE: two-week-sample]

**Days 1 and 2: arrive and rest.** Keep the first day light: check in, eat well, sleep. On long-haul trips we keep the first two days relatively gentle and schedule more demanding activities from the third day onwards. That is our planning approach to jet lag, not medical advice. Smartraveller separately advises taking action to [prevent blood clots caused by long-haul travel](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-health), and packing a small first aid kit with SPF 30 to 50+ sunscreen and insect repellent.

**Days 3 to 6: settle into the first base.** This is where the honeymoon starts to feel like one. For a honeymoon we favour slower mornings, longer evenings and time to enjoy the property itself, rather than treating every day as a chance to explore. Build in a rest day or a half-day around the middle, so Day 5 might hold nothing but the pool and a long dinner.

**Day 7: the travel day.** Whenever the base changes, we recommend a dedicated transfer day, and we keep it flexible. Check out without hurrying, travel, arrive in daylight if you can, and book nothing that evening beyond dinner where you are sleeping. If the route includes a stopover, remember that [some countries will not let you enter](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/passport-services) unless your passport is valid for six months after you plan to leave, some airlines apply this rule themselves, and it can apply even when you are only transiting.

[IMAGE: travel-day]

**Days 8 to 12: the second base.** Choose a contrast to the first: if the first week was water and quiet, the second might be streets to walk, markets and tables to book. Keep to one or two core experiences a day and plan another half-day of rest. If a boat day, a dive or a safari drive is on the list, check your insurance covers it (see below).

**Days 13 and 14: a slow finish.** Leave the last full day loosely planned and the final morning open before the flight home. Pack the night before, so the last breakfast is a breakfast and not a checklist.

What fills each block depends on where you go. Browse our [destination pages](/destinations), or, if you married in [Bali](/destinations/asia/bali), [Greece](/destinations/europe/greece) or [Fiji](/destinations/oceania/fiji), start there and let the first base be somewhere you already love.

## Adapting it to your trip

**Straight after a destination wedding.** Our [honeymoon travel page](/travel) describes the honeymoon as "One place or several, straight after or months later, with the transitions handled." If you are flying out the morning after a wedding week, treat Days 1 and 2 as recovery and move the first real experience to Day 3 or later. Then think about names. After a change of family name through marriage, you can get a new passport or keep using your current one, and the Australian Passport Office recommends [booking travel in the name that is in your passport](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender). If either of you holds two nationalities, Smartraveller says Australians, including dual nationals, should [depart and enter Australia on their Australian passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/passport-services).

**Shorter trips.** For ten nights or fewer, drop to one base and drop the travel day; the seven-day planner linked above suits a single week.

**Longer trips.** Add a third base only if you are willing to give another day to travel, because every change of base costs one. Our guide to [how long a honeymoon should be, by destination](/expert-advice/how-long-is-a-honeymoon-a-guide-by-destination) helps you set the number of nights, and our guide on [when to go on honeymoon](/expert-advice/a-guide-on-when-to-go-on-honeymoon) helps with the months.

A free Discovery Call with a Wedded Wonderland advisor is [a quick way to test your two bases](/wonderland-concierge) before anything is booked.

## Documents, health and timing before you go

About two months before you fly is when the passport, visa and doctor's tasks overlap, so start there. Requirements are confirmed by the relevant government office, embassy or consulate for your own passports.

[IMAGE: documents]

**Passports.** Smartraveller's essentials checklist says to confirm your passport is [valid for at least six months beyond the end of your trip](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist). If you need a new one, [allow at least six weeks to get your passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/passport-services), and where possible do not book flights without a valid passport.

**Visas and entry rules.** [Get the right visa](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) for every country you are visiting or transiting through. Visa conditions often change, so check with the embassy or consulate of each destination before every trip. Getting the right visa is your responsibility, rules can change at short notice, and [Smartraveller cannot tell you which visa to get](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/getting-foreign-visa). Some countries also [hold your passport during visa processing](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/getting-foreign-visa), so you cannot travel anywhere else meanwhile. Do not leave the application to the last minute.

**Copies.** Smartraveller advises that you [photocopy your passport ID page, visa and key documents](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist), take one copy and leave one with someone you trust, keep hard copies of online bookings, and share your itinerary with friends or family. The template above is that itinerary.

**Insurance.** Smartraveller says [the right travel insurance is as essential as a passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance). For a two-base trip, choose a policy that [covers every country on the trip, including stopovers and transit points](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance), and check whether cancellation is covered if the advice level rises after you book. Insurers [exclude many activities from standard policies](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance), even common ones such as cruises, so check the included and excluded activities list. Basic policies [do not cover higher-risk activities](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-health), some cover them only if you pay extra, and it is worth checking that your tour and service providers have a good reputation.

Always [read the Product Disclosure Statement](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) before buying; basic medical-only policies usually do not cover cancellation or property. Tell your insurer about [all pre-existing health conditions](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance), and note that Smartraveller says most policies have an age limit. The reason it matters: you [cannot access Medicare or the PBS overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-health), hospital stays overseas often cost thousands of dollars per day, and [the Australian Government cannot pay travellers' bills](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance).

**Doctor's visit.** Smartraveller words this two ways. Its essentials checklist says to [get a basic health check at least 8 weeks before you go](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) and to get the recommended vaccinations. Its travel health page says to [see your doctor 6 to 8 weeks before you go](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-health), so you have time to act on their advice, and to ask about protecting yourself against blood clots on long-haul travel. Booking at least eight weeks out satisfies both.

**Medicines.** [Check your medication is legal and available](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) where you are going, take enough to last the whole trip, and carry copies of your prescription and a letter from your doctor. Smartraveller notes that [your medicine may not be available or legal](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-health) where you are going. For the rest of the bag, see our [honeymoon essentials checklist](/honeymoon-essentials-checklist).

**Updates.** Smartraveller no longer asks you to register your trip. Instead, [subscribe to Smartraveller's email updates and alerts](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/smartraveller-subscription) for each destination as soon as you start thinking about going.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many places should we visit on a two week honeymoon?

Two bases is a comfortable rhythm for two weeks. Our sample uses two, with a dedicated transfer day whenever the base changes, so you never lose a holiday day to packing and checking out. Add a third base only if you are happy to give up another day to travel.

### How far ahead should we book our honeymoon?

Our [packages page](/destination-packages) puts the ideal lead time for a honeymoon at six to nine months, to lock in the right suite category. Book stays first, then flights and transfers, then private experiences and restaurants. These are indicative windows; lead times vary by destination, season and experience.

### Should we book the honeymoon in my married name?

Book in the name on the passport you will travel on. After a change of family name through marriage, you can get a new passport or keep using your current one, and the Australian Passport Office recommends [booking travel in the name that is in your passport](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender).

### Do we need travel insurance for our honeymoon?

Smartraveller says [the right travel insurance is as essential as a passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance). Choose a policy that covers every country on the trip, including stopovers and transit points, read the Product Disclosure Statement and check the list of included and excluded activities.

### When should we see a doctor before the honeymoon?

At least eight weeks before you fly. Smartraveller's essentials checklist says to [get a basic health check at least 8 weeks before you go](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist), and its travel health page says to [see your doctor 6 to 8 weeks before you go](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-health), so booking eight weeks out satisfies both.
