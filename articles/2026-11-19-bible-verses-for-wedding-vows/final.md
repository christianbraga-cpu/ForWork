# Bible Verses for Wedding Vows and Ceremony Readings

[IMAGE: bible-verses-wedding-vows-couple-chapel-vows-01.webp]

Bible verses for wedding vows give a ceremony something older than both of you: a few lines your grandparents might know by heart, spoken in your own voice. If you are searching for wedding vows in the Bible, you will find verses rather than a script. The vow wording people recognise comes from church services and marriage law, and couples borrow scripture about love and commitment to sit inside or beside it.

This guide gives you verses that work inside personal vows, passages that work as readings, a simple way to weave one in, and the part other lists leave out: the legal words an Australian ceremony still needs, and how readings work at a ceremony overseas. Every verse is quoted from the World English Bible, with the reference linked to the full text. Sources current as at November 2026.

## Does the Bible contain wedding vows?

Not as a set form of words. When you hear "to have and to hold", you are hearing church liturgy: a church, a religious law or a marriage statute sets traditional vow wording, not a single passage of scripture. If you want set words rather than verses, see [the full traditional wording, from the Book of Common Prayer onward](/expert-advice/traditional-wedding-vows).

What the Bible does offer is language about loyalty, love and unity that couples have long adapted. One familiar example is a reminder to read context. In [Ruth 1:16-17](https://ebible.org/eng-web/RUT01.htm), Ruth is speaking to Naomi, her mother-in-law, not to a spouse:

> "...where you go, I will go; and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God my God." (Ruth 1:16, World English Bible)

Couples adapt these words as a promise to each other, and knowing who first said them adds depth rather than taking any away.

## Short Bible verses for wedding vows

These are short enough to speak from memory or from a small card. Each is quoted exactly from the World English Bible (WEB).

### Commitment and loyalty

- [Genesis 2:24](https://ebible.org/eng-web/GEN02.htm): "Therefore a man will leave his father and his mother, and will join with his wife, and they will be one flesh." A line about leaving one household and forming another.
- [Mark 10:9](https://ebible.org/eng-web/MRK10.htm): "What therefore God has joined together, let no man separate." Fits in one breath, and closes a vow with weight.

### Love in action

- [Proverbs 3:3](https://ebible.org/eng-web/PRO03.htm): "Don’t let kindness and truth forsake you. Bind them around your neck. Write them on the tablet of your heart." A good frame for promises about honesty and gentleness.
- [1 John 4:18](https://ebible.org/eng-web/1JN04.htm): "There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear..." Short, and suited to a vow about safety and trust.

### Unity and partnership

- [Ecclesiastes 4:9-12](https://ebible.org/eng-web/ECC04.htm): "Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their labor." The passage ends: "...a threefold cord is not quickly broken." Either line works alone.
- [Ephesians 4:2-3](https://ebible.org/eng-web/EPH04.htm): "with all lowliness and humility, with patience, bearing with one another in love..." The verse begins mid-sentence, which suits a vow that picks it up as "I promise to live with patience, bearing with you in love."

### Faithfulness

- [Song of Solomon 8:6-7](https://ebible.org/eng-web/SNG08.htm): "Set me as a seal on your heart, as a seal on your arm; for love is strong as death... Many waters can’t quench love, neither can floods drown it." An image of love as a seal, set where it can be seen.

[IMAGE: wedding-reading-card-ribbon-hands-detail-01.webp]

A note on wording: [the WEB uses "Yahweh"](https://ebible.org/eng-web/RUT01.htm) where many translations say "LORD", and its spelling is American. Quote it exactly. If your church uses another translation, read the verse from that Bible on the day; the reference stays the same.

## Bible readings for weddings

A reading is longer than a vow line and is usually read by someone else. [Our guide to choosing readings of every kind](/expert-advice/which-readings-work-at-a-wedding) suggests two to three minutes, and your officiant decides where a reading fits.

These passages work well read aloud:

- **[1 Corinthians 13:4-8a](https://ebible.org/eng-web/1CO13.htm).** This version stops at verse 8a: "Love is patient and is kind. Love doesn’t envy. Love doesn’t brag, is not proud... bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, and endures all things. Love never fails." You can add the chapter's close from [1 Corinthians 13:13](https://ebible.org/eng-web/1CO13.htm): "...The greatest of these is love."
- **[Colossians 3:12-14](https://ebible.org/eng-web/COL03.htm).** A list of qualities to "put on", ending "Above all these things, walk in love, which is the bond of perfection." It reads well aloud because it builds.
- **[1 John 4:7-8](https://ebible.org/eng-web/1JN04.htm).** "Beloved, let’s love one another, for love is of God..." Short enough for a nervous reader.
- **Ecclesiastes 4:9-12 and Song of Solomon 8:6-7**, both above, also work as full readings.

[IMAGE: wedding-reading-parent-lectern-garden-01.webp]

A reading is the natural role for a parent or grandparent who wants to be part of the ceremony without standing at the front for long. Choose a passage that suits their voice, send it early, and print it in a large font. Some traditions have requirements about which passages you can use, so check with your church or officiant before you print anything.

## How to weave a verse into your own vows

Scripture inside a personal vow should sound like you, not a sermon. Three patterns work:

1. **Open with it.** Say the verse, then say what it means in your life together.
2. **Echo one phrase.** Take a single image ("a threefold cord", "Set me as a seal") and make it your promise.
3. **Close with it.** Make your promises in plain words, then let the verse land last.

An example, written for this guide, using the echo pattern:

> "Sam, there is a line in Ecclesiastes: two are better than one. I learned what that means the winter my father was ill, when you drove four hours every weekend without being asked. So here is my promise. When you fall, I will lift you. When it is cold, I will be there. Today, and for the rest of our lives, two are better than one."

[IMAGE: handwritten-vow-card-pen-table-01.webp]

Keep the whole vow to the length you would give any personal vow; [our guide to writing your own vows](/how-to-write-wedding-vows) suggests 250 to 300 words, about two minutes aloud.

## The legal words you still need in Australia

Scripture in your wedding vows can be part of a legal Australian ceremony, but it sits alongside the words the law requires, never instead of them. [The Attorney-General's Department says](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) you must be married by an authorised celebrant, use specific words during the ceremony, and have the celebrant, the two of you and two witnesses physically present.

**In a church.** Under [section 45(1) of the Marriage Act 1961](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text), a minister of religion may use the form and ceremony recognised by their religious body. So Christian wedding vows in a church follow that church's rite, and the minister or parish decides whether personal vows or chosen readings have a place. In a Catholic wedding, the consent comes from the approved rite and vows you write yourselves cannot replace it; your verses belong in the readings, which you choose with your priest. See [what a Catholic ceremony allows](/expert-advice/catholic-wedding-vows).

**With a civil celebrant.** Where the celebrant is not a minister of religion, [section 45(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text) requires each of you to say to the other, in the presence of the celebrant and witnesses:

> "I call upon the persons here present to witness that I, A.B. (or C.D.), take thee, C.D. (or A.B.), to be my lawful wedded wife (or husband, or spouse)"

or words to that effect, with your names in place of the letters. Before that, under [section 46(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text), the celebrant says the monitum, a set statement that ends:

> "Marriage, according to law in Australia, is the union of 2 people to the exclusion of all others, voluntarily entered into for life."

Your verse can come before or after those lines. Your celebrant, minister or parish confirms the final wording and the order of service.

## Readings at a destination or symbolic ceremony

Some couples marry legally at home, with the words above, and hold a ceremony abroad for family and friends. A symbolic ceremony is not a legal marriage, so the legal words are not required there and you have more freedom over readings, within what your officiant and venue agree. A legal ceremony overseas follows local rules, and the local registry or town hall confirms what it can include and what your passports need. For one country's options side by side, see [civil, Catholic and symbolic ceremonies in Italy](/destination-weddings/civil-ceremony-in-italy-vs-catholic-and-symbolic-rites), or start with [our Italy destination guide](/destinations/europe/italy).

[IMAGE: symbolic-ceremony-italian-terrace-setup-01.webp]

The readings themselves need more care abroad than at home:

- **Readers.** Confirm who reads what before anyone books flights, and include each reader in the rehearsal.
- **Copies.** Give each reader a printed copy in a large font, and a spare to your planner. Phones die.
- **Sound.** Outdoors, wind and water swallow quiet voices. Ask for a microphone at the lectern, not only for the officiant.
- **Language.** If local guests do not share your language, print the verse in both languages in the order of service, and ask the local officiant or your planner who checks the translation.
- **Weather.** Agree a covered backup for the ceremony site, and where the reader will stand in it.

[IMAGE: order-of-service-cards-ceremony-chairs-01.webp]

For layout ideas, see [how an order of service is laid out](/expert-advice/wedding-order-of-service), and print each verse with its reference and translation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can we use Bible verses in a civil ceremony in Australia?

Talk to your celebrant first. The Marriage Act sets the words that must be said, and personal vows, including verses, sit alongside them. Celebrants differ in what they are comfortable including, so ask early, share the full reading, and let your celebrant confirm the final wording and its place in the ceremony.

### Which Bible translation should we quote at our wedding?

For printing and reading aloud, the World English Bible is [public domain, according to its publisher](https://ebible.org/eng-web/copr.htm). If you prefer the NIV, ESV, NRSV or King James Version, name it rather than printing its text, and ask your minister or celebrant which translation to read from; a minister may ask you to use the church's own.

### Why is 1 Corinthians 13 read at weddings?

Our readings guide calls it the best known wedding reading, "often called the love chapter". Its verses describe love as patient and kind and say that love never fails. To use it inside your vows rather than as a reading, echo one line, such as "Love never fails."

### Can a parent or grandparent do a Bible reading at a destination wedding?

Yes, and it is a meaningful role to offer. Send the passage weeks ahead, print it in a large font, and include the reader in the rehearsal. In a Catholic or other church ceremony, the priest or minister confirms who may read and which passages.

### Do we both need to quote scripture in our vows?

No. One of you can open with a verse while the other speaks in plain words. Agree on tone and length beforehand, and tell your celebrant or minister so the two vows sit comfortably side by side. Respect for each other's faith matters more than matching.

### Can we print Bible verses in our order of service?

Yes. [The World English Bible's publisher](https://ebible.org/eng-web/copr.htm) allows copying, publishing and quoting, and asks only that a changed text not be called the World English Bible. Print the verse exactly, with its book, chapter and verse, and label the translation.
