# How to Brief a Travel Advisor for Your Honeymoon

[IMAGE: hero]

Knowing how to brief a travel advisor can save you rounds of revisions. A good brief tells the advisor your dates, a budget range and what it covers, the pace and style you want, your must-haves and deal-breakers, and how the honeymoon connects to the wedding. With that, the first proposal can be much closer to what you want.

This guide keeps it practical: what to agree first, what to tell the advisor, the days after the wedding that are easy to forget, the questions to ask back, and a template you can copy and send. You can also see [how our travel team plans honeymoons alongside the wedding](/travel).

## What a good honeymoon brief does

An advisor can only shortlist what they understand. A vague request ("somewhere warm, a nice hotel, in June") can bring back the obvious famous resorts, and several rounds of replying "not quite". A specific brief works the other way: it rules places out, so the two or three options left actually fit you.

It also cuts the admin. One clear document, sent once, means the first reply can be a proposal rather than a list of questions. Our own travel page asks clients for honesty about budget, a decision timeline, and any accessibility, allergy or dietary needs, which is a fair summary of what any good advisor needs from you.

If you are still deciding whether to use an advisor at all, start with [what a specialist advisor handles across the whole wedding trip](/travel-guides/travel-advisor-wedding).

## Agree these five things as a couple first

Before you contact anyone, sit down together for an hour with the calendar open. These five decisions shape every option an advisor will send.

[IMAGE: agree-first]

1. **Dates and length.** Fixed dates or a window, and how many nights. Length decides how far it makes sense to fly; see [how many nights suit each destination](/expert-advice/how-long-is-a-honeymoon-a-guide-by-destination).
2. **A total budget range, and what it includes.** Say whether the number covers flights, and in which cabin, or only the stays. Give a range you are both comfortable with rather than one figure. [What different budgets buy by destination](/expert-advice/luxury-honeymoon-budget-which-destination-suits-yours) is a useful reality check.
3. **Pace.** One place, or two or three stops. Every move means a checkout, a transfer and a new check-in, so two stops in ten nights trades some beach time for travel time.
4. **Non-negotiables, one list each.** A private pool, a reef you have wanted to dive for years, a villa on the Amalfi Coast, a masseria in Puglia, one long dinner somewhere memorable. Writing them separately shows where you overlap and where one of you cares far more.
5. **What you would happily skip.** Nightlife, group tours, early starts, long boat transfers. This is often the fastest filter of all.

If the destination is still open, browse [our destination guides](/destinations) together and bring two or three contenders to the brief.

## What to tell a travel agent: the details that change the shortlist

Advisor and travel agent are used for the same role here; either way, the person on the other end works only from what you give them. These are the details that move a shortlist most.

[IMAGE: details]

- **Say it is a honeymoon, and give the wedding date.** It explains your timing and lets the advisor raise the occasion with each property.
- **Room priorities, ranked.** View, a private pool, space to spread out, a villa or a hotel room. Ranking them tells the advisor what to protect if the budget gets tight.
- **Hotels you loved and did not, and why.** Two or three of each, with one line of reason. "We loved the quiet, we disliked the buffet" says more about your taste than any adjective.
- **Health, diet and access.** Dietary needs, allergies, accessibility, and anything that rules out long transfers, small boats or altitude.
- **Loyalty status and points.** Our travel page asks whether either of you holds hotel loyalty status or points worth using, so list programs and member numbers.
- **Anything already booked.** Perks usually cannot be added to a booking already made elsewhere, so mention existing bookings before the advisor starts.
- **Who is paying, and how.** If a parent or family member is contributing, say whose name holds the booking, who pays the deposits and in what currency.

## The days between the wedding and the honeymoon

This part is easy to leave out of a brief, and it is where separate bookings come apart. Give your advisor the wedding venue, the time the last event ends and the morning you can realistically leave.

[IMAGE: days-between]

- **Leave straight away or rest first.** A late start, or a quiet day before a long flight, can be worth more than an extra night at the far end. If you are weighing it up, read about [leaving straight after the wedding or waiting](/expert-advice/a-guide-on-when-to-go-on-honeymoon).
- **Checkout and the onward transfer.** Ask for the checkout, the transfer and the next check-in to be planned as one sequence, not three separate bookings.
- **Wedding clothes and extra luggage.** Decide who takes the dress and suits home, or whether they need storing or sending on. Our guide to packing the suit and the dress for the flight helps here.

**Documents in the right name.** The [Australian Passport Office](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender) recommends booking travel in the name that is in your passport. If one of you is changing a family name after the wedding, you can keep using your current passport for now, so book the honeymoon in the name on the passport you will actually carry. Smartraveller says some countries will not let you enter unless your passport is valid for six months after you plan to leave that country, that some airlines apply the rule themselves, and that it can apply even when you are only transiting or stopping over. Allow at least six weeks for a new passport. If either of you is a dual national, Smartraveller says Australians should depart and enter Australia on their Australian passport, so tell your advisor which passport each of you will use. Rules differ by passport and destination, so each of you should confirm passport validity and entry requirements for your own passport with the embassy or consulate of every country you will visit or transit, and any name-change questions with the Australian Passport Office.

**Visas, health and insurance.** Visas are your responsibility, not your advisor's, and the rules can change at short notice. Smartraveller says to check with the embassy or consulate of each country you are visiting or transiting through before every trip; its [guide to foreign visas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/getting-foreign-visa) explains why. Smartraveller suggests a basic health check at least eight weeks before you go. Smartraveller calls the right travel insurance as essential as a passport, and suggests a policy that covers every country on the trip, including stopovers. You can also [subscribe to Smartraveller updates](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/smartraveller-subscription) for your destinations as soon as you start planning.

If the wedding is abroad too, one specialist can coordinate the whole trip, with the wedding at one resort and the honeymoon at another, or the same stay extended.

## Questions to ask a travel agent before you say yes

Ask for the answers in writing, attached to each option, so you are comparing like with like.

1. Which inclusions are confirmed for this booking, and which are requests? Upgrades and early or late check times usually depend on occupancy, so they should be shown as requests.
2. What is the payment schedule: deposit amounts, due dates and the currency we will be charged in?
3. What are the cancellation and change terms for each hotel and each flight?
4. Does the travel insurance we are looking at cover cancellation? Smartraveller notes that basic medical-only policies usually do not.
5. Who do we call while we are away, and what hours does that number answer?
6. What happens to the plan if the wedding date moves?

For the questions about fees and how the person you are briefing is paid, see [how advisors are paid and what to check before you sign](/travel-guides/travel-advisor-wedding).

## Frequently asked questions

### How far ahead should we brief a travel advisor for our honeymoon?

Our packages page suggests six to nine months for a honeymoon to lock in the right suite category. Brief early enough to leave room for documents too: Smartraveller suggests allowing at least six weeks to get a passport.

### How much should we budget for a honeymoon?

There is no single right figure. Agree a range you are both comfortable with, decide what it covers (flights, stays, experiences) and put both in the brief, so the advisor can show what it buys in each place.

### What is a mini honeymoon?

A short trip taken straight after the wedding, with a longer honeymoon later. Tell your advisor it is two trips and give dates for each, so they are planned as a pair rather than one squeezed into the other.

### What is a buddymoon?

A honeymoon shared with friends, often some of your wedding guests. It changes the brief: more rooms, possibly a group booking, and a clear note on who pays for what before any deposit is taken.

### Who pays for the honeymoon?

There is no single rule, and customs differ between families and cultures. If anyone is contributing, tell your advisor whose name holds the booking, who pays the deposits and in what currency.

### Is there a downside to using a travel agent?

One is avoidable: a vague brief brings back generic options and several rounds of revisions. The other is cost clarity, so ask how the advisor is paid and whether any fee applies before any work starts.
