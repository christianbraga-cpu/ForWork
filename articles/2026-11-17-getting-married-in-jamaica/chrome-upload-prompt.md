## Article body

# How to Get Married in Jamaica Step by Step

[IMAGE: jamaica-north-coast-great-house-lawn-couple-golden-hour-01.webp]

Getting married in Jamaica is open to two Australians who live at home. Under "Residency" on its licence page, Jamaica's Ministry of Justice and Constitutional Affairs requires people living abroad to be on the island at least 24 hours before the marriage ceremony. The legal key is a Minister's Marriage Licence, and a Marriage Officer marries you. Confirm the requirements for your own passports with the Ministry of Justice, the Registrar General's Department and DFAT.

Sources current as at November 2026.

## Getting married in Jamaica: can foreigners marry legally?

Yes. The Ministry's [Minister's Marriage Licence page](https://moj.gov.jm/node/119) lets parties who live abroad give a temporary local address on their forms.

The usual sequence, step by step (the Ministry of Justice confirms the detail):

1. **Decide your route** (below).
2. **Gather documents in Australia** (Documents from Australia).
3. **Prepare originals and copies** (Translations and apostilles).
4. **Apply for the Minister's Marriage Licence** (Licence and timeline).
5. **Arrive at least 24 hours ahead and marry before a Marriage Officer** (The civil ceremony in Jamaica).
6. **Order the certified certificate and use it at home** (Recognition and name change).

| | Marry legally in Jamaica | Marry in Australia, celebrate in Jamaica |
|---|---|---|
| Who it suits | Couples who want the legal moment and the celebration on the same day, in Jamaica | Couples who would rather keep every legal step at home |
| Where the legal part happens | In Jamaica, before a Marriage Officer | In Australia, before an authorised celebrant |
| Extra paperwork | Form of Declaration, stamped licence form, birth certificates, a certificate order | A Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM) |
| The Jamaica day | Your legal ceremony | A symbolic ceremony with no legal effect, shaped as you like |

[IMAGE: jamaica-palm-drive-villa-couple-dusk-01.webp]

Like Italy and [Malta](/destination-weddings/malta-wedding), Jamaica opens a legal route to couples who live abroad, while France requires a residence link (see [how the Italian legal process compares](/expert-advice/getting-married-in-italy-the-complete-legal-guide-for-destination-couples) and [where else you can legally marry abroad](/expert-advice/weddings-abroad-where-can-you-legally-get-married)).

## Residency and eligibility for getting married in Jamaica

The Ministry requires people living abroad to be on the island at least 24 hours before the ceremony.

You must both be at least 18 (16 and 17 year olds need parental consent), neither of you may already be married, and you may not be blood relatives.

[Smartraveller's Jamaica page](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/americas/jamaica) says Australians need no visa for a tourism stay of under six months, and every traveller completes the free C5 immigration and customs form online before arrival. Connecting through the US means meeting US entry and transit requirements. Entry conditions can change at short notice, and Smartraveller advises passports be valid for at least six months beyond the trip (see [whether a wedding trip needs a special visa](/expert-advice/do-you-need-a-wedding-visa-to-get-married-abroad)).

## Prefer to marry in Australia? Marry at home, celebrate in Jamaica

The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) sets the steps. You give a NOIM to an authorised celebrant no more than 18 months and at least one month before the legal ceremony. The celebrant must be physically present with you and two witnesses over 18 (no video-link weddings), and an Australian celebrant cannot marry you overseas. For the detail, see [how to marry legally in Australia first](/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements).

This route removes the Form of Declaration, the licence and stamp duty, the 24-hour rule and the certificate order, and gives you an Australian certificate for any name change.

## Documents from Australia

The Ministry of Justice sets the final list; confirm it before ordering. Step 2:

1. Check both passports and that your names match across every paper.
2. Order birth certificates from your state or territory registry.
3. If either of you was married before, order the final divorce document or your late spouse's death certificate.
4. Copy everything.

| Document | Issued by | What the Ministry accepts (copies certified by a JP or Notary Public) |
|---|---|---|
| Passport (picture ID) | Australian Passport Office | Original with a copy, or a certified copy |
| Birth certificate | Your state or territory registry | Original with a copy, or a certified copy |
| If divorced: final divorce decree or certificate; if widowed: your late spouse's death certificate | The court that granted it; state or territory registry | Original with a copy, or a certified copy |
| Form of Declaration and licence form (Page 2) | The Ministry of Justice (blank forms) | Originals only |

[IMAGE: jamaica-wedding-paperwork-passports-flat-lay-01.webp]

The Ministry's list does not include a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI), a DFAT document confirming you are free to marry; the Form of Declaration states there is no legal impediment. If one is requested, [DFAT's CNI guide](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-certificate-no-impediment-marriage) covers applying in Australia, by mail or through a mission; at least one of you must be an Australian citizen or permanent resident. It costs from AUD 181 (2026 fee), and [DFAT's notarial fees](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services) rise with CPI each 1 January.

Australia has no embassy or high commission in Jamaica. The [Australian High Commission in Port of Spain](https://trinidadandtobago.highcommission.gov.au/ptsp/notarialservices.html), Trinidad and Tobago, is accredited to Jamaica and offers limited notarial services (ask it about CNIs). An honorary consul in Kingston provides limited consular services and passport interviews.

## Translations and apostilles

Jamaica has been a party to the Apostille Convention since 3 July 2021 and Australia since 16 March 1995, according to the [Hague Conference status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41), so an Australian document for use in Jamaica is legalised by a DFAT apostille rather than consular legalisation.

The Ministry's own list does not ask for apostilles. The [Consulate General of Jamaica in Miami](https://congenmiami.mfaft.gov.jm/marriage_jamaica) adds that notarised copies go to your Marriage Officer ahead of time and the originals travel with you. If an apostille is requested, the order is: certificate from the issuing registry or court, DFAT apostille (from AUD 105 each, 2026 fee), then the licence file. DFAT says [processing times vary](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised).

All documents must be in English, so standard Australian certificates need no translation.

## Licence and timeline

Step 4, as the Ministry describes it:

1. Obtain a blank Form of Declaration and a blank licence form (Page 2), printed on legal-size paper.
2. A declarant, who must know of no legal reason preventing the marriage, signs the Form of Declaration before a Justice of the Peace in Jamaica (who may be the declarant is on the Questions list below).
3. Page 2 goes, blank, to a Stamp Duty Office for J$4,000 stamp duty (subject to change, per the Ministry of Justice).
4. The Declaration, stamped Page 2 and supporting documents are lodged with the Ministry. You need not lodge in person: either of you, a relative or friend, your Marriage Officer, a planner or a hotelier may submit.

The licence is valid only in Jamaica and expires 90 days after issue. The Ministry encourages applying at least two weeks before the wedding; applications through a hotel are processed within three weeks. Walk-in files lodged by noon Monday to Thursday, or 11 a.m. on Friday, may be processed the same or the next business day. The licence authorises the ceremony; it is not proof of marriage.

As an illustration only (the Ministry and your Marriage Officer set the real timeline):

| When | Marry legally in Jamaica | Marry in Australia |
|---|---|---|
| 12 months out | Choose the route and your Marriage Officer; check passports | Give the NOIM to your celebrant (no more than 18 months and at least one month ahead) |
| 6 to 4 months out | Order certificates; make copies | Plan the Jamaica celebration |
| 3 months to 6 weeks out | Send copies to your Marriage Officer (some hotels want documents six weeks ahead) | Fix the legal ceremony date |
| 90 days to 2 weeks out | Stamp duty, licence application (three weeks via a hotel; declarant: see Questions) | Legal ceremony in Australia, a few weeks before you fly |
| Wedding week | Arrive at least 24 hours before; ceremony; certificate order | The celebration (no legal step) |

**Questions to ask the Ministry of Justice or your Marriage Officer**

- Do you want a CNI or any other proof of single status from Australians?
- Who may act as declarant, and can the declaration be signed before we arrive?
- Does any Australian document need an apostille?
- Are there rules on where and at what hours the ceremony may take place?
- How old must our witnesses be, and do they need ID?
- Does a religious ceremony by a minister have legal effect on its own?
- What does the certified marriage certificate cost, how long does it take now, and how do we order further copies from Australia?
- Which Jamaican office issues apostilles on a marriage certificate, and at what fee?

## The civil ceremony in Jamaica

The [Ministry's FAQ](https://moj.gov.jm/frequently-asked-questions-faq) says a ceremony under the Minister's Licence may be performed by an appointed Marriage Officer or any person the Minister appoints. The Consulate General in Miami recommends a recognised Marriage Officer, who can arrange the ceremony in a church or a location of your choice, daily between 6:00 and 20:00. That is the consulate's guidance, so confirm it with your Marriage Officer before paying a venue deposit.

Step 5:

1. Arrive at least 24 hours before the ceremony, with your original documents.
2. Marry before your Marriage Officer and two witnesses.
3. Sign the register. The [Jamaica Information Service](https://jis.gov.jm/radio_programs/getting-married-in-jamaica-pt-2-accessing-a-marriage-certificate/) says five signatures make the marriage valid: yours, both witnesses' and the Marriage Officer's (see [who can act as a witness and what they do](/expert-advice/wedding-witnesses)).

[IMAGE: jamaica-clifftop-lawn-ceremony-setup-01.webp]

The register copy you receive on the day is not a legal document, says the consulate; the Ministry's FAQ names the Registrar General's Department certificate as your legal proof. Your Marriage Officer may apply for the certified copy, which the consulate says takes seven working days to one month, with an express service available. Agree before the wedding who orders it and how it reaches you in Australia.

## Religious and symbolic ceremonies

Whether a church wedding carries legal effect on its own is a question for the Ministry (it is on the list above).

A symbolic ceremony has no legal effect. If you married in Australia first, your Jamaica celebration can take any shape you like, and you might draw on [Jamaican wedding traditions you can weave in](/6-jamaican-wedding-traditions-you-might-not-know-about).

## Recognition and name change back in Australia

If you married in Jamaica, [Smartraveller's advice on marrying overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) says the marriage cannot be registered in Australia, but in most cases it is recognised if it was valid where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. The certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, though the Attorney-General's Department notes that some states and territories do not accept overseas certificates for a change of name. See [what to do with a marriage certificate issued overseas](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate). If you married in Australia first, your Australian certificate is the record.

You can keep travelling on your current passport; the [Australian Passport Office](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender) recommends booking travel in the name on it. For a passport in a married name, a spouse born in Australia who married overseas needs a change of name certificate from their state or territory registry. A foreign certificate is accepted only where no Australian equivalent exists, and it must be legalised.

[IMAGE: australia-home-jamaica-wedding-photo-certificate-01.webp]

By contrast, in NSW a person legally married in Australia can take their spouse's family name without registering a change of name. Check your own registry.

## FAQ

### How do I get a marriage licence in Jamaica?

Applications cannot be made online. The completed file goes to the Ministry of Justice's Customer Service Branch at 61 Constant Spring Road, Kingston 10. Stamp Duty Offices that stamp the licence form include Kingston (72 Harbour Street) and the May Pen and Montego Bay Revenue Centres.

### Do you need a blood test to get married in Jamaica?

No. The Consulate General of Jamaica in Miami's guideline for visitors states that no blood test is required, and the Ministry of Justice's document list does not include one.

### Can same-sex couples get married in Jamaica?

No. Section 18(2) of Jamaica's Constitution allows marriage only as the voluntary union of one man and one woman, and the Ministry of Justice issues the Minister's Marriage Licence to a man and a woman. Smartraveller also notes that same-sex relationships are illegal in Jamaica.

### Is a marriage in Jamaica legally recognised in Australia?

Generally yes, with exceptions Smartraveller lists: either person already married, not old enough, too closely related or forced to marry. Jamaica's Ministry of Justice also puts the onus on foreign couples to confirm recognition at home before the ceremony, so settle it before you lodge the licence application.

### How long after arriving can you get married in Jamaica?

Twenty-four hours, says the Consulate General of Jamaica in Miami, provided the licence application has already been made. The clock starts when you land, so a delayed long-haul connection pushes back the earliest possible ceremony. Leave two or more days between landing and the wedding, and confirm the timing with your Marriage Officer.
