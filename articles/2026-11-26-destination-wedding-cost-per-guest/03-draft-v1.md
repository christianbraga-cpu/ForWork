faq:
  - q: "How much does a destination wedding cost per guest?"
    a: "There is no single figure, because it depends on the destination and on which lines you host. As one starting point, our Italy cost guide puts catering at €150 to €220 per guest for a strong destination-wedding standard, before drinks, transfers and taxes. The table above sets out what our other guides publish, each in its own currency."
  - q: "Do you have to pay for guests at a destination wedding?"
    a: "Not for everything. Our guide to guest travel notes that guests usually cover their own flights and most accommodation, while couples host the celebration. Anything beyond that, such as group transfers, a welcome event or rooms for close family, is your choice, so tell guests early and in writing what is covered."
  - q: "Is a destination wedding cheaper per guest than a wedding at home?"
    a: "Not reliably. A wedding abroad carries lines a home wedding never does, such as guest rooms, transfers and local taxes. Our side-by-side comparison of overseas and home budgets sets the two out in full."
  - q: "Why is our cost per guest higher with fewer guests?"
    a: "Because fixed lines, minimum spends and guest minimums are shared by fewer people. Our guide to small weddings in Italy budgets €800 to €1,600 per guest for a micro wedding of 10 to 20 guests, and €500 to €1,200 at 20 to 40 guests."

# Destination Wedding Cost per Guest Explained

[IMAGE: hero]

Somewhere between the first venue brochure and the first deposit, the question changes. It stops being "what will the wedding cost?" and becomes "what does each person we invite actually cost us?" Abroad, that answer has more moving parts than at home: guests fly in, sleep somewhere, need getting to the venue and are often fed on more than one night.

This guide explains destination wedding cost per guest line by line: which costs rise with every name on your list, which stay fixed, who usually pays for what, and the per-guest figures our own cost guides publish. Each figure is quoted as its guide states it, in its own currency, with its guest count and date, and linked to that guide. Sources current as at November 2026.

## What a per-guest cost is made of

Every quote for a wedding abroad splits into two kinds of line. Some rise with each guest you add: dinner, drinks, transfers priced per seat and any per-person levy. Others stay relatively flat between 40 guests and 80, such as sound, staging and basic decor. Knowing which is which tells you what one more name on the list really costs.

**Venue hire.** Venues charge for space in different ways. Some step the fee up in guest-count bands rather than per head: [our Lake Como budget guide](/expert-advice/how-much-does-a-lake-como-wedding-cost) lists FAI's 2026 rate for a two-hour ceremony after 6pm at Villa del Balbianello, for up to 50 guests, at €9,800 plus 22% VAT. Others double as accommodation. [Our Italy cost guide](/expert-advice/how-much-does-a-wedding-in-italy-cost) notes that exclusive hire of a Tuscan borgo for 60 to 80 guests across three nights typically starts at around €27,000 to €30,000 in 2026, and can replace a separate hotel block.

**Catering and drinks.** These are the clearest per-guest lines. [Our Bali guide for 80 guests](/real-weddings/wedding-in-bali-cost) notes that food and beverage costs are usually calculated per guest, and suggests an itemised quotation that identifies the menu, beverage selection, service duration, minimum guest commitment and any additional per-person charges. Drinks can be priced by the hour or by the night: our Italy cost guide says an open bar adds around €12 per person per hour, or a flat €50 to €100 per guest for the night.

**Minimum spends.** [Our Dubai cost guide](/destination-weddings/dubai-wedding-cost) defines a minimum spend as the least the venue will charge, however few guests you invite. It is a floor, not a per-head price, so it matters most when your list is short.

**Guest rooms.** Accommodation usually sits outside the wedding quote. Our Lake Como budget guide lists guest rooms, flights, legal documents and welcome or farewell events as usually extra, and [our Santorini cost breakdown](/real-weddings/santorini-wedding-cost-breakdown) treats accommodation as a separate part of the budget.

**Transfers.** Where guests travel by water or air, getting there becomes a per-guest line. [Our Maldives cost guide](/real-weddings/maldives-wedding-cost) notes that seaplane and speedboat transfers are priced per person, so a guest list of 20 turns transfers into a line of its own. On the Amalfi Coast, our Italy cost guide notes that guests are usually moved by boat, and says to budget for water transfers from the start.

**Welcome and farewell events.** Each extra gathering brings its own catering, venue and transport. Our Santorini cost breakdown says that including welcome drinks, a rehearsal gathering or a farewell lunch in the initial budget gives a more realistic view of the celebration, and our Lake Como budget guide advises pricing side events at the start.

**Taxes and service charges.** A per-head menu price is rarely the final per-head cost. [Our guide to small weddings in Italy](/destination-weddings/small-wedding-in-italy-cost) puts VAT at 22 percent on most services and 10 percent on hotel stays and restaurant meals. Our Maldives cost guide lists tax and service charge at 27% (a 10% service charge plus 17% GST). Our Dubai cost guide notes that one venue's published rate included a 7% municipality fee, a 10% service charge and 5% VAT, and says to ask whether a quoted rate includes those charges or adds them on top.

**Per-person levies.** Some destinations add a charge for each visitor. Our Maldives pages list a green tax charged per person, so confirm the current rate and how it is charged with your resort before you budget it.

Our guide to [the costs a headline quote tends to leave out](/destination-weddings/hidden-costs-destination-wedding) covers the remaining lines in depth.

## Who pays for what

The convention is simpler than it looks. Our guide to [guest travel and who covers each part](/travel-guides/who-pays-guest-travel) puts it this way: guests usually cover their own flights and most accommodation, while couples host the celebration. Our Fiji cost breakdown says the same of flights, and adds that guest accommodation and transfers sit outside its package estimates, to be calculated separately.

Beyond that, what you host is a choice. The who-pays guide sets out four levels:

- **Essential.** You host the events only.
- **Considered.** You add group transfers, a welcome dinner and a farewell brunch.
- **Hosted.** You add accommodation for parents and the wedding party.
- **Fully hosted.** You add accommodation for every guest.

Some venues move a line for you. Our hidden-costs guide notes that Le Ciel in Santorini requires couples to arrange guest transfers, and our Amalfi Coast cost guide, budgeting a wedding for 60 to 80 guests, carries guest transport and shuttles as its own line at €2,000 to €6,000. Even at the Essential level, our who-pays guide notes that accommodation is often arranged by the couple and paid for by each guest, through a held room block that gives guests a defined place to book.

Whichever level you choose, tell guests early and in writing what is covered, so they can plan their own spend. The who-pays guide is candid about the trade-off: taking on more of the travel and accommodation costs may mean inviting fewer guests.

**If a parent is contributing.** Agree at the start which lines the contribution covers, such as the welcome dinner or guest transfers, rather than a share of an overall total. A named line is easier to follow when deposits fall due on different dates and in different currencies. ## How guest count changes the bill

Fewer guests lower the total, but they rarely lower the cost per head in step, because fixed lines and minimums are shared by fewer people. Our guide to small weddings in Italy shows this in its own figures: budget €800 to €1,600 per guest for a micro wedding of 10 to 20 guests, and €500 to €1,200 at 20 to 40 guests, before VAT.

Minimums sharpen the effect. Our Dubai cost guide puts it plainly: for a small wedding, a high minimum can cost more than the per-person rate suggests. Some venues set a minimum on numbers instead. Our hidden-costs guide notes that Le Ciel requires at least 20 guests over the age of 10 between 20 May and 30 September, and that couples below this number are charged an additional €190 for each missing guest. The same venue makes drinks packages compulsory for all guests during the ceremony cocktail hour and two-hour dinner, and expects a gratuity of 7 percent of the food and beverage total, paid in cash at the pre-wedding meeting.

Space can shape the list as much as budget. Our Santorini cost breakdown notes that guest lists at many caldera venues are capped at about 30, and says to check published menu prices for applicable taxes, service charges and minimum guest commitments.

Going the other way, each guest you add brings their own per-head lines. In Fiji that is the FJD 130 to 180 or more per person our breakdown gives for each guest beyond the package; in the Maldives it is a separate transfer fare for every guest. That is why the most useful question for any venue is the cost of one more guest, and one fewer, in writing.

Packages follow the same logic. Our guide to [how package prices work, line by line](/destination-weddings/all-inclusive-wedding-cost) describes a base fee plus a per person rate, wrapped around a guest minimum: below the minimum, you pay for seats nobody fills. It also notes that guest travel is never in the package, and that welcome events, recovery brunches and rehearsal dinners are add-ons almost everywhere.

## Protecting deposits and guests

Deposits for a wedding abroad tend to go out months ahead, to several suppliers, on separate schedules. Our who-pays guide notes that accommodation deposits, room blocks and hosted experiences may each have separate payment schedules and cancellation conditions, and that properties in popular destinations may require a minimum number of consecutive nights in peak periods.

The ACCC's guidance on [paying for services you have not received](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/not-receiving-products-or-services-that-are-paid-for) says that under the Australian Consumer Law, businesses must not accept payment if they don't intend to supply, or if they know, or should know, that they won't be able to supply by the promised date or within a reasonable time. The same page says that where a third party prevents supply, such as government restrictions, your right to a refund or replacement will generally depend on the terms and conditions of the agreement.

Its page on [refunds and cancelling a service](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel) says that when a service has a major problem, you can cancel and get a refund, which may not be a full refund because you pay a reasonable amount for work already done. You are not entitled to a refund under the consumer guarantees if you simply changed your mind. This is general guidance, not legal advice. It describes Australian law and does not settle how an overseas contract treats a cancellation, so read the cancellation and postponement terms of every contract before you pay a deposit.

Your guests carry their own risk. Smartraveller says [medical assistance is usually the highest unexpected cost](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) for Australians overseas, that most countries won't give you free or subsidised care, and that it expects all Australians travelling overseas to take out appropriate insurance. It also says to always read the Product Disclosure Statement before buying. Put that in your guest travel guide, beside arrival times and transfer details.

Currency is the last risk. Figures here stay in the currency each guide uses, and we have not converted them. If your savings are in Australian dollars and your contracts are in euros, dirhams or Fijian dollars, the rate can move between deposit and final payment, so convert on the day you pay and keep a separate buffer for movement.

## Working out your own cost per guest

An honest working figure for your own list takes a few steps:

1. **List the per-head lines.** Catering, drinks, per-person transfers and any per-person levies.
2. **List the fixed lines.** Venue hire or its guest-count band, planning, photography, styling, sound and staging.
3. **List the guest-paid lines.** Flights and rooms, then mark any you have chosen to host.
4. **Ask for every quote in the same format.** Our Lake Como budget guide suggests guest count, hours and VAT on each line, so offers sit side by side.
5. **Ask for the cost of one extra guest, and one fewer.** Get it in writing, with taxes and service charges included.
6. **Add a buffer.** Our guide to small weddings in Italy suggests holding back 8 to 10 percent for the unexpected.
7. **Keep it in one place.** [Wonderland Studio](/wonderland-studio) is free, includes a multi-currency budget and needs no credit card.

Your per-head lines tell you what each extra guest costs. Your fixed lines explain why a shorter list rarely saves as much as you hope.

## Questions about cost per guest, answered

### How much does a destination wedding cost per guest?

There is no single figure, because it depends on the destination and on which lines you host. As one starting point, our Italy cost guide puts catering at €150 to €220 per guest for a strong destination-wedding standard, before drinks, transfers and taxes. The table above sets out what our other guides publish, each in its own currency.

### Do you have to pay for guests at a destination wedding?

Not for everything. Our guide to guest travel notes that guests usually cover their own flights and most accommodation, while couples host the celebration. Anything beyond that, such as group transfers, a welcome event or rooms for close family, is your choice, so tell guests early and in writing what is covered.

### Is a destination wedding cheaper per guest than a wedding at home?

Not reliably. A wedding abroad carries lines a home wedding never does, such as guest rooms, transfers and local taxes. Our [side-by-side comparison of overseas and home budgets](/destination-weddings/destination-wedding-vs-home-cost) sets the two out in full.

### Why is our cost per guest higher with fewer guests?

Because fixed lines, minimum spends and guest minimums are shared by fewer people. Our guide to small weddings in Italy budgets €800 to €1,600 per guest for a micro wedding of 10 to 20 guests, and €500 to €1,200 at 20 to 40 guests.

### What per-person taxes apply at a destination wedding?

It depends on the destination. Our Dubai cost guide notes that one venue's published rate included a 7% municipality fee, a 10% service charge and 5% VAT, and our Maldives pages list a green tax charged per person. Confirm the current rate and how each charge applies with your venue or resort before you budget it.
