## Article body

# Destination Wedding Budget Planned Line by Line

[IMAGE: destination-wedding-budget-couple-reviewing-plans-villa-terrace-01.webp]

The first figure you see for a wedding abroad is usually the venue's opening quote. The real budget is longer. Guests fly in and need rooms, boats or shuttles, documents may need translating, and there may be a dinner the night before and a lunch the day after, each priced in a currency you do not earn.

This guide lays out a destination wedding budget line by line: what each line covers, what to ask before you sign, and which of our cost guides publishes figures for it. Every figure is quoted as its guide words it; nothing is converted. Sources current as at November 2026.

## How to budget for a destination wedding

Abroad, three decisions come before any line is priced. Our guide to [setting an overall wedding total](/expert-advice/wedding-budget) covers the first money conversation.

**Start from guest count and destination.** Our [Phuket cost guide](/real-weddings/wedding-in-thailand-cost) puts it plainly: food and drink scale directly with guest numbers, while fixed items such as sound systems, stages and basic decor stay relatively flat regardless of head count. The destination sets the rest: the taxes on each invoice, how guests reach the venue and whether the venue can also sleep them. Our [side-by-side look at weddings abroad and at home](/destination-weddings/destination-wedding-vs-home-cost) shows which lines appear only overseas.

**Set a ceiling.** Agree one all-in figure, in the currency your savings are in, before the first quote arrives. As our guide to [the costs a headline quote can leave out](/destination-weddings/hidden-costs-destination-wedding) puts it, a destination wedding budget should reflect the cost of delivering the entire celebration, not simply the venue's opening quote.

**Decide who pays for what.** Our guide to [how guest travel costs are usually shared](/travel-guides/who-pays-guest-travel) says that traditionally, guests pay for their own travel and couples pay for the celebration itself. It sets out four hosting levels, from Essential (the events only) to Fully hosted (accommodation for every guest). If a parent is contributing, tie the gift to named lines, such as the welcome dinner, rather than a share of the total.

## Destination wedding budget template, line by line

Use this as your template. Each row says what the line covers and what to ask before you sign. The notes below give figures where our cost guides publish them, each a reference point from that guide rather than a price for your wedding.

| Line | What it covers | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Venue | Ceremony and reception spaces, hours, chairs and housekeeping | Is the fee hire, a minimum spend, or both? |
| Catering and drinks | Every meal and drink you host, priced per guest | What do tax, service and extra hours add per head? |
| Accommodation | Your rooms, any rooms you host, the guest room block | What are the release dates and minimum stays? |
| Transfers | Airport arrivals, shuttles, boats | Per vehicle or per person, and who arranges them? |
| Legal and documents | Registry fees, translations, legalisation | Which documents are needed, and by when? |
| Celebrant | Whoever leads the ceremony | Is the ceremony legal or symbolic, and is travel in the quote? |
| Photo and video | Hours, editing, the team's travel | Are the team's flights and rooms in the quote? |
| Florals and styling | Ceremony, tables, personal flowers, lighting, hire | What does the venue already provide? |
| Entertainment | Ceremony music, band or DJ, sound | Does the venue allow outside entertainers? |
| Attire | Outfits, alterations, hair and makeup | When is the last fitting before you fly? |
| Welcome and farewell events | Welcome drinks, rehearsal dinner, farewell lunch | Can the same venue host them, and at what rate? |
| Guest support | Travel guide, room block, transfer schedule, levies | Which visitor levies apply, and who pays them? |
| Planner | Design, sourcing, contracts, coordination on the day | How is the fee calculated, and what is excluded? |
| Contingency | A reserve for what no quote foresees | What may draw on it, and who decides? |

### Venue, food and drink

Our [50-guest Tuscany budget](/real-weddings/wedding-in-italy-cost) lists venue hire, per day, at €3,000 to €20,000 or more (EUR, 50 guests, indicative rates at the time of writing, no year stated). It also notes that a minimum spend may contribute towards the catering budget rather than represent an additional fee, so check before you count it twice.

[Our Italy cost guide](/expert-advice/how-much-does-a-wedding-in-italy-cost) puts catering at €150 to €220 per guest for a strong destination-wedding standard and €220 to €300 for premium service. It says an open bar adds around €12 per person per hour, or a flat €50 to €100 per guest for the night (EUR, 2026 prices, no guest count set).

For any menu, ask for an itemised quotation showing the menu, beverage selection, service duration, minimum guest commitment and any additional per-person charges, as our Bali cost guide suggests. Packages work differently; see [how package prices are built](/destination-weddings/all-inclusive-wedding-cost).

### Rooms, transfers and guest support

Our Italy cost guide notes that in Tuscany, exclusive hire of a borgo (a hamlet) for 60 to 80 guests across three nights typically starts at around €27,000 to €30,000 in 2026, and can replace a separate hotel block. [Our Santorini cost breakdown](/real-weddings/santorini-wedding-cost-breakdown) adds that room-block rates, release dates and minimum booking requirements should be confirmed before they are presented as guaranteed.

[Our Amalfi Coast cost guide](/real-weddings/amalfi-coast-wedding-cost), budgeting a wedding for 60 to 80 guests, carries guest transport and shuttles as its own line at €2,000 to €6,000 (EUR, no year stated), and says the final figure depends on guest numbers, distances and whether private boat transfers are required. [Our Maldives cost guide](/real-weddings/maldives-wedding-cost) notes that each guest travels on a separate seaplane or speedboat fare. If grandparents are coming, ask for short legs, help at every jetty and seats held for them.

[IMAGE: destination-wedding-guests-boat-transfer-older-guest-jetty-01.webp]

Welcome and farewell events belong in the budget from day one, since they bring separate catering, venue and transport needs. [Our Lake Como budget guide](/expert-advice/how-much-does-a-lake-como-wedding-cost) treats each day as its own line in a quote, so price side events at the start.

Guest support is the work around all of this. [Our Fiji cost breakdown](/real-weddings/fiji-wedding-cost) describes the pattern: guests usually book their own flights, while we arrange room blocks and the transfer schedule and prepare a travel guide with arrival times. Price visitor levies against the number of guests and nights, and decide whether guests pay them individually or within your accommodation arrangements.

### Legal, documents and the celebrant

Our Italy cost guide says town hall fees for a civil ceremony vary by comune (the local council), typically ranging from €100 to €2,000 and averaging about €500, and that paperwork in Italy and your home country adds roughly €600 (EUR, no year stated). The Tuscany budget adds that government fees, document legalisation, translations and municipal charges should be budgeted separately from the wedding costs.

If a parent is asking whether the marriage will count at home, read [where you can legally marry abroad](/expert-advice/weddings-abroad-where-can-you-legally-get-married). Confirm the legal requirements for your own passports with the authorities that decide: the comune where you marry, and the Australian Embassy or consulate, or [DFAT's guidance on getting married overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas). Some couples complete the legal marriage at home and hold the ceremony abroad, which removes Italian town hall fees and paperwork. The celebrant then leads a symbolic ceremony; see [civil, religious and symbolic rites in Italy](/destination-weddings/civil-ceremony-in-italy-vs-catholic-and-symbolic-rites). Ask any celebrant for a written quote that includes travel.

### Photo, styling, entertainment, attire and planner

The Tuscany budget lists photography and video at €2,000 to €8,000 or more (EUR, 50 guests, no year stated), and notes that established destination specialists may charge travel, accommodation and transfer expenses when travelling from outside the region. Our guide to [what photographers charge in Italy](/what-does-an-italy-wedding-photographer-cost) goes further.

For florals, the same budget says lighting becomes particularly important for evening celebrations. A beach, a vineyard or a villa garden already looks the part, so let the setting carry more of the design.

On entertainment, our Italy cost guide says a DJ runs about €500 to €1,200, while a live band can reach €10,000 for one evening (EUR, no year stated). Check the venue's rules on outside entertainers before you book.

Attire is the line your own taste sets. Book the last fitting well before you fly, and schedule hair and makeup around photography coverage.

The same Italy guide says planner fees start from around €2,000 to €8,000 and rise with full-service planning (EUR, no year stated). Ask any planner for references from couples who married abroad, and how the fee is calculated and what it excludes. Our guide to [what planners charge](/expert-advice/cost-of-wedding-planner) is a useful next read.

## How an Italy budget splits

Percentages help as a sense check once quotes arrive, but only where their source is stated. Our Italy cost guide cites earlier research from Florence's Centro Studi Turistici, which found food and beverage accounted for 35% of spend, the venue 21%, hospitality 17% and ancillary services 27%.

Our [budget percentages by category](/expert-advice/wedding-budget-percentages) describe a wedding at home. Treat this split as Italian, not universal, and if your Italy quotes sit far from it, ask your suppliers why. For more on marrying there, see [weddings in Italy](/destinations/europe/italy).

## Contingency and currency

**Contingency.** Hold a reserve that belongs to no single line, and decide in advance what may draw on it: a supplier's price change, an extra hour, a wet-weather plan B. Our Amalfi Coast cost guide sets contingency at about 10% of the total, and our small Italy wedding guide says to hold back 8 to 10 percent for the unexpected. Set it as its own line before you commit to suppliers, and keep it out of the line totals so it is not quietly spent on upgrades. Our hidden-costs guide is a good checklist for what it may need to meet.

[IMAGE: destination-wedding-covered-terrace-wet-weather-plan-01.webp]

**Currency.** If your savings are in Australian dollars and your contracts are in euros, the rate can move between deposit and final payment. Moneysmart says to [look for a good exchange rate and check the fees when sending money overseas](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/sending-money-overseas). It notes that providers choose the exchange rate they use and also charge fees.

Cards carry their own costs. If a charge is in a currency other than your card's, your bank or card provider may add its own foreign-transaction charge, so compare bank transfers and card payments rather than assuming the quoted amount is the amount that will leave your account. Keep the currency buffer as its own line.

## Deposits and insurance

Deposits go out early, to several suppliers, on separate schedules. Our who-pays guide notes that accommodation deposits, room blocks and hosted experiences may each have separate payment schedules and cancellation conditions. Terms can be strict: our hidden-costs guide notes that Le Ciel in Santorini requires a 50 percent deposit of €2,100, with the remaining venue fee due 365 days before the wedding, and that payments are non-refundable and by bank transfer only.

The ACCC's guidance on [paying for services you have not received](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/not-receiving-products-or-services-that-are-paid-for) says that under the Australian Consumer Law, businesses must not accept payment if they don't intend to supply, or if they know, or should know, that they won't be able to supply by the promised date or within a reasonable time. Where a third party prevents supply, your right to a refund will generally depend on the terms and conditions of the agreement. Its page on [refunds and cancelling a service](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel) adds that the consumer guarantees give no refund if you simply changed your mind. This is general guidance, not legal advice, so read the cancellation and postponement terms before every deposit.

Smartraveller says the right travel insurance is as essential as a passport, and that [medical assistance is usually the highest unexpected cost](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) for Australians overseas. It expects all Australians travelling overseas to take out appropriate insurance, and says to always read the Product Disclosure Statement before buying. Check what your policy says about a postponed wedding, and put the same advice in your guest travel guide.

## Destination wedding budget questions, answered

### How do I break down a destination wedding budget?

Start from your guest count and destination, set one all-in ceiling and agree who pays for what. Then give each line in the table above its own allowance, and hold contingency and a currency buffer as separate lines.

### What is a realistic destination wedding budget?

It depends on the destination, the guest count and how much you host. As one example, our Amalfi Coast cost guide says a wedding for 60 to 80 guests typically costs €60,000 to €110,000 or more.

### How much contingency should a destination wedding budget hold?

Our Amalfi Coast cost guide sets contingency at about 10% of the total, and our small Italy wedding guide says to hold back 8 to 10 percent for the unexpected. Set it as its own line before you commit to suppliers, keep it separate from the currency buffer, and agree in advance what may draw on it.

### Should we budget in Australian dollars or the local currency?

Set your ceiling in the currency your savings are in, and record each quote in the currency it is written in. Convert on the day you pay, [compare exchange rates and fees](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/sending-money-overseas) as Moneysmart suggests, and keep a separate buffer for movement between deposit and final payment.
