# Bridal Shower Guest List Etiquette and Who to Invite

[IMAGE: hero]

A bridal shower guest list usually starts with the bridal party, then the mothers, sisters and grandmothers on both sides, then the closest friends. The long-standing convention is that everyone invited to the shower is also invited to the wedding. The host draws up the list and sends the invitations, but the names come from the bride, who approves the final list. A destination wedding with a smaller list changes the picture, so we cover guests who cannot travel further down.

In Australia you may still hear it called a kitchen tea. We say "bride" because that is the convention, but all of this works for a groom, two brides or a couple sharing one shower. It is convention and advice, not law, with the reasoning behind it so you can decide with both families in mind. Sources current as at November 2026.

## Who to invite to a bridal shower

Think of the shower list as a smaller circle inside the wedding list. Our [host etiquette guide](/celebration-ideas/who-hosts-bridal-shower) puts it simply: guests are usually the bridal party, close family and friends of the bride who are also invited to the wedding. In order of priority:

1. The bridal party, led by the maid of honour
2. Mothers, sisters and grandmothers on both sides
3. The partner's close female relatives (or close relatives of any gender, if the shower is co-ed)
4. Close friends
5. Close family friends, often the friends of each mother

[IMAGE: who-to-invite]

**Why the shower sits inside the wedding list.** Our guide to [who you are expected to invite to the wedding](/expert-advice/wedding-guest-list) explains that an invitation to one pre-wedding celebration naturally signals an invitation to the next. As [our destination shower guide](/celebration-ideas/destination-bridal-shower) adds, a shower invitation also comes with the expectation of a gift. Asking someone to bring a present, then leaving them off the wedding, is the hurt this convention exists to prevent. It is the closest thing shower etiquette has to a fixed point.

Colleagues not on the wedding list can mark the news their own way at work, perhaps a morning tea, kept separate from the shower. And if the wedding list is not settled yet, start by [building the wedding list in tiers](/expert-advice/how-to-make-a-wedding-guest-list), then draw the shower list from it.

## Who hosts the bridal shower and who decides the guest list

The usual split is simple: the host drafts and sends, the bride supplies and approves. Our [planning guide](/celebration-ideas/bridal-shower-planning) says the bride or her family should supply the list, and that everyone on it should have a wedding invitation too. Even a surprise shower needs the bride's list, [so the right people are there](/celebration-ideas/who-hosts-bridal-shower). For [who usually hosts and pays](/celebration-ideas/who-hosts-bridal-shower), see the host guide.

[IMAGE: who-decides]

Mothers come next. Family hosts tend to have a say in the guest list and the setting, especially when they are paying. The fairest approach borrows from [our advice on guest lists for parents who contribute](/expert-advice/wedding-guest-list): agree a set number of places for each side early, and let each mother choose within it. Ask both mothers at the same time, with the same number, so neither side feels second.

When someone wants to add a name, a kind no is short and honest: "We would love to, but we are keeping the shower to people coming to the wedding, and both sides have the same number of places."

## How many guests to invite to a bridal shower

There is no right number. The shower list follows three things: the wedding list, the venue and the format. Because everyone at the shower is also invited to the wedding, the wedding list sets the ceiling, and if your wedding will be small, our guest list guide advises that you [keep the earlier guest lists small too](/expert-advice/wedding-guest-list).

Then let the format do the rest. An afternoon tea at home is sized by the living room; a hotel high tea or a restaurant private room by the booking. A winery, garden or spa day, as our planning guide notes, suits a smaller group that wants an experience as much as a party. For the venue and the order of jobs, use [our shower planning timeline](/celebration-ideas/bridal-shower-planning).

## Family, in-laws and work colleagues

This is where feelings get hurt, so choose one line and apply it to both families. If first cousins are in on one side, they are in on the other. A paying parent can then explain the list to anyone, because the same test was applied to everyone.

- **Cousins and aunts.** Invite by closeness, not by category, with the same cut-off on both sides.
- **Friends of the mothers.** Count them within each mother's set number.
- **The partner's family.** Ask for names directly and early, so nobody on that side hears about the shower second-hand.
- **Work colleagues.** Our guest list guide suggests colleagues belong on the wedding list only if you [share a friendship outside work](/expert-advice/wedding-guest-list). Invite the ones coming to the wedding; the rest can celebrate at work.
- **Plus-ones.** A traditional shower does not include guests' partners (co-ed showers aside). For the wedding, see [plus-ones at the wedding itself](/expert-advice/plus-one-etiquette).

**More than one shower.** A bride who lives far from her family [may have two showers](/celebration-ideas/who-hosts-bridal-shower), one with friends near her home and one with relatives in her home town. Keep the two lists separate where you can, so nobody is asked for two gifts; our [gift ideas for the shower](/celebration-ideas/bridal-shower-gifts) help guests choose one.

## Partners, men and co-ed showers

Traditionally, showers were women only. As we set out in [our take on men at the shower](/should-men-be-invited-to-the-bridal-shower), the couple decides: invite the men who matter to you both, or keep it women only, and both are correct. A co-ed shower, often called a Jack and Jill, a wedding shower or a couple's shower, works especially well when the couple already shares one social circle. Going co-ed brings guests' partners onto the list, so recheck the numbers against the room.

The bridal shower and the hens are separate events with separate guest lists. The shower tends to be the gentler, multi-generational gathering; the hens has its own logic, covered in our guide to [the hens guest list](/expert-advice/bachelorette-party-etiquette).

## Bridal shower guest list for a destination wedding

A destination wedding guest list is usually smaller and gathered closely around the inner circle, because the invitation asks guests for their time, their leave and their travel, as [our guest list etiquette guide](/expert-advice/wedding-guest-list) explains. The people who love you at home have not shrunk in number, and the shower is where that shows.

**The convention still holds.** Our destination shower guide is clear that anyone invited to the wedding can come to the shower, including guests who cannot make the trip, and that people not invited to the wedding are left off. If there are people you love who are not on the wedding list, talk to your host before a single invitation goes out, and decide together how to handle it kindly. For [a very small wedding list](/expert-advice/micro-wedding-guest-list), the same thinking applies with fewer names.

**Guests who are invited but cannot travel.** For an older relative, a young family or a friend who cannot take the leave, the shower at home may be their main celebration with you. A shower is [worth hosting when many people close to the bride cannot travel](/celebration-ideas/destination-bridal-shower): it lets them share the lead-up without a flight, a hotel and time off work. When they decline the wedding, respond warmly without asking why, and [keep those guests in mind](/expert-advice/wedding-guest-list) for a gathering at home afterwards. Our attendance guide covers [who is likely to make the trip](/destination-weddings/destination-wedding-guest-attendance).

**Older guests.** Choose a venue at home with a lift or step-free entry, proper seating and a time of day that suits them.

**At home or near the wedding.** Two options, and some families do both:

- **At home.** Our planning guide suggests you [hold the shower at home well before the bride flies out](/celebration-ideas/bridal-shower-planning), and steer guests towards gifts that are delivered rather than carried.
- **Near the wedding.** If the bride's friends are travelling for the wedding anyway, [the shower can be held there](/celebration-ideas/destination-bridal-shower): a brunch or afternoon tea early in the wedding week, optional and close to where everyone is staying, since guests are already paying for flights and accommodation. Hold it on a different day from the [welcome party](/expert-advice/do-you-need-a-wedding-welcome-party), or fold the two together if the guest lists match.

For the hosting steps, read our guide to [hosting a shower before a wedding abroad](/celebration-ideas/destination-bridal-shower).

**Virtual guests.** A grandmother who cannot fly or a friend overseas can still be at the table. Set up a laptop or tablet, put one person in charge of it, test the link the day before and check the time difference. Ask remote guests to post gifts ahead, and keep the gift opening short on camera.

## Bridal shower guest list FAQs

### Do you have to invite everyone from the bridal shower to the wedding?

Yes, by long-standing convention. Everyone invited to the shower is also invited to the wedding, because a shower invitation implies a wedding one and comes with the expectation of a gift. Colleagues who are not coming to the wedding can mark the news separately at work.

### Can you invite someone to the bridal shower who is not invited to the wedding?

It is best avoided. A shower invitation implies a wedding one, so a guest can feel they were asked for a present but not for the day itself. If someone you love is not on the wedding list, talk to your host before any invitations go out and decide together how to handle it kindly.

### How many people should be on a bridal shower guest list?

There is no set number. The wedding list sets the ceiling, since shower guests are wedding guests, and our advice is that a small wedding calls for a small shower. Within that, the venue and format decide. A home afternoon tea holds fewer than a hotel high tea, and a winery or spa day suits a smaller group.

### Who makes the bridal shower guest list?

The host draws it up and sends the invitations, but the names come from the bride or her family, and the bride approves the final list. Family hosts, especially those paying, tend to have a say. Give each mother the same set number of places and let her choose within it, so both sides are treated alike.

### Should you have a bridal shower if many guests cannot travel to the wedding?

Often, yes. A shower at home lets the people who cannot travel share in the lead-up without a flight, a hotel and time off work. Anyone invited to the wedding can come, including those who cannot make the trip. Hold it at home well before you fly out, and suggest gifts that are delivered rather than carried.
