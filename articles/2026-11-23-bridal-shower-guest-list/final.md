# Bridal Shower Guest List Etiquette and Who to Invite

[IMAGE: bridal-shower-afternoon-tea-guests-long-table-01.webp]

A bridal shower guest list usually starts with the bridal party and immediate family on both sides, then the bride's closest friends. The long-standing convention is that everyone invited to the shower is also invited to the wedding. The host draws up the list and sends the invitations, but the names come from the bride, who approves the final list. A destination wedding changes the picture, so we cover guests who cannot travel below.

In Australia you may still hear it called a kitchen tea. We say "bride" because that is the convention, but all of this works for a groom, two brides or a couple sharing one shower. This is convention and advice, not law.

## Who to invite to a bridal shower

Our host guide puts it simply: guests are usually the bridal party, close family and friends of the bride who are also invited to the wedding. Our suggested order of priority:

1. The bridal party, led by the maid of honour
2. Mothers, sisters and grandmothers on both sides
3. The partner's close female relatives (or close relatives of any gender, if the shower is co-ed)
4. Close friends
5. Close family friends, often the friends of each mother

[IMAGE: bridal-shower-three-generations-tea-verandah-01.webp]

**Why the shower sits inside the wedding list.** Our guide to [who you are expected to invite to the wedding](/expert-advice/wedding-guest-list) explains that an invitation to one pre-wedding celebration naturally signals an invitation to the next. A shower invitation also comes with the expectation of a gift, and asking for a present then leaving someone off the wedding is the hurt this convention prevents. It is the closest thing shower etiquette has to a fixed point.

If the wedding list is not settled yet, start by [building the wedding list in tiers](/expert-advice/how-to-make-a-wedding-guest-list), then draw the shower list from it.

## Who hosts the bridal shower and who decides the guest list

The usual split is simple: the host drafts and sends, and the bride or her family supplies the names, which the bride approves. Even a surprise shower needs the bride's list so the right people are there, and our host guide covers [who usually hosts and pays](/celebration-ideas/who-hosts-bridal-shower).

[IMAGE: bridal-shower-guest-list-handwritten-table-01.webp]

Family hosts tend to have a say in the guest list and the setting, especially when they are paying. Our wedding list advice for contributing parents applies: agree a set number of places for each side early, and let each mother choose within it. Ask both mothers at the same time, with the same number, so neither side feels second. If your mother is also hosting, agree the number for each side together at the start, so the other side's places are set before invitations go out.

When someone wants to add a name, a kind no is short and honest: "We would love to, but we are keeping the shower to people coming to the wedding, and both sides have the same number of places."

## How many guests to invite to a bridal shower

There is no right number. The shower list follows three things: the wedding list, the venue and the format. Because shower guests are wedding guests, the wedding list sets the ceiling, and our guest list guide advises keeping earlier lists small if the wedding will be small.

An afternoon tea at home is sized by the living room, a hotel high tea or a restaurant private room by the booking. A winery, garden or spa day suits a smaller group that wants an experience as much as a party, and [our shower planning timeline](/celebration-ideas/bridal-shower-planning) covers the venue and the order of jobs.

## Family, in-laws and work colleagues

This is where feelings get hurt, so choose one line and apply it to both families. If first cousins are in on one side, they are in on the other. A paying parent can then explain the list to anyone.

- **Cousins and aunts.** Invite by closeness, not by category, with the same cut-off on both sides.
- **Friends of the mothers.** Count them within each mother's set number.
- **The partner's family.** Ask for names directly and early, so nobody on that side hears about the shower second-hand.
- **Work colleagues.** Our wedding list advice is to invite colleagues only if you share a friendship outside work. Colleagues who are not invited can mark the news at work, perhaps with a morning tea kept separate from the shower.
- **Plus-ones.** Traditionally, guests' partners are not invited to a shower (co-ed showers aside), and we suggest keeping it that way unless you go co-ed. For the wedding, see [plus-ones at the wedding itself](/expert-advice/plus-one-etiquette).

**More than one shower.** A bride who lives far from her family may have two showers, one with friends near her home and one with relatives in her home town. Keep the two lists separate where you can, so nobody is asked for two gifts, and our [gift ideas for the shower](/celebration-ideas/bridal-shower-gifts) help guests choose one.

## Partners, men and co-ed showers

Traditionally, showers were women only. As we set out in [our take on men at the shower](/should-men-be-invited-to-the-bridal-shower), the couple decides: invite the men who matter to you both, or keep it women only, and both are correct. A co-ed shower, often called a Jack and Jill, a wedding shower or a couple's shower, works especially well when the couple already shares one social circle. If you go co-ed and invite guests' partners, recheck the numbers against the room.

[IMAGE: co-ed-wedding-shower-garden-lunch-long-table-01.webp]

The bridal shower and the hens are separate events with separate guest lists. The shower tends to be the gentler, multi-generational gathering, so draw up [the hens guest list](/expert-advice/what-a-hen-party-should-involve) on its own.

## Bridal shower guest list for a destination wedding

A destination wedding guest list is usually smaller and gathered closely around the inner circle, because the invitation asks guests for their time, their leave and their travel, as our guest list etiquette guide explains, and our attendance guide looks at [who is likely to make the trip](/destination-weddings/destination-wedding-guest-attendance).

**The convention still holds.** Anyone invited to the wedding can come to the shower, including guests who cannot make the trip. For people you love at home who are not invited abroad, here is what we suggest. Some couples keep the shower to wedding guests and mark the wider circle another way, such as relaxed drinks or an afternoon tea after you are home, with no gifts expected. Talk it through with your host first, so everyone hears the same plan.

**Guests who are invited but cannot travel.** For an older relative, a young family or a friend who cannot take the leave, the shower at home may be their main celebration with you. A shower is worth hosting when many people close to the bride cannot travel, because it lets them share the lead-up without a flight, a hotel and time off work. When they decline the wedding, respond warmly without asking why, and keep those guests in mind for a gathering at home afterwards.

**Older guests.** For the shower at home, choose a venue with a lift or step-free entry, proper seating and a time of day that suits them.

**At home or near the wedding.** You can do either, or both.

- **At home.** Hold the shower in Australia before you fly out, leaving enough time to post any gifts on, and steer guests towards gifts that are delivered rather than carried.
- **Near the wedding.** If the bride's friends are travelling for the wedding anyway, the shower can be held there: a brunch or afternoon tea early in the wedding week, optional and close to where everyone is staying. Hold it on a different day from the welcome party, or fold the two together if the guest lists match, and our guide to [hosting a shower before a wedding abroad](/celebration-ideas/destination-bridal-shower) has the hosting steps.

**Virtual guests.** A grandmother who cannot fly or a friend overseas can still join by video. Set up a laptop or tablet with one person in charge, and test the link the day before. Ask remote guests to post gifts ahead, and keep the gift opening short on camera.

## Printable bridal shower guest list checklist

Copy, print or share it with your host.

[IMAGE: bridal-shower-guest-list-checklist-printable-01.webp]

- [ ] Confirm the wedding guest list first
- [ ] Agree the format and venue, then the size the room can hold
- [ ] Host drafts and sends, bride supplies the names and approves them
- [ ] Give each mother the same set number of places
- [ ] Apply one cut-off for cousins, aunts and family friends on both sides
- [ ] Ask the partner's side for names directly
- [ ] Decide women only or co-ed, and add partners if co-ed
- [ ] Leave off anyone not invited to the wedding
- [ ] Flag wedding guests who cannot travel, and note older guests' access needs: lift, step-free entry, seating

## Bridal shower guest list FAQs

### Do you have to invite everyone from the bridal shower to the wedding?

Yes, by long-standing convention. Everyone invited to the shower is also invited to the wedding, because a shower invitation implies a wedding one and comes with the expectation of a gift. Colleagues who are not coming to the wedding can celebrate separately at work.

### Can you invite someone to the bridal shower who is not invited to the wedding?

It is best avoided, because a shower invitation implies a wedding one and a guest can feel asked for a present but not the day itself. If someone you love is not on the wedding list, a relaxed afternoon tea after the wedding with no gifts expected is one kind option.

### How many people should be on a bridal shower guest list?

There is no set number. The wedding list sets the ceiling, so a small wedding means a smaller shower. An afternoon tea at home is sized by the living room, a hotel high tea by the booking, and a winery or spa day suits a smaller group.

### Who makes the bridal shower guest list?

The host draws it up and sends the invitations, and the bride or her family supplies the names, with the bride approving the final list. Give each mother the same set number of places, so both sides are treated alike.

### Should you have a bridal shower if many guests cannot travel to the wedding?

Often, yes. A shower at home lets the people who cannot travel share in the lead-up without a flight, a hotel and time off work. Hold the shower in Australia before you fly out, leaving enough time to post any gifts on.
