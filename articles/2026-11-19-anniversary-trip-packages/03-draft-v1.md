# Anniversary Trip Packages for Couples Worth Booking

[IMAGE: hero]

Anniversary trip packages promise to take the planning off your hands, so the two of you can spend the week on each other rather than on transfer times. The good ones do. The others are a bundle anyone could click, sold at a premium, with the part that made it an anniversary left for you to sort out on arrival.

This is the buyer's view: what a package should include, how a tailored trip differs from a catalogue bundle, the work an advisor does that you never see, and the checks to run before you pay. It is written for couples marking a fifth or a tenth, and for anyone planning a parent's 25th or 50th as a gift. Sources current as at November 2026.

## What do anniversary trip packages actually include?

An anniversary trip package is a trip with its parts planned and booked together around a date that matters: the stay, the touring, the transfers and the extras, handled by one team from the first call to the trip home. A good one lists, in writing, what is included, what is not, and the terms.

That is how [our packages page](/packages) describes a travel package, and its Anniversaries tile puts the anniversary version simply: "A first, a tenth or a fortieth, back where it began or somewhere new. We arrange the room, the table and the moment you want to share."

The room, the table and the moment are the right place to start. Instead of comparing package names, ask these questions of any quote:

- **Flights and transfers.** Are international flights in the price, or quoted separately? Ours are quoted without international flights, which we arrange on request. Who meets you at the airport, and who books the boat, seaplane or car between places?
- **The room.** Which room category is booked by name, and is the view written into the confirmation or only shown in the brochure?
- **The table.** Which meals are included, and is the anniversary dinner a private set-up on the night itself, or a booking at the hotel restaurant?
- **The experiences.** Which experiences are held for you on fixed dates, and which are suggestions you will need to book once you land?
- **Cover and flexibility.** What are the change and cancellation terms, and do they match the insurance you plan to buy?

[IMAGE: inclusions]

Then look for the gaps. The transfer between islands or towns, the private dinner, any upgrade, and timing around the actual date are the four details worth seeing named in the quote. If a quote is silent on one of them, ask, and get the answer in writing.

## Should you book a package or plan the trip yourself?

There are three honest routes, and each suits a different trip.

**A catalogue bundle.** Fixed nights, a fixed room, a price you can see before you call anyone. It works for a short stay close to home where the hotel itself is the point. The risk is the one that worries people paying a premium: the same bundle anyone else could buy, and nothing on the trip that knows it is your anniversary.

**Planning it yourself.** If you enjoy the research, know exactly what you like, and the trip is simple (one flight, one hotel), doing it yourself is a fair choice. You keep control and deal with each supplier directly. The cost is time, and being the person who fixes it when a connection slips on the way.

**A tailored package through an advisor.** Here the trip starts with the two of you rather than a preset menu. [Our honeymoon packages page](/destination-honeymoon-packages) describes the approach as a specialist who "matches the property, suite, experiences and pacing to you, and coordinates transfers and travel", and [our travel team](/travel) lists "an anniversary return" among the trips it plans.

Plenty of packages for couples look alike on the page. Three tests separate a tailored one from a generic one: the inclusions are in writing, the private moment is held on a named date, and the room category is named rather than "or similar".

## How does a travel advisor shape an anniversary trip?

The best part of an advisor's work is the part you never notice. On an anniversary trip, that usually means:

- **Flight timing around the date.** Arriving the day before the anniversary, not on the night, with sensible gaps between flights. [The ACCC suggests](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/travel-delays-and-cancellations) allowing extra time between connections.
- **Transfers door to door.** Every leg planned, so no one is negotiating a taxi with luggage at dusk.
- **The room in writing.** Our travel team sends two or three options with the rate, the cancellation terms and the confirmed inclusions, so the room category and view are settled before you pay.
- **The moment held.** The private dinner or vow renewal booked for a set evening, with an indoor or covered back-up agreed in case the weather turns.
- **One contact when something changes.** Through [Wedded Wonderland Concierge](/wonderland-concierge), your Wedded Advisor is your single point of contact for the trip and anyone joining it, including their travel, accommodation and payments in other currencies. From Australia, that is the difference between one call and five time zones.

[IMAGE: advisor]

Stays booked this way can carry extras. Our travel page says: "Eligible stays may include daily breakfast, a hotel credit, upgrade priority and other amenities at no extra cost to you", and confirms what applies in writing before you book. On our packages page these are Wedded Privileges: "Breakfast, hotel credit and early arrival or late departure at the properties that take part. Benefits vary by property and booking." Upgrades and later checkouts depend on how full the hotel is that day, so treat them as a welcome surprise, not a plan.

One honest note on rights. [The ACCC says](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/travel-delays-and-cancellations) the consumer guarantees under the Australian Consumer Law apply to travel services whether you book directly, through a travel agent or through a package provider, and that when you book a package through an agent, both the agent's and the provider's terms apply. An advisor does not add legal rights. The value is in reading those terms with you before you commit, and in getting anything unclear confirmed in writing, which the ACCC also advises.

## Which package suits which milestone?

Keep the package in proportion to the year, and to who is coming.

**The early years.** A shorter trip to one place often suits, with the effort spent on the dinner and the room rather than on covering ground.

**The tenth and beyond.** More nights make long-haul worthwhile, and a two-stop trip starts to make sense, which is exactly where transfers and timing need an advisor's eye.

**The 25th, 40th and 50th.** These often become family trips. Our packages page notes that "Milestone birthdays, anniversaries and family trips often bring several households together", and the work becomes rooms that fit everyone and days paced for all ages. For a 50th, Australians can apply for [a message from the Governor-General](https://www.gg.gov.au/about-governor-general/receiving-anniversary-message-governor-general); for the 60th, 65th, 70th and each anniversary after that, the message comes from His Majesty The King and the Governor-General. Each occasion needs its own application, made no later than two months before the date, although late requests are accepted up to six months after. Add it to the planning list alongside passports.

## Where should you go for an anniversary trip package?

Choose the place for the two of you, then ask what the package must cover there.

- **[Bali](/destinations/asia/bali).** Ask whether the villa, the private dinner and the airport transfers sit in one booking, and who you call on the island if a plan changes.
- **[Fiji](/destinations/oceania/fiji).** For an island resort, ask how the transfer from the airport works, who books it, and what happens if the weather delays it.
- **[The Amalfi Coast](/destinations/europe/italy/amalfi-coast-wedding).** Ask how you will move between towns, and whether the table with the view is reserved for your night or simply requested.
- **[Greece](/destinations/europe/greece).** If you plan two islands, ask how the crossing between them is booked and what the plan is if it is cancelled.

Our packages page also lists private tours, small group trips, safaris and cruises, if your anniversary is better spent moving than staying put. If you would rather gather everyone in one place, see [places that work for an anniversary celebration with family](/expert-advice/where-do-you-hold-an-anniversary-party).

## What should you check before you book?

Run through this before any money moves:

- **The dates.** Arrival at least a day before the anniversary, and the dinner or moment booked on the date itself.
- **What is in writing.** Room category, view, the private dinner, held experiences, and what is not included.
- **The transfer plan.** Every leg, with a name and number for each.
- **Change and cancellation terms.** [The ACCC is clear](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/travel-delays-and-cancellations) that providers do not have to give a refund under the consumer guarantees if you change your mind or miss the service for unrelated reasons; your rights then depend on the booking's terms. Read them before you pay.
- **Travel insurance.** [Smartraveller says](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) the right travel insurance is as essential as a passport. Read the Product Disclosure Statement, choose a policy that covers every country on the trip including stopovers, and check the activities list, since even cruises can be excluded from standard policies. Declare every pre-existing condition; Smartraveller notes most policies have an age limit and mature Australians often pay more, which matters for a 25th or 50th. Policies often limit each valuable item, so check the limit before an anniversary piece of jewellery travels with you.
- **Passports.** [Smartraveller's essentials checklist](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) says to make sure your passport is valid for at least six months beyond the end of the trip, as some countries and airlines enforce this, including in transit. Allow at least six weeks for a new one.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best anniversary trip for a couple?

The one built around how you two like to spend time together, not around a hotel's preset menu. Start with the feeling you want on the night itself, quiet, celebratory or adventurous, then choose the place and the pace to suit. A trip that knows it is your anniversary beats a grander one that does not.

### Where can we go for an anniversary from Australia?

Start with how many nights you have. A long weekend suits somewhere close, so you are not spending the anniversary in transit. A week or more opens long-haul options in Europe, where an extra night either side of the flight gives you time to settle before the date that matters.

### How far ahead should we book an anniversary trip package?

Our packages page suggests 3 to 6 months ahead for most trips, and earlier for July to October in Europe and Africa and anywhere over Christmas. If the trip includes a vow renewal or brings family along, start sooner.

### Is a travel advisor worth it for a short anniversary trip?

For a single hotel and a short stay, it can still be worth a call. Our travel page says a straightforward hotel booking usually carries no planning fee, while complex itineraries, flights or group work may carry a fee agreed upfront. The first conversation takes about 15 minutes, with no obligation and no cost to ask.

### Can we renew our vows on an anniversary trip?

Yes. Our Vow renewals tile puts it this way: "We arrange the setting, the photographer, the flowers and the dinner after." Decide early who you want there, since that shapes the venue and the rooms, and read about [who hosts, who speaks and what to wear at a vow renewal](/celebration-ideas/vow-renewal-etiquette).
