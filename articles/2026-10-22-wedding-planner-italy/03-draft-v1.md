# How to Choose a Wedding Planner in Italy

[IMAGE: hero]

You have a date in mind, a guest list of around 90 and a shortlist of Italian planners whose Instagram feeds all look equally lovely. What you cannot see from Sydney is who answers the email in March, who is standing at the villa gates when the shuttle buses arrive, and where your deposits actually go. Choosing a wedding planner in Italy is less about taste than about scope, money and trust.

This guide covers the kind of help your wedding needs, where your planner should be based, how Italian planners charge, the questions that reveal a reliable one and how to protect the budget when every invoice is in euros. It is written for Australian couples [planning a wedding in Italy](/destinations/europe/italy), and for the parents who are often helping to pay.

## First, decide what kind of help your wedding needs

Nearly half of foreign couples who married in Italy in 2025 (46.8%) used a professional wedding planner, according to a 2025 industry report presented with Italy's Ministry of Tourism. That also means plenty did not. The right level of help depends on your guest count, how many events you are hosting and how much happens outside a single property.

Here is the ladder, from lightest to most complete.

| Type of help | What it covers | What it usually does not cover |
|---|---|---|
| Venue in-house coordinator | Your day inside the property, with the venue's own team | Outside suppliers, guest transport, events at other places. Works for the venue, not for you |
| On-the-day coordinator | Takes over a plan you have built, confirms suppliers and runs the timeline on the day | Finding the venue or suppliers, negotiating contracts |
| Partial planner | Selected parts, such as the venue search and key suppliers, then coordination | Everything you keep for yourselves |
| Full planner | Venue and supplier sourcing, contracts, budget, guest logistics, the run sheet and leading the team on the day | The planner's own travel and extra events, unless written into the scope |
| Design-led studio | Full planning plus a strong creative direction, styling and production | Ask which parts of the fee are planning and which are design |
| Advisory or matchmaking service | Helps you choose the destination, the venue and the level of planning, then introduces the right people | Running your wedding day |

The venue coordinator deserves a closer look. A venue's coordinator works for the venue, and their scope varies from one venue to the next. They often start working with you about a month out and focus on running the day rather than shaping the plan. Our [guide to whether you need a planner at all](/expert-advice/do-you-really-need-a-wedding-planner-for-a-destination-wedding) goes deeper on that choice.

Not every wedding needs a full planner. A wedding for 30 at one hotel with a strong in-house events team may need only a good coordinator and a clear contract. A celebration for 70 to 110 guests over three days is different. When most guests are flying from Australia, with a welcome dinner in one place, the ceremony in another and transfers in between, there is a lot of work that no venue coordinator is responsible for. That is usually full-planning territory, not because it is grander, but because someone has to own the gaps.

## Italian, Australian or both: where your planner should be based

There are three common set-ups, and each has a trade-off.

- **Italy-based planner.** Lives inside the supplier network, speaks the language and can walk your venue next week. The trade-off is the time difference, and contracts and invoices drafted the Italian way.
- **Australia-based or dual-based planner.** Takes calls in your hours and understands Australian expectations about contracts and communication. Ask who their team in Italy is, and how many weddings they have run in your region.
- **Both.** An Australian lead with an Italian team on the ground. This works when the contract says exactly who is responsible for what.

Italy is also not one wedding market. In 2025 Tuscany drew the most foreign weddings, followed by Lombardy, Campania, Piedmont and Sicily, and the most requested areas were Lake Como, the Amalfi Coast and Chianti. [Lake Como](/destinations/europe/italy/lake-como-wedding), the [Amalfi Coast](/destinations/europe/italy/amalfi-coast-wedding) and [Tuscany](/destinations/europe/italy/tuscany-wedding) each run on their own suppliers, transport and local rules. A planner who knows the Chianti hills well may never have moved 90 guests along a lake. Ask where they have worked in the past two seasons, in your month, at venues like yours.

### Planning across the time difference

Sydney is between eight and 10 hours ahead of Italy, depending on the time of year: eight hours from early April to early October, 10 hours from late October to late March, and nine in the short weeks between. In practice, a 7pm call in Sydney lands between 9am and 11am in Italy. Perth is two or three hours behind Sydney, so the overlap shifts again.

Good planners make the gap work for you. Look for:

- a fixed weekly or fortnightly call slot (Sydney evening, Italy morning);
- one shared planning document that both sides update;
- an agreed reply time for email, written into the contract;
- a short written summary after every call.

Then ask the question that matters most: who will be physically on site on the day, and is that the person you have been planning with? If the lead you have spoken to for a year hands over to a coordinator you have never met, you want to know now.

## How Italian wedding planners charge, and who they work for

Planners usually charge in one of three ways: a flat fee for a written scope, a percentage of the total wedding budget, or a hybrid of a smaller percentage plus a flat fee. None is better in principle. What matters is that the scope is written down and you can see how the number was reached. Ask, too, whether there is a minimum fee.

For a sense of scale, figures from the Italian consumer association Federconsumatori, reported by [ItaliaOggi in June 2026](https://www.italiaoggi.it/le-guide-per-tutti/matrimoni-spese-alle-stelle-ridurre-i-costi-e-possibile-nn96f4l2), put planner fees in Italy at:

- **On-the-day coordination:** about EUR 1,000 to EUR 3,500 (about AUD 1,600 to AUD 5,600).
- **Full organisation:** from about EUR 2,500 to EUR 10,000 to 15,000 or more (about AUD 4,000 to AUD 24,000 or more), for more complex weddings or the most established professionals.
- **Percentage fees:** averaging around 10% of the total wedding budget.

AUD figures use the [Reserve Bank of Australia](https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/frequency/exchange-rates.html) rate on 22 October 2026 (EUR 1 is about AUD 1.60). These are whole-of-Italy figures, and they include domestic weddings. Treat them as a starting point rather than a guide for a multi-day destination wedding, and ask every planner to quote against the same written brief.

Check what the fee leaves out. Common exclusions to ask about are travel and accommodation for the planner's team, site visits, extra events such as a welcome dinner, and overtime on the night. Ask whether each quote includes Italian VAT (IVA): the standard rate is 22%, although treatment can vary from one business to another.

### Who your planner works for

The other half of the money question is whether anyone else pays your planner. Ask every planner, in writing, the question our own cost guide recommends:

> "Do you receive any payment, discount or fee from suppliers you recommend, and do you mark up décor, production, entertainment or sound you supply?"

This is a standard question, not an accusation, and a good planner answers it plainly. An arrangement you know about can be fine. One you discover later is a problem.

## The questions that separate a great planner from a great Instagram

A beautiful feed tells you about taste. These questions tell you about reliability. Listen to how each answer is given as closely as to what it says.

| Ask | A good answer sounds like | A warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| How many weddings do you run a season, and how many on the same weekend? | A clear number, and who leads each one | "We can always fit you in" |
| Who leads our wedding, and who is on site on the day? | A named lead, plus a named back-up | "The team", with no names |
| When did you last work at our venue, or in our region, in our month? | Specific weddings, with detail on access, music curfew and covered space | Only styled shoots, or only other regions |
| Can we speak to two past couples, ideally one who planned from overseas? | Names offered without hesitation | Written reviews only |
| What is the Plan B at our venue, and when is the call made? | The actual covered space and a set decision time on the day | "It never rains in June" |
| How do you choose suppliers, and can we bring our own? | Clear criteria, and openness to your choices | Their list or nothing |
| How do you quote and invoice? | A written scope, a payment schedule and VAT shown | Verbal figures, or one lump sum |
| What is your partita IVA and registered business name? | Given straight away, and it matches the contract | Reluctance, or a different name on invoices |

Foreign weddings in Italy cluster in May, June and July, although October and November made up 22.4% of foreign couples' celebrations in 2024. If your date is a peak Saturday, the first question matters most.

Italy has no licence or public register for wedding planners, so you check the business rather than a licence number. Ask for the planner's partita IVA (Italian VAT number) and full business name, then use the [Agenzia delle Entrate's free online check](https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/verifica-partita-iva), which shows whether a VAT number is valid and active. For a business registered elsewhere in the EU, the European Commission's VIES service does the same job.

You may also hear about UNI 11954 and UNI 11955, standards for wedding planning services and for wedding planners that [Italy's national standards body published in 2024](https://www.uni.com/wedding-planning-di-qualita-ora-ci-sono-le-norme-uni/). They are voluntary. A planner who works to them is showing care; one who does not is not a red flag. It is also reasonable to ask what professional insurance they hold, and to see the certificate.

Finally, ask to see a complete run sheet from a past wedding (with names removed) and one full gallery, not a highlight reel. A run sheet shows you how a planner thinks when the schedule slips by 40 minutes. For a broader vetting method, see [how to vet suppliers you have never met](/how-to-choose-wedding-vendors-abroad) and [our general checklist for any destination](/how-to-choose-the-right-destination-wedding-planner).

## The work you never see, and how to check they handle it

Styling is the part you see. Much of a planner's value sits in the parts nobody photographs. Ask each planner to walk you through how they handle these.

- **Guest transfers.** Airport to hotels, hotels to the venue, and back again after a late finish. Ask for a draft transport plan with timings, vehicle sizes and the contact on the night.
- **Older or less mobile guests.** Stairs, cobblestones, boat jetties and long walks between ceremony and dinner. Ask how they would get a grandparent from the car to their seat, and where that guest can rest.
- **Room blocks.** Who holds them, when unused rooms are released and who follows up guests who have not booked. Our [wedding and honeymoon travel](/travel) page covers how we help with guest travel and accommodation.
- **Welcome and farewell events.** Is the same team running them, and are they in the fee?

### Heat and wet-weather plans

No one can promise the weather, so ask for both plans in writing. Heat now deserves as much thought as rain. July 2026 was the hottest month recorded in Tuscany since observations began in 1955, and around Florence the maximum topped 36°C on 27 days across June and July 2026. A good heat plan covers ceremony timing, shade, water and a cool room for older guests. A good wet-weather plan names the covered space, how it will be set and the time on the day when the decision is made.

### Music and noise

In Italy, playing SIAE-protected music, live or recorded, at a wedding anywhere other than your own home needs a [SIAE licence](https://www.siae.it/it/notizie/siae-matrimonio-feste-private/). One of the couple requests it through a private account on SIAE's portal for private events. A venue or planner cannot buy it there for you, and a venue's background-music subscription does not cover your wedding. Ask your planner to guide you through it and to check it is active before the day.

Noise rules are set town by town, so there is no single national curfew. Ask what the music curfew is at your venue, and who applies for any permit to play later.

### Legal, symbolic or both

A planner can coordinate the paperwork, but a planner cannot make you married. Legal validity comes from the Italian civil (or recognised religious) process. For Australians, the main steps are:

1. **Atto Notorio.** A declaration signed in person at the [Italian consulate for your state](https://conssydney.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-italiano/stato-civile/getting-married-in-italy-2/atto-notorio/), with four witnesses who are not related to you. It is valid for six months, so it happens close to the date.
2. **Nulla Osta.** A declaration sworn in person in Italy, by appointment, at the [Australian Embassy in Rome or the Consulate-General in Milan](https://italy.embassy.gov.au/files/rome/MARRIAGE%20IN%20ITALY.pdf).
3. **Town marriage office.** The declaration of intent and the civil ceremony, then an Apostille on the Italian marriage certificate so it can be used in Australia.

The Embassy notes that appointments can be hard to get from May to September, so ask your planner how they secure town-hall dates. According to [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas), an overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia, but it is usually recognised if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law.

Symbolic ceremonies are the most common format: in 2025 they made up 65.4% of foreign couples' weddings in Italy. A symbolic ceremony has no legal effect, so if you choose one, plan the legal marriage separately. We checked these sources on 23 October 2026. Confirm the current requirements for your own passports, and see [our step-by-step guide to the Italian legal process](/expert-advice/getting-married-in-italy-the-complete-legal-guide-for-destination-couples).

## Contracts, deposits and paying in euros from Australia

This is the section the person paying will read twice. A planner contract should state, at minimum:

- the full scope, with inclusions and exclusions;
- the named lead planner, and who replaces them if they cannot attend;
- the payment schedule, with amounts and due dates in EUR;
- cancellation and postponement terms, for both sides;
- how supplier payments are handled.

**Paying suppliers.** You may pay suppliers directly or through your planner, and either can work. If money flows through the planner, ask for each supplier's own invoice and a running record of what was paid to whom, and when.

**Language.** Ask for the contract in English, or for a translation of the key terms (scope, payments, cancellation), before you sign.

**Currency.** Deposits and balances in EUR fall due over 12 to 18 months, and the AUD cost moves with the exchange rate. On 22 October 2026 the RBA rate was 1 AUD to 0.6241 EUR, and over the three days shown it moved between 0.6217 and 0.6246. Small moves add up across a budget paid in instalments. Keep every amount in EUR and AUD in one place with its due date, and talk to your bank about how you will pay. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a wedding planner in Italy cost?

Federconsumatori figures reported by ItaliaOggi in June 2026 put on-the-day coordination at about EUR 1,000 to EUR 3,500 (about AUD 1,600 to AUD 5,600) and full organisation from about EUR 2,500 to EUR 10,000 to 15,000 or more (about AUD 4,000 to AUD 24,000 or more). Planners who charge a percentage average around 10% of the total budget. These figures cover all weddings in Italy, so ask for written quotes against the same brief for a multi-day destination wedding. AUD figures use the RBA rate on 22 October 2026.

### Do we need a planner if our venue has its own wedding coordinator?

Sometimes not. A venue coordinator works for the venue and their scope varies, so ask in writing whether it covers outside suppliers, guest transport and the run sheet before the day. For a small wedding inside one hotel that may be enough. For a multi-day wedding across several places, you usually need someone working for you.

### Should we hire an Italian planner or one based in Australia?

Either can work. An Italy-based planner brings local suppliers and language; an Australia-based or dual-based planner works in your hours, with Sydney eight to 10 hours ahead of Italy. What matters most is recent experience in your region and a named lead on site on the day.

### Can an Italian wedding planner make our marriage legal in Australia?

No. A planner can coordinate the paperwork, but legality comes from the Italian civil (or recognised religious) process, including declarations you sign in person at an Italian consulate and before an Australian consular officer in Italy. Smartraveller says an overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia but is usually recognised if it was valid where it took place and would be valid under Australian law. Confirm the current requirements for your own passports.

### Do Italian wedding planners take commissions from suppliers?

Ask rather than assume. Put this to every planner in writing: do you receive any payment, discount or fee from suppliers you recommend, and do you mark up décor, production, entertainment or sound you supply? A clear written answer is what you are looking for, and an arrangement you know about can be fine.

### How far ahead should we book a wedding planner in Italy?

Earlier is safer for peak dates. Foreign weddings in Italy cluster in May, June and July, and the Australian Embassy notes that appointments can be hard to get from May to September.
