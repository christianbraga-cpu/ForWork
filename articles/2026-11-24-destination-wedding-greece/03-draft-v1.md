# Destination Wedding in Greece Guide for Couples

[IMAGE: hero]

You have Greece on the shortlist, perhaps an island already in mind, and a guest list that will cross the world to be there. Planning a destination wedding in Greece from Australia comes down to six decisions, best made in order: where to marry, when, the legal route, the budget, how your guests travel, and the timeline that ties it together.

This guide gives you enough on each to decide, then points you to the page that holds the detail. It is written for couples planning from Australia, and for the parents who may be helping to pay. Sources current as at November 2026.

## Where in Greece to marry, islands or mainland

Greece is never just one island, as [our Greece destination page](/destinations/europe/greece) puts it, and the place you choose sets your guest list, your budget and how everyone gets there. Our venues guide puts it more plainly still: guest count decides the region. Start with the number of people you want beside you, then read the cards below.

[IMAGE: where-islands]

### Santorini

Santorini is the caldera island: clifftop terraces, whitewashed walls and a ceremony with the sea falling away below you. Guest limits can differ from one terrace to the next within the same hotel, so ask each venue for the cap on the exact space you want, not the property as a whole, before you fall for the view. [Our Santorini cost breakdown](/real-weddings/santorini-wedding-cost-breakdown) shows how quickly guest count moves the budget here.

### Mykonos

Our Greece page describes Mykonos as built for couples who want a celebration with energy: beach-club receptions, whitewashed chapels and late nights. It also suggests pairing the two famous islands, with an intimate ceremony on Santorini and the reception or welcome event on Mykonos. If you do, [Smartraveller says](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) ferries can be delayed or cancelled in bad weather, mainly because of strong winds, so leave a spare day between islands.

### Crete

Crete brings beaches and estates, and our venues guide notes that venues in Crete and on the Athens Riviera publish space for several hundred guests. It is also where two municipalities, [Chania](https://www.chania.gr/files/57/44617/civil_marriages_documents_for_foreigners_of_abroad_no5_%28en%29.pdf) and [Heraklion](https://www.heraklion.gr/en/citizen/civil-marriages/marriage-foreigners.html), publish document lists for foreigners who live abroad: a practical help when you are planning the legal side from home.

### Paros, Naxos and the smaller Cyclades

Our Greece page lists Paros and Naxos among its islands and pairs Paros with Corfu for quieter romance. Think of them when you want the Cycladic whitewash at a gentler pace than the two famous names. Plan the island leg as carefully as the venue, because the same ferry and wind advice applies.

### Corfu and the Ionian

Corfu sits in the Ionian Sea, off Greece's west coast, far from the Cycladic islands above. Our best-time guide tracks Corfu (Kerkyra) on its own, so check its months separately rather than borrowing the Cycladic pattern.

### Athens Riviera and the mainland

The Athens Riviera brings glamour, in our Greece page's words, and one practical advantage: no ferry. Guests land in Athens and transfer by road, and venues here publish space for large guest lists. For a big family wedding with older relatives, the mainland deserves a serious look. For venue by venue detail, see [our guide to venues from the islands to Athens](/destination-weddings/greece-wedding-venues).

## When to marry in Greece, month by month

[Our best-time guide](/destination-weddings/best-time-to-get-married-in-greece) sets out each month, island by island, using Greek national weather-service averages, so this is the short version. [Smartraveller warns](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) that heatwaves are frequent over the summer months, that wildfires occur from June to September and that Saharan dust storms are increasing, particularly in spring. High summer needs shade, water and evening timing, and every month needs a plan for wind.

For a Greek Orthodox wedding, the church calendar matters too. Our best-time guide lists the dates when the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Australia says marriages are not permitted, including 1 to 15 August and the weeks from Great Lent to Easter Sunday. For a church wedding in Greece, ask your priest and the local Metropolis (the diocese).

Then remember the hemispheres. A Greek May falls in late autumn at home, June in early winter and September in early spring, so check school holidays if guests are bringing children.

## The legal route for Australians in brief

[IMAGE: legal]

Three routes are open to you:

- **Civil:** a legal marriage under Greek law, open to same-sex couples under Law 5089/2024.
- **Greek Orthodox:** also legal, with the church's own conditions on top.
- **Symbolic:** no legal effect; you marry legally in Australia first, then celebrate in Greece as you like.

The [gov.gr civil licence entry](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_civil_marriage_licence) says civil and Orthodox weddings both need a licence from the mayor. The [Australian Embassy in Athens](https://greece.embassy.gov.au/athn/marriages.html) says Greek municipal and religious bodies require a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) for each non-Greek partner, and that the acceptable place of issue can differ between organisations. [DFAT issues the CNI](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-certificate-no-impediment-marriage). [Gov.gr's licence entry for foreign residents](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_marriage_licence_for_foreign_residents_in_Greece) accepts documents from Hague Convention countries such as Australia only with an apostille and a Greek translation, and [DFAT adds apostilles](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised) to Australian documents.

Back home, [Smartraveller's advice on marrying overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) says you cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia, but in most cases it is legally recognised if it was valid where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) adds that the right to marry in Australia no longer depends on sex or gender; here is [what same-sex couples need](/destination-weddings/same-sex-marriage-greece).

Confirm the current requirements for your own passports with the municipality where you will marry and the Australian Embassy in Athens. Our guide to [the licence, the documents and the eight-day wait](/destination-weddings/greece-marriage-licence) has the full detail.

## Getting guests from Australia to Greece

[IMAGE: guests]

Greece is a long-haul trip from any Australian city, and the islands add one more leg. [[NEEDS: current routes from Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane and Perth to Athens, connection hubs, total travel time and island connections (research N-08)]]

[Smartraveller's Greece advice](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) says Australians can travel to the Schengen area, which includes Greece, without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. On arrival, guests have fingerprints and a photo taken under the EU Entry/Exit System (EES), which is free and needs nothing done before the border. ETIAS, the EU's planned travel authorisation, has no confirmed start date; the EU will announce it several months ahead.

After that flight, give guests a reason to stay. A shape that works: arrive, a free day to recover from jet lag, a welcome dinner, the wedding, then a slow farewell lunch. Placing the wedding mid-stay also protects you if a flight or ferry runs late.

Think hardest about older guests. Smartraveller says some ferry services lack suitable access for people with disabilities, that in most cases you carry your luggage up and down stairs, and that from May to October popular seaports bring high temperatures and large crowds. For grandparents, that argues for porters, cars to the door and a seat in the shade, arranged before anyone lands.

Smartraveller also notes there is no free healthcare agreement between Australia and Greece, that evacuation is more likely on a Greek island, and that Greece is an active seismic zone, including near Santorini and Crete. Ask every guest to buy travel insurance that covers all medical costs overseas, including evacuation.

## What your advisor handles that guests never see

[IMAGE: invisible-work]

Much of what can go wrong at a Greek wedding sits in details your guests will never notice if they are handled well. A sound plan covers these before anyone boards a plane:

- **Wind and water.** Our venues guide puts it simply: the summer meltemi wind that cools a terrace can also hold up a ferry, so plan for both. That means a spare day between islands and a ceremony set-up that can move.
- **Transfers.** Timed around flights and ferries, with cars to the door for anyone who cannot manage steps.
- **Step-free routes.** Walked and confirmed for older guests, from the road to the ceremony and on to dinner.

## A planning timeline from 18 months out

[IMAGE: timeline]

- **18 to 12 months:** choose the island and month, set your guest count and budget, hold a venue date, and bring in an advisor if you want one.
- **12 to 9 months:** send save-the-dates, set up the room block, and book your planner and key vendors.
- **9 to 6 months:** ask the town hall in writing which documents it wants, how recent they must be and where your CNI should be issued, then apply for CNIs and apostilles. DFAT [publishes no fixed processing time](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised) and warns of delays at the end of the school year and in peak holiday periods, so do not leave it to the Australian summer.
- **6 to 3 months:** book guest travel and transfers, settle menus, and confirm the wind and wet-weather alternative in writing.
- **Final month and wedding week:** take your originals to the town hall and bring your witnesses (Heraklion, for one, asks each partner to bring an adult witness with ID). After the ceremony, the marriage is registered at the [registry office of the municipality](https://mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:%CE%9B%CE%B7%CE%BE%CE%B9%CE%B1%CF%81%CF%87%CE%B9%CE%BA%CE%AE_%CF%80%CF%81%CE%AC%CE%BE%CE%B7_%CE%B3%CE%AC%CE%BC%CE%BF%CF%85) where it took place. Ask the town hall before you fly how to have the certificate apostilled for use at home.

## Questions to ask before you book a venue or planner

From the other side of the world, good questions do the work of a site visit. Ask in writing and keep every answer:

1. What is the guest cap for the space we want, seated and standing, rather than for the property?
2. Is there a curfew, or a limit on amplified music?
3. Which ceremony times can we book, including a sunset slot?
4. Where exactly do the ceremony and dinner move in strong wind or rain?
5. How many steps lie between the road and the ceremony, and is there a step-free route?
6. What does the quote leave out: VAT, service charges, transfers, set-up time, extended hire, meals for suppliers?
7. What is the deposit schedule, in which currency, and what is the cancellation policy?
8. Who will be on the ground on the day, and who steps in if they cannot be?
9. Must we use your suppliers, or can we bring our own?
10. What are the room block terms, release dates and minimum stay?

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a destination wedding in Greece cost?

It depends on the island and your guest count. For Santorini, our cost breakdown (November 2026) gives EUR 4,000 for an elopement to EUR 55,000 or more, depending on guest count and style, with a standard wedding of 50 to 80 guests at EUR 30,000 to EUR 55,000. We have not published ranges for other islands or the mainland, so ask for itemised quotes in euros.

### Is a wedding in Greece legally recognised in Australia?

In most cases, yes. [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) says an overseas marriage is legally recognised in Australia if it was valid where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. You cannot register it in Australia, and your Greek marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage.

### What is the best month for a wedding in Greece?

There is no single answer: it depends on the island, how much heat your guests can manage and, for an Orthodox wedding, the church calendar. Our [best-time guide](/destination-weddings/best-time-to-get-married-in-greece) sets out each month, island by island. [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) warns that summer heatwaves are frequent and that wildfires occur from June to September.

### Can we marry legally in Australia and have a symbolic ceremony in Greece?

Yes. You complete the legal marriage at home under Australian rules, then hold a symbolic ceremony in Greece with no legal effect there. The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) says an Australian-authorised celebrant cannot perform a marriage overseas, so on this route the legal ceremony happens in Australia before you fly.

### Do our guests need a visa to attend a wedding in Greece?

Not for a short stay on an Australian passport. [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) says Australians can travel to the Schengen area without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Guests have fingerprints and a photo taken under the EU Entry/Exit System on arrival, which is free, and ETIAS has no confirmed start date. Guests on other passports should check their own rules.
