## Article body

# Destination Wedding in Greece Guide for Couples

[IMAGE: hero]

You have Greece on the shortlist, perhaps an island already in mind, and a guest list that will cross the world to be there. Planning a destination wedding in Greece from Australia comes down to six decisions, best made in order: where to marry, when, the legal route, the budget, how your guests travel, and the timeline that ties it together.

This guide gives you enough on each to decide, then points you to the page that holds the detail. It is written for couples planning from Australia, and for the parents who may be helping to pay. Sources current as at November 2026.

## Where in Greece to marry, islands or mainland

Greece is never just one island, as [our Greece destination page](/destinations/europe/greece) puts it, and the place you choose sets your guest list, your budget and how everyone gets there. Our venues guide puts it more plainly still: guest count decides the region. Start with the number of people you want beside you, then read the cards below.

[IMAGE: where-islands]

### Santorini

Santorini is the caldera island: clifftop terraces, whitewashed walls and a ceremony with the sea falling away below you. Before you fall for the view, ask each venue in writing for the guest cap on the exact space you want, seated and standing, rather than for the property as a whole. Compare the terraces themselves in our guide to [Santorini venues with caldera views](/destination-weddings/santorini-wedding-venues).

### Mykonos

Mykonos suits couples who want a celebration with energy: beach-club receptions, whitewashed chapels and late nights; [our Mykonos planning guide](/destination-weddings/mykonos-wedding) covers the island in full. You can also pair the two famous islands, with an intimate ceremony on Santorini and the reception or welcome event on Mykonos; see [how the two islands compare](/destination-weddings/santorini-or-mykonos-wedding). If you do, [Smartraveller says](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) ferries can be delayed or cancelled in bad weather, mainly because of strong winds, so leave a spare day between islands.

### Crete

Crete brings beaches and estates, and our venues guide notes that venues in Crete and on the Athens Riviera publish space for several hundred guests. For more on the island, see [our Crete planning guide](/destination-weddings/wedding-in-crete).

### Paros, Naxos and the smaller Cyclades

Paros and Naxos give you the Cycladic whitewash at a gentler pace than the two famous names; Paros, like Corfu, suits a quieter, more romantic wedding.

### Corfu and the Ionian

Corfu (Kerkyra) is an Ionian island, and our best-time guide tracks it on its own, so check its months separately rather than borrowing the Cycladic pattern.

### Athens Riviera and the mainland

The Athens Riviera brings glamour and one practical advantage: no ferry. Guests land in Athens and transfer by road, and venues here publish space for large guest lists. For a big family wedding with older relatives, the mainland deserves a serious look. For venue-by-venue detail, see [our guide to venues from the islands to Athens](/destination-weddings/greece-wedding-venues).

[IMAGE: where-mainland]

## When to marry in Greece, month by month

[Our best-time guide](/destination-weddings/best-time-to-get-married-in-greece) sets out each month, island by island, using Greek national weather-service averages, so this is the short version. [Smartraveller warns](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) that heatwaves are frequent over the summer months, that wildfires occur from June to September and that Saharan dust storms are increasing, particularly in spring. High summer needs shade, water and evening timing, and an island wedding needs a plan for wind.

For a Greek Orthodox wedding, the church calendar matters too. Our best-time guide lists the dates when the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Australia says marriages are not permitted, including 1 to 15 August and the weeks from Great Lent to Easter Sunday. For a church wedding in Greece, ask your priest and the local Metropolis (the diocese).

If guests are bringing children, check your dates against Australian school holidays.

## The legal route for Australians in brief

[IMAGE: legal]

Three routes are open to you:

- **Civil:** a legal marriage under Greek law, open to same-sex couples under Law 5089/2024; here is [what same-sex couples need](/destination-weddings/same-sex-marriage-greece).
- **Greek Orthodox:** also legal, with [the church's own conditions](/destination-weddings/greek-orthodox-wedding) on top.
- **Symbolic:** no legal effect; marry legally in Australia first, then celebrate in Greece.

The [gov.gr civil licence entry](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_civil_marriage_licence) says civil and Orthodox weddings both need a marriage licence; couples who live abroad are usually asked for a document from home instead, so ask your town hall which one it accepts. The [Australian Embassy in Athens](https://greece.embassy.gov.au/athn/marriages.html) says Greek municipal and religious bodies require a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI), which [DFAT issues](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-certificate-no-impediment-marriage), from each non-Greek partner, and that the acceptable place of issue can differ between organisations. Australian documents need an apostille, which [DFAT adds](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised), and a Greek translation, according to [gov.gr's licence entry for foreign residents](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_marriage_licence_for_foreign_residents_in_Greece).

Back home, [Smartraveller's advice on marrying overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) says you cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia, but in most cases it is legally recognised if it was valid where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law.

Confirm the requirements for your passports with the municipality where you marry and the Australian Embassy in Athens, then read our guide to [the licence, the documents and the eight-day wait](/destination-weddings/greece-marriage-licence).

## What a destination wedding in Greece costs

We publish detailed figures for Santorini, so start there. [Our Santorini cost breakdown](/real-weddings/santorini-wedding-cost-breakdown) (November 2026) puts a Santorini wedding at EUR 4,000 for an elopement to EUR 55,000 or more, depending on guest count and style. By size, it gives:

- Elopement, 2 to 20 guests: EUR 4,000 to EUR 12,000
- Micro wedding, 20 to 40 guests: EUR 18,000 to EUR 30,000
- Standard wedding, 50 to 80 guests: EUR 30,000 to EUR 55,000
- Luxury wedding, 80 or more guests: EUR 55,000 and up

These are Santorini figures. Mykonos, Crete, the smaller islands and the mainland price differently, so ask every venue for an itemised quote in euros. On Santorini, catering runs EUR 75 to EUR 160 or more a head and a wedding planner EUR 3,000 to EUR 5,000 or more; ask whether the fee is for full planning or on-the-day coordination, and see [how planner fees compare](/expert-advice/cost-of-wedding-planner). Accommodation is a separate part of the budget. Budget guest flights separately as well.

Our guide to [what Greek venue packages include](/destination-weddings/greece-wedding-packages) explains what packages bundle and where they differ; on Santorini, venues quote each wedding.

The paperwork is a small line by comparison: [DFAT's 2026 fees](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services) are AUD 181 for a CNI and AUD 105 for each apostille, rising with CPI each 1 January.

Greece uses the euro, so deposits fall due in EUR across many months while the exchange rate moves between them. Wedded Wonderland Concierge coordinates deposits, payments and currency across borders, so the person paying has one point of contact for the schedule.

## Getting guests from Australia to Greece

[IMAGE: guests]

Greece is a long trip from any Australian city, and an island wedding can add a further flight or ferry. Check current routes, connections and total travel time with your airline or travel advisor before you lock in the date, and share them with guests early.

[Smartraveller's Greece advice](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/greece) says Australians can travel to the Schengen area, which includes Greece, without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. On arrival, guests have fingerprints and a photo taken under the EU Entry/Exit System (EES), which is free and needs nothing done before the border. ETIAS, the EU's planned travel authorisation, has no confirmed start date; the EU will announce it several months ahead.

After the long journey, give guests a reason to stay. A shape that works: arrive, a free day to recover from jet lag, a welcome dinner, the wedding, then a slow farewell lunch. Placing the wedding mid-stay also protects you if a flight or ferry runs late.

Think hardest about older guests. Smartraveller says some ferry services lack suitable access for people with disabilities, that in most cases you carry your luggage up and down stairs, and that from May to October popular seaports bring high temperatures and large crowds. For grandparents, that argues for porters, cars to the door and a seat in the shade, arranged before anyone lands.

Smartraveller also notes there is no free healthcare agreement between Australia and Greece, that evacuation is more likely on a Greek island, and that Greece is an active seismic zone, including near Santorini and Crete. Ask every guest to buy travel insurance that covers all medical costs overseas, including evacuation.

Room blocks and guest stays can sit in the same plan through our [wedding and honeymoon travel](/travel) service. Ask about hotel benefits on eligible stays. For everything else, send guests [our guest guide](/destination-weddings/destination-wedding-guest-guide).

## What your advisor handles that guests never see

[IMAGE: invisible-work]

Much of what can go wrong sits in details guests never notice when they are handled well. A sound plan covers these before anyone flies:

- **Wind and water.** The summer meltemi wind that cools a terrace can also hold up a ferry, so plan a spare day between islands and a ceremony set-up that can move.
- **Transfers.** Timed around flights and ferries, with cars to the door for anyone who cannot manage steps.
- **Step-free routes.** Asked of every venue in writing for older guests, from the road to the ceremony and on to dinner.
- **A covered or indoor alternative.** Named in the contract, not promised on the day.
- **Room blocks.** With clear release dates, so unbooked rooms go back before you pay for them.
- **Payments in euros.** Deposits scheduled across suppliers and currencies, with one person tracking them.

## A planning timeline from 18 months out

[IMAGE: timeline]

- **18 to 12 months:** choose the island and month, set your guest count and budget, hold a venue date, and bring in an advisor if you want one.
- **12 to 9 months:** send save-the-dates, set up the room block, and book your planner and key vendors.
- **9 to 6 months:** ask the town hall in writing which documents it wants, how recent each must be when you lodge it, and where your CNI should be issued. Then time your CNI and apostille applications to that answer, so nothing is too old by the wedding. DFAT [publishes no fixed processing time](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised) and warns of delays at the end of the school year and in peak holiday periods, so do not leave it to the Australian summer.
- **6 to 3 months:** book guest travel and transfers, settle menus, and confirm the wind and wet-weather alternative in writing.
- **Final month and wedding week:** take your originals to the town hall and bring your witnesses ([Heraklion](https://www.heraklion.gr/en/citizen/civil-marriages/marriage-foreigners.html), for one, asks each partner to bring an adult witness with ID). After the ceremony, the marriage is registered at the [registry office of the municipality](https://mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:%CE%9B%CE%B7%CE%BE%CE%B9%CE%B1%CF%81%CF%87%CE%B9%CE%BA%CE%AE_%CF%80%CF%81%CE%AC%CE%BE%CE%B7_%CE%B3%CE%AC%CE%BC%CE%BF%CF%85) where it took place. Ask the town hall before you fly how to have the certificate apostilled for use at home.

## Questions to ask before you book a venue or planner

From the other side of the world, good questions do the work of a site visit. Ask in writing and keep every answer:

1. What is the guest cap for the space we want, seated and standing, rather than for the property?
2. Is there a curfew, or a limit on amplified music?
3. Which ceremony times can we book, including a sunset slot?
4. Where exactly do the ceremony and dinner move in strong wind or rain?
5. How many steps lie between the road and the ceremony, and is there a step-free route?
6. What does the quote leave out: VAT, service charges, transfers, set-up time, extended hire, meals for suppliers?
7. What is the deposit schedule, in which currency, and what is the cancellation policy?
8. Who will be on the ground on the day, and who steps in if they cannot be?
9. Must we use your suppliers, or can we bring our own?
10. What are the room block terms, release dates and minimum stay?

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a destination wedding in Greece cost?

It depends on the island and your guest count. For Santorini, our cost breakdown (November 2026) gives EUR 4,000 for an elopement to EUR 55,000 or more, depending on guest count and style. Other islands and the mainland price differently, so ask for itemised quotes in euros.

### Is a wedding in Greece legally recognised in Australia?

In most cases, yes. Smartraveller says an overseas marriage is legally recognised in Australia if it was valid where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. You cannot register it in Australia, and your Greek marriage certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage.

### What is the best month for a wedding in Greece?

There is no single answer: it depends on the island, how much heat your guests can manage and, for an Orthodox wedding, the church calendar. Our [best-time guide](/destination-weddings/best-time-to-get-married-in-greece) sets out each month, island by island. Smartraveller warns that summer heatwaves are frequent and that wildfires occur from June to September.

### Can we marry legally in Australia and have a symbolic ceremony in Greece?

Yes. You complete the legal marriage at home under Australian rules, then hold a symbolic ceremony in Greece with no legal effect there. The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) says an Australian-authorised celebrant cannot perform a marriage overseas, so on this route the legal ceremony happens in Australia before you fly.

### Do our guests need a visa to attend a wedding in Greece?

Not for a short stay on an Australian passport. Smartraveller says Australians can travel to the Schengen area without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Guests have fingerprints and a photo taken under the EU Entry/Exit System on arrival, which is free, and ETIAS has no confirmed start date. Guests on other passports should check their own rules.
