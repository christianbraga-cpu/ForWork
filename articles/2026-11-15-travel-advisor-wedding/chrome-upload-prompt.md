## Article body

# Wedding Travel Advisor and Why It Pays to Use One

[IMAGE: couple-planning-itinerary-coastal-terrace-morning-01.webp]

A wedding travel advisor plans and books the travel side of a destination wedding: the room block for your guests, flights from several cities, transfers on arrival day and the honeymoon that follows. They also stay accountable for those bookings before and during the trip, so when something changes you call one person instead of four suppliers.

That is the short answer to whether a travel advisor is worth it for a wedding abroad. You can also see [how our advisors plan wedding and honeymoon travel](/travel).

## What a wedding travel advisor actually does

Our own travel page describes the work as planning the celebration-week hotel, the nights before and after, where your guests stay and the honeymoon as one connected trip rather than a stack of separate bookings, and staying reachable while you are away.

### Travel advisor vs travel agent

Our own service page is headed "Destination wedding travel agent", and "advisor" usually signals the planning and advice side of the same role. According to Consumer Affairs Victoria, state travel agent licensing and the Travel Compensation Fund were phased out from mid-2014, leaving consumers to rely on Australian Consumer Law and credit card chargebacks. Accreditation, written terms and a person who answers tell you far more than a job title.

## Room blocks, flights and transfers: the work your guests never see

[IMAGE: guests-transfer-arrival-countryside-villa-golden-hour-01.webp]

A room block is a set of rooms a hotel holds for your guests. On our wedding packages, for example, guest accommodation is paid separately, usually at negotiated group rates. The fine print matters more than the rate. Published hotel group terms use a cut-off date, after which unreserved rooms go back on general sale, and an attrition clause, which charges the group if bookings fall below the number agreed. Both vary by property and can be negotiated, so the dates and numbers in your own contract are the ones that count.

This is where an advisor earns their keep. They read those clauses before you sign, set the cut-off with your RSVP date in mind, chase the guests who have not booked and send the hotel a rooming list (who sleeps where, arriving when). At Wedded Wonderland, managing room blocks and guest travel sits with the same person who handles your venue and your payments.

**Flights.** Your guests will rarely land on the same day, or from the same city. When arriving on time is critical, the ACCC suggests considering a fare class with better options if there is a delay or cancellation, allowing extra time between connections and asking for unclear terms to be confirmed in writing. An advisor turns that into plain guest advice: which fares to look for, which connections to avoid and why landing two days before the welcome drinks is worth the extra night.

**Transfers and arrival day.** For a wedding at a villa in Tuscany or a resort in Bali, arrivals spread across a day become transfer waves: a car for the late flight, a van for the 2 pm cluster, room for garment bags and golf clubs. Someone has to match each name to a vehicle and tell the hotel who is checking in after midnight. Send guests a note on [how to pack the suit and the dress for the flight](/how-to-pack-for-a-destination-wedding) well before they leave.

**Older and less mobile guests.** Our travel page asks every client about accessibility, allergy and dietary needs in the party, and this is the moment to raise them. A step-free room near the lift, a ground-floor room for a grandparent and the shortest available transfer are far easier to request months ahead than at the front desk.

**Wet weather or a venue change.** If rain moves the reception to a covered terrace across town, or the ceremony time shifts, the transfers have to move with it. An advisor rebooks vehicles and tells guests directly, so your guests are not relying on a group chat.

## VIP perks and partner-property benefits, read properly

Perks are worth understanding before you count on them. At Wedded Wonderland Travel, our Wonderland Benefits apply at partner, preferred and consortia properties, and they vary by hotel and by program.

[IMAGE: breakfast-tray-balcony-water-view-morning-01.webp]

Eligible bookings may include daily breakfast, a hotel credit, upgrade priority, early check-in and late checkout, a welcome amenity and, depending on the booking, extras such as transfers or spa access. Inclusions vary by hotel, rate and dates, and we confirm exactly what applies to your stay in writing before you book. Here is how to read a list like that, from us or anyone else.

**"Priority" is not a promise.** Upgrades and extended check-in or checkout generally depend on how full the hotel is that day, so a good advisor shows you which inclusions are confirmed and which are requests. On the morning after your wedding, a late checkout is a request until the hotel confirms it.

**A credit is not a discount.** A hotel credit is typically one per room, per stay, spent on eligible charges such as dining or spa rather than taken off the room bill. Unused value usually expires at checkout.

**Your room block is a separate contract.** Group contracts work differently to individual bookings, so these perks do not carry across a room block automatically; we negotiate and document group terms separately. If anyone promises perks on every guest room, ask to see it in the contract.

**Timing matters.** Perks usually cannot be added to a booking already made elsewhere. All inclusions sit under partner program terms that may change, alongside the supplier's own deposit and cancellation terms. If either of you holds hotel loyalty status, mention it at the start; our travel page asks about it for a reason.

## Your honeymoon, planned as part of the same trip

[IMAGE: couple-walking-villa-terrace-late-afternoon-01.webp]

The morning after the wedding is where separate bookings come apart. The suit and dress need to go somewhere other than your next stop, and an onward flight leaves at an hour nobody chose with a late night in mind. Planned together, that day becomes one sequence: a checkout time requested in writing, a transfer straight to the airport or the next stay, such as a villa in Tuscany or a resort in Bali, wedding clothes packed for home and a quiet first night.

We can stage the wedding at one resort and the honeymoon at another, or extend the same stay, with one specialist coordinating the whole trip, as with [a wedding and honeymoon planned at one resort or two](/destination-packages). ## When things go wrong, who picks up the phone

[IMAGE: traveller-phone-passport-airport-lounge-evening-01.webp]

This is the part of the job you hope never to need. Four situations are worth planning for:

- a guest's flight is cancelled the day before the welcome dinner;
- a hotel cannot honour part of the room block;
- a guest falls ill and cannot travel;
- Smartraveller raises the advice level for your destination after you have booked.

In each case the work is practical: finding the next flight, re-allocating rooms, talking to the property and checking what the terms allow. No one can promise the outcome. On a Wedded Wonderland Travel booking, you contact us; we have your booking, your written inclusions and the relationship with the property, and we take it up on your behalf.

**What the law says.** The ACCC says Australian Consumer Law guarantees apply whether you book directly, through a travel agent or through a third party, covering travel services within Australia and services by international businesses departing Australia. If a delayed or cancelled service is not provided within a reasonable time, the provider must offer a replacement or a refund; a court or tribunal decides what is reasonable, and the purpose of your travel may be relevant. The guarantees do not apply if you change your mind, or if a third party such as a government restriction stops the service. Through an agent, both sets of terms apply and the agent is usually bound by the supplier's, so an advisor adds no legal rights. Their value is reading the terms before you book and pressing your case after.

The ACCC does not resolve individual complaints: start with the provider, then a state or territory consumer agency or tribunal, as the [ACCC's guide to travel delays and cancellations](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/travel-delays-and-cancellations) explains. The ACCC also explains what happens [when a business goes bust](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/stay-protected/when-a-business-goes-bust): you may become an unsecured creditor, and you can ask your bank or card provider about a chargeback. Ask how each deposit is paid before you transfer anything.

**Insurance for every guest.** Smartraveller says the right travel insurance is as essential as a passport. Guests should choose a policy covering every country on the trip, including stopovers, and check whether it covers cancellation if the advice level rises. Medical-only policies usually do not cover cancellation costs, and free credit card cover often has lower limits and usually has to be activated. Every guest must declare all pre-existing conditions to their insurer, and most policies have an age limit, which matters for older guests. Check rings, gowns and suits against item limits and the excess, and share [Smartraveller's travel insurance advice](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) with your guest list early.

## How travel agents are paid, and what to ask before you sign

Our own answer on fees, word for word from our travel page: "A straightforward hotel booking usually carries no planning fee. More complex itineraries, flights or group work may carry a fee, agreed with you upfront before any work begins." With [Wedded Wonderland Concierge](/wonderland-concierge), you also have one dedicated point of contact, and your concierge handles deposits, payments and currency so nothing gets lost across borders.

No official source we checked sets out a standard way Australian agents are paid, so with any other advisor, ask rather than assume.

Accreditation is the other half. According to ATIA, the Australian Travel Industry Association, it retired the ATAS designator on 25 October 2024, and eligible businesses are now "ATIA Accredited". ATIA says accreditation is voluntary and is not a solvency guarantee or a compensation scheme.

Four questions to ask any advisor before you sign:

1. Which inclusions are confirmed in writing, and which are only requests?
2. Who holds our deposits, in what currency, and what are the cancellation terms?
3. Can we see the room block contract, including the cut-off and attrition clauses?
4. Who do we call at 2 am abroad, and who covers when you are away?

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a travel advisor and a travel agent?

In everyday use, very little: both plan and book travel, and "advisor" usually signals more of the planning and advice. Neither title is a licence since, according to Consumer Affairs Victoria, state travel agent licensing was phased out from mid-2014, so look for ATIA accreditation and read the written terms.

### Do we need a travel advisor if we already have a wedding planner?

A planner shapes the celebration and its vendors; a travel advisor moves guests, rooms and flights. If many of your guests are flying in, ask your planner who will manage the room block, transfers and the honeymoon, and get the answer in writing.

### Does using a travel advisor cost more than booking direct?

It depends on how the advisor is paid on your booking, so ask before any work starts. At Wedded Wonderland, a straightforward hotel booking usually carries no planning fee, and more complex itineraries, flights or group work may carry a fee agreed upfront.

### Do our guests have to book inside the room block?

That is their choice, but bookings made outside the block usually do not count toward it, so check how your contract counts pickup. If too few guests book inside, an attrition clause can charge the group, and unbooked rooms go back on sale after the cut-off date. Check both clauses before you sign and set the cut-off with your RSVP date in mind.

### Can a travel advisor plan our honeymoon as well as the wedding travel?

Yes. We can stage the wedding at one resort and the honeymoon at another, or extend the same stay, with one specialist coordinating the whole trip.

### What happens if a guest's flight is cancelled before the wedding?

The guest, or their advisor, contacts the airline first. Under Australian Consumer Law, if a service covered by the guarantees is not provided within a reasonable time, the provider must offer a replacement or refund; travel insurance with cancellation cover fills the gaps. This covers travel services within Australia and services by international businesses departing Australia; a guest flying in from overseas should check the airline's own terms and their travel insurance.
