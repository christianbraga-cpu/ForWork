faq:
  - q: "How do you pay wedding vendors in another country?"
    a: "Follow each contract's payment schedule and method. Before each payment, compare the exchange rate, fees and transfer time, confirm the vendor's bank details by phone on a number you found yourself, and keep proof of every payment with the contract."
  - q: "Should I pay my overseas wedding vendors in AUD or their currency?"
    a: "With an invoice in the vendor's currency, the amount in that currency is fixed and your Australian dollar cost moves with the rate until you pay. A quote in Australian dollars still involves a conversion, so ask which rate it uses and whether it is fixed."
  - q: "How much deposit do overseas wedding vendors ask for?"
    a: "It varies, and each contract sets its own amount and dates. Before you sign, find the deposit, any interim payments, the balance date, the payment method and the point at which each payment stops being refundable."
  - q: "Is it safe to pay a wedding vendor overseas by bank transfer?"
    a: "It can be, if you confirm the details first. The ACCC urges Australians to check payment details directly with a business before paying an emailed invoice, by calling a number you have found yourself."
  - q: "What if my vendor abroad wants to be paid in cash?"
    a: "Treat it like any other payment term, with the amount, currency, date and recipient written into the contract. Ask for a written receipt for every cash payment, have one trusted person hand it over, and keep the receipts with your contracts."

# Paying Wedding Vendors Overseas in Another Currency

[IMAGE: paying-wedding-vendors-overseas-couple-planner-terrace-documents-01.webp]

The venue has accepted your date, the photographer has sent a contract, and every invoice is in euros. Paying wedding vendors overseas means sending large sums, months apart, to people you may have met only on a video call, in a currency you do not earn. It is also where a few steady habits remove the most worry.

Official guidance is linked where we rely on it. Sources current as at November 2026.

This is general information only and does not take into account your objectives, financial situation or needs. Check fees and terms with your bank or provider, or speak to a licensed financial adviser.

## What changes when you pay a wedding vendor overseas

At home, a deposit moves between two Australian accounts under Australian rules. Abroad, four things change at once: the currency, the cost of moving the money, the time it takes to arrive, and the law your contract sits under.

Moneysmart, ASIC's money guidance site, says to [look for a good exchange rate and check the fees when sending money overseas](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/sending-money-overseas). Providers choose the exchange rate they use and also charge fees, and the costs depend on how much you send, where you send it and how you send it.

Our guide to [the costs a headline quote can leave out](/destination-weddings/hidden-costs-destination-wedding) gives the wedding version. Before transferring a deposit, review the payment schedule, cancellation policy, refund conditions and accepted currencies. If paying internationally, compare the costs of bank transfers and card payments rather than assuming the quoted amount is the amount that will leave your account.

### What the taxes on an invoice mean

Invoices abroad can carry charges and notations you will not see at home. Three examples from our cost guides, as each words them:

- Our [guide to a small wedding in Italy](/destination-weddings/small-wedding-in-italy-cost) lists 22 percent VAT among the costs to plan for: 22 percent on most services and 10 percent on hotel stays and restaurant meals.
- Our [Lake Como cost guide](/expert-advice/how-much-does-a-lake-como-wedding-cost) notes that guest rooms, flights, legal documents and welcome or farewell events are usually extra, and suggests asking for every offer in the same format, with guest count, hours and VAT on each line.
- Our [Phuket cost guide](/real-weddings/wedding-in-thailand-cost) notes that resort quotations may use the notation '++', with a common hotel calculation applying a 10% service charge followed by 7% VAT. Where both apply in sequence, it puts the combined increase at 17.7%.

Each is one page's example, not a rule for your wedding. For every quote, ask whether tax and service are in the figure or added on top, and have the answer in writing.

## How deposits, instalments and final balances usually run abroad

A vendor contract abroad sets its own payment schedule, and no two need match. Expect a deposit to hold your date, possibly one or more interim payments, and a final balance due by a date the contract names. Your venue, planner and photographer may each run on a different timetable, in a different currency.

Terms can be firm. Our hidden-costs guide notes that Le Ciel in Santorini requires a 50 percent deposit of €2,100, with the remaining venue fee due 365 days before the wedding, and that payments are non-refundable and by bank transfer only. That is one venue's terms, not a norm, but it shows the three things to find in every contract: how much, by when, and by what method.

Keep any refundable bond, such as a security deposit against damage, on its own line.

The ACCC's guidance on [paying for services you have not received](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/not-receiving-products-or-services-that-are-paid-for) says that under the Australian Consumer Law, businesses must not take payment for something they don't intend to supply, or know, or should know, they can't supply on time, and that where a third party prevents supply, a refund generally depends on the terms of the agreement. Its page on [refunds and cancelling a service](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel) adds that the consumer guarantees give no refund if you simply changed your mind.

That guidance describes the Australian Consumer Law, which applies to Australian businesses. With a vendor abroad, treat the contract as the document that governs refunds, and read its cancellation and postponement terms before the deposit leaves your account. If a contract is in Italian or another language, ask for an English version or have it translated before you sign. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

## Which currency to pay in, and who carries the exchange rate risk

Between deposit and balance, which can be many months apart (one venue in our hidden-costs guide sets the balance 365 days before the wedding), the rate can move either way. With an invoice in euros, the euro figure is fixed and the Australian dollar cost is not, so that movement sits with you.

Picture a venue balance in euros, due a year after your deposit. The invoice figure will not change; its cost in Australian dollars depends on the rate on the day you pay, which nobody can tell you when you sign. That is why a currency buffer belongs in your budget as its own line, apart from contingency.

A quote in Australian dollars does not remove the conversion: someone still converts, and the rate they use is usually built into the price. Ask which rate an AUD quote uses and whether it is fixed until you pay.

If an advisor books on your behalf, our guide to [what a travel advisor adds to a wedding abroad](/travel-guides/travel-advisor-benefits) covers the payment questions to put to them.

## What to compare between a bank transfer, a card and a transfer service

Moneysmart says it's a good idea to [shop around before you send money overseas](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/sending-money-overseas), because different companies can charge very different prices. Its list of features to compare includes:

- **Exchange rate:** how much your money is worth in another country, and whether the advertised rate is the rate you get.
- **Exchange rate guarantee:** whether the rate stays the same or can change.
- **Transfer fees and commission:** whether the conversion fee is a flat rate, a percentage or both; whether the receiver pays any fees; and the 'margin' fee to convert your currency.
- **Transfer time:** it could be instant, take a few minutes, or take up to five business days.

Ask each vendor whether their bank charges them to receive a payment and who covers it, so the amount that arrives matches the amount due. When a deposit holds a date, send early enough that it lands before the deadline in the vendor's time zone, and keep the confirmation.

Money transfer services in Australia must be registered with AUSTRAC (the Australian Transaction Reports and Analysis Centre), so check that a transfer business is registered before you use it.

Cards work differently. An [international transaction fee](https://moneysmart.gov.au/glossary/international-transaction-fee) may be charged by credit card providers on purchases from overseas-based merchants, or from Australian-based merchants who process payments overseas, even if the transaction is in Australian dollars. Protections such as a chargeback, where a disputed card payment is reversed, depend on your card issuer, so read its terms before you pay a deposit by card.

## When a vendor asks for the balance in cash

If a vendor asks for part of the balance in cash, treat it like any other payment term and settle the detail well before you fly.

- **Put it in the contract:** the amount, the currency, the date and the person who will receive it.
- **Ask for a written receipt** for every cash payment, showing the vendor's name, the date and the amount.
- **Speak to your bank before you travel** about how you will access the funds abroad, and plan where the cash is kept until it is handed over.
- **Choose one trusted person**, a planner or a family member, to hand it over on the day, so neither of you is counting notes in your wedding clothes.

AUSTRAC says that when carrying money in and out of Australia, you must [declare cash worth A$10,000 or more or foreign currency equivalent](https://www.austrac.gov.au/general-public/moving-money-overseas), with Australian and foreign currency counted together. Money transferred through a bank or money transfer business does not need declaring this way, and sharing cash among travellers to avoid declaring it is against the law.

Keep those receipts with your contracts and policy documents, in case you ever need to make a claim. Tips, if you give them, are simplest in local currency, in named envelopes handed over by that same person.

## How to protect every payment from invoice scams

Wedding payments are large, and they often arrive as emailed invoices from businesses you have never visited. In an April 2024 warning about [fake invoices from scammers impersonating businesses](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/beware-of-fake-invoices-from-scammers-impersonating-businesses), the ACCC urged Australians to check payment details directly with a business before paying an emailed invoice. It said Scamwatch had received reports that travel companies and their customers had been targeted.

Scammers get into a business's email account, or change the email address, and alter the payment details on the invoice; reply to query the change and the scammer will justify it. Invoices can look genuine because logos and ABNs are easy to copy.

The safeguard is to call the business on a number you have found yourself and confirm the payment details. For a wedding abroad, make that a routine:

1. Before the first transfer, call the vendor on a number from their website or your signed contract, never the invoice email, and read back the account name and number.
2. Treat any change to bank details as a stop sign until you have confirmed it the same way.
3. Do not let a deadline rush you; a day spent confirming costs less than a lost deposit.
4. Note the date of each call and who confirmed the details.

[IMAGE: destination-wedding-couple-phone-call-checking-bank-details-01.webp]

If something feels wrong, act quickly: if you have shared financial information or transferred money, contact your bank immediately and report it to Scamwatch. Moneysmart warns that if you send money to someone who turns out to be a scammer, it's almost impossible to get your money back. For a problem with a genuine transfer, contact the bank or transfer business first; if you are not happy with their response and they are a member of the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA), you can complain to AFCA.

## Matching each payment to its contract, cancellation terms and insurance

Every payment should trace back to a signed contract, a cancellation date and a line in your records.

**Who signs and who pays.** If a parent is paying for the welcome dinner or the band, agree whose name goes on that contract and who the vendor deals with on changes. Put it in writing, so the payer, the signer and the vendor all know.

**Cancellation dates beside payment dates.** For each vendor, list every payment next to the date it stops being refundable under the contract. A deposit that is non-refundable from the day it is paid carries more risk than a balance with a later cut-off.

**Records.** Keep the contract, each invoice, each transfer confirmation, your note of each phone check and every cash receipt in one place. [A vendor list with contact and payment fields](/planning/wedding-vendor-list-template) holds the details for each supplier, and [a spreadsheet that tracks each deposit and balance](/expert-advice/wedding-expenses-spreadsheet) shows what is still due.

**Insurance.** Smartraveller says [the right travel insurance is as essential as a passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) for overseas travel, for every traveller. Once the first deposit is paid, look at what wedding cover would protect it and what it would not. Our guide to [how wedding cover works for a celebration overseas](/destination-weddings/wedding-insurance) explains the difference.

## Paying wedding vendors overseas, your questions answered

### How do you pay wedding vendors in another country?

Follow each contract's payment schedule and method. Before each payment, compare the exchange rate, fees and transfer time, confirm the vendor's bank details by phone on a number you found yourself, and keep proof of every payment with the contract.

### Should I pay my overseas wedding vendors in AUD or their currency?

With an invoice in the vendor's currency, the amount in that currency is fixed and your Australian dollar cost moves with the rate until you pay. A quote in Australian dollars still involves a conversion, so ask which rate it uses and whether it is fixed.

### How much deposit do overseas wedding vendors ask for?

It varies, and each contract sets its own amount and dates. Before you sign, find the deposit, any interim payments, the balance date, the payment method and the point at which each payment stops being refundable.

### Is it safe to pay a wedding vendor overseas by bank transfer?

It can be, if you confirm the details first. The ACCC urges Australians to check payment details directly with a business before paying an emailed invoice, by calling a number you have found yourself.

### What if my vendor abroad wants to be paid in cash?

Treat it like any other payment term, with the amount, currency, date and recipient written into the contract. Ask for a written receipt for every cash payment, have one trusted person hand it over, and keep the receipts with your contracts.
