## Article body

# Elopement Ceremony Script for Two

[IMAGE: elopement-ceremony-coastal-headland-celebrant-witnesses-01.webp]

An elopement ceremony script for two has to do more with fewer words than a script for a full room. There is no processional to fill and no front row to play to: only the two of you, the people the law needs there, and the view. In Australia, a few of those sentences are set by law. Abroad, a symbolic ceremony can be entirely your own.

This guide sets out the legal minimum for eloping in Australia, a full template script with the legal words marked, and what changes when you elope overseas.

Sources current as at November 2026.

## What an elopement ceremony script needs

**A legal ceremony in Australia** needs an authorised celebrant, two adult witnesses, the celebrant's monitum, a vow line each of you says to the other and the signing of the certificates, and your celebrant confirms the wording before the day. **A symbolic ceremony abroad** is not a legal marriage, so none of those legal passages is required and the ceremony can say anything you like.

Either way, a good script for two has the same bones: a welcome, a few words about you, vows, rings, a pronouncement and a moment to take it in. For a legal ceremony, the signing closes it.

## The legal minimum for eloping in Australia

Eloping does not shorten the legal process. [The Attorney-General's Department says](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) that to marry in Australia you must, among other things, be at least 18, understand what marriage means and freely agree to it, use specific words during the ceremony, and be married by an authorised celebrant. You do not need to be an Australian citizen or permanent resident.

The checklist for a ceremony of two:

1. **Give notice in time.** You give a Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM, the form that starts the legal process) to an authorised celebrant [at least one month and no more than 18 months before](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) the ceremony. A spontaneous elopement in Australia still needs that month.
2. **Less than a month away?** Talk to your celebrant. The department lists reasons a shorter notice period may be approved, including travel commitments and wedding arrangements, but approval is not automatic.
3. **Sign the NOIM from anywhere.** The NOIM can be [witnessed by audio-visual link](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/publications/notice-intended-marriage) and completed overseas; the ceremony cannot.
4. **Meet your celebrant separately.** Your celebrant must meet each of you separately and in person before the ceremony.
5. **Have two adult witnesses.** [Section 44 of the Marriage Act 1961](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text) requires at least two witnesses who are, or appear to the celebrant to be, over 18.
6. **Be in the same place.** [Since 12 June 2024 your celebrant must be physically present](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) with you and your two witnesses. In practice, that means at least five people at a legal elopement, and no ceremony by video link.
7. **Sign.** On the day, you, your celebrant and your two witnesses sign [three marriage certificates](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married), and your celebrant lodges the paperwork with your state or territory registry of births, deaths and marriages within 14 days.

[IMAGE: elopement-signing-marriage-certificate-witnesses-01.webp]

The monitum, set out in [section 46(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text), is said by a celebrant who is not a minister of a recognised denomination, to you both, in the presence of your witnesses:

> "I am duly authorised by law to solemnise marriages according to law. Before you are joined in marriage in my presence and in the presence of these witnesses, I am to remind you of the solemn and binding nature of the relationship into which you are now about to enter. Marriage, according to law in Australia, is the union of 2 people to the exclusion of all others, voluntarily entered into for life."

or words to that effect. If you are marrying with a minister of religion, [section 45(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text) lets them use the form their religious body recognises, so the template below is written for a civil celebrant.

## A full elopement ceremony script for two

This elopement script is our own template, written for this guide. Replace everything in square brackets, cut what does not sound like you, and send the full draft to your celebrant early: they confirm the legal wording and set the final order.

**1. Welcome (celebrant)**

"[Name] and [Name], welcome. And thank you, [Witness 1] and [Witness 2], for standing here with them. They chose to marry with only the people this moment needs. That makes it a closer wedding, not a smaller one."

**2. Words about you (celebrant)**

"[Name] and [Name] met [where and how]. Since then they have [one or two specifics: a city you moved to, a habit you share, a hard year you came through together]. Today is about the life they already have, and the one they are choosing now."

**3. The monitum (celebrant, legal ceremony only)**

[Your celebrant says the monitum from section 46(1) to you both in front of your witnesses.]

**4. The legal vows (each of you, legal ceremony only)**

Under [section 45(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text), where the celebrant is not a minister of religion, each of you says to the other, in the presence of the celebrant and the witnesses:

> "I call upon the persons here present to witness that I, A.B. (or C.D.), take thee, C.D. (or A.B.), to be my lawful wedded wife (or husband, or spouse)"

or words to that effect (the Act uses A.B. and C.D. for your two names). Choose wife, husband or spouse.

**5. Your own elopement vows (each of you)**

"[Name], there is no audience here to perform for, so I will say it plainly. I promise to [something specific to your life together], to [a second specific], and to tell you the truth when it is hard to hear. I chose you before today. I will keep choosing you."

**6. Ring exchange (each of you)**

"[Name], this ring is for what I promised today. Wear it, and remember this place, and me."

**7. Pronouncement (celebrant)**

"[Name] and [Name], you have made your promises in front of your witnesses and in front of me. It is my pleasure to declare you married. You may kiss."

**8. A moment with the view (celebrant)**

"Before anyone moves, take a moment. Just the two of you, and all of this."

**9. Signing (legal ceremony only)**

[Your celebrant invites you and your two witnesses to sign the three marriage certificates, and signs them too.]

For a symbolic ceremony, leave out steps 3, 4 and 9, or keep any line you love as a personal promise. If your legal marriage has not happened yet, change step 7 to "It is my pleasure to celebrate your marriage", because a symbolic ceremony does not make you legally married.

## Making it personal without making it long

With two people, every addition is felt. Choose one or two, not all of them, and place them where the script already pauses.

- **A reading, after the welcome.** One short passage read by your celebrant or a witness sets the tone, so pick one you would happily hear twice.
- **A ritual, after the rings.** A handfasting, a letter each of you wrote and seals for an anniversary, or a toast with something from home. Keep it to something you can do without props going astray in the wind.
- **A moment with the view, before the signing.** Without guests to greet, this pause is the part worth protecting.

Write your vows to a similar length and tone, and read them aloud before the day. For help with the words, [our guide to writing your own vows](/how-to-write-wedding-vows) walks through the method. If you later plan a celebration with family, [a fuller ceremony written with guests in mind](/expert-advice/simple-wedding-ceremony-script) may suit that day better. No guests is the point, but if family will feel the absence, tell them before rather than after and plan a dinner once you are home, where you could read your vows again.

## Eloping abroad with a symbolic ceremony

A symbolic ceremony is not a legal marriage, so the Australian legal words, the NOIM, the witness rule and the signing do not govern it. Your script can be any words you like, in any order, in any language.

The legal step happens somewhere else, in one of two ways:

- **Marry legally at home first.** Give your NOIM in time, hold a short legal ceremony in Australia with your celebrant and two witnesses, then fly out for the ceremony that feels like yours. The script above works for both: the full version at home, the symbolic version abroad.
- **Marry under the destination's law.** Where a country lets visitors marry legally, its own rules set the paperwork, any waiting period and the witnesses. That country's registry or embassy confirms the steps for your passports, and you confirm them before you book.

To compare options, see [where Australians can legally marry abroad](/expert-advice/weddings-abroad-where-can-you-legally-get-married). If Italy is on your list, our guide to [civil, Catholic and symbolic ceremonies in Italy](/destination-weddings/civil-ceremony-in-italy-vs-catholic-and-symbolic-rites) sets out each route.

## Setting and timing for a ceremony of two

The setting does more work when there are no guests to look at. A few practical points shape the script as much as the words do. For places and formats to choose from, see our [elopement ideas for an intimate wedding](/destination-weddings/elopement-ideas).

- **Light.** Golden hour is short and moves fast. Set the ceremony time around the light you want, keep the script tight enough to finish inside it, and agree a fallback spot if the weather turns. No one can promise the sky.
- **Sound.** Wind and waves swallow quiet voices, and with two people you will speak more softly than you think. If you are filming, ask your videographer about small lapel microphones for each of you.
- **Your photographer.** Walk them through the script beforehand so they know where the rings, the pause and the signing fall, and where they can stand without stepping into the view. If you would like your photographer to also be one of your witnesses, ask your celebrant before the day.
- **A celebrant who travels.** For a legal ceremony in Australia, your celebrant has to be there in person, so choose one who is happy to reach your spot.
- **The place itself.** Ask the venue or land manager whether a ceremony there needs a booking or permit, and how you will reach it in ceremony clothes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you elope with no witnesses in Australia?

No. [Section 44 of the Marriage Act 1961](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1961A00012/latest/text) says a marriage cannot be solemnised unless at least two people over 18 (or who appear to the celebrant to be over 18) are present as witnesses, and since 12 June 2024 they must be physically present with you and your celebrant.

### How long is an elopement ceremony?

Personal vows of 250 to 300 words take about two minutes each, as [our vows guide](/how-to-write-wedding-vows) explains; time the rest by reading it aloud.

### Can we write our own words?

Yes. At a legal ceremony in Australia with a civil celebrant, your own words sit alongside two passages the Marriage Act sets (or words to that effect): the celebrant's monitum and the vow line each of you says.

### Do we need a NOIM to elope?

To marry legally in Australia, yes. You give a Notice of Intended Marriage to an authorised celebrant [at least one month and no more than 18 months before](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) the ceremony, and you can complete it while overseas.

### Can we elope abroad and have it recognised in Australia?

Check before you book. [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) says you cannot register an overseas marriage in Australia, but in most cases it is recognised if it was valid under the law of the country where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law had it happened here. A symbolic ceremony is not a marriage, so this applies only to a legal ceremony. A legal marriage abroad follows the destination's law, so start with that country's registry or embassy and confirm the steps for your passports. [Our guide to overseas marriage certificates](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate) explains what happens to the paperwork afterwards.
