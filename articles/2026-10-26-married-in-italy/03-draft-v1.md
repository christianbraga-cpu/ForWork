key_takeaways:
  - "US documents for Italy count for only six months from the date of issue and the Atto Notorio for about 90 days, so order and apostille birth certificates four to six months out and swear the Atto Notorio last."
  - "The US does not issue a Nulla Osta certificate: each of you swears a Dichiarazione Giurata at the US post for your wedding region (USD 50 per notary seal), then has it legalized at a Prefettura in the same consular district with a EUR 16 revenue stamp."
  - "The Italian consulates in New York and Los Angeles ask for four Atto Notorio witnesses who are not related to you, while the US Embassy says two, so confirm the number with your consulate before you ask anyone."
  - "A marriage valid in Italy is automatically valid in the US: apostille the Italian certificate at the Prefettura before you fly home, then update Social Security first, followed by your passport and driver's license."
  - "Official stamps and consular fees are small, but town hall hire runs from EUR 500 to EUR 9,200 (about USD 576 to USD 10,595 at the Italian consular rate for October to December 2026), and extra nights in the consulate city are where budgets slip."

# How to Get Married in Italy From the USA

[IMAGE: hero]

You have found the villa, your guests are asking about flights, and someone now has to own the paperwork. For an American couple, getting married in Italy means documents from your home state, a sworn declaration at an Italian consulate before you fly, and a few appointments in Italy before the wedding. The order matters as much as the papers, because most US documents count for only six months.

This guide sets out that order, with official sources, fees in EUR and USD, and what to do at home. Sources were checked on 27 October 2026, but rules change and town halls vary, so an advisor at Wedded Wonderland Concierge confirms the current process for your own passports before you book.

## Can Americans legally get married in Italy?

Yes, and you do not need to live there: the US Embassy's requirements for Americans include no residency period. It also counts at home. The [US Embassy and Consulates in Italy](https://it.usembassy.gov/getting-married-in-italy/) say a marriage valid in Italy is automatically valid in the US, with the Italian certificate and an apostille as proof. An apostille is a Hague Convention certificate that lets a public document from one member country be accepted in another.

Italian marriage law is national, but each comune (municipality) may interpret it differently, and its registrar has the final word on your document list. For regions and venues, see [our Italy destination page](/destinations/europe/italy); for other passports, see [our legal guide for couples of every nationality](/expert-advice/getting-married-in-italy-the-complete-legal-guide-for-destination-couples).

## Legal in Italy or legal at home first?

There are two routes: marry legally in Italy, or marry legally in the US and hold a symbolic ceremony in Italy. With 60 to 90 guests and two careers, paperwork and days off usually decide it.

[IMAGE: town-hall]

| | Civil in Italy | Catholic in Italy | US license, symbolic in Italy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal where | Italy, and automatically the US | Italy (the priest registers it), and the US | The US |
| Paperwork | US documents, two sworn declarations and three offices in Italy (all explained below) | Church papers (baptismal and confirmation certificates); ask about civil ones | Your county's license |
| Days needed in Italy | Several, per the US Embassy | Ask the parish and comune | None for the legal part |
| Venue | Set by the comune | A church | Your choice |
| Language | Italian, with an interpreter if you need one | Ask your parish | Your choice |

A ceremony led by non-Catholic clergy needs a civil ceremony first, the Embassy says. On the US route, license windows vary, so check with your county clerk: a [New York State license](https://www.health.ny.gov/publications/4210/) is valid for 60 days from the day after issue, after a 24-hour wait, and a [Los Angeles County license](https://www.lavote.gov/home/county-clerk/marriage-licenses-ceremonies/apply-for-a-marriage-license/recording-your-marriage-certificate) for 90 days. For how the rites differ, see [how civil, Catholic and symbolic ceremonies compare](/destination-weddings/civil-ceremony-in-italy-vs-catholic-and-symbolic-rites).

## The US documents you need and where each comes from

Two rules shape everything: Italy accepts public documents issued outside the EU for only six months from issue, and every US document needs an apostille and an Italian translation. Bring valid US passports too.

[IMAGE: documents]

| Document | Issued by | Apostille from | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Certified long-form birth certificate, with both parents' names | Your birth state's vital records office (New York City: the Health Department, with a Letter of Exemplification) | That state's secretary of state | New York requires it; Los Angeles only if the town hall asks. Bring one. |
| Final divorce or annulment decree, or a former spouse's death certificate | The court or office that issued it | That state's secretary of state | New York asks for a professional translation with a Certificate of Accuracy, apostilled too |
| A federal document, if requested (such as a naturalization certificate copy) | The federal agency | [US Department of State](https://travel.state.gov/en/replace-certify/authenticate-documents/request-services.html), Form DS-4194, USD 20 | Mail takes about five weeks or more |

If a woman's previous marriage ended within 300 days, she needs a waiver from the Procura della Repubblica (public prosecutor) at the court in the wedding city. It is issued on medical evidence that she is not pregnant.

Nulla Osta means "nothing stands in the way": confirmation that you are free to marry. The US has no document or authority that certifies this, says the [State Department](https://fam.state.gov/fam/07fam/07fam1450.html), so Americans swear an affidavit called the Dichiarazione Giurata (sworn declaration) before a US consular officer in Italy. The Embassy's own forms call it the Nulla Osta, and Italian offices accept it as one.

## Step by step: the order that keeps every document valid

1. **12 to 15 months out: choose the route, comune and venue.** Ask the town hall's marriage office for its document list, whether it needs a birth certificate and the Atto Notorio (step 4), and how far ahead it books; popular towns have waiting lists, especially in May, June and September. For a private venue, read [whether a Tuscan villa can host the legal ceremony](/expert-advice/can-you-get-married-in-a-tuscan-villa).
2. **Six to four months out: order and apostille your documents.** Any earlier and they expire. A [New York City birth certificate](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01020) needs three stops (city Health Department, New York County Clerk, then the [New York State Department of State](https://dos.ny.gov/apostille-certificate-authentication)), so start at month six.
3. **Then translate.** The [Italian consulate in Los Angeles](https://conslosangeles.esteri.it/it/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-italiano/traduzione-e-legalizzazione-dei-documenti/) says the translator need not be official, only professional, and the consulate then certifies the translation. Ask your comune first.
4. **Within 90 days of the wedding: the Atto Notorio,** a declaration sworn with witnesses who know you that nothing in US law prevents the marriage. The Embassy advises doing it at an Italian consulate before you leave, as some Italian courts have long waiting lists. [New York](https://consnewyork.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-italiano/servizi-notarili/atto-notorio-for-marriages-to-be-celebrated-in-italy/atto-notorio-for-marriages-in-italy-us-citizens/) requires it within three months of the wedding and [San Francisco](https://conssanfrancisco.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-straniero/foreign-non-eu-citizens-marrying-in-italy/) treats it as valid for 90 days, so it is your last US step.
    - **Which consulate:** the [New York consulate](https://consnewyork.esteri.it/en/chi-siamo/la-rete-consolare/) serves New York State, Connecticut, 13 New Jersey counties and Bermuda. Southern California generally uses [Los Angeles](https://conslosangeles.esteri.it/it/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-straniero/us-citizens-wishing-to-marry-in-italy/) and Northern California San Francisco; check your consulate's jurisdiction page.
    - **Witnesses:** the Embassy says two, but New York and Los Angeles ask for four, and San Francisco two per partner, all over 18, with valid ID and unrelated to you. Plan on four unless your consulate says otherwise.
    - **Booking:** New York takes requests at newyork.notarile@esteri.it, books through its Prenot@mi online system and accepts money orders only. San Francisco advises bringing your translated birth certificate so the consulate can certify the translation.
    - **Fallback:** a court in Italy, with an interpreter and proof you are in Italy (a plane ticket works). Allow four to 10 days, or pay more for same-day.
5. **Once your travel dates are set: book the Dichiarazione Giurata** online with the US post for your region. Fill in the form in advance but sign only before the consular officer, and book your town hall interpreter at the same time.

Your guests never see this part. It shapes the days before the welcome dinner, including one in the city of your US post and Prefettura (the provincial office of Italy's Ministry of the Interior).

Pairings follow the State Department's [list of US consular districts in Italy](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal-considerations/judicial/country/italy.html) (last updated in 2013) and the Embassy's [Milan](https://it.usembassy.gov/getting-married-in-the-consular-district-of-milan/), [Florence](https://it.usembassy.gov/getting-married-in-the-consular-district-of-florence/), [Naples](https://it.usembassy.gov/getting-married-in-the-consular-district-of-naples/) and [Rome](https://it.usembassy.gov/getting-married-in-the-consular-district-of-rome/) pages; a Wedded Wonderland advisor confirms your post. The Naples page says Amalfi Coast couples generally do not need the Atto Notorio; check with your town hall.

1. **Dichiarazione Giurata at the US post:** [USD 50 per notary seal](https://it.usembassy.gov/services/notarials/), paid on the day (by card online in Rome, at the cashier in Milan, Florence and Naples). Each of you swears your own, so expect USD 50 each (confirm with your post).
2. **Legalization at the Prefettura:** a EUR 16 revenue stamp (marca da bollo) per document, bought at a tabacchi (licensed tobacconist), during limited weekday-morning hours.
3. **Declaration of Intention to Marry** at the comune's marriage office (Ufficio Matrimoni), before the civil registrar, with an interpreter if needed. The date is then formally fixed, and the banns (the public notice of a marriage) are waived or posted for one day to a week.
4. **The ceremony,** led by the mayor or a deputy, with two witnesses (any nationality, over 18, photo ID) and an interpreter if needed, who cannot also be a witness.

As an illustration, never a promise: arrive Monday, consulate and Prefettura Tuesday, comune Wednesday, welcome dinner Friday, wedding Saturday, if your comune's banns period allows. You attend the Dichiarazione, the Declaration of Intention and the ceremony in person; a local planner, if you use one, books the appointments, interpreter and translations and fits the consulate trip around guest arrivals. For a parent helping to pay: the fees are small, paid in USD at the US post and in EUR stamps, and the apostilled Italian certificate proves the marriage at home.

## After the wedding: making it count in the US

[IMAGE: home-again]

Before you leave Italy, get a copy of your marriage certificate from the town hall and have it apostilled at the Prefettura covering the place you married. Your advisor will confirm whether a multilingual extract (estratto plurilingue) is also worth requesting; no US source we found says it replaces an English translation. For the certificate itself, see [how to get and use a marriage certificate from abroad](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate).

At home there is nothing to register: no US authority records a marriage abroad, and one valid in Italy is valid in the US. Validity questions go to your state's attorney general, per the [Foreign Affairs Manual](https://fam.state.gov/fam/07fam/07fam1450.html). Then the admin:

- **Social Security first.** [Request a replacement card](https://www.ssa.gov/life-events/change-name), often online, with an original or certified copy of the certificate. There is no fee, and a foreign-language document needs a certified translation.
- **Then your passport.** Within a year of issue, [Form DS-5504](https://travel.state.gov/en/passports/renew-replace/change-correct-passport.html) has no fee (USD 60 expedited); after that, renew on Form DS-82 if eligible or apply in person. A certified English translation is required.
- **Then your driver's license,** under your state DMV's rules.
- **Taxes:** filing status follows your marital status on the last day of the year, so the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/filing/filing-status) generally treats you as married for that whole year.
- **Health insurance:** marriage can open a 60-day [Special Enrollment Period](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/special-enrollment-period/). Check your employer plan's window too.
- **If one of you is not a US citizen,** see an immigration attorney; USCIS is the official source.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do we need to live in Italy to get married there?

No. The US Embassy's requirements for Americans include no residency period, and banns are waived or shortened for non-residents.

### How many days before the wedding do we need to be in Italy?

No official source sets a number. Procedures can take several days, per the US Embassy, and banns for non-residents are waived or last up to a week, so ask your comune and plan around its answer.

### Can our parents or siblings be our witnesses?

At the ceremony, the US Embassy's rules (two adults with photo ID, any nationality) do not exclude relatives, but confirm with your comune. For the Atto Notorio, no: those witnesses cannot be family, future family or in-laws.

### Is a marriage in Italy legally recognized in the US?

Yes. A marriage valid in Italy is automatically valid in the US, and the Italian certificate with an apostille from the Prefettura is your proof. There is no US registration step.

### Can a same-sex couple from the US marry in Italy?

Italian law offers same-sex couples civil unions, not marriage, under Law 76/2016. A Wedded Wonderland advisor confirms how this applies to two foreign nationals, and [our guide for same-sex couples](/destination-weddings/is-gay-marriage-legal-in-italy) sets out the options.
