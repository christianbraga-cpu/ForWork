# Destination Wedding Packing List for Couples and Guests

[IMAGE: hero]

A destination wedding packing list is really two lists. One is everything you could buy, borrow or press again at the other end. The other is short and cannot be replaced: the passports, the rings, the wedding outfits, your medicines and the paperwork that makes the marriage count. Get the second list right and the first one barely matters.

This list is split by who packs what. There is the bag that never leaves you, the couple's list for the whole wedding week, the wedding-day kit, and a guest list you can send to everyone flying in. The Australian rules on medicines, batteries, duty free and cash come at the end, each linked to the official source at the point it applies. Print it, share it, tick it off. Sources current as at November 2026.

## How to use this destination wedding packing list

The short answer: four lists, each with one owner. Documents and carry-on (both of you), the couple's wedding week, the wedding-day kit (one trusted person), and the guests' list (shared ahead). Then a set of rules for medicines, batteries and bringing things home, which apply to everyone in the party, including parents.

Start about eight weeks out. Smartraveller suggests [a basic health check at least eight weeks before you go](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist), and says to [allow at least six weeks for a new passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/passport-services), so the documents list needs that lead time even if the suitcase does not. Run this list alongside your [month-by-month destination wedding checklist](/expert-advice/destination-wedding-checklist), which sits within our [full wedding planning checklist](/expert-advice/wedding-planning-checklist).

This page is the list. For the styling side, from fabrics that survive a long-haul flight to shoes for each venue, read our guide on [what to wear and how to style it](/how-to-pack-for-a-destination-wedding).

## Documents and carry-on, the bag that never leaves you

Anything that would stop the wedding if it went missing stays in your hands from your front door to the venue. Pack this bag first.

[IMAGE: documents-carry-on]

**The carry-on checklist**

- [ ] Passports for both of you, checked against the rules below
- [ ] Visas or entry approvals for every country you visit or transit through
- [ ] Travel insurance policy number and the insurer's emergency phone line
- [ ] Copies of passports and key documents, one set packed separately and one stored digitally
- [ ] Marriage paperwork: originals, translations and any certificates your celebrant or registry needs
- [ ] Printed run sheet, vendor names and phone numbers, and hard copies of every booking
- [ ] Rings, in a small case you can keep on your person
- [ ] Wedding outfits, in garment bags, with shoes and accessories
- [ ] Prescription medicines and your doctor's letter (see the rules below)
- [ ] Phone, chargers, travel adaptors and any power banks

**Passports.** [Smartraveller's essentials checklist](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) says to make sure your passport is valid for at least six months beyond the end of your trip. Smartraveller also notes that [some countries will not let you enter](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/passport-services) unless your passport is valid for six months after you plan to leave, that some airlines apply this rule themselves, and that it can apply even when transiting or stopping over. Getting it wrong can mean being stranded. Smartraveller also says to get the right visa for each country, and because visa conditions often change, to check with the embassy or consulate of each destination before every trip.

If one of you plans to change your family name, you can choose to get a new passport or keep using your current one, and the Australian Passport Office recommends [booking travel in the name that is in your passport](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender). In practice, book the honeymoon flights in the name on the passport you will actually carry. Dual nationals in your party should [depart and enter Australia on their Australian passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/passport-services).

**Copies.** Smartraveller advises that you [take copies of your passport ID page, visa and key documents](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist), leave one with someone you trust, keep hard copies of online flight, accommodation and event bookings, and share your itinerary with friends or family. A parent at home with a full set is a quiet safety net.

**Marriage paperwork.** Some documents travel with you and some are issued after the ceremony. Read [which marriage documents travel with you and which follow later](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate) before you pack the folder, and confirm what your destination requires for your own passports with its registry or the relevant embassy or consulate.

**Insurance and valuables.** Smartraveller says [the right travel insurance is as essential as a passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance). Policies often limit each valuable item's value, so check the item limit and the excess before the rings and cameras leave home. Free credit card insurance often covers fewer situations, with lower item limits, and usually has to be activated. For the rings themselves, read our advice on [keeping your rings safe while you travel](/all-the-practical-ways-to-keep-your-engagement-ring-safe-while-traveling).

**The dress and the suit.** Keep them with you wherever you can. Airlines differ on garment bags and cabin space, so check with your airline before you fly and ask what they can do for a garment bag on board.

## Medicines, batteries and what you cannot bring home

These are the rules that apply to everyone leaving and re-entering Australia. Each is worded as the official source words it, with the source linked.

**Medicines.** Smartraveller says to [check your medication is legal and available](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) where you are going, take enough prescribed medicine to last the whole trip, and carry copies of your prescription and a letter from your doctor.

[The TGA's guidance on leaving Australia with medicines](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/consumer-information-and-resources/travelling-medicines-and-medical-devices/leaving-australia) says it is a good idea to carry a letter from your doctor naming each prescription medicine, how much you are taking, and that it is for your personal use only, and to ask your doctor to include complementary medicines. Carry the letter with your prescriptions. Some medicines legally available in Australia, such as those containing codeine, may not be allowed in other countries or may require a permit, and restrictions may also apply to vitamins and traditional medicines. If in doubt, contact the destination's embassy or consulate; the TGA cannot advise on each country's rules.

- [ ] Doctor's letter, including complementary medicines
- [ ] Copies of prescriptions
- [ ] Enough medicine for the whole trip, [carried in your hand luggage](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/consumer-information-and-resources/travelling-medicines-and-medical-devices/leaving-australia) (the TGA notes medicines are exempt from the 100ml limit on liquids, aerosols and gels)

The TGA is also clear on [PBS medicines](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/consumer-information-and-resources/travelling-medicines-and-medical-devices/leaving-australia): you cannot take them overseas unless they are for you or an immediate family member travelling with you. Taking PBS medicines overseas for someone else is illegal, with penalties of up to AUD 11,000, two years' imprisonment, or both. A parent should not carry an aunt's tablets, however kindly meant.

**Batteries.** [CASA's lithium battery rules](https://www.casa.gov.au/packright/lithium-batteries) say to always take spare batteries and power banks with you in the cabin and never place them in checked luggage. Devices with batteries can go in checked or carry-on baggage if they are switched off completely (not on sleep or hibernation) and protected from damage and from being switched on. Protect spare battery terminals with the original packaging, tape over the terminals, or a separate bag or case for each battery. Batteries under 100Wh are common in phones, laptops and cameras; 100Wh to 160Wh need airline approval; over 160Wh are prohibited in passenger baggage, except mobility aids. Airlines may limit how many spares you carry, so check with your airline before flying.

**Duty free coming home.** If you are 18 or over, the Australian Border Force says you can bring up to [AUD900 worth of general goods into Australia duty free](https://www.abf.gov.au/entering-and-leaving-australia/duty-free), or AUD450 if you are under 18. General goods include gifts, souvenirs, cameras, electronic equipment, leather goods, perfume concentrates, jewellery, watches and sporting equipment, so wedding gifts and jewellery bought overseas count. Personal items such as clothing, footwear and grooming articles can come back in your accompanied baggage free of duty and tax, but limits apply to goods purchased overseas.

Families on the same flight can pool their limits if they stay together through Customs clearance, and the ABF counts a married couple or de facto partners (including same-sex couples) as a family. If you go over, duty and tax apply to all items of that type, not just the excess. Declare excess goods and keep proof of purchase; failure to declare can result in penalties, prosecution or visa cancellation. Duty free liquids also follow the powders, liquids, aerosols and gels restrictions: the ABF recommends checked baggage where possible, and items in carry-on that do not meet the requirements must be surrendered at screening.

**Cash.** There is no limit on how much cash you can bring in or take out of Australia, but you must [declare amounts of AUD 10,000 or more](https://www.abf.gov.au/entering-and-leaving-australia/can-you-bring-it-in/categories/misc) in Australian currency or the foreign equivalent. Failure to report is an offence, and prosecution and financial penalties could apply. If vendor balances or tips are being paid in cash, count the total across your party before you fly.

**Flowers, favours and food.** Check Australia's biosecurity rules before bringing back bouquets, favours or food, and decide what comes home before the wedding rather than at the airport.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I carry my wedding dress on the plane or check it in?

Keep it with you wherever you can. It is the one item that cannot be replaced at the other end. How a garment bag travels varies by airline, so check with yours before you fly and ask what they can do for it in the cabin. Check your travel insurance too, since Smartraveller notes policies often limit each valuable item's value.

### Can I bring my wedding bouquet or flowers back into Australia?

Check Australia's biosecurity rules before bringing back bouquets, favours or food, and do it before the wedding rather than at the airport. If a keepsake matters to you, plan it with your florist and photographer ahead of time, and decide what stays behind.

### What should guests pack for a destination wedding?

An outfit for each event in its dress code, shoes that suit the ground at each venue, sun protection, a light layer for the evening, and their passport, insurance details and medicines in their carry-on. Older guests should add flat shoes with grip and a doctor's letter for any prescriptions.

### Can I take my prescription medication overseas for the wedding?

Yes, with care. Smartraveller says to check your medicine is legal and available where you are going, take enough for the whole trip, and carry copies of your prescription and a letter from your doctor. The TGA notes some medicines, such as those containing codeine, may not be allowed in other countries or may need a permit, and suggests contacting the destination's embassy or consulate if in doubt.

### What goes in a wedding day emergency kit?

A sewing kit, fashion tape, stain remover, blotting papers, pain relief, plasters, snacks and water, a charged phone and charger, the vow cards, and envelopes for any tips agreed in advance. Give it to one named person, so it is never left in a room upstairs.
