# Destination Wedding Packing List for Couples and Guests

[IMAGE: hero]

A destination wedding packing list is really two lists. One is everything you could buy, borrow or press again at the other end. The other is short and cannot be replaced: the passports, the rings, the wedding outfits, your medicines and the paperwork that makes the marriage count. Get the second list right and the first one barely matters.

Print it, share it, tick it off. Sources current as at November 2026.

## How to use this destination wedding packing list

Four lists, each with one owner: documents and carry-on, the couple's wedding week, the wedding-day kit and the guests' list. Then come the rules on medicines, batteries and bringing things home, which apply to everyone, parents included.

Start about eight weeks out. Smartraveller suggests [a basic health check at least eight weeks before you go](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist), and says to [allow at least six weeks for a new passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/passport-services), so start the documents first. Run this list alongside your [month-by-month destination wedding checklist](/expert-advice/destination-wedding-checklist), part of our [full wedding planning checklist](/expert-advice/wedding-planning-checklist).

For the styling side, read our guide on [what to wear and how to style it](/how-to-pack-for-a-destination-wedding).

## Documents and carry-on, the bag that never leaves you

Anything that would stop the wedding if it went missing stays in your hands from your front door to the venue. Pack this bag first.

[IMAGE: documents-carry-on]

**The carry-on checklist**

- [ ] Passports for both of you, checked against the rules below
- [ ] Visas or entry approvals for every country you visit or transit through
- [ ] Travel insurance policy number and the insurer's emergency phone line
- [ ] Copies of passports and key documents, packed separately
- [ ] Marriage paperwork: originals, translations and any certificates your celebrant or registry needs
- [ ] Printed run sheet, vendor contacts and hard copies of every booking
- [ ] Rings, in a small case you can keep on your person
- [ ] Wedding outfits, in garment bags, with shoes and accessories
- [ ] Prescription medicines and your doctor's letter (see the rules below)
- [ ] Phone, chargers, travel adaptors and any power banks

**Passports.** [Smartraveller's essentials checklist](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) says to make sure your passport is valid for at least six months beyond the end of your trip. [Some countries will not let you enter](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/passport-services) unless it is valid for six months after you plan to leave, some airlines apply this rule themselves, and it can apply even when transiting or stopping over. Then [get the right visa for each country](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) you visit or transit through, and because visa conditions often change, check with each destination's embassy or consulate before every trip.

If one of you plans to change your family name, you can get a new passport or keep using your current one, and the Australian Passport Office recommends [booking travel in the name that is in your passport](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender).

**Copies.** Smartraveller advises that you [take copies of your passport ID page, visa and key documents](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist), leave one with someone you trust, keep hard copies of online flight, accommodation and event bookings, and share your itinerary with friends or family.

**Marriage paperwork.** Read [which marriage documents travel with you and which follow later](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate) before you pack the folder, and confirm what your destination requires for your own passports with its registry or the relevant embassy or consulate.

**Insurance and valuables.** Smartraveller says [the right travel insurance is as essential as a passport](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance). Policies often limit each valuable item's value, so check the item limit and the excess before the rings and cameras leave home. For the rings themselves, read our advice on [keeping your rings safe while you travel](/all-the-practical-ways-to-keep-your-engagement-ring-safe-while-traveling).

**The dress and the suit.** Keep them with you wherever you can. Airlines differ on garment bags and cabin space, so check with your airline before you fly. For packing, cabin and steaming tips, read our guide to [travelling with your wedding dress](/destination-weddings/how-to-travel-with-wedding-dress).

## Medicines, batteries and what you cannot bring home

These rules apply to everyone leaving and re-entering Australia.

**Medicines.** Smartraveller says to [check your medication is legal and available](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) where you are going. [The TGA's guidance on leaving Australia with medicines](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/consumer-information-and-resources/travelling-medicines-and-medical-devices/leaving-australia) suggests a letter from your doctor naming each prescription medicine, how much you are taking and that it is for your personal use only. Where possible, carry the letter with your prescriptions. Some medicines legal in Australia, such as those containing codeine, may not be allowed in other countries or may need a permit, so if in doubt, contact the destination's embassy or consulate; the TGA cannot advise on each country's rules.

- [ ] Doctor's letter, including complementary medicines
- [ ] Copies of your prescriptions
- [ ] Enough medicine for the whole trip, [in your hand luggage](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/consumer-information-and-resources/travelling-medicines-and-medical-devices/leaving-australia) (the TGA notes medicines are exempt from the 100ml limit on liquids, aerosols and gels)

The TGA is also clear on [PBS medicines](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/consumer-information-and-resources/travelling-medicines-and-medical-devices/leaving-australia): you cannot take them overseas unless they are for you or an immediate family member travelling with you. Taking them overseas for someone else is illegal, with penalties of up to AUD 11,000 and/or two years' imprisonment. A parent should not carry an aunt's tablets, however kindly meant.

**Batteries.** [CASA's lithium battery rules](https://www.casa.gov.au/packright/lithium-batteries) say to always take spare batteries and power banks with you in the cabin and never place them in checked luggage. Airlines may limit how many spares you carry, so check with your airline before flying.

**Duty free coming home.** If you are 18 or over, the Australian Border Force says you can bring up to [AUD900 worth of general goods into Australia duty free](https://www.abf.gov.au/entering-and-leaving-australia/duty-free), or AUD450 if you are under 18. Gifts and jewellery bought overseas count towards it. Most personal items such as clothing, footwear and grooming articles can come back in your accompanied baggage free of duty and tax. Families on the same flight can [pool their limits](https://www.abf.gov.au/entering-and-leaving-australia/duty-free) if they stay together through Customs clearance, and the ABF counts a married couple or de facto partners (including same-sex couples) as a family.

**Cash.** There is no limit on how much cash you can bring in or take out of Australia, but you must [declare amounts of AUD 10,000 or more](https://www.abf.gov.au/entering-and-leaving-australia/can-you-bring-it-in/categories/misc) in Australian currency or the foreign equivalent. Failure to report is an offence. If vendor balances or tips are being paid in cash, work out the amounts before you fly so you know whether a declaration applies.

**Flowers, favours and food.** On arrival in Australia you must [declare risk goods, including food, animal products and plant material (including wooden articles)](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/travelling/to-australia), or put them in the bins at the airport. If a bouquet, favour or food gift is coming home, declare it on arrival, and decide what stays behind before the wedding rather than at the airport.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I carry my wedding dress on the plane or check it in?

Keep it with you wherever you can. It is one of the few things that cannot be replaced at the other end. Check garment bags with your airline, and since Smartraveller notes [policies often limit each valuable item's value](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance), check your item limit.

### Can I bring my wedding bouquet or flowers back into Australia?

Declare it. On arrival you must [declare risk goods, including food and plant material](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/travelling/to-australia), or dispose of them in the airport bins; declared goods are inspected and may be treated, exported or destroyed. If a keepsake matters, plan it with your florist and photographer before the wedding.

### What should guests pack for a destination wedding?

An outfit for each event in its dress code, shoes that suit the ground at each venue, sun protection and a light evening layer, with passport, insurance details and medicines in carry-on. Older guests should add flat shoes with grip and a doctor's letter.

### Can I take my prescription medication overseas for the wedding?

Yes. [Smartraveller says](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/essentials-checklist) to check your medicine is legal and available where you are going and to carry copies of your prescription and a doctor's letter. [The TGA notes](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/consumer-information-and-resources/travelling-medicines-and-medical-devices/leaving-australia) some medicines, such as those containing codeine, may need a permit or not be allowed.

### What goes in a wedding day emergency kit?

A sewing kit, fashion tape, stain remover, blotting papers, pain relief, plasters, snacks and water, a charged phone and charger, the vow cards, and envelopes for any tips agreed in advance. Give it to one named person, so it is never left upstairs.
