You have an island shortlist, a guest list taking shape and a month in mind. What you need now is the paperwork in the right order, because that decides when your guests can book their flights. This guide to getting married in Greece from the UK sets out each step for British passport holders: the document that shows you are free to marry, the FCDO apostille, Greek translations, the town hall, and what happens when you come home with a Greek marriage certificate.

Each legal rule links to its official source at the point it appears. Sources current as at November 2026. Greek town halls apply national rules in their own way, so confirm every requirement for your own passports with the municipality where you will marry and, on the UK side, with the FCDO through gov.uk.

## Can British couples marry legally in Greece

Yes. Getting married in Greece is open to couples who live in the UK, and the legal side runs through the Greek municipality (the town hall) where you marry. [gov.uk's guidance on marrying abroad](https://www.gov.uk/marriages-civil-partnerships-abroad) says whether you can marry abroad depends on local laws, so you need to check the local process and which documents prove you are free to marry.

Greek law requires a licence from the mayor before a civil wedding or a Greek Orthodox wedding, according to the [gov.gr civil licence entry](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_civil_marriage_licence). For couples living abroad, a 4 January 2019 [Interior Ministry circular](https://www.ypes.gr/UserFiles/f0ff9297-f516-40ff-a70e-eca84e2ec9b9/1969-04012019.pdf) indicates that a foreign document showing you are free to marry counts as that licence when it also names your partner, so no Greek licence needs to be issued. Municipalities such as [Chania](https://www.chania.gr/files/57/44617/civil_marriages_documents_for_foreigners_of_abroad_no5_%28en%29.pdf) and [Heraklion](https://www.heraklion.gr/en/citizen/civil-marriages/marriage-foreigners.html) publish document lists for foreigners who do not live in Greece.

On the UK side, that document comes from the FCDO. Which document Greece needs from you, and what it costs, appears only inside the gov.uk online application once you start it, so check the gov.uk service for your own case before you plan around any one route.

If you are still choosing between countries, our guide to [how British citizens marry in Italy](/expert-advice/how-do-british-citizens-get-married-in-italy) sets out the Italian route step by step.

## Residency and eligibility for British passport holders

Greece has a route for residents and a route for couples who live abroad. The [gov.gr foreign-resident licence entry](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_marriage_licence_for_foreign_residents_in_Greece) covers foreign nationals legally residing in Greece, whose licence comes from their municipality of residence. The [Municipality of Athens](https://www.cityofathens.gr/en/service/issuance-of-civil-marriage-license/) serves permanent residents of Athens and Greek citizens registered there. Neither is the route for a couple living in the UK.

Your route is the one for non-residents. Chania's list is for foreigners living abroad and Heraklion's for foreigners "not residing permanently in Greece". Both start from a document issued by your own country, not from Greek residence.

Every foreign couple does need proof of being lawfully in Greece. The 2019 circular calls an official document such as a passport, visa or residence permit an essential precondition. For British citizens, the [FCDO's Greece entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/greece/entry-requirements) say your passport must have been issued less than 10 years before the day you arrive and must expire at least three months after the day you plan to leave the Schengen area. You can visit without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180 days. Heraklion's list asks for "a residence permit or VISA (whose validity period has not expired)", so ask Heraklion how that applies to a visa-free visit.

The gov.uk online service for marriage documents needs a valid British passport. If one of you is not British, ask your municipality which document it wants from that partner's own country. If your partner is a Greek citizen registered with a Greek municipality, the [Greek Embassy in London](https://www.mfa.gr/uk/en/services/citizen-services/registration-birth-marriage-divorce-death/issuance-of-marriage-license/) runs a marriage licence application at its Consular Office. You both attend, each completing an application, an authorisation and a Solemn Declaration of No Impediment to Marriage, and the papers go to the competent municipality. The Embassy lists EUR 10 for the declaration and EUR 10 for a copy, or the sterling equivalent.

## Prefer to marry in the UK? Marry at home, celebrate in Greece

If the Greek paperwork feels like one job too many, there is a simpler order. [gov.uk's guidance on marriages registered abroad](https://www.gov.uk/marriages-civil-partnerships-abroad/register-your-marriage-or-civil-partnership-abroad) says that if you want a UK marriage certificate, you can choose a legal ceremony in the UK and a commitment ceremony abroad.

On this route, your legal paperwork is UK paperwork. The free-to-marry document, apostilles, translations and town-hall steps in the rest of this guide belong to the legal route in Greece. Ask the register office in the nation where you will marry (England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland) for its notice period and the documents it needs. ## Documents for getting married in Greece from the UK

Lists differ between municipalities, so confirm the final list with your municipality in Greece before you order anything. The table below draws on the official lists for couples living abroad and the UK services that issue each document.

| Document | Issued by | Apostille | Greek translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proof you are free to marry (a certificate of no impediment, or the equivalent Greece asks for) | FCDO, through the gov.uk online service. Consular fee GBP 50 per document (FCDO fee schedule, 9 April 2025) | Ask your municipality | Yes |
| Birth certificate | The UK register that holds your birth record [[NEEDS: GRO, NRS and GRO NI certificate ordering pages]] | Paper apostille from the FCDO Legalisation Office, GBP 45 per document (November 2026) | Yes |
| Proof a previous marriage ended, if either of you was married before | The court or register that issued it | Ask your municipality | Yes |
| Passport | HM Passport Office | Ask your municipality | Ask your municipality |

On proof of freedom to marry, [gov.uk's page on documents for marrying abroad](https://www.gov.uk/marriages-civil-partnerships-abroad/get-documents-for-a-marriage-or-civil-partnership-abroad) says some countries ask for a certificate of no impediment or a marital status affirmation. Its online service lets you check which documents you might need, then apply and pay. An application cannot be saved and times out after 30 minutes of inactivity, so set aside time and have your passport to hand.

The FCDO's [consular fee](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/consular-services-fees) for a certificate of no impediment, or a local equivalent document, is GBP 50 per document. Which document Greece needs from you, and the total the service charges, show only inside the application, so check the gov.uk service for your own case. One detail matters more than the rest: Chania will not accept a home-country document that does not name the person you are marrying. Whether yours does is the first question for your municipality.

Dates matter too. The Municipality of Athens wants every document it receives to have been issued within the last six months, and a marital status certificate within the last three. That rule is for Athens residents, but it shows why certificates should not be ordered too early; Chania asks for a "recent" birth certificate and for originals, not photocopies.

## Apostilles from the FCDO and Greek translations

Greece accepts documents from Hague Convention countries such as the UK only with an apostille and "translated into Greek by a Greek translator", says the gov.gr foreign-resident licence entry. The Chania and Heraklion lists for non-residents also ask for apostilles.

In the UK, the [FCDO Legalisation Office](https://www.gov.uk/get-document-legalised) attaches the apostille to UK public documents, such as a birth certificate from a public registry or a document certified by a UK notary or solicitor. General Register Office certificates cannot get an e-Apostille; they need the paper one. The paper service costs GBP 45 per document plus courier or postage (courier return within the UK is GBP 6 per 1.5kg). It usually takes up to 25 working days plus delivery time, so build five working weeks into your plan.

Translation comes after the apostille. Since 1 September 2021, the [Greek Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.mfa.gr/en/announcement-regarding-the-simplification-of-procedure-for-the-official-translation-of-documents/) has used a simpler procedure under Law 4781/2021: you choose a translator from the Electronic Register of Certified Translators at metafraseis.services.gov.gr and contact them directly. Register translators officially translate foreign public documents into Greek, provided documents from Hague countries carry an apostille.

### Questions to ask the municipality

Write to the town hall where you plan to marry and keep every reply with your file; Chania, for example, accepts documents in person or by email. If you are marrying on Santorini (Thira) or Mykonos, send these questions to that municipality in writing.

- Will you accept the document from the gov.uk service in place of a Greek marriage licence, and must it name my partner?
- Which documents need an apostille and a Greek translation, which translators do you accept, and how recent must each document be?
- Do couples living abroad need a newspaper notice or a waiting period?
- What proof do you need if one of us was married before?
- How many witnesses do we need, and what ID must they bring?
- Which days, times and places can we book, and what is the fee?
- How many working days before the ceremony do you need our original documents?
- After the ceremony, how and by when is the marriage registered, how do we order copies of the certificate, and which Greek authority adds an apostille to it?

## The Greek marriage licence and your timeline

For couples who live in Greece, the licence follows a fixed path. The clock starts when the application is submitted: staff check every document and the newspaper notice, then issue a protocol number, and an incomplete application is returned so the procedure starts again. The mayor then waits one week for any objection from third parties and grants the licence if none is made. The civil licence entry lists eight days in all, and the licence is valid for six months.

For couples who live abroad, the circular and the Chania and Heraklion lists point another way. A document from home stands in for the Greek licence, and neither town's non-resident list includes a newspaper notice or a week's wait. If your municipality does run a Greek licence for you, count the week from the day it accepts a complete file, never from the day you land. For more detail, see [how the Greek licence wait works](/destination-weddings/greece-marriage-licence).

Here is an illustrative timeline, counted back from the wedding. Your municipality's written answers set the real dates.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 12 months or more before | Choose your island and write to that municipality with the questions above. Where anything is uncertain, hold off on non-refundable venue deposits until the municipality confirms your date in writing. |
| Nine months before | Check both passports against the FCDO issue-date and expiry rules. Start the gov.uk online application to see which document Greece needs for you, and time it as the service and your municipality advise. |
| Six months before | Order birth certificates and any proof a previous marriage ended, timed to your municipality's rules on how recent they must be. Send them to the FCDO Legalisation Office (up to 25 working days for paper apostilles, plus postage). |
| Three months before | Arrange Greek translations from the register and send copies to the municipality if it accepts them by email. |
| Wedding week | Take your original documents, passports and witnesses to the town hall when it asks. |
| After the ceremony | The marriage is registered at the municipality where it took place; order certificate copies before you fly home. |

That last step matters. A Greek marriage certificate presupposes that the civil or religious marriage has been registered at the registry office of the municipality where it took place, says the [gov.gr marriage certificate entry](https://mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:%CE%9B%CE%B7%CE%BE%CE%B9%CE%B1%CF%81%CF%87%CE%B9%CE%BA%CE%AE_%CF%80%CF%81%CE%AC%CE%BE%CE%B7_%CE%B3%CE%AC%CE%BC%CE%BF%CF%85). Copies are free, with a 10-day completion deadline, from municipalities or KEP centres, or online through gov.gr with Greek TAXISnet log-ins. Ask your municipality who registers the marriage and by when.

## The civil ceremony, and religious or symbolic options

[IMAGE: town-hall]

A civil wedding in Greece is a municipal matter: the licence comes from the mayor, and the ceremony is arranged through the town hall. Heraklion holds its civil ceremonies in the Municipal Council Hall, and its page lists EUR 100 for Tuesday or Thursday at 12:00 and EUR 150 for Wednesday or Friday from 18:00. Those fees come from a 2007 City Council decision, so confirm them. Heraklion also asks each person marrying to bring an adult witness with ID or a passport, and a relative may be a witness. Whether a ceremony can take place outside the town hall is a question for your municipality.

Same-sex couples can marry in a civil ceremony: Law 5089/2024 provides for civil marriage between people of the same sex, and gov.gr cites it as a legal basis for the civil licence. Confirm the documents for your own passports with your municipality.

The same gov.gr entry says the mayor's licence is needed for a marriage solemnised by the Eastern Orthodox Church, not only for a civil one. The church sets its own conditions, so speak to the parish priest before you book anything.

A symbolic ceremony carries no legal weight on its own. If you want one, pair it with a legal ceremony at home, the route gov.uk describes above.

## Recognition and name change back in the UK

[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/marriages-civil-partnerships-abroad/register-your-marriage-or-civil-partnership-abroad) says a foreign marriage is recognised in the UK if it was lawful in the country where it took place and would be allowed under UK law. When you marry abroad, that country registers the marriage in its own civil records, and gov.uk is plain about the rest: "You cannot get a UK marriage or civil partnership certificate if your marriage or civil partnership happened abroad. Only UK marriages and civil partnerships are registered in the UK." That holds whether you live in England, Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland. Your Greek certificate is your proof, so order more than one copy.

gov.uk adds that you may want the certificate translated, which can help if you plan to use it outside the country that issued it. If a UK bank, mortgage lender or pension provider asks to see it, ask them whether they want a translation too. For an apostille on the Greek certificate, the FCDO Legalisation Office cannot legalise documents issued outside the UK; they must be legalised in the country that issued them, so arrange it in Greece before you leave.

For your passport, [HM Passport Office](https://www.gov.uk/changing-passport-information/name-marriage-and-civil-partnership) says you can change your name before or after the ceremony. After it, you send your marriage certificate with an application in your new name, including a double-barrelled name. An online application costs GBP 102 and a paper one GBP 115.50, and you do not need a new passport if you keep your name. Book your flights to Greece in the name on your current passport, because the two must match.

A post-dated passport in your new name is possible up to three months before the ceremony. It cannot be used before the ceremony, your old passport is cancelled, and a form must be signed by the minister or registrar who will conduct the ceremony. For a wedding abroad, where your passport also shows you are lawfully in Greece, the simpler order is to travel and marry in your current name and change it afterwards. Ask HM Passport Office whether it needs a translation of your Greek certificate.

## FAQ

### How do UK couples get married in Greece?

You apply through gov.uk for the FCDO document that shows you are free to marry, have your birth certificate apostilled by the FCDO Legalisation Office, and have both translated into Greek. You then lodge them with the municipality where you will marry, which confirms your date and conducts the civil ceremony. The Greek marriage certificate is your proof of marriage back in the UK.

### Is a wedding in Greece legal in the UK?

Yes, if it was lawful in Greece and would be allowed under UK law, according to gov.uk. It is not registered in the UK and no UK certificate is issued, so keep your Greek certificate safe.

### Do British citizens need a certificate of no impediment to marry in Greece?

You need a document proving you are free to marry. gov.uk says some countries ask for a certificate of no impediment and others for a different document, such as a marital status affirmation, and its online service shows which one applies to your case. Chania also wants it to name your partner.

### How long do we need to be in Greece before the wedding?

Your municipality decides. The one-week wait on gov.gr belongs to the licence for residents of Greece, and Chania's and Heraklion's lists for couples living abroad do not include it. Ask how many working days the town hall needs with your originals before the ceremony.

### Is it different if we live in Scotland or Northern Ireland?

The FCDO and Greek steps are the same for every British passport holder, and gov.uk says no marriage abroad is registered anywhere in the UK. What changes is where your birth certificate comes from (National Records of Scotland or GRO Northern Ireland) and, if you marry at home first, which register office takes your notice.
