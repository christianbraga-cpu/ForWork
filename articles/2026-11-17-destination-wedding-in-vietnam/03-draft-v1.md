key_takeaways:
  - "Vietnam's Law on Civil Status provides for marriage registration by foreigners residing in Vietnam; the official sources we could read do not confirm that two non-resident Australians can register, so ask the provincial Department of Justice or the Australian Embassy in Hanoi first."
  - "Since 1 July 2025, marriages involving foreigners are registered by commune or ward People's Committees, within 5 working days of a complete dossier or up to 10 if verification is needed."
  - "Australians need a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI): the Australian Consulate-General in Ho Chi Minh City issues it in English and Vietnamese the next working day, and DFAT in Australia charges AUD 181 (2026 fee)."
  - "Vietnam's apostille rules took effect on 11 September 2026, but Smartraveller still describes the older embassy authentication step, so confirm the route with DFAT and the Embassy of Vietnam in Australia before you order anything."
  - "Marrying legally in Australia (Notice of Intended Marriage at least one month ahead) and celebrating in Vietnam removes the CNI, legalisation, Vietnamese translations and the People's Committee step."
faq:
  - q: "Can foreigners get married in Vietnam?"
    a: "Vietnam's Law on Civil Status provides for registration where a foreigner residing in Vietnam asks to marry, and for a Vietnamese citizen marrying a foreigner. The Australian Consulate-General in Ho Chi Minh City confirms that Vietnamese authorities solemnise marriages there and that Australians must submit a CNI. Residence is the question to settle first."
  - q: "Can two Australians get married in Vietnam without a Vietnamese partner?"
    a: "The official sources we could read do not confirm it. The law's wording ties registration between foreigners to residence in Vietnam, and Decree 120/2025 sends requests from people with no residence to the provincial Department of Justice. Ask that office, or the Australian Embassy in Hanoi, in writing before you plan a legal ceremony."
  - q: "Is a marriage in Vietnam legally recognised in Australia?"
    a: "Smartraveller says an overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia but in most cases is recognised if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. The certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, but may not be enough for a change of name."
  - q: "How long does marriage registration in Vietnam take?"
    a: "Decree 120/2025 sets 5 working days from receipt of a complete and valid dossier, extendable to no more than 10 working days where verification is needed. That clock starts only once the dossier is complete, so allow separate time for the CNI, legalisation and Vietnamese translations."
  - q: "When is the best time of year for a destination wedding in Vietnam?"
    a: "Smartraveller notes that Vietnam's storm season usually runs from June to November, with heavy rain, floods and typhoons that can affect flights, roads and power. We cannot promise weather in any month; whatever date you choose, build in a wet-weather plan and flexible travel."
  - q: "Do our Australian guests need a visa for Vietnam?"
    a: "Yes, a visa or a visa exemption certificate. Smartraveller says Australians cannot get a visa on arrival; the tourism eVisa, if granted, may allow up to 90 days. Guests should apply only through the official eVisa website, check every detail and carry a printed copy."

# Destination Wedding in Vietnam Legal Steps and Paperwork

[IMAGE: hero]

If you are planning a destination wedding in Vietnam, here is the honest legal answer. Vietnamese law provides for marriage registration by foreigners residing in Vietnam. The official sources we could read do not confirm that two non-resident foreigners can register. Before you plan a legal wedding there, confirm with the provincial Department of Justice or the Australian Embassy in Hanoi.

That leaves two routes, and this guide walks through both: registering in Vietnam if the authorities confirm it is open to you, or marrying legally in Australia and holding your celebration in Vietnam. We checked every source on 17 November 2026. Vietnam's rules on foreign documents changed in September 2026 and are still moving, so confirm each requirement with the deciding authority, for your own passports, close to your date.

## Destination wedding in Vietnam: can foreigners marry legally?

Yes in some cases, and residence is the deciding question. Vietnam's Law on Civil Status 2014 (Article 37) provides for registration where a foreigner residing in Vietnam asks to marry, and separately where a Vietnamese citizen marries a foreigner. We read that wording in a secondary publication of the law text, not the official gazette, so treat it as a strong pointer rather than the last word.

Since 1 July 2025, [Decree 120/2025](https://hatinh.gov.vn/vi/bai-viet/uy-ban-nhan-dan-xa-phuong-dac-khu-thuc-hien-tham-quyen-dang-ky-ho-tich) has moved marriage registration from district offices to commune or ward People's Committees (the local government office). Where the person asking has no place of residence in Vietnam, the same decree sends the request to the provincial Department of Justice, which guides them on where to file. It does not say marriage is open to them. That office is the right one to ask, in writing, before you plan anything legal in Vietnam.

The [Australian Consulate-General in Ho Chi Minh City](https://hcmc.consulate.gov.au/hchi/CNI.html) confirms the other half: the Vietnamese government has the authority to solemnise marriages in Vietnam, and Australians must submit a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) when they register.

| | Register in Vietnam | Marry in Australia, celebrate in Vietnam |
|---|---|---|
| Who it suits | Couples the provincial Department of Justice confirms can register (for example, a partner residing in Vietnam) | Couples who both live in Australia, or anyone who prefers the legal step at home |
| Where the legal part happens | A commune or ward People's Committee | In Australia, before an authorised celebrant |
| Extra paperwork | CNI, passports, apostille or authentication as confirmed, notarised Vietnamese translations | A Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM) given to your celebrant |
| The Vietnam day | Registration at the People's Committee, then your celebration | A symbolic celebration at your venue, with no legal effect |

[IMAGE: routes]

Italy works differently: two non-residents can marry legally at a comune (town hall) there, so if you are still choosing a country, see [how the Italian legal process compares](/expert-advice/getting-married-in-italy-the-complete-legal-guide-for-destination-couples). Requirements change and offices apply them differently; confirm with the registering authority and for your own passports.

## Residency and eligibility for marrying in Vietnam

Vietnam's Marriage and Family Law 2014 (Article 8), as published in secondary law texts we could read, sets the minimum age at fully 20 for a man and fully 18 for a woman. Marriage must be voluntary, and neither partner may have lost civil act capacity (the legal capacity to make binding decisions). Search snippets disagree on the men's age, so check it with the registering office.

The same article states that the State does not recognise marriage between persons of the same sex. That is a statement about legal recognition, from the same secondary text, and we suggest same-sex couples raise the legal side with the Australian Embassy in Hanoi before planning.

On residence, the Article 37 wording ties registration between foreigners to residing in Vietnam. The official sources we read do not say whether a tourist stay counts. A tourism eVisa, if granted, may allow up to 90 days, but that is an entry permission, not a ruling on residence. We do not give visa or residency advice; the provincial Department of Justice decides.

## Can't marry legally in Vietnam? Marry in Australia, celebrate in Vietnam

This is our practical recommendation for two Australians, and it removes the Vietnamese paperwork entirely. You marry legally at home, then hold the celebration in Vietnam exactly as you want it.

The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) sets the steps. You give a NOIM to an authorised celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. Since 12 June 2024 it can be witnessed remotely by audio-visual link, or overseas by an authorised witness such as an Australian consular officer or a notary public. The ceremony itself cannot be remote: the celebrant meets each of you separately in person and is physically present with you and two witnesses over 18. An Australian celebrant cannot legally marry you overseas.

Within 14 days your celebrant sends the paperwork to the registry of the state or territory where you married, so your legal record is Australian from day one. For the detail, see [how to marry legally in Australia first](/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements).

What this route takes off your list:

- **No CNI appointment** at an Australian mission in Vietnam.
- **No apostille or authentication question** while Vietnam's new rules settle.
- **No notarised Vietnamese translations** of Australian documents.
- **No People's Committee lodgement**, and no 5 to 10 working-day processing window to fit around guest arrivals.
- **No recognition question at home**, and an Australian certificate for any name change.

A small ceremony with two witnesses, a few weeks before you fly, is a sound choice in its own right, not a compromise.

## Documents from Australia

If the Department of Justice confirms the Vietnamese route is open to you, the registering office in Vietnam sets the final dossier; ask it for the full list in writing. What official sources confirm today is below.

Every Australian marrying in Vietnam needs a CNI. [Smartraveller's Vietnam page](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/asia/vietnam) says you can apply through the Australian Embassy in Hanoi, the Australian Consulate-General in Ho Chi Minh City, or DFAT in Australia. At the Consulate-General you book a CNI appointment under Notarial Services and complete the form without signing it, because a Consular Officer must witness your signature. Bring originals or certified copies of both passports and any divorce or death certificate. The fee is non-refundable and payable in Vietnamese dong by bank card only. The CNI, in English and Vietnamese, is ready the following working day between 9am and 12pm.

[IMAGE: documents]

The Consulate-General also says Australian citizens do not need to give Vietnamese local authorities a statutory declaration of marital status, a single status certificate or a divorce certificate.

| Document | Issued by | Apostille or authentication | Vietnamese translation | Fee and timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CNI (in Vietnam) | Australian Embassy Hanoi or Consulate-General Ho Chi Minh City | Ask the registering office; see the next section | Issued in English and Vietnamese | Fee in VND; next working day |
| CNI (in Australia) | DFAT, through Australian Passport Offices or by mail | Confirm with DFAT and the Embassy of Vietnam in Australia | Notarised or certified under Vietnamese law | AUD 181 (2026 fee) |
| Passports | Australian Passport Office | Not stated by official sources | Ask the registering office | At least 6 weeks for a new passport |
| Divorce or death certificate | The Australian court or registry that issued it | Needed for the CNI application if it applies to you | Ask the registering office | Bring originals or certified copies |

For a DFAT CNI, at least one of you must be an Australian citizen or permanent resident, and you sign before an authorised witness (see [DFAT's CNI guide](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-certificate-no-impediment-marriage)). DFAT itself advises checking whether the CNI must be issued in the country of marriage. Fees are on [DFAT's notarial fees](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services) page and rise with CPI each 1 January.

## Translations, legalisation and the apostille question

Two terms first. Legalisation is the check of the signature, seal or stamp on an Australian public document, after which an apostille or an authentication certificate is attached. An apostille is a single certificate that replaces consular legalisation (an extra stamp from the destination country's embassy) between countries in the Hague Apostille Convention.

Vietnam joined that Convention, in force for Vietnam from 11 September 2026, according to [Vietnam's Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://vnconsulate-shanghai.mofa.gov.vn/tin-chi-tiet/chi-tiet/thong-bao-ve-viec-ap-dung-chung-nhan-apostille-thay-cho-hop-phap-hoa-lanh-su-ke-tu-ngay-11-thang-9-nam-2026-61957-1205.html). [Decree 293/2026](https://pbgdpl.cantho.gov.vn/tuyen-truyen-cong-uoc-apostille-va-nghi-dinh-so-2932026nd-cp-don-gian-hoa-thu-tuc-tao-thuan-loi-cho-nguoi-dan-va-doanh-nghiep), issued 23 July 2026, says agencies in Vietnam may not ask for further legalisation of a document that carries an apostille. It also excludes documents made by diplomatic or consular officers, which could matter for a CNI issued by an Australian mission. Documents legalised the old way before 11 September 2026 remain valid.

Here is the conflict. Smartraveller's Vietnam page still says a DFAT CNI must be authenticated by Vietnam's embassy or consulate in Australia before the Department of Justice in Vietnam will recognise it. The [Hague Conference status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) lists Australia as a party since 1995 and notes that one or more states objected to Vietnam's accession, without showing which. Until DFAT and the Embassy of Vietnam in Australia confirm the route for your document, do not order an apostille or an authentication; ask them, and ask the registering office, which one it accepts. A DFAT apostille costs AUD 105 per document (2026 fee), and DFAT says [processing times vary](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised).

Any document not in Vietnamese needs a Vietnamese translation, notarised or certified under Vietnamese law. Before anyone translates, check that every name is spelt identically across passports, CNI and any certificate.

## Timeline: from first paperwork to wedding day

Decree 120/2025 gives the People's Committee 5 working days from receipt of a complete and valid dossier to register a marriage involving a foreigner, extendable to no more than 10 working days where verification is needed. That clock starts only when the file is complete. The official sources we read do not say whether both of you must attend to lodge or to collect the certificate, so ask.

As an illustration only (your registering office and celebrant set the real timeline):

| Wedding week | Allow 5 to 10 working days before the celebration | Legal ceremony done at home; fly to Vietnam |

Rules are changing in Vietnam, so check every step again close to your date.

## The civil registration in Vietnam

Registration happens at the commune or ward People's Committee, a government office, not at a resort. The Vietnamese government solemnises the marriage, so plan the registration as its own day, separate from your celebration on the coast.

Ceremony format, witnesses, interpreters and fees were not in the official sources we could read. Ask the People's Committee, through the provincial Department of Justice, what happens on the day and whether you need an interpreter.

If you will use the Vietnamese marriage certificate back home, note that Vietnamese public documents are apostilled by the competent authority in Vietnam, not by Vietnam's consulates abroad. Details are published at apostille.lanhsuvietnam.gov.vn.

## Religious and symbolic ceremonies

Vietnamese authorities hold the power to solemnise marriages in Vietnam, and we found no official source saying a religious ceremony has legal effect there. Ask the officiant and the registering office before relying on one.

A symbolic ceremony carries no legal weight, which is exactly what makes it flexible. If you married in Australia first, your celebration in Vietnam can be on a lawn above the sea, at the hour, in the language and with the officiant you choose. ## Recognition and name change back in Australia

If you married in Australia, your celebrant has already lodged the record with your state or territory registry, and the Vietnam celebration needs no recognition.

If you registered in Vietnam, [Smartraveller's advice on marrying overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) says you cannot register the marriage in Australia, but in most cases it is recognised if it was valid where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. It is not recognised if either of you was already married, not old enough, too closely related or forced to marry. The legal basis is Part VA of the Marriage Act 1961. The certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, though some states and territories do not accept overseas certificates for a change of name. For the general steps, see [what to do with a marriage certificate issued overseas](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate).

You can keep travelling on your current passport, and the [Australian Passport Office](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender) recommends booking travel in the name on it. For a passport in a married name, an applicant born in Australia who married overseas needs a change of name certificate from their state or territory registry. A foreign certificate is accepted only where an Australian equivalent is not available, and it must be legalised and fully translated by an approved service.

In NSW, a person legally married in Australia can take their spouse's family name without registering a change of name, and foreign documents need a NAATI-accredited translation. Check your own registry.

## FAQ

### Can foreigners get married in Vietnam?

Vietnam's Law on Civil Status provides for registration where a foreigner residing in Vietnam asks to marry, and for a Vietnamese citizen marrying a foreigner. The Australian Consulate-General in Ho Chi Minh City confirms that Vietnamese authorities solemnise marriages there and that Australians must submit a CNI. Residence is the question to settle first.

### Can two Australians get married in Vietnam without a Vietnamese partner?

The official sources we could read do not confirm it. The law's wording ties registration between foreigners to residence in Vietnam, and Decree 120/2025 sends requests from people with no residence to the provincial Department of Justice. Ask that office, or the Australian Embassy in Hanoi, in writing before you plan a legal ceremony.

### Is a marriage in Vietnam legally recognised in Australia?

Smartraveller says an overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia but in most cases is recognised if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. The certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, but may not be enough for a change of name.

### How long does marriage registration in Vietnam take?

Decree 120/2025 sets 5 working days from receipt of a complete and valid dossier, extendable to no more than 10 working days where verification is needed. That clock starts only once the dossier is complete, so allow separate time for the CNI, legalisation and Vietnamese translations.

### When is the best time of year for a destination wedding in Vietnam?

Smartraveller notes that Vietnam's storm season usually runs from June to November, with heavy rain, floods and typhoons that can affect flights, roads and power. We cannot promise weather in any month; whatever date you choose, build in a wet-weather plan and flexible travel.

### Do our Australian guests need a visa for Vietnam?

Yes, a visa or a visa exemption certificate. Smartraveller says Australians cannot get a visa on arrival; the tourism eVisa, if granted, may allow up to 90 days. Guests should apply only through the official eVisa website, check every detail and carry a printed copy.
