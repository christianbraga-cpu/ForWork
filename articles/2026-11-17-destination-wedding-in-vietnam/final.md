# Destination Wedding in Vietnam Legal Steps and Paperwork

[IMAGE: vietnam-central-coast-beachfront-lawn-couple-golden-hour-01.webp]

If you are planning a destination wedding in Vietnam, here is the honest legal answer. Vietnamese law provides for marriage registration by foreigners residing in Vietnam. The official sources we read do not confirm that two non-resident foreigners can register. Before you plan a legal wedding there, get written confirmation from the provincial Department of Justice, the office that handles requests from people with no residence in Vietnam. Do not pay venue deposits until you have that answer.

That leaves two routes: registering in Vietnam if the authorities confirm it is open to you, or marrying legally in Australia and celebrating in Vietnam. We last reviewed these sources in November 2026. Vietnam's rules on foreign documents changed in September 2026 and are still moving, so confirm each requirement close to your date.

## Destination wedding in Vietnam: can foreigners marry legally?

Yes in some cases, and residence is the deciding question. Vietnam's Law on Civil Status 2014 (Article 37) provides for registration where a foreigner residing in Vietnam asks to marry, and separately where a Vietnamese citizen marries a foreigner.

Since 1 July 2025, [Decree 120/2025](https://hatinh.gov.vn/vi/bai-viet/uy-ban-nhan-dan-xa-phuong-dac-khu-thuc-hien-tham-quyen-dang-ky-ho-tich) has moved marriage registration from district offices to commune or ward People's Committees (the local government office). Where the person asking has no residence in Vietnam, the decree sends the request to the provincial Department of Justice, which guides them on where to file. That is the office to ask.

The [Australian Consulate-General in Ho Chi Minh City](https://hcmc.consulate.gov.au/hchi/CNI.html) confirms that the Vietnamese government solemnises marriages in Vietnam, and that Australians must submit a Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage (CNI) when they register.

| | Register in Vietnam | Marry in Australia, celebrate in Vietnam |
|---|---|---|
| Who it suits | Couples the provincial Department of Justice confirms can register | Couples who both live in Australia |
| Where the legal part happens | A commune or ward People's Committee | In Australia, before an authorised celebrant |
| Extra paperwork | CNI, passports, apostille or authentication as confirmed, notarised Vietnamese translations | A Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM) |
| The Vietnam day | Registration, then your celebration | A symbolic celebration with no legal effect |

[IMAGE: vietnam-hoi-an-lantern-lane-couple-dusk-01.webp]

Italy works differently: two non-residents can marry legally at a comune (town hall), so if you are still choosing a country, see [how the Italian legal process compares](/expert-advice/getting-married-in-italy-the-complete-legal-guide-for-destination-couples).

## Getting married in Vietnam: residency and eligibility

Vietnam's Marriage and Family Law 2014 (Article 8) sets the minimum age at fully 20 for a man and fully 18 for a woman. Marriage must be voluntary, and neither partner may have lost civil act capacity (the legal capacity to make binding decisions). Confirm the age rules with the registering office.

The same article states that the State does not recognise marriage between persons of the same sex. Same-sex couples should confirm the position with the provincial Department of Justice before planning.

Whether a tourist stay counts as residence is not set out in the published rules; the provincial Department of Justice decides.

## Can't marry legally in Vietnam? Marry in Australia, celebrate in Vietnam

This is our practical recommendation for two Australians. It removes the Vietnamese marriage registration: you marry legally at home, then celebrate in Vietnam as you want. You and your guests still need a visa or visa exemption certificate; the tourism eVisa, if granted, may allow up to 90 days.

The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) sets the steps. You give a NOIM to an authorised celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. It can be witnessed remotely by audio-visual link, or overseas by an authorised witness such as an Australian consular officer or a notary public. The ceremony itself cannot be remote: the celebrant meets each of you separately in person and is physically present with you and two witnesses over 18. An Australian celebrant cannot legally marry you overseas.

Within 14 days your celebrant sends the paperwork to your state or territory registry, so your legal record is Australian from day one. For the detail, see [how to marry legally in Australia first](/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements).

What this route takes off your list:

- **No CNI appointment** at an Australian mission in Vietnam.
- **No apostille or authentication question** while Vietnam's new rules settle.
- **No notarised Vietnamese translations.**
- **No People's Committee lodgement** or processing window to fit around guest arrivals.
- **No recognition question at home**, and an Australian certificate for any name change.

A small ceremony with two witnesses, a few weeks before you fly, is a sound choice in its own right.

## Documents from Australia

If the Vietnamese route is open to you, the registering office sets the final dossier, so ask it for the full list.

Every Australian marrying in Vietnam needs a CNI. [Smartraveller's Vietnam page](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/asia/vietnam) says you can apply through the Australian Embassy in Hanoi, the Australian Consulate-General in Ho Chi Minh City, or DFAT in Australia. At the Consulate-General you book a CNI appointment under Notarial Services and complete the form without signing it, because a Consular Officer must witness your signature. Bring originals or certified copies of both passports and any divorce or death certificate.

## Translations, legalisation and the apostille question

Legalisation is the check of the signature, seal or stamp on an Australian public document, after which an apostille or an authentication certificate is attached. An apostille is a single certificate that replaces consular legalisation (an extra stamp from the destination country's embassy) between countries in the Hague Apostille Convention.

The Convention has been in force for Vietnam since 11 September 2026, according to [Vietnam's Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://vnconsulate-shanghai.mofa.gov.vn/tin-chi-tiet/chi-tiet/thong-bao-ve-viec-ap-dung-chung-nhan-apostille-thay-cho-hop-phap-hoa-lanh-su-ke-tu-ngay-11-thang-9-nam-2026-61957-1205.html). [Decree 293/2026](https://pbgdpl.cantho.gov.vn/tuyen-truyen-cong-uoc-apostille-va-nghi-dinh-so-2932026nd-cp-don-gian-hoa-thu-tuc-tao-thuan-loi-cho-nguoi-dan-va-doanh-nghiep), issued 23 July 2026, says agencies in Vietnam may not ask for further legalisation of an apostilled document. It also excludes documents made by diplomatic or consular officers, which could matter for a CNI issued by an Australian mission. Documents legalised the old way before 11 September 2026 remain valid.

Here is the conflict. Smartraveller's Vietnam page still says a DFAT CNI must be authenticated by Vietnam's embassy or consulate in Australia before the Department of Justice in Vietnam will recognise it, and the [Hague Conference status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) notes objections to Vietnam's accession. Ask the registering office which one it accepts, then confirm with DFAT and the Embassy of Vietnam in Australia how to obtain it; until then, do not order an apostille or an authentication. A DFAT apostille costs AUD 105 per document (2026 fee), and DFAT says [processing times vary](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-your-documents-legalised).

Any document not in Vietnamese needs a Vietnamese translation, notarised or certified under Vietnamese law. Before anyone translates, check that every name is spelt identically across passports, CNI and certificates.

## Timeline: from first paperwork to wedding day

Decree 120/2025 gives the People's Committee 5 working days from a complete and valid dossier to register a marriage involving a foreigner, or up to 10 where verification is needed. So lodge the complete dossier at least 2 weeks (10 working days) before your celebration.

Registration happens at the People's Committee, a government office rather than your venue, so plan it as its own appointment. Ask the office whether both of you must attend and whether you need an interpreter.

## Recognition and name change back in Australia

If you married in Australia, your celebrant has already lodged the record with your registry, and the Vietnam celebration needs no recognition.

If you registered in Vietnam, [Smartraveller's advice on marrying overseas](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/planning-trip/advice-your-planned-activities/getting-married-overseas) says you cannot register the marriage in Australia, but in most cases it is recognised if it was valid where it happened and would have been valid under Australian law. It is not recognised if either of you was already married, not old enough, too closely related or forced to marry. The legal basis is Part VA of the Marriage Act 1961. The certificate is usually accepted as proof of the marriage, though some states and territories do not accept overseas certificates for a change of name. See [what to do with a marriage certificate issued overseas](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate).

You can keep travelling on your current passport, and the [Australian Passport Office](https://www.passports.gov.au/change-name-or-gender) recommends booking travel in the name on it. For a passport in a married name, an applicant born in Australia who married overseas needs a change of name certificate from their state or territory registry. A foreign certificate is accepted only where an Australian equivalent is not available, and it must be legalised and fully translated by an approved service.

[IMAGE: australia-home-vietnam-wedding-photo-certificate-01.webp]

In NSW, a person legally married in Australia can take their spouse's family name without registering a change of name, and foreign documents need a NAATI-accredited translation. Check your own registry.

A Vietnamese marriage certificate is apostilled by the competent authority in Vietnam, not by its consulates abroad.

## FAQ

### Can foreigners get married in Vietnam?

Vietnam's Law on Civil Status provides for registration where a foreigner residing in Vietnam asks to marry, and for a Vietnamese citizen marrying a foreigner. The Australian Consulate-General in Ho Chi Minh City confirms that Australians must submit a CNI. Residence is the question to settle first.

### Can two Australians get married in Vietnam without a Vietnamese partner?

Only if the authorities confirm it. The law ties registration between foreigners to residence in Vietnam, and Decree 120/2025 sends requests from people with no residence to the provincial Department of Justice. Get that office's answer in writing before you plan a legal ceremony.

### Is a marriage in Vietnam legally recognised in Australia?

Smartraveller says an overseas marriage cannot be registered in Australia but in most cases is recognised if it was valid where it took place and would have been valid under Australian law. The certificate is usually accepted as proof, but may not be enough for a change of name.

### When is the best time of year for a destination wedding in Vietnam?

Smartraveller notes that Vietnam's storm season usually runs from June to November, with heavy rain, floods and typhoons that can affect flights, roads and power. We cannot promise weather in any month; whatever date you choose, build in a wet-weather plan and flexible travel.

### Do our Australian guests need a visa for Vietnam?

Yes, a visa or a visa exemption certificate. Smartraveller says Australians cannot get a visa on arrival; the tourism eVisa, if granted, may allow up to 90 days. Guests should apply only through the official eVisa website and carry a printed copy.
