# Marriage Licence Validity and How Long It Lasts

[IMAGE: hero]

Australia does not issue a marriage licence, so at home there is no marriage licence validity to track. The legal step is the Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM), and its 18 months is a window for giving notice, not a period for which the form stays valid. You give it to your celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding.

The clocks that really can catch you out sit elsewhere: the 14 days your celebrant has to register your marriage, and the shelf life of papers you carry overseas, from Bali's three-month rule on a CNI to Greece's six-month licence. This guide puts every time limit in one place. For the steps themselves, see [our full guide to marriage licence requirements in Australia](/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements). We checked every source on 13 November 2026; confirm anything that shapes your plans with the authority that decides it.

## Does a marriage licence expire in Australia?

No, because there is no licence to expire. Section 42 of the Marriage Act 1961, as set out on AGD's NOIM form and [AGD's Get married page](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married), requires notice at least one month and not more than 18 months before the wedding. After the wedding, no official source we read gives your registry marriage certificate an expiry date.

Here is every time limit in this guide on one page.

| Document | Time limit | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| NOIM (Australia) | Give it at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding | Marriage Act 1961, s 42 |
| Registering your marriage (Australia) | Your celebrant sends the paperwork to the registry within 14 days | Marriage Act 1961; state and territory registries |
| Official registry marriage certificate | No published expiry | State or territory registry |
| Ceremonial certificate from the day | Not a legal record, whatever its age | State or territory registry |
| Certificate of No Impediment (CNI) | No validity printed by DFAT; the destination sets its own limit | The country you marry in |
| Apostille | No validity published by DFAT | The receiving authority |
| Greece: marriage licence | Valid for six months | Greek registry |
| Italy: Atto Notorio (Australian citizens) | Valid for six months, so not requested earlier than six months before the wedding | Italian consulate for your state |
| France: CNI (one partner must be a French citizen or legal resident) | Valid for 90 days from issue | French law, via the Australian Embassy in Paris |
| Bali: CNI | No expiry printed, but usually refused if issued more than three months before | Local Civil Registry Office |
| Fiji: special licence | A short period from issue; official sources conflict, so confirm the current figure | Fiji's registry |

## The NOIM window: what "18 months" really means

Several celebrant websites describe the NOIM as "valid for 18 months". The official wording is different: you give the NOIM to your celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. The 18 months limits how early you can give notice, and the window is counted back from your wedding date, not forward from the day you sign.

[IMAGE: noim-window]

**Example, for illustration only:** for a wedding on Saturday 14 October 2028, the earliest you could give notice is around mid-April 2027, and the latest is around mid-September 2028. Your celebrant confirms the exact first and last days.

If you are inside the final month, talk to your celebrant: a prescribed authority may agree to shorten the notice period, but approval is not automatic. For the forms, witnesses and documents, our pillar guide explains [how to give your Notice of Intended Marriage](/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements).

## If your date moves: postponing or changing celebrant

Because the window runs back from the wedding date, your NOIM fits only a wedding held inside it. If your new date falls more than 18 months after the day you gave notice, ask your celebrant whether you need to give a new NOIM. We have not found official wording that settles this, so do not assume either way.

Moving the date earlier has its own limit: you still need at least one month between giving notice and the wedding, unless a prescribed authority shortens it.

Changing celebrant is simpler than many couples expect. Since 12 June 2024, a NOIM can be transferred to another authorised celebrant, including at your request, so a change of celebrant does not by itself mean starting again.

The harder part of a date change is everything around it. Your venue contract, your celebrant's diary, your guests' flights and any overseas document with its own clock all move together. A CNI ordered for an October wedding in Bali, for example, may be too old for the registry if the date slips by more than a few months.

## After the wedding: does a marriage certificate expire?

Within 14 days of the ceremony, your celebrant sends the NOIM, your declarations and the official certificate to the Births, Deaths and Marriages registry in the state or territory where you married. The NSW, Queensland and WA registries all confirm the 14 days.

The certificate your celebrant hands you on the day is ceremonial. [WA Registry's marriage FAQ](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/department-of-justice/the-registry-of-births-deaths-and-marriages/getting-married-australia-frequently-asked-questions) says it cannot be used for any legal purpose, and Victoria's registry notes that most organisations accept only the registry certificate. You apply for that one yourself; Queensland, for instance, does not send it automatically.

[IMAGE: certificate]

Once you hold it, no official source we read gives it an expiry date. WA's registry says a certified copy is usually enough to change your personal documents, and the Australian Passport Office links your names with a birth or citizenship certificate plus an Australian registry marriage certificate, with no age limit stated.

Recency matters mostly abroad. Some overseas authorities set their own rules on how recently a document was issued: the Australian Embassy in France, for example, tells couples to check whether a birth certificate must be less than six months old. Ask before you order a fresh copy.

If you marry overseas, the marriage cannot be registered in Australia, though it is generally recognised if it was valid where it took place and would be valid under Australian law. WA's registry advises getting the foreign certificate before you fly home, as it can be hard to obtain from Australia. Our article covers [what to check when you use a foreign marriage certificate at home](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate).

## Documents with a shelf life when you marry abroad

[DFAT's guide to the Certificate of No Impediment](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-certificate-no-impediment-marriage) states no validity period for a CNI. It tells you to confirm with the overseas authority whether it needs a CNI at all, whether it must be issued in that country, and whether it needs an apostille. The destination then decides how old a CNI may be: the Philippines, for example, normally refuses one issued more than six months before the wedding.

An apostille works the same way. DFAT applies it directly to the document and publishes no validity period for it, and it cannot tell you which certification you need: the receiving authority decides. DFAT will not legalise a ceremonial or commemorative certificate, as its page on [documents DFAT can legalise](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/documents-we-can-legalise) explains. In practice, an apostille is only as useful as the document it is attached to.

Supporting certificates add their own steps. Australians marrying in Italy, for example, need a birth certificate and a Single Status Certificate, each with an official Italian translation and a DFAT apostille.

The practical rule: order time-limited documents last, once the deciding authority has told you exactly what it wants, and inside its limit. DFAT publishes no fixed processing time, so leave a margin.

## How long a marriage licence lasts in popular destinations

Each country runs its own clock, and some put the limit on the licence while others put it on the papers you bring. Rules change, so confirm with the destination's authorities and the Australian embassy there, for your own passports, before you book.

### Greece

According to [Greece's official registry entry](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_civil_marriage_licence), a Greek marriage licence is valid for six months, and if the marriage does not take place within six months of the newspaper notice, the notice must be repeated. Couples marrying from abroad may follow a different route, where a home-country document of no impediment that names the other spouse stands in for the Greek licence. Our guide to [the Greek licence and its eight-day wait](/destination-weddings/greece-marriage-licence) covers the process.

### Italy

Australians need a Nulla Osta from the Australian Embassy in Rome, and first an Atto Notorio, signed with four unrelated witnesses at the Italian consulate for your state or at a tribunal in Italy. The [Italian Consulate in Adelaide's Atto Notorio guide](https://consadelaide.esteri.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/How-To_ATTO_NOTORIO_EN-2022.pdf) says it is valid for six months, so it cannot be requested earlier than six months before the wedding. That guide dates from 2022, so check with your own state's consulate. Read more on [how the legal process works in Italy](/expert-advice/getting-married-in-italy-the-complete-legal-guide-for-destination-couples).

### France

First, the condition: a legal wedding in France takes place at a town hall (mairie), and at least one of you must be a French citizen or a legal resident of France. If neither of you is, the legal part has to happen elsewhere, and France becomes the celebration. If you qualify, the [Australian Embassy in France](https://france.embassy.gov.au/pari/Marriage.html) says the CNI it produces in French is valid for only 90 days from issue, and processing can take up to 10 working days. See [weddings in France](/destinations/europe/france).

### Bali

Your CNI comes from the Australian Consulate-General in Bali. The [Australian Embassy in Indonesia](https://indonesia.embassy.gov.au/jakt/MarriageInd.html) places no expiry on it, but local Civil Registry Offices do not normally accept one issued more than three months before. For non-Muslim marriages, notice goes to the Civil Registry Office at least 10 days before the wedding, and the Embassy says requirements vary by province. See [weddings in Bali](/destinations/asia/bali).

### Fiji

Visitors in Fiji for a short stay may be granted a Special Licence to Marry, which waives the 21-day notice, according to [Fiji's Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.gov.fj/?p=38738). The licence lasts only a short time from issue, and the official sources we read give different figures, so confirm the current period with Fiji's registry before you set travel dates. See [weddings in Fiji](/destinations/oceania/fiji).

## Putting the clocks together: a validity timeline for a destination wedding

This is an example built on the limits above, not legal advice. Your celebrant, registry or destination authority has the final word.

- **As soon as you choose a country:** ask its authority which documents it needs, where each must be issued, and how recent each must be.
- **18 months to one month before (if the legal wedding is in Australia):** the NOIM window.
- **About six months before (Italy):** the earliest you can request the Atto Notorio.
- **Inside three months (Bali):** the CNI the Civil Registry Office will normally accept.
- **Inside 90 days (France):** the CNI's validity.
- **Inside six months (Greece):** the licence or notice window.
- **Before you fly home:** collect the foreign marriage certificate.
- **Within 14 days of an Australian ceremony:** your celebrant registers the marriage; then order your official certificate.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long is a NOIM valid in Australia?

A NOIM has no validity period as such. You must give it to your celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding date, so the 18 months is a window for giving notice, counted back from the wedding.

### Does a marriage certificate expire?

No official source we checked gives an Australian registry marriage certificate an expiry date. Some overseas authorities ask for documents issued recently, so check their rule before you order a fresh copy.

### What happens if we postpone our wedding after giving our NOIM?

If the new date is still within 18 months of the day you gave notice, the window still fits. If it falls later, ask your celebrant whether you need to give a new NOIM, and check every overseas document that has its own time limit.

### What is the best proof of marriage in Australia?

The official certificate from the registry in the state or territory where you married. The certificate your celebrant hands you on the day is ceremonial, and most organisations accept only the registry certificate.

### How long is a marriage licence valid overseas?

It depends on the country. A Greek marriage licence is valid for six months, and Fiji's special licence lasts only a short time from issue. In France, Italy and Bali the time limit sits on supporting documents, such as the CNI or Italy's Atto Notorio.

### Does a Certificate of No Impediment (CNI) expire?

DFAT publishes no validity period for a CNI, but the country you marry in decides how old it may be. Indonesian civil registries normally refuse one issued more than three months earlier, and France gives the CNI 90 days.
