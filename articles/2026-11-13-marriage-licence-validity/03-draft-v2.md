# Marriage Licence Validity and How Long It Lasts

[IMAGE: hero]

Australia does not issue a marriage licence, so at home there is no marriage licence validity to track. The legal step is the Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM), and its 18 months is a window for giving notice, not a period for which the form stays valid. You give it to your celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding.

The clocks that really can catch you out sit elsewhere: the 14 days your celebrant has to register your marriage, and the shelf life of papers you carry overseas, from Bali's three-month rule on a CNI to Greece's six-month licence. This guide puts every time limit in one place. For the steps themselves, see [our full guide to marriage licence requirements in Australia](/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements). We checked every source on 13 November 2026; confirm anything that shapes your plans with the authority that decides it.

## Does a marriage licence expire in Australia?

No, because there is no licence to expire. Section 42 of the Marriage Act 1961, cited on AGD's NOIM form, requires notice at least one month and not more than 18 months before the wedding; [AGD's Get married page](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) gives the same window. After the wedding, no official source we read gives your registry marriage certificate an expiry date.

| Document | Time limit | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| NOIM (Australia) | Give it at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding | Marriage Act 1961, s 42 |
| Registering your marriage (Australia) | Your celebrant sends the paperwork to the registry within 14 days | Marriage Act 1961; state and territory registries |
| Official registry marriage certificate | No published expiry | State or territory registry |
| Ceremonial certificate from the day | Not a legal record, whatever its age | State or territory registry |
| Certificate of No Impediment (CNI) | No validity printed by DFAT; the destination sets its own limit | The country you marry in |
| Apostille | No validity published by DFAT | The receiving authority |
| Greece: marriage licence (if issued in Greece) | Valid for six months | Greek registry |
| Italy: Atto Notorio (Australian citizens) | Valid for six months, so not requested earlier than six months before the wedding (Italian Consulate in Adelaide guide, 2022; check your state's consulate) | Italian consulate for your state |
| France: CNI (one partner must be a French citizen or legal resident) | Valid for 90 days from issue | French law, via the Australian Embassy in Paris |
| Bali: CNI | No expiry printed, but usually refused if issued more than three months before | Local Civil Registry Office |
| Fiji: special licence | A short period from issue; official sources conflict, so confirm the current figure | Fiji's registry |

## The NOIM window: what "18 months" really means

Several celebrant websites describe the NOIM as "valid for 18 months". The official wording is different: you give the NOIM to your celebrant at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. The 18 months limits how early you can give notice, and the window is counted back from your wedding date, not forward from the day you sign.

[IMAGE: noim-window]

**Example, for illustration only:** for a wedding on Saturday 14 October 2028, the earliest you could give notice is around mid-April 2027, and the latest is around mid-September 2028. Your celebrant confirms the exact first and last days.

For the forms, witnesses and documents, our pillar guide explains [how to give your Notice of Intended Marriage](/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements).

## If your date moves: postponing or changing celebrant

Because the window runs back from the wedding date, your NOIM fits only a wedding held inside it. If your new date falls more than 18 months after the day you gave notice, ask your celebrant whether you need to give a new NOIM. We have not found official wording that settles this, so do not assume either way.

Moving the date earlier has its own limit: you still need at least one month between giving notice and the wedding, unless a prescribed authority shortens it.

Since 12 June 2024, a NOIM can be transferred to another authorised celebrant, including at your request, so a change of celebrant does not by itself mean starting again.

The harder part of a date change is everything around it. Your venue contract, your celebrant's diary, your guests' flights and any overseas document with its own clock all move together. A CNI ordered for an October wedding in Bali, for example, may be too old for the registry if the new date falls more than three months after the CNI was issued.

## After the wedding: does a marriage certificate expire?

Within 14 days of the ceremony, your celebrant sends the NOIM, your declarations and the official certificate to the Births, Deaths and Marriages registry in the state or territory where you married. The NSW, Queensland and WA registries all confirm the 14 days.

The certificate your celebrant hands you on the day is ceremonial. [WA Registry's marriage FAQ](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/department-of-justice/the-registry-of-births-deaths-and-marriages/getting-married-australia-frequently-asked-questions) says it cannot be used for any legal purpose, and Victoria's registry notes that most organisations accept only the registry certificate. You apply for that one yourself; Queensland, for instance, does not send it automatically.

[IMAGE: certificate]

Once you hold it, no official source we read gives it an expiry date. WA's registry says a certified copy is usually enough to change your personal documents, and the Australian Passport Office links your names with a birth or citizenship certificate plus an Australian registry marriage certificate, with no age limit stated.

Some overseas authorities want recently issued copies, so ask before you order one.

If you marry overseas, WA's registry advises getting the foreign certificate before you fly home, as it can be hard to obtain from Australia. Our article covers [what to check when you use a foreign marriage certificate at home](/expert-advice/overseas-marriage-certificate).

## Documents with a shelf life when you marry abroad

[DFAT's guide to the Certificate of No Impediment](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/how-get-certificate-no-impediment-marriage) states no validity period for a CNI. It tells you to confirm with the overseas authority whether it needs a CNI at all, whether it must be issued in that country, and whether it needs an apostille. The destination then decides how old a CNI may be: the Philippines, for example, normally refuses one issued more than six months before the wedding.

An apostille works the same way. DFAT applies it directly to the document and publishes no validity period for it, and it cannot tell you which certification you need: the receiving authority decides. DFAT will not legalise a ceremonial or commemorative certificate, as its page on [documents DFAT can legalise](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/our-role/notarial-services/documents-we-can-legalise) explains.

The practical rule: order time-limited documents last, once the deciding authority has told you exactly what it wants, and inside its limit. DFAT publishes no fixed processing time, so leave a margin.

If you would rather marry legally in Australia first and hold a symbolic ceremony abroad, see [the pillar guide for the routes](/expert-advice/marriage-licence-requirements).

## Marriage licence validity in popular destinations

Each country runs its own clock, and some put the limit on the licence while others put it on the papers you bring. Rules change, so confirm with the destination's authorities and the Australian embassy there, for your own passports, before you book.

[IMAGE: destination]

### Greece

According to [Greece's official registry entry](https://en.mitos.gov.gr/index.php/%CE%94%CE%94:Issue_of_a_civil_marriage_licence), a Greek marriage licence is valid for six months, and if the marriage does not take place within six months of the newspaper notice, the notice must be repeated. Our guide to [the Greek licence and its eight-day wait](/destination-weddings/greece-marriage-licence) covers the process.

### Italy

For Australians, the order is an Atto Notorio (a statement signed with four unrelated witnesses at an Italian consulate) and then a Nulla Osta from the Australian Embassy in Rome; [our Italy guide](/expert-advice/getting-married-in-italy-the-complete-legal-guide-for-destination-couples) covers the steps. The [Italian Consulate in Adelaide's Atto Notorio guide](https://consadelaide.esteri.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/How-To_ATTO_NOTORIO_EN-2022.pdf) says it is valid for six months, so it cannot be requested earlier than six months before the wedding. That guide dates from 2022, so check with your own state's consulate.

### France

A legal wedding in France takes place at a town hall (mairie), and at least one of you must be a French citizen or a legal resident of France. If you qualify, the [Australian Embassy in France](https://france.embassy.gov.au/pari/Marriage.html) says the CNI it produces in French is valid for only 90 days from issue, and processing can take up to 10 working days. See our notes on [planning a wedding in France](/destinations/europe/france).

### Bali

Your CNI comes from the Australian Consulate-General in Bali. The [Australian Embassy in Indonesia](https://indonesia.embassy.gov.au/jakt/MarriageInd.html) places no expiry on it, but local Civil Registry Offices do not normally accept one issued more than three months before. The Embassy says requirements vary by province. Browse [ideas for a Bali wedding](/destinations/asia/bali).

### Fiji

Short-stay visitors may be granted a Special Licence to Marry, which waives the 21-day notice, according to [Fiji's Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.gov.fj/?p=38738). It lasts only a short time from issue, and the official sources we read give different figures, so confirm the period with Fiji's registry before you set travel dates. See [our Fiji destination guide](/destinations/oceania/fiji).

## Putting the clocks together: a validity timeline for a destination wedding

An example only, not legal advice.

- **As soon as you choose a country:** ask its authority which documents it needs, where each must be issued, and how recent each must be.
- **18 months to one month before (if the legal wedding is in Australia):** the NOIM window.
- **About six months before (Italy):** the earliest you can request the Atto Notorio.
- **Inside three months (Bali):** the CNI the Civil Registry Office will normally accept.
- **Inside 90 days (France):** the CNI's validity.
- **Inside six months (Greece):** the licence or notice window.
- **Before you fly home:** collect the foreign marriage certificate.
- **Within 14 days of an Australian ceremony:** your celebrant sends your paperwork to the registry; once it is registered, order your official certificate.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long is a NOIM valid in Australia?

It has no validity period as such. You give it at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding, counted back from your date.

### Does a marriage certificate expire?

No official source we checked gives a registry marriage certificate an expiry date, though some overseas authorities want recently issued copies.

### What happens if we postpone our wedding after giving our NOIM?

If the new date falls more than 18 months after you gave notice, ask your celebrant whether you need a new NOIM, and recheck any overseas documents.

### What is the best proof of marriage in Australia?

The official certificate from the registry in the state or territory where you married, not the ceremonial one from the day.

### How long is a marriage licence valid overseas?

A marriage licence issued in Greece is valid for six months (couples marrying from abroad may use a home-country document instead). Elsewhere the limit often sits on supporting documents.

### Does a Certificate of No Impediment (CNI) expire?

DFAT publishes no validity period; the destination decides. Bali's registries normally refuse one over three months old, and France (where one partner must be a French citizen or legal resident) gives it 90 days.
